# What the three essays argue

These short introductions describe the submitted texts and the questions a reader can press. Their claims have different scopes: a mathematical example, a bounded reparative duty and a conditional model of recollective comparison.

## E050 Holding and release

**Question.** If we know every long-run outcome while variables are held fixed, do we know what will happen after release?

**Answer.** In the essay’s specified family of dynamical systems, that evidence can agree while release outcomes differ. A suitably ordered release still succeeds throughout the family from the stated initial condition. Identifying the hidden rate and finding a successful action are different tasks.

**Why it matters.** Exhaustive evidence is exhaustive relative to the experiments performed. A complete menu of holding outcomes can leave a withdrawal decision unresolved.

**The remaining challenge.** The constructive result relies on the chosen equations, time scale, initial condition and permitted interventions. Applying it to a real system requires independent grounds for those assumptions. Its broader significance must also be assessed against existing work on dynamic causal models and robust control.

## E012 Compensation and a later defence

**Question.** After an institution pays fairly for destroying evidence, can it still owe help when a later decision misrepresents what happened?

**Answer.** Sometimes. If the institution’s undertaking protected a later use of the evidence, its wrongful destruction can leave a duty to provide proportionate substitute assistance. Payment for one lost opportunity need not repair another protected use. Assistance supports a fair opportunity to challenge a misdescription; it does not establish that the original claim was true.

**Why it matters.** A justified remedy can travel as if it were a finding of historical fact. The argument distinguishes a person’s entitlement to payment from what that payment proves.

**The remaining challenge.** The protected later use and the evidence’s usefulness need independent support. A duty to communicate accurately alone does not establish the stronger duty to help. The essay applies an established reparative principle to a particular continuing impairment; the contribution should be judged at that narrower level.

## E051 Recollection without reconstruction

**Question.** Must recollection recreate an experience before a comparison can reveal a difference between two earlier experiences?

**Answer.** Full reconstruction is not logically necessary. If a justified comparison procedure cannot manufacture a difference from matching originals, differing outputs imply differing originals. Such a procedure can still lose detail and erase other genuine differences.

**Why it matters.** Losing the original experience’s character need not eliminate every possible inference about contrasts between experiences.

**The remaining challenge.** The model does not show that actual human memory meets the condition. Confounds, noise and the evidence for an observed phenomenal contrast require separate justification. Detecting some difference also does not identify its cause or establish a thesis about cognitive phenomenology.

## What the process adds

The laboratory’s record shows how questions and judgments changed under criticism. Its 2,400 primary midterm reviews produced no rejection recommendation, so high ratings did not supply a useful shortlist. Later work tested planted errors, comparison order and the coordinator’s own choices. These cases support practical lessons about checking reviewers and comparing concrete objections; they do not establish which generation method is generally best.
