# The details behind the method

## The October 18–19 selection method

**Current destination:** [Three recommended essays and two reserves, October 31](final-shortlist.md). This chapter describes earlier stages; their decisions and counts remain dated below.

The accepted configuration was `gpt-6-astra`, `ultra`, `default` for both reviewer groups. Each individual review used a fresh context with the fixed full manuscript, common rubric, and its assigned profile where applicable. Planned assignments and observed accepted outputs remain separate records.

Completion is bounded to the accepted package. Earlier capture gaps remain in the record; this is not a claim that the complete history of either provider was captured. Exact visible fragments preserve selected text and its source hashes without exporting full native envelopes or hidden reasoning.

An English index of 121 topics in the October 12 retrospective: decisions, practices, findings, and open questions. Read the [chapter guide](decisions.md) for the shorter route, then [after the midterm](post-midterm.md) and the [current selection](selection.md) for later work. ## Governance and the archive

- **G01.** Methodology was an intended result from the beginning, alongside the competition essays.
- **G02.** The lab adapted a mathematical research framework while replacing proof-oriented assumptions with philosophical assessment.
- **G03.** A broad catalogue of possible methods preceded finite, explicitly selected campaigns; listing an idea did not launch it.
- **G04.** Human direction of process was recorded separately from AI-origin philosophical contributions.
- **G05.** The full archive belonged to the coordinator and the project files, not automatically to a fresh researcher’s context.
- **G06.** A research round ended with a decision, limitations, and a next step, rather than merely a generated text.
- **G07.** Each essay needed a biography above the general conversation log, tracing its own development.
- **G08.** A biography recorded the applicable method and its changes, not just a creation date.
- **G09.** Corrections and new versions were appended; earlier records remained intact.
- **G10.** Logical tasks, individual attempts, and unknown outcomes were distinct states.
- **G11.** The time an event occurred was distinguished from the time it was captured.
- **G12.** A local collector logged available text and metadata without requiring a standing stream of language-model calls.
- **G13.** A live check tested whether the final answer was captured without a subsequent human prompt.
- **G14.** Selected portability tests denied access to original source paths to check whether a transferred package was sufficient.
- **G15.** Completeness of visible conversation text was distinguished from completeness of the technical provider trace.
- **G16.** The collector itself needed repair, including contention and growth-related failures; infrastructure was not presumed infallible.
- **G17.** Inventory counts required interpretation: human chats, subagents, CLI sessions, and internal runtime records were not interchangeable.
- **G18.** Resource policy changed in response to actual subscription limits; model changes and pauses remained part of the data.
- **G19.** A preference for clear language developed into a more explicit author instruction and a separate evaluation question.
- **G20.** The contest ceiling was a limit, not a target or a proxy for philosophical quality.
- **G21.** Reusing a sealed packet was distinguished from conducting fresh research or a new experiment.
- **G22.** Failures and abandoned approaches were evidence about the method, not debris to remove from its story.
- **G23.** Future intentions remained labeled as intentions; unperformed proposals were not listed as results.
- **G24.** The repository needed several reading depths: a short front page, methodological chapters, essay histories, and full retained logs.

## Generating the essays

- **N01.** The first pilot used a complete but simple author–critic–revision loop before more elaborate selection machinery.
- **N02.** AI chose philosophical content; the human set process, resources, style requests, and selection decisions.
- **N03.** Fresh contexts and assigned input packets separated essay lineages; an audit checked actual reads rather than relying only on instructions.
- **N04.** Independent starts still converged thematically, motivating broad fields assigned before seeing outputs.
- **N05.** The second batch used a cheaper setting, without presenting the resulting comparison as a controlled model experiment.
- **N06.** A twenty-essay plain-language batch made prose style a concrete intervention; a bounded reading check examined the openings.
- **N07.** Resource-driven model changes were recorded per affected essay instead of concealed behind a uniform batch label.
- **N08.** Named-judge profiles first worked as critics within production, before serving as evaluators of sealed manuscripts.
- **N09.** Unstarted work could be cancelled while preserving and finishing selected work already under way.
- **N10.** Five fictional worlds supplied unusual thought experiments while retaining philosophical theses, objections, and sources.
- **N11.** Bounded later completion was an actual production mode, with its own configuration and limitations.
- **N12.** The Claude series deliberately included five author-only essays and five with separate criticism and revision.
- **N13.** The version actually delivered to a critic mattered more than the blanket claim that an essay “was reviewed.”
- **N14.** Argument criticism and contribution criticism were assigned different jobs.
- **N15.** After three unsuccessful manuscripts, the contribution check moved ahead of drafting.
- **N16.** Contribution checks could change or reject the proposed claim, rather than merely changing its presentation.
- **N17.** Six methods were allocated before the new results in the hundred-essay program.
- **N18.** Three open investigations deliberately omitted an independent critic; their self-review remained labeled as such.
- **N19.** The idea tournament narrowed 120 admitted proposals to 24 expansions, six research probes, and three essay directions.
- **N20.** Semantic duplicates were replaced in the admitted pool while their rejected histories remained available.
- **N21.** Independent assessments, disagreements, and room for unusual candidates informed selection; agreement was not the only signal.
- **N22.** The coordinator could see the portfolio, while new authors received only their assigned lineages.
- **N23.** Debate meant actual separately written objections and replies, followed by a fresh third author—not one text impersonating both sides.
- **N24.** Close primary-source reading sometimes generated the question instead of decorating a thesis chosen in advance.
- **N25.** Formal models and executable counterexamples tested limited philosophical claims without becoming general truth certificates.
- **N26.** Two lineages preserved a common D0 and explored zero, one, and three critique–revision cycles.
- **N27.** A critic exposed to another branch’s handoff was stopped and replaced, preserving the contaminated attempt as history.
- **N28.** The Astra-to-Luna continuation kept the selected methods but changed the conditions under which outputs could be compared.
- **N29.** Length correction was checked for losses of meaning rather than treated as automatically harmless formatting.
- **N30.** An essay biography tracked ancestry and selection, including rejected directions, rather than serving as a retrospective success story.
- **N31.** Archival failures sometimes stopped dependent work; recorded protocol breaches and later repairs were not erased.
- **N32.** Candidate quality, process quality, resource use, and evidential strength remained separate questions.

## Judge profiles and the midterm

- **J01.** The aim was to imitate particular judges’ intellectual approaches and tastes through instructions and source context. No model weights were trained, and fidelity was not established.
- **J02.** The September set of 16 profiles expanded to the 23 judges announced when checked on October 7; old versions were preserved.
- **J03.** Two profile critics during writing and a full panel reviewing sealed manuscripts were separate uses of the same research resource.
- **J04.** Profiles connected distinctive argumentative moves to sources, applicability, and confidence. Familiarity, prestige, doctrinal agreement, and technical style earned no automatic bonus.
- **J05.** The source inventory distinguished metadata, excerpts, full access, unavailable items, and unread items. Thousands of discovered links were not described as thousands of readings.
- **J06.** Access failures and incomplete reading remained visible, including an error page returned with HTTP 200 and unverified diagrams or formal sections.
- **J07.** Missing evidence about public intellectual temperament remained unknown. Namesakes, anonymous student ratings, and institutional prose were not turned into personality evidence.
- **J08.** Some profiles predicted objections before opening a held-out public review. These were small prospective checks with uncontrolled prior model familiarity, not validated impersonation benchmarks.
- **J09.** Prediction misses were kept, including reviewers proving less stereotypically formalist or more receptive than expected.
- **J10.** Independent rechecks of three later prediction tests produced seven matches and two partial matches. Generic predictions limited what this could show; it was not a percentage accuracy score for imitation.
- **J11.** The human replaced a proposed distribution of judges across texts with a common full panel: 100 deep neutral reviews and 2,300 profile reviews.
- **J12.** A 75-review pilot preceded scaling. Its three length-selected essays did not cover Russian texts or the longest manuscript; those received later technical checks.
- **J13.** Evaluators saw complete manuscripts and bibliographies while production history, biographies, author models, and previous ratings were withheld. Two blinded copies removed explicit provenance lines.
- **J14.** The rubric separated current quality, argument strength, significance, provisional originality, and revision potential, and requested an argument reconstruction and minority case.
- **J15.** Three neutral Luna controls explored whether profiles were adding distinctive criticism. One draw per text and different answer lengths prevented a clean causal comparison.
- **J16.** All 2,400 primary reviews completed, but none selected “set aside.” Compressed scores were treated as weak discrimination, not equal quality; no artificial quota of negative judgments was imposed.
- **J17.** Format checks found many overlong reviews and nonverbatim quotations. Originals were retained, and consequential passages were checked against the manuscripts.
- **J18.** Selection was not majority voting: E078 survived with one of 23 profile supporters, while E096’s stronger panel support did not remove a substantive objection.
- **J19.** Nine selective Claude reviews covered leaders, disputes, and chosen outsiders. The actual outsider sample was purposive, not a random estimate of false rejection.
- **J20.** A critic examined the coordinator’s selection reasoning as well as the essays, allowing an excluded candidate’s case to be reconsidered.
- **J21.** Criticism of the shortlist changed the treatment of E047; earlier reasoning and the correction were both retained.
- **J22.** E036, E050, and E078 received separately registered revision branches, preserving the originals and the predeclared comparison plan.
- **J23.** Preference for a revision did not settle whether its narrowed thesis remained sufficiently ambitious; disagreement over E036 was preserved.
- **J24.** Reversed Codex comparison orders and the fact that Claude chose B in all three early comparisons were recorded. Convenient additional repetitions were not added after seeing the result.
- **J25.** The human’s uncertainty about judging changed the closure plan: evaluators themselves needed diagnostics, and leading claims needed separate literature audits.
- **J26.** A0 validated the test materials before the evaluators saw them. Eight fixture pairs remained TEST_ONLY and outside the manuscript lineages.
- **J27.** A0 found two problematic wording pairs. One bounded repair and fresh validation were allowed; initial failures, residual attribution uncertainty, and presentation confounds remained recorded.
- **J28.** A1 tested four planted error types. Available responses detected 11 of 11 errors and made no critical false accusation in 11 controls; two slots were missing.
- **J29.** A2 found identical within-pair content scores in 23 responses, with plain prose judged clearer in all 23. This paired, explicitly instructed test did not establish the absence of prestige bias in ordinary reviewing.
- **J30.** A3’s 34 available comparisons all preferred R1, balanced 17 as A and 17 as B. Direction was more stable than exact scores; missing slots and lower-confidence cases limited the result.
- **J31.** All 23 profiles detected one easy planted error, but four falsely demanded a premise already supplied in the sound control. This did not establish profile superiority or human fidelity.
- **J32.** Five missing diagnostic slots stayed missing: 125 of 130 were accepted. Internal resampling, transport changes, and unusually long output remained part of the record.
- **J33.** Scale misunderstandings and null scores made pooled numerical rankings unreliable. A later correction fixed an analytical description without rewriting primary responses.
- **J34.** A5 analyzed the archived diagnostics rather than acting as another blind review. The working rule became: use AI objections, but check serious accusations against exact passages and sources.
- **J35.** Three leading texts each received independent Codex and Claude literature searches before reconciliation. Search limits and source-access boundaries remained explicit.
- **J36.** Reconciliation compared thesis, closest source, overlap, difference, significance, objection, access, and open question. It corrected overstatements by both researchers rather than counting votes.
- **J37.** Claimed reading was checked against logs. Later access to a full text did not retroactively change what an earlier author had been able to read.
- **J38.** Dependencies controlled parallel work: fixture-dependent tests waited for A0; reconciliation waited for both searches; the final decision waited for diagnostics and literature work. Shared Claude calls serialized.
- **J39.** One fresh Claude critique of the closure documents prompted responses to four major and eight minor points. Its six-document reading scope was not expanded into a claim to have reread every essay and source.
- **J40.** Closure meant a bounded program completed with limitations: nine candidates remained, the next research queue was not a ranking, E036’s version choice stayed open, and older incomplete capture statuses were preserved.

## Building the Codex–Claude bridge

- **B01.** The bridge was meant to use both subscriptions and distribute heavy research work, not merely pass messages for its own sake.
- **B02.** Codex’s orchestration and file handling were kept distinct from Claude’s substantive authorship.
- **B03.** Fresh role contexts and declared dependencies replaced a shared memory spanning unrelated essays.
- **B04.** Early manual archive gates evolved into a stable entry point for prepared work.
- **B05.** Prepared, accepted, and currently running were distinct states, so a saved packet could not masquerade as a live job.
- **B06.** File origins and hashes were checked against actual saved bytes instead of trusting a model’s completion report.
- **B07.** The number of actions required from the human became an acceptance criterion.
- **B08.** An observed subagent restriction was overgeneralized into a broader transport limitation, adding avoidable complexity.
- **B09.** Validation needed to enforce the meaning of commands while accepting harmless variations in form.
- **B10.** Separate source retrieval preserved available full texts without pretending they were identical to a WebFetch summary.
- **B11.** Incremental file delivery was intended to preserve useful progress during long work, with clear completion boundaries.
- **B12.** Quota exhaustion, cancellation, and unknown outcomes needed different recovery paths.
- **B13.** Existing wrappers were investigated. Superset was installed and rolled back; Sendbird was considered but not installed.
- **B14.** The eventual simpler runner launched separate principal CLI sessions from file packets.
- **B15.** Subscription routing and explicit model/effort settings replaced assumptions about defaults or fallback behavior.
- **B16.** Live smoke tests checked promised transport capabilities; a large unit-test count alone was not acceptance of the research workflow.
- **B17.** Anonymization was refined after practical failures. Ordinary local paths were not automatically treated as personal-context contamination.
- **B18.** Model name, native effort, UI mode, and actual child-agent launches were separate observations.
- **B19.** Bounded, repairable output failures could return to the same model session automatically, with the correction recorded.
- **B20.** A background process running did not imply that a coordinator was continuously observing or analyzing it.
- **B21.** Earlier failures, unused packets, and superseded designs stayed in the archive after the bridge improved.
- **B22.** Claude10 varied criticism intensity, but differences in conditions prevented a controlled comparison of the two approaches.
- **B23.** The saved draft and the draft actually shown to a critic were checked as separate facts.
- **B24.** Model transitions were separate events. Completing an archive did not justify repeating completed generation.
- **B25.** The history needed to be readable enough to change strategy; a stack of technical receipts was insufficient by itself.
