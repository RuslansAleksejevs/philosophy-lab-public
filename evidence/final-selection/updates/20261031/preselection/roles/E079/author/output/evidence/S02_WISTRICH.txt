Exact short excerpt, p.1337, final verdict question (17 words):
“Based solely on the evidence admitted at trial, would you find Mr. Geiger guilty of sexual assault?”

Verified reported data, p.1302: no-exposure 27/55 (49.1%); exclude-admissibility subgroup 7/35 (20%); admit-admissibility subgroup 1/13 (7.7%); exposed total 48. Fisher exact p=.01 compares no exposure with exclusion subgroup; p=.42 compares exclusion with admission subgroups.
Procedure, pp.1282–84 and appendix: independent anonymous questionnaire responses; between-subject control/exposure versions. Exposed judges read additional sexual-history evidence and rule on admissibility, then answer a guilt question requiring only admitted evidence. Admissibility subgroups reflect their own choices, not separately assigned rule-response treatments. No claim of random assignment is made here beyond what these read pages explicitly establish.
Discussion, p.1303: authors consider whether judges who admit the evidence regard it as more probative. Their interpretation is residual influence despite inadmissibility, not successful complete removal by an identified subset.
Earlier version, footnote spanning pp.1301–02: low baseline conviction and no verdict-rate difference; judges exposed to sexual-history material expressed greater confidence in acquittal. The later case altered evidential details to raise baseline conviction. These are distinct versions, not extra measurements of the reported later sample.
