# Errors, Repairs, and Evidence about Essence

## 1. The question

A mistake about what a sentence says can weaken our confidence in a judgment about reality. But how far should that loss of confidence extend? Conversely, when a clearer formulation removes the mistake, how much confidence should return? These questions arise together in the debate about essence. Answering only one invites either an indiscriminate attack on essentialist judgment or an undeserved immunity for judgments that survive a reformulation.

My thesis is that the two controls in this debate must be assessed as changes to **joint evidence**: the disputed judgment, the known error, and the judgment's response to correction. An error can undermine a judgment even when it is equally likely under rival metaphysics. A successful correction can favor one account of judgment over another without vindicating the judgment's metaphysical content. The crucial comparison concerns supported accounts of the process, not just their ability to accommodate an outcome.

Fine's singleton example supplies the target. Under the relevant modal assumptions, Socrates cannot exist without belonging to the set whose only member is Socrates. Nevertheless, Fine distinguishes the singleton's having Socrates as a member from Socrates's belonging to the singleton: the first belongs to the set's nature, the second not to the person's. Necessary connection does not capture constitutive direction. The modal assumptions, including the singleton's existence whenever Socrates exists, are substantive premises, not truths accepted by every theory of sets. [Fine 1994, singleton discussion; consulted author-manuscript rendering, lines 275–333](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/366643157_ESSENCE_AND_MODALITY_Kit_Fine_Philosophy_NYU).

Call an account *modal* if essentiality cannot distinguish necessarily equivalent predications of the same object once the relevant existence conditions are fixed. Call an account *finer* if it permits such a distinction. These are local characterizations, not complete theories. A finer view need not endorse every Finean judgment; a modal view need not identify all necessarily equivalent structured contents.

The controls come from the exchange between Williamson and Fine. Williamson compares the target judgments with judgments about functional terms that, on his semantics, are demonstrably mistaken. Fine contests that semantics, offers a narrower diagnosis of the mistakes, and reformulates the target with variables. My argument grants a semantic setting in which the control errors occur. It does not resolve the original semantic dispute.

The general requirements are not new. Williamson already asks whether an error diagnosis transfers; Fine already distinguishes reference from what a proposition is about. White distinguishes losing a supporting route from losing all justification and warns against treating one's own belief as automatically evidence for itself. Berker demands an asymmetric comparison in a related debate about moral cognition. The proposed contribution is a local reconstruction: these two controls change different parts of the evidence, and their effects depend on what was supporting the target judgment. [Williamson 2025, §§3, 5](https://metaphysicsjournal.com/articles/10.5334/met.195); [Fine 2025, §3](https://metaphysicsjournal.com/articles/10.5334/met.209); [White 2010, §4.1, author manuscript pp. 10–11](https://web.mit.edu/rog/www/papers/youjust.pdf); [Berker 2009, §V, pp. 324–325](https://sberker.scholars.harvard.edu/sites/g/files/omnuum4776/files/sberker/files/berker-norm-insignif-neuro.pdf).

## 2. What a detour control establishes

Let a name an object. Define a constant function d that returns a for any input: d(b) = a, whatever b is. Stipulate that the complete term d(b) contributes only a to the content of sentences containing it. The stipulation concerns its use inside essentialist claims as well as outside them. Compare:

(1) It is essential to a that a = a.

(2) It is essential to a that a = d(b).

Under this interpretation, the embedded identity claims have the same content. Assigning them different essentiality verdicts therefore produces at least one error. Nothing requires us to settle whether self-identity is essential. If it is, both claims are true; if not, both are false.

The control can reveal a confusion between the input to a referring expression and the object contributed by the whole expression. Someone may reject (2) because b seems extraneous to a. But b's appearance in the term does not make the embedded identity claim about b. The detour did not assert a relation to b and subsequently erase it; it supplied a route to a from the outset.

Now let b be a simple name for a's singleton and consider:

(3) It is essential to a that a belongs to b.

Replacing either name by a co-referring name leaves a membership predication. It does not turn membership into self-identity. A simple structured-content illustration represents the embedded contents as Identity(a,a) and Membership(a,b). Term substitution is compatible with distinguishing them.

That distinction does not vindicate (3)'s denial. A modalist may retain both structured contents while determining essentiality by their modal profiles. Conversely, a difference between contents does not establish a difference in essence. There are two questions: what is expressed, and what determines whether it is essential. Neither should be silently answered by an example about the other.

The semantic stipulation also has a clear limit. Fine challenges the extension of ordinary referential semantics into essentialist contexts; he questions whether functional terms' contributions there reduce to their values. Establishing an error in our stipulated language does not settle that challenge. It establishes a conditional control that both metaphysical sides can use if they accept the interpretation. [Fine 2025, §2](https://metaphysicsjournal.com/articles/10.5334/met.209).

Still, an error need not remain confined to its exact example. Discovering that a thermometer is distorted by heat in one room can rationally lower confidence in its readings in another. The relevant bridge is not room identity but a shared cause of failure. The question about essence is likewise whether the feature responsible for a control error also affects the target judgment.

## 3. Neutral error evidence can still undercut

We must first specify whose evidence is being evaluated. Consider a researcher receiving a thinker's reports. Let **J** be the report denying that singleton membership is essential to the person. Let **E** be an unequal-verdict error on a detour control. These are reports about judgments, not the metaphysical proposition itself. The researcher might use J as evidence because the thinker appears competent, while using E as evidence about that competence.

This observational model does not describe every route to philosophical knowledge. A thinker might reason directly from an account of constitution rather than infer a proposition from the fact of their own assent. Explaining assent would then fail to address the argument unless it also undermined its premises or inferential basis. White's warning matters here: one's belief is not normally an extra confirming observation simply because it is one's belief. [White 2010, §4.1, author manuscript p. 11](https://web.mit.edu/rog/www/papers/youjust.pdf).

Let F and M be two particular finer and modal accounts, respectively, which disagree about the target and accept the control semantics. For the following illustration, F endorses the target denial; M rejects it. Let K contain the agreed interpretation and other background evidence, but not J or E. Posterior odds are given by:

O(F:M | J,E,K) = O(F:M | K) × P(J,E | F,K) / P(J,E | M,K).

Here O(F:M) means the ratio of confidence in F to confidence in M. The equation says to compare the rival likelihoods of the **combined** observations, not merely the likelihood of the control error considered alone.

A finite model shows why. Suppose the researcher assigns equal prior probability to F and M. Under either account, the thinker has an equal chance of being in either of two process states: R, relatively reliable, or H, less reliable. Both are fallible.

| Process state | P(J given F) | P(J given M) | P(E), under either account |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| R | .8 | .2 | .1 |
| H | .5 | .5 | .8 |

Assume that J and E are independent once both the metaphysical account and process state are fixed. The numbers and independence assumption are invented for the illustration. They are not estimates of anyone's psychology.

The initial likelihoods of J are .65 under F and .35 under M. Observing J therefore raises confidence in F from .5 to .65. Yet the likelihood of E is .45 under either account: half of .1 plus half of .8. Considered alone, E supplies no likelihood advantage to M or F.

The joint likelihoods tell a different story:

P(J,E | F,K) = .5 × .8 × .1 + .5 × .5 × .8 = .24.

P(J,E | M,K) = .5 × .2 × .1 + .5 × .5 × .8 = .21.

After J and E, confidence in F is .24/(.24 + .21), approximately .5333. The error has removed most of J's earlier advantage, despite its standalone neutrality. It shifts probability toward H, under which J discriminates less well between the metaphysical accounts.

Equivalently, once J is known, P(E|F,J,K) is .24/.65, while P(E|M,J,K) is .21/.35. Those quantities are not equal. Conditioning on a report that initially favored F changes the comparison in which the next observation is assessed. A shared process mixture and equal standalone error rates do not prevent undercutting.

This yields three distinct claims:

1. If an observation has the same likelihood under two accounts against the same relevant background, it supplies no relative likelihood advantage.
2. Equal likelihood of a control error before the target report does not establish equal likelihood after that report.
3. An error can diminish the target report's evidential value without showing that the reported proposition is false.

The first claim is a limited mathematical result. The second and third explain why it is not a defense against targeted debunking. An essentialist need never have assumed infallibility to suffer an asymmetric loss of support. A reasonably trusted but fallible process can become less reasonably trusted.

The model's lesson also has a limit. It assumes a shared process state relevant to both reports. If the control error results from misinterpreting a functional term, while the target is judged through an independently supported account of constitution, the target may retain its force. We need not choose between complete neutrality and complete defeat. The loss depends on how much of the original support used the process now in question.

Nor does a verbal description of a mechanism fix its likelihoods. Saying that both sides allow reference mistakes shows compatibility, not equal prediction. Comparative conclusions require sufficiently specified process accounts and defensible weights. The illustration demonstrates a possible undercutting structure; it does not establish its magnitude in the actual dispute.

## 4. How strong is the actual bridge?

Williamson's argument is stronger than the bare observation that someone makes mistakes. His predecessor/successor examples reproduce the directional pattern of the set judgments. His inverse-singleton term takes the very singleton in the target case and returns its member. Denying the resulting essential identity conflicts, on his semantics, with accepting self-identity. His proposed relevance filter and theoretical comparison are meant to explain why the pattern recurs. [Williamson 2025, §3, examples (5*)–(7); §4; §5](https://metaphysicsjournal.com/articles/10.5334/met.195).

These similarities matter. The inverse-singleton case removes a weakness of an arbitrary detour through a mountain: the apparently extraneous object is now the same set as in the target. The arithmetic cases also show that the reactions are not simply indiscriminate hostility to complexity. A predecessor-based presentation can be rejected while a successor-based presentation is accepted. A successful diagnosis must explain the direction as well as the error.

Compare two accounts of that pattern.

On a **relational-misreading** account, the thinker reads an identity involving a functional term as asserting a constitutive relation to the term's input. Under an iterative conception, 8's immediately succeeding 7 can belong to its nature, while its being the predecessor of 9 need not. The same misreading treats the identity with the inverse-singleton value as a relation to the singleton. The mistaken judgments follow because the thinker answers a different, relational question.

On a **relevance-substitution** account, the thinker uses ordinary explanatory helpfulness to decide essentiality. Material that would not help explain the person or number triggers rejection, including material inside a referring term whose whole contribution is just its value. That process can extend to a correctly understood membership claim.

The first account can reproduce both the direction of the arithmetic pattern and the inverse-singleton error. It is not an arbitrary exception invented for one troublesome example. But its scope is narrower: when a membership relation is genuinely asserted, reading it relationally is no misreading. The second account offers broader coverage, but must establish that ordinary explanatory helpfulness, rather than a genuine constitutive relation or a reference-specific confusion, is doing the relevant work.

This is the significance of Fine's alternative diagnosis. It is a competitor about the source of the control error, not a proof of the target's truth. The control pattern alone does not select between these accounts, because both generate that pattern. It also does not establish that they predict it equally well or possess equally credible independent grounds. [Fine 2025, §3, functional-term paraphrase diagnosis](https://metaphysicsjournal.com/articles/10.5334/met.209).

The same point limits how an account may be broadened. A process acting only on correctly resolved identity content sees no extraneous singleton there. To explain the control error and the target rejection, it might instead preserve a presentation of the route while assessing relevance. That is coherent, but it adds a claim about the working representation. It is not warranted merely by pointing to the term's referent or by invoking ordinary reference tracking.

What verdict follows? The close structural analogy provides a reason for caution about an unexamined target report. It is not nullified by a possible alternative diagnosis. But the stronger claim—that the source of the target judgment has been identified as the same unreliable process—remains unsettled. The competing diagnosis shows precisely where an additional assumption enters: the relevant defect must extend beyond the mistaken treatment of functional terms.

No experiment is required before that assumption may become reasonable. Phenomenological similarity, explanatory coverage and independently credible theory can support it. The evidence available here does not establish their comparative weight. The conclusion is therefore neither a refutation of Williamson nor an evidential tie. It is an account of what his analogy supports, and of the further claim needed to extend that support.

## 5. What survives a variables repair?

Fine's variables maneuver is explicitly limited. He allows other errors, then removes the original referring structure from the essentialist claim and reports that the directional judgments persist. His target is linguistic interference, not every possible unreliable process. [Fine 2025, §3, final paragraphs](https://metaphysicsjournal.com/articles/10.5334/met.209).

Let V be the persistence of the target verdict after such a reformulation. Suppose a narrowly specified filter rejects the original sentence because it contains a particular displayed constituent. If that constituent is removed, with no corresponding cue supplied elsewhere, persistence is less expected under that filter than under a process assessing the understood membership claim. V can therefore count against the narrow filter. That is a genuine comparative use of the control, not merely a failure to prove truth.

But the description *linguistic* does not itself impose this narrow specification. A process might operate on an internally retrieved paraphrase, or use the assignment of a label to recover a familiar presentation of its object. Changing the written sentence need not change that working representation. The distinction between semantic value and cognitive guise is already explicit in Williamson's earlier work; a guise need not be the displayed expression or even something linguistic. [Williamson 2022, §4, author draft pp. 19–23](https://www.philosophy.ox.ac.uk/files/metaphilosophy2021pdf).

Two possibilities then remain. A thinker may understand the membership claim and judge it through a correct account of constitution. Or the thinker may understand it and substitute its usefulness for an ordinary explanatory task for its essentiality. Both can survive variables. Their compatibility with persistence does not give them equal likelihoods or equal support.

Reference tracking alone entails neither a descriptive paraphrase nor the erroneous substitution. The second possibility is not an established diagnosis. It cannot inherit the control errors' evidential credentials merely because it too involves relevance. A reference mistake in an identity claim and an explanatory shortcut applied to correctly understood membership can be different defects.

The resulting assessment is asymmetric. The known control error directly defeats an unequal verdict under the stipulated interpretation. The variables repair can remove an identified cue and thereby weaken a specific diagnosis. Neither directly tests whether ordinary explanatory irrelevance is a legitimate guide to constitution. That remaining question needs its own reasons.

V must also be assessed against the evidence already learned. The relevant comparison is between P(V|F,J,E,K) and P(V|M,J,E,K), with their respective process accounts included. It is not automatically the comparison between the same theories before any error was found. A repair can rationally restore some confidence by excluding a failure state; how much it restores depends on which other failure states remain credible. Neither complete restoration nor no restoration follows simply from the word *control*.

## 6. A residual hypothesis must take a risk

The post-reference proposal would be empty if it could adapt to every result. One restricted version can take a clear risk. Call it H-audience: after understanding the membership content, the thinker bases the essentiality verdict on whether that claim helps the ordinary explanatory task of a currently salient audience. It is a hypothesis about the task actually governing the judgment, not about mere competence with the labels.

Compare it with G: a thinker who has mastered a fixed constitutive account applies it to the understood proposition. For this comparison, G predicts no systematic audience-driven change when the object, proposition, accepted premises and metaphysical question stay fixed. Neither hypothesis is a complete account of human reasoning; their nuisance errors would need specification before empirical use.

A possible contrast would prime two ordinary tasks while asking the same subsequent question about the person's real nature. One audience wants an ordinary account of the person; another is using singleton-indexed records, for which mentioning membership can help locate the person in its catalogue. Both conditions use the same person and singleton, the same membership proposition, and the same question about constitution rather than identification. The manipulation adds no metaphysical premise.

The test must check that helpfulness to the ordinary audience actually changes. It must also check the reference assignment, understanding of membership, and the distinction between helping identify someone and constituting what they are. If the prime changes the interpreted metaphysical question, there is no fixed-content comparison.

H-audience predicts a corresponding shift toward acceptance of essentiality when the membership claim becomes more helpful to the primed task. G predicts no such systematic coupling after mastery and comprehension checks. Stable essentiality judgments despite a reliable shift in helpfulness would count against H-audience. A coupled shift would favor it over this restricted G, subject to ruling out newly noticed substantive reasons and differential misunderstanding. Those alternatives could make the result inconclusive.

This is a proposed contrast, not a completed design or a reported finding. It neither tests essence directly nor refutes every helpfulness heuristic. A process keyed to a fixed default explanatory task could resist the prime; that is a different hypothesis and would need a different test. The point is to restrict a particular residual explanation enough that evidence can count against it. An unrestricted appeal to whatever representation must have produced the verdict earns no advantage.

## 7. What theory can contribute

The defender of the finer view need not wait for psychological measurements. Fine's theoretical reply appeals to developed logic and semantics and to using essence to articulate iterative conceptions and explain well-foundedness through real definition. These are proposed sources of support beyond isolated case judgments. [Fine 2025, §4](https://metaphysicsjournal.com/articles/10.5334/met.209).

Their force must nevertheless be distinguished. A sound and complete formal system shows how specified notions and principles fit together; by itself it does not show that the intended interpretation describes reality. A concept's usefulness in formulating an iterative conception is a real benefit, but usefulness in articulation is not yet indispensable explanation.

The well-foundedness proposal is stronger. If an independently credible account of real definition constrains constitution and explains several structural features, it could support the target direction without relying wholly on that target judgment. But the move requires a credible premise connecting constitutive definition to well-founded construction. It cannot merely redescribe an accepted pattern as essence and count the same pattern twice. Whether the required premise and additional explanatory success are available remains open here.

This is not a requirement that support be wholly unrelated to the disputed domain. Independence is graded. A theory may develop from examples and still earn support by connecting them to other constraints, ruling out alternative patterns, and explaining features not chosen merely to preserve the examples. The relevant question is whether the whole account does more evidential work than returning our original verdict.

A modal account has a corresponding obligation. Simplicity may give it an advantage even after the examples have been discounted, but that advantage belongs to an articulated theory. It must include the cognitive and semantic commitments used to explain the remaining reports. A sophisticated error account cannot be granted free of charge on one side while the other is required to defend every initial reaction. Conversely, allowing both sides to repair their accounts does not make every repair respectable: flexible exceptions can purchase fit without predictive strength.

Berker's related point about moral judgment is instructive. Identifying the features to which a process responds, evaluating their relevance, and establishing an advantage over rival judgments are different steps. In the present case, labeling the singleton irrelevant risks presupposing the very constitutive assessment being challenged. A successful comparison must say which relevance is meant: reference, ordinary explanatory usefulness, or ontological dependence. [Berker 2009, §V, pp. 324–325](https://sberker.scholars.harvard.edu/sites/g/files/omnuum4776/files/sberker/files/berker-norm-insignif-neuro.pdf).

Fine's appeal to dependence might provide a valid argument: if essential membership requires dependence and the person does not depend on the set, the denial follows. But validity does not settle the epistemic status of either premise. If their only support is the original sense of explanatory irrelevance, the argument has not independently answered the challenge. If they have broader support, explaining the initial report as a heuristic does not erase it.

The unresolved issues therefore have definite consequences. We lack calibrated process likelihoods, an established transfer mechanism, and a settled assessment of the independent theoretical grounds. We cannot infer that the target has been debunked, vindicated, or left with exactly equal support on both sides. Nor is a possibility argument about a residual mechanism enough to defeat tentative acceptance supported elsewhere.

The two controls nevertheless clarify inquiry. The error calls for an update to the credibility of the route that generated it, potentially reducing a target judgment's support without favoring either ontology on its own. The repair tests a specified cause of failure, potentially restoring support without excluding every other cause. Their lessons are not symmetrical vetoes. They are conditional changes to a shared evidential problem.

An error is evidence, and so is a successful repair. Their philosophical force depends on what they change in our supported account of judgment. That is why neither is yet a metaphysical verdict.
