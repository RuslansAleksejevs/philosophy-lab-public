# Sources and novelty checkpoint

See sources/retrieval-notes.md for exact bibliographic records, locators, retrieval boundaries and failed access attempts. Focused searches covered associative/cognitive distinctions, evidence of absent animal capacities, competence versus performance, and recovery of goal-directed control.

The original broad conjecture overlaps strongly with Buckner’s implementation/exclusion distinction and Dacey’s evidentialism. The intended contribution is therefore narrower: an explicit account of the separate evidential bridges needed to move from an adequate countermodel, to non-exercise of a capacity, to non-possession within a specified opportunity domain. A worked hypothetical architecture will show why evidence can favor a habit model without discriminating possession from non-possession, and why testing latent capacity can require changing the modeled opportunity rather than improving fit on the original task. A symmetric demand for independently constrained opportunities will prevent unfalsifiable rescue.

No exhaustive novelty claim. Dickinson supplies an actual experimental paradigm; Bouton supplies evidence that non-exercise need not mean erasure. Starzak/Gray situate the conceptual problem; Brown/Key supply the strongest nearby warning against blanket dismissal of absence evidence. The manuscript will avoid adjudicating consciousness or attributing its proposed diagnostic to these authors.

Status: sources sufficiently bounded for a draft; empirical application and originality remain open to a fresh critic.
