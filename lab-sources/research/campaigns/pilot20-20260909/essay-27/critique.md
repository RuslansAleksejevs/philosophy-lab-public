# Critique of “What Eternity Cannot Make Up For”

## Reconstruction and verdict
The essay rejects one inference: unlimited later benefits do not make a reasonable time-specific promise presently dispensable. Its argument is that an assurance gives its recipient a claim to a particular performance, whose terms the promisor cannot replace unilaterally with a welfare payment. This can sustain a meaningful form of friendship without requiring mortality, permanent damage or endless guilt. That is a plausible conditional argument and worth revising. It does not yet establish a substantial original contribution: the main normative distinction has close antecedents, and the link to existential seriousness remains the least defended step.

## 1. Originality and scholarship: major revision
The draft admirably credits the established scarcity reply. But its proposed strengthening imports an established feature of promissory morality without acknowledging that literature. Kolodny and Wallace, “Promises and Practices Revisited,” pp.126–127, explicitly distinguish loss prevention through compensation or warning from the duty of performance, defeasible by the promisee's consent: https://www.ocf.berkeley.edu/~ngkolodny/PandPR.pdf . Their account presents Scanlon's argument; it is not a new distinction developed here. Cite this nearby precedent and investigate the original if attributing directly to Scanlon.

Molina, “Promises, Rights, and Deontic Control” (2020), abstract and introduction, identifies promisees' control over obligations as distinct from their interest in assurance or performance: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10982-020-09380-9 . The draft moves among these grounds as if interchangeable. It should either defend that combination or explicitly borrow the modest claim that valid promises confer authority over substitution, leaving its ultimate basis open.

A defensible contribution is an application: a form of temporal urgency survives complete eventual compensation and legitimate emotional closure. State precisely why these two stress conditions improve a local-deadline reply. Do not describe compensation/fulfillment itself as novel. Whether this application is unprecedented remains unknown, especially without Ferrero's full text.

## 2. Clarify the target and avoid an overstated necessity claim
The opening constructs a compensation argument without naming a proponent who actually argues that every reason is exhausted by eventual welfare. It may be a useful objection developed by the essay, but mark it as such. Oskam and Buben's urgency discussion, pp.180–181, addresses prioritization and deadlines, with possible perpetual regret as supplementary reinforcement: https://www.sav.sk/journals/uploads/0416171510.31577filozofia.2026.81.2.4.pdf . Your description is accurate; it would be inaccurate to suggest their reply requires perpetual regret.

The assertion that the dispensability inference requires reduction of *any* reason to aggregate value is too strong. An opponent could recognize nonaggregative reasons but hold that this particular performance claim expires morally when equivalent support is assured, or that promissory rights become weaker under immortal conditions. Neither position reduces all reasons to value. Replace the universal claim with the narrower point that unlimited compensation does not by itself defeat this directed claim; the opponent owes an additional account of why it does so here.

## 3. Serious objection: permission, importance and motivation differ
The essay proves most convincingly that a trivial diversion does not justify a particular breach. That is a permission claim. An immortal might accept it while finding the incident utterly unimportant in her self-understanding and feeling no urgency at all. The draft's mortal-friendship comparison shows that fidelity does not disappear with repetition, but it does not show that the experienced or existential weight of each occasion remains constant. A duty to return a library book can survive even when it makes almost no contribution to life's meaning.

Section 3 recognizes this gap and gives a credible positive account of companionship. Strengthen it by distinguishing (a) bindingness, (b) value of this form of relationship, (c) justified attention now, and (d) inexhaustible motivation. Argue for (a)–(c), explicitly decline (d). Define the relevant “seriousness” as a reason to organize present action around another's worthwhile undertaking, rather than as great importance within an entire biography. Then explain why a modest individual occasion can instantiate a significant practice without itself being momentous. This would make the conditional result precise rather than sliding between meanings of seriousness.

## 4. Serious counterexample: benevolent substitution
The mild diversion case gives Ivo no independent benefit-based justification. Consider instead a genuinely better, guaranteed future act, whose availability requires missing this performance; Mara's welfare gain is substantial, and there are no downstream trust costs. Does her refusal still control? If yes, defend why paternalistically overriding her choice wrongs her despite the guaranteed gain. If no, specify what threshold or feature permits substitution and why sheer *availability* of unlimited future time never supplies it. The essay need not solve all beneficent promise-breaking cases; it should distinguish available later opportunities from benefits causally obtainable only through this breach. Otherwise “no independent justifying consideration” sidesteps the strongest compensatory challenge.

Also avoid suggesting nonfulfillment logically entails impermissibility. A justified emergency breach remains nonfulfillment. Historical irreversibility establishes the identity of the action, while the promissory argument establishes its normative force. The draft mostly understands this, but several formulations about what later goods cannot make true risk letting the semantic observation do argumentative work.

## 5. Repair and infinite histories
The fulfillment/repair distinction is useful and clear. Keep the possibility of forgiveness, but distinguish elimination of ongoing remedial duties from retrospective justification; forgiveness need not make the original breach justified. No perpetual distress follows from a fixed historical fact.

Avoid depending on whether an infinite life has a completed total. Mathematically intelligible measures can be defined on infinite sequences without an experienced final point. Your argument is stronger if it grants any coherent welfare comparison and then denies that welfare superiority alone settles permission. The paragraph about no final total is dispensable and risks opening an unnecessary dispute.

## Readability and revision priorities
The title, central example, and three-way distinction are accessible. The opening states the question early and avoids decorative scenery. “Practical authority,” “existential weight,” and “significance” need stable meanings rather than more exposition. Some recurrence across sections 2, 4 and 5 can be trimmed after the main distinction is explicit. The concluding phrase “something final at stake” risks reintroducing inflated existential stakes; use a precise claim about an expiring opportunity to fulfill a worthwhile commitment.

Revise in this order: acknowledge promissory precedents; identify the limited target inference; separate bindingness from meaningful engagement and motivation; address guaranteed beneficial substitution; retain emotional repair without historical revision. The result can be a carefully defended application rather than a new general theory. No judgment of truth, eligibility or competitive success is certified.
