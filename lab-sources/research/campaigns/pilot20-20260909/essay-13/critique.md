# Critique: Can Tomorrow Cancel Yesterday's Survival?

## Verdict and reconstruction

Revise. This is a clear and honest diagnostic essay with a valid conditional core. It does not yet establish a substantial new philosophical result beyond familiar reduplication reasoning, and the strongest premise needs a more exact formulation and defense.

The argument is: a unique adequate actual reconstruction A preserves O; a history identical through A's activation must preserve that identity even if B is later activated; equally qualified independent successors must receive equal identity verdicts despite activation order; therefore both distinct successors would be O, contradicting identity's logic. Given bivalent verdicts, fixed reference, the stipulated cases, and the strong equality premise, this is valid. It makes useful room between unrestricted intrinsicness and unrestricted future dependence. The essay correctly distinguishes dependence on future facts from retroactive change of facts.

## Most consequential revisions

### 1. Neutrality is stronger than its intuitive label suggests

Indifference to activation order can mean that swapping activation dates while preserving everything else swaps neither identity entitlement nor some measure of continuity. It need not mean that within an asymmetric history the two successors receive the same identity verdict. A rule might select a bearer by a hidden physical difference, a primitive identity fact, or a non-temporal property that the continuity criterion ignores. Such a rule could respect temporal neutrality while accepting sole-successor sufficiency and stability. The present premise excludes that possibility only through “otherwise equally qualified,” which risks meaning “equal in every identity-relevant respect,” making the conclusion almost built into the setup.

State the needed premise explicitly: in this case, any difference in identity verdict must be grounded in a difference in the specified continuity structure or activation order; with order excluded, A and B must receive equal verdicts. Call it equal-treatment of continuity ties, or explain that activation-order neutrality includes this anti-arbitrariness principle. Either restrict the theorem to reductionist continuity accounts or acknowledge primitive identity as a further exit that does not obviously fit the advertised three descriptions. This does not invalidate the conditional proof, but it matters greatly to what the trilemma diagnoses.

### 2. The strongest priority response is causal, not a race rule

When B appears on Tuesday, A has a Monday-to-Tuesday history that B lacks. A Tuesday-stage normally depends on A Monday-stage, whereas B's Tuesday-stage depends on O's older record. A local transition account can identify O with A Monday, and A Monday with A Tuesday, excluding B because B does not continue the most recent bearer. This invokes the architecture of causal continuation and transitivity, not the mere desirability of settling identities as time passes. The draft mentions causal superiority but has not shown this advantage can be held fixed while one successor lives earlier and continues to coexist with the other.

Distinguish comparisons of O with each activation-stage from comparisons of the two Tuesday persons. Draw the causal chain in prose and grant the local account its best argument: an established continuation supplies a new relevant source for further continuation. Then explain that this defeats neutrality as formulated across activation-stages even if the initial O-to-activation connections tie. This would make the essay a better map of live positions. Do not say it refutes the trilemma; say it substantially reduces the intuitive cost of its priority exit.

A useful test: let A live for a year before B awakens with O's old memories. Does neutrality still demand equal identity treatment? If yes, defend why A's intervening life is irrelevant. If no, identify whether a one-second interval differs in principle or only degree. The current tiny-delay observation gestures at this without answering it.

### 3. The sole-successor formulation needs temporal precision

A continuing person has many later stages. “No other actual successor” should mean no independently generated rival career or person, not only one later stage. But “person” and “career” may import precisely the identity judgments the theory is meant to analyze. Define candidate continuers by reconstruction events and causal paths, independently of numerical identity. Describe history One as one reconstruction event followed by ordinary unbranched continuation, with B never activated. Specify that ordinary A continuation is assumed in both histories; otherwise the claimed first-arrival solution is not yet a full persistence account.

### 4. Prefix matching should exclude the target identity fact

The requirement that all physical, psychological, causal, and institutional facts match is appropriately broad, but psychological or institutional facts can themselves contain identity assumptions: memory rather than quasi-memory; a legally individuated person rather than a reconstruction event. Say that the shared prefix consists of the identity-neutral base accepted by the theory under examination. The origin/process method helps reference, but does not by itself establish that a unique continuant has been designated under overlapping-worm theories. Keep this as a substantive scope restriction, not a dismissal of their ordinary-language claims.

## Scholarship and originality

Gustafsson is a genuinely close precedent, acknowledged accurately. His proposal (11) explicitly controls which branching witnesses lie between endpoints, yet a later connection can still matter. This is the strongest existing account to engage directly: instantiate it in the essay's graph and say which commitment it rejects. His discussion also points to Yi as an earlier critic of scanning and later replication. No need for a large bibliography, but treating the strongest nearby proposal as a worked target would strengthen the claimed advance. See PDF pp. 16–17, notes 38–40: https://d-nb.info/1167625374/34 .

Noonan's chapter 7, section 7.2, printed pp. 126–128, offers a useful additional precedent for the difficulty of specifying intrinsic identity dependence without presupposing who the relata are. It also distinguishes local and global closest-continuer accounts. This was independently read; Nozick's original was not. https://eclass.uoa.gr/modules/document/file.php/PHS688/Metaphysics_Bibliography/Noonan%20Personal%20Identity.pdf . Do not cite this as establishing an exact prior trilemma. It shows why a brief search cannot substantiate novelty of the broader diagnosis.

The possible contribution is an explicit compatibility result for extension-stable continuity criteria, with a careful account of the exact neutrality it sacrifices. Merely renaming premises and listing three negations is not yet a significant advance. A worked comparison with one actual criterion and a stronger analysis of the causal priority response could earn the distinction. Originality remains unverified, not disproven.

## Source accuracy and interpretation

The basic Sider description is accurate, but “changes what persistence claims mean” sounds like a concession Sider would reject. He argues that counterpart semantics vindicates ordinary tensed identity claims while rejecting timeless identity across stages. Present this as an alternative analysis of ordinary language, not simply changing the subject; the restricted theorem still uses strict timeless identity. See section 2, PDF pp. 6–9: https://tedsider.org/papers/all_the_worlds_a_stage.pdf .

Gustafsson's sequential-page locator 16–17 is defensible; specify p. 17 for the future-dependence paragraph. His gap restriction is an available response, explicitly not his favored one. The draft does not falsely attribute it as his final theory, but can make this clearer. Journal issue pagination was not independently verified here; do not add it without checking.

## Significance and readability

The question is intelligible immediately; the opening examples are brief and useful. The three named commitments and the two histories make the proof easy to follow. There is no need to expand toward the word ceiling. “Completed-persistence stability” is long but defined; “prefix” needs a one-clause gloss at first use. “Numerically each successor” and “global individuation” deserve simpler formulations.

Section 4 is useful only if it fairly marks the theorem's scope. Section 5 repeats the diagnostic conclusion and can yield space to the serious causal-priority objection. The title's cancellation metaphor is acceptable because the opening promptly disclaims literal change to the past. The final assertion that stability gives temporal priority a role should be restricted to the tie-resolving continuity accounts actually covered; it is too broad without the anti-arbitrariness assumption.

Preserve the modest conclusion: a specified package is inconsistent, not that future dependence is absurd, persons are stages, or priority is metaphysically arbitrary. No truth, eligibility, or originality certification is offered. Retrieval scope and gaps are in sources/critic/retrieval-notes.md.
