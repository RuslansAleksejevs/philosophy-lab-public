# Critique: What Introspection Preserves

Verdict: revise. The essay has a clear, defensible methodological core and unusually good control of its scope. Its principal risk is presenting elementary implications as a substantial new theory while leaving the evidential version underdeveloped. A precise repair could make the modest contribution considerably stronger.

## Argument reconstructed

Grant that a report correctly describes whether feature F is present after attention. It does not follow that F had the same status before attention. For a deductive positive transfer, excluding absence-to-presence transitions suffices; for a deductive negative transfer, excluding presence-to-absence transitions suffices. Neither condition requires complete phenomenal invariance. Repetition after the initial intervention cannot discriminate histories that predict the same subsequent reports. Thus current-report accuracy and justified retrospective use are separable achievements.

The implications and the two-history argument are valid under stable feature specification, correctly identified temporal targets, and the stipulated accurate report. This does not prove either transition condition actually holds. The essay appropriately does not infer unconsciousness from underdetermination or pretend to refute overflow as a whole.

## Main revision priorities

### 1. Separate deductive transfer, probabilistic confirmation, and warranted belief

The opening says a positive report supports an earlier positive claim only insofar as production is constrained; §2 later substitutes unlikely production for strict nonproduction. These formulations blur three questions: does the inference entail its conclusion, does the report increase its probability, and is its posterior probability high enough for warranted belief?

Counterexample: let earlier presence and absence each have probability 0.5. Suppose P(F-after | F-before)=0.9 and P(F-after | not-F-before)=0.8. Production is very common, yet an accurate positive observation raises earlier presence from 0.5 to approximately 0.529. Positive evidence exists despite highly likely production. Conversely, if retention is 0.001 and production 0.01, production is rare but a positive observation is evidence against earlier presence. The draft acknowledges priors but misses that ordinary incremental confirmation depends on the likelihood comparison rather than rarity alone.

Repair: keep the strict implications explicitly as sufficient conditions for deduction. Add a short account: positive observation favors earlier presence iff its likelihood is higher given presence than given absence; a negative observation favors earlier absence under the same likelihood ordering in this binary model. Priors then matter to posterior confidence. This also qualifies the claimed independence of polarities: the two strict zero-transition conditions are independent, whereas the directions of ordinary binary confirmation share the same likelihood ordering. Do not let “probabilistic versions” suggest the strict asymmetry simply carries over unchanged. This is the most consequential conceptual revision.

### 2. State what the transition includes and what kind of condition is asserted

Written as a material implication about one actual pair of times, “if F-after then F-before” is almost just the desired conclusion once F-after is granted. It does not by itself describe a method or exclude alternative histories. Specify that the condition holds across the relevant epistemic alternatives or is supported as a context-specific transition generalization. It must be justified independently of this positive observation, though not necessarily by a wholly independent evidence source.

Also include elapsed time, stimulus changes, memory dynamics and other intervening events in the relevant transition. Attention may never create F while a bodily fluctuation creates F during the interval. A condition merely about attention's causal power would then fail to license the earlier claim. The essay sometimes says “transition,” which is correct, but sometimes invites the narrower reading that attention alone is responsible for every change. A single clarification would prevent this counterexample.

### 3. Give the framework one real argumentative payoff beyond relabeling

The cueing section accurately illustrates the familiar constructive-access objection, but it currently mostly restates that objection. The negative-report paragraph gestures at a more distinctive payoff without identifying a defended target argument. Either develop a hypothetical with fully explicit positive and negative inference routes, or use a concrete philosophical argument where the distinction changes an assessment. Do not invent an empirical case in which nonproduction or nonelimination is known.

A nearby primary source provides a promising test: Anna Giustina and Uriah Kriegel, “Inner awareness: the argument from attention,” Philosophical Studies 181 (2024):2451–2475, https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11098-024-02188-6 , especially §§5.3–5.4. They confront attention changing the identity of its experiential object and defend a possibility premise about nondestructive attention. This is not the same claim as actual preservation in an observed transition. Your framework should acknowledge this distinction rather than criticize them for lacking an actual-history guarantee their modal argument does not request. Their discussion also helps motivate why feature continuity is different from token-experience continuity. I read these sections, not their whole argument; fuller engagement requires your own reading.

### 4. Strengthen the repetition result without overstating it

The absorbing-transformation example is good. Explain that its force is conditional observational equivalence: if the candidate models assign the same probabilities to every further report under the procedure, those reports do not change their relative odds. Repetition can otherwise reveal decay, recovery or interaction effects that favor one original history. The draft already allows this, so a brief sharper formulation is enough. Avoid “even unlimited agreement” sounding like a general result about all possible re-examination.

## Originality and significance

The broad access/method and altered-target distinction is already present in Spener, and the cued-detail production alternative in Sergent and Rees. The draft honestly acknowledges both. Its plausible contribution is the explicit polarity-sensitive deductive decomposition plus the stabilized-successor example. These are useful clarifications, but no extensive literature check supports a strong novelty claim. The newly located attention argument makes the surrounding terrain richer than the bibliography suggests. The 2024 Spener book remains an acknowledged gap in the author's notes.

For competition strength, the essay needs a result that changes an assessment rather than only naming conditions for an inference. The probabilistic refinement above could itself supply that result: an important difference between the independent strict constraints and the shared ordering needed for incremental confirmation. This is a recommendation, not a demand to adopt my philosophical position. The author should decide whether it improves the intended argument.

## Source accuracy and coverage

Spener (2022), §§1 and 2.2, supports the distinction between accurate access and methodological use, including the limits of extrapolating from forced episodes: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s13164-021-00597-8 . The attribution is accurate. Be cautious with the introduction's suggestion that familiar responses “overlook” selective preservation; the source itself does not establish that broad historical claim.

Sergent and Rees (2007), printed p.524, explicitly offer attentional modification as an alternative interpretation of cued recall and distinguish coarse access from detailed identification: https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/177536/1/5878.pdf . The essay fairly limits its use of this commentary. Neither my check nor the author's notes establish that the full positive overflow case has been answered.

## Readability

The opening identifies the question immediately; examples are functional, prose generally clear, and the length is proportionate. “Nonproduction” and “nonelimination” earn their use because the implications are stated in ordinary language. The weakest sentence is the unsupported claim that two familiar responses “overlook” the middle possibility: replace it with a direct statement of what the essay investigates. Specify whether “definite pressure” means pressure at all, apparent intensity, or spatial determinacy in the opening example, before changing granularity later. The conclusion repeats several earlier cautions; compress some repetition to make room for the crucial evidential distinction. No decorative rewriting or substantial expansion is needed.

Overall: retain the local target distinction and the repetition case, repair the probabilistic claims, clarify the modal scope of transition constraints, and earn the proposed contribution through one sharper application. No truth, novelty, or eligibility certification is provided.
