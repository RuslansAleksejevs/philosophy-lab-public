# Critique: Losing the Concern, Keeping the Debt

## Verdict and reconstruction

The manuscript is clear and intellectually careful, but requires substantive revision before its proposed contribution is well defended. Its strongest achievement is separating three matters often run together: wrongful motivational alteration, responsibility traceable to that alteration, and the present reason to perform a still-available action. The release comparison also cleanly distinguishes a creditor’s normative power from a debtor’s psychological power. Neither achievement establishes that a historical motivational connection provides a distinctive ground of continuing reasons.

The argument is valid as a conditional incompatibility argument. Complete deletion plus present-base dependence entails no present reason to return the fund. Continued obligation plus local moral rationalism entails such a reason. The substantive dispute therefore concerns the bridge and the deletion assumption, not the incompatibility itself. The paper acknowledges this, which should be preserved. My main concern is that the history does less argumentative work than the introduction and conclusion suggest.

## 1. The internalist can accept the entire relational argument

The most consequential objection is an internalist countermodel. Mara has a motive-dependent reason on Wednesday. Leila’s claim persists on Friday, along with moral standards governing custodians, but Mara’s reason does not. The release case changes the claim; the intervention case changes only Mara’s reasons. These changes have different normative effects, just as the manuscript insists. Nothing requires the internalist to confuse Leila’s normative power with Mara’s psychological power.

Consequently, the statement that the two changes should not be assigned the same normative effect does not yet favor reason persistence. The internalist already assigns different effects: in one case there is no debt, in the other a debt without an internal reason. In the conclusion, a theory need not “identify those two losses”; it may explicitly distinguish them. The paper should withdraw that characterization and state precisely what the countermodel sacrifices.

The attempted defense of the bridge also overreaches when it says the obligation’s normative significance shifts “entirely” to others’ attitudes and actions. The internalist can preserve an applicable moral standard for Mara, an appropriate moral assessment of her act, and perhaps her knowledge of that assessment, while denying a practical reason in the target sense. Calling this insufficient practical authority expresses the disputed rationalist commitment; it does not demonstrate that the alternative eliminates every bearer-directed normative significance.

Revision: present the countermodel in its strongest form, then explain why ordinary creditor demands support a bearer-directed reason rather than merely an applicable moral assessment. If no further argument succeeds, describe the result explicitly as a diagnostic of a local rationalist commitment, not a new defeat of internalism.

## 2. Does having once had a reason make a difference?

Compare Mara with Nara, who accepts identical custody solely to obtain access to the fund, already lacks every motive toward return, and makes an equally valid undertaking. On Friday their current psychology, knowledge, identity-continuity, capacity and outstanding creditor claims are relevantly alike; only their initial motives differ. Local moral rationalism gives both a present reason. Present-base dependence denies both. This suggests that the manuscript’s reason-retention question reduces to the familiar amoralist question once its bridge is accepted.

The historical repair makes the issue sharper. Does it grant a reason only to Mara? If so, it owes a defense of why Leila’s otherwise identical claim has practical authority over Mara but not Nara. If it grants both reasons, initial motivation was not necessary, and the repair is an externalist account of undertakings rather than a diachronic motivational account. The current section5 notices causal versus normative grounding but does not expose this discriminatory consequence.

Revision: introduce this comparison. Either defend a distinct historical asymmetry or acknowledge that the argument isolates conditions of obligation discharge without establishing a special mechanism of reason retention. A well-defended negative result about the limits of historical repair could be a more distinctive contribution than the current open-ended suggestion.

## 3. Loss of a reason is not an exercise of authority

An agent can remove a reason by changing a grounding fact without exercising a normative power to release herself. Someone can remove a reason to attend a picnic by ceasing to enjoy it; no person authorizes the disappearance. The manuscript recognizes such cases, but its entry/exit language can suggest that reasons must have authorized discharge conditions. That generalization requires defense.

The relational structure shows why a claim cannot be unilaterally cancelled. It does not by itself show that every fact once providing an agent a reason because of that claim must persist. An internalist can say the claim and the motivational connection were jointly necessary throughout. That is not an inference from entry to exit: it is the original theory applied at successive times.

Revision: distinguish extinguishing a claim, removing a reason’s enabling condition, and losing responsiveness to an independently grounded reason. The argument should explain why motivation occupies the third role rather than the second here, or explicitly mark that choice as the disputed premise.

## 4. The agency reply deserves its own strongest version

The draft correctly preserves general practical competence and distinguishes ability to transfer money from moral sensitivity. But performing the same movement for a reward does not establish ability to act for the creditor’s claim. An internalist concerned with possible reason-explanation can concede physical capacity while denying the relevant rational capacity. This is a motivation for the theory, not necessarily a definition rigged against the example.

Revision: grant the difference between acting and acting for this consideration. State which possibility condition on normative reasons is accepted: possible after non-deliberative psychological change, possible for a suitably different self, or possible through present sound deliberation. The proposed historical reason must meet or reject that condition openly. Otherwise the historical repair simply inherits the explanatory difficulty motivating internalism.

## 5. Originality and scholarship

Hurtig’s drug-induced loss of motivation and historical-base discussion are correctly acknowledged; the essay is not original merely in introducing self-alteration. The manuscript’s separation of tracing from present reasons and its creditor-release control are promising refinements, but priority is unestablished.

A closer missed precedent is John Searle’s “Rationality in Action. A Lecture,” numbered point8: https://www.psychomedia.it/jep/number12-13/searle.htm. Searle argues that undertaking a commitment creates desire-independent reasons and uses ordering and drinking beer before considering whether to pay as an example. This closely anticipates the contrast between voluntary entry and subsequent desire-independence. The draft should directly distinguish its conditional argument and refusal to identify obligations definitionally with reasons from Searle’s stronger position. I read the lecture, not the full book.

Ruth Chang’s “Commitments, Reasons, and the Will” is another relevant lead: https://openresearch-repository.anu.edu.au/items/92727fab-9840-45d2-8a48-b2351a93947c. Its abstract distinguishes commitments grounding reasons from promises triggering reasons under existing norms. Only abstract and metadata were accessible in this review; the former Rutgers PDF redirected. Do not claim engagement with its full argument without retrieval. This distinction is directly useful for sharpening section5, but Searle is the more immediately important omission.

## 6. Source accuracy, significance and readability

Williams’s publisher opening supports the narrow attribution actually made: https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/moral-luck/internal-and-external-reasons/055B6EC455C5A0210BFC59BB7B593FE2. Full chapter was not read in this review. Hurtig’s repository manuscript confirms the reported historical-base and pill cases (§V, manuscript pp22–30): https://www.diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2:208967/FULLTEXT02. Suber §5 confirms deletion of guilt-related second-order motivation: https://legacy.earlham.edu/~peters/writing/selfmod.htm. No material citation error found in those limited attributions. Full source reading and exhaustive novelty checking are not claimed.

The issue is philosophically significant: whether obligations retain practical authority across deliberate psychological change. Yet the valid trilemma alone is a modest restatement of a familiar conflict. Greater significance depends on establishing an additional retention principle or demonstrating why the historical repair fails.

Readability is generally strong. The question, example and stakes appear early; there is little decorative prose. “Present-base dependence” is defined but repeatedly alternates with “desire-based,” “motivational,” and “internal” views: retain one precise target and avoid implying all these families are equivalent. The introduction lists three exits while section4 lists four responses because it restores the deletion assumption; make that structure explicit. “Moral reason” in the opening should be immediately distinguished from an obligation that may supply no practical reason. Section3’s bridge discussion is the argumentative center and deserves more space; later repetition about owedness versus responsiveness can be cut to supply it.

Priority order: (1) strengthen and answer the internalist countermodel; (2) test the historical repair against the never-concerned custodian; (3) engage Searle and calibrate novelty; (4) specify the action-for-a-reason constraint. Preserve the draft’s conditional scope and honest source-access boundaries. This critique is one separate AI review, not a truth, originality, or eligibility certificate.
