The promising contribution is narrower than the initial question suggested. Hurtig already uses a drug-induced motivational change to challenge internalism, and Suber explicitly discusses deleting guilt or second-order resistance. The essay must acknowledge those precedents. It will distinguish reason persistence from blame that traces to an earlier wrong, using an unfulfilled, still-performable entrusted-property obligation. It will offer a conditional trilemma, not an alleged knockdown refutation: present motivational dependence, persistence of the interpersonal requirement’s reason, and complete deletion of the relevant deliberative route cannot all be retained. A historical repair requires a selective discharge account, rather than treating every abandoned desire as binding.

The strongest objection remains that morality and reasons can come apart: continued moral obligation need not furnish a present practical reason. The essay will make that cost explicit rather than assume away it. Novelty is tentative and bounded by the small literature search.
