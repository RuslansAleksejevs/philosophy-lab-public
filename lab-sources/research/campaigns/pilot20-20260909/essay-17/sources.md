# Sources stage

The independently selected initial question has narrowed. The general claim that responsibility practices should sustain agency has clear predecessors in McGeer, while McKenna already permits considerations that override deserved blame. I therefore do not claim either as a new contribution.

The intended essay develops a narrower constraint on escalating accountability: when a blaming process makes a requested response unavailable, the resulting nonresponse cannot by itself count as a fresh culpable refusal. If a person controls the response conditions and wishes to add refusal to the charges, that person must secure an adequate response opportunity or forgo that inference. I will distinguish this from a general obligation to rehabilitate or forgive and test its implications for collectively overwhelming but individually moderate blame.

Sources actually read and access boundaries are preserved in sources/reading-notes.md, with retrieval-attempt metadata in sources/retrieval.json. Principal argumentative interlocutor: McKenna (2019), selected full-text pages. McGeer (2013 introduction and 2019 abstract) establishes close antecedents but remains incompletely accessible. Brunning and Milam (2023), selected full-text sections, supplies the objection from victim burdens and wrongful disengagement. No broad novelty certification is claimed.

The manuscript will use hypothetical cases rather than empirical assertions about clinical incapacity. Its conclusion is conditional on adequate evidence that the demanded response was unavailable, and will address disagreement about that evidence. No source establishes that ordinary discomfort is incapacitating.
