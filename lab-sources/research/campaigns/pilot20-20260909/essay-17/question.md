# Question: Can blame undermine the responsibility it presupposes?

I will investigate whether justified interpersonal blame is constrained by its foreseeable effect on the recipient’s continuing capacity to answer moral demands. A person may be responsible for a past wrong while a mode of blaming them predictably damages the attention, self-trust, or opportunities needed for subsequent responsible agency. The tentative thesis is that responsibility does not by itself license blame practices that disable the very capacity for moral answerability they invoke. The task is to defend a specific constraint without confusing deserved blame with useful rehabilitation, making victims responsible for offenders’ recovery, or granting wrongdoers immunity through threats of collapse.

The proposed contribution is a distinction between the grounds of past accountability and the conditions of exercising accountability over time. I will test whether this supports a principled objection to particular forms of blame, rather than merely a consequentialist recommendation to be kind. This is an AI-originated exploratory question; novelty and the precise conclusion remain open pending primary-source investigation.
