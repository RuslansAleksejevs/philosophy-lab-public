# Independent critique of essay 25

## Verdict and reconstruction

REVISE. This is a clear, restrained, potentially useful essay, but its strongest contribution is presently a diagnostic refinement of contestability rather than a demonstrated new account of agency. Its central example also slides between learning how to realize a fixed goal and revising what the goal should be. Resolve that ambiguity and confront the closest precedents before strengthening any originality claim.

The strongest reconstruction is: (1) a role can confer authority over the operative description of successful work; (2) responsibly exercising that authority sometimes requires exploratory activity before a specific objection can be stated; (3) an arrangement that admits exploration only after a specific objection is established can disable that exercise; therefore (4) an arrangement claiming to preserve that role has a defeasible reason to provide a feasible noncircular route to inquiry. This conclusion does not oppose automation, require personal understanding of mechanisms, or guarantee resources for every experiment. Those limitations are well handled.

The promising contribution is the identification of a procedural admission rule that prevents people from developing the reasons needed to satisfy that rule. The useful normative extension is to contexts where wrongdoing and liability are absent. Neither the distinction between execution and problem framing nor the need for epistemic support is independently new on the evidence presently offered.

## Most consequential revisions

### 1. Make the central example discriminate between specification change and better implementation

The original instruction already includes later disassembly. Discovering that release tears surrounding wood can simply be discovering that a candidate does not satisfy that instruction under the ordinary meaning of successful restoration. A sufficiently complete initial standard already prohibits destroying the table. The workshop then illustrates familiar testing for a previously unnoticed failure, rather than authority over what counts as good work.

This does not destroy the circular-admission argument, which applies even to discovery of ordinary defects. But it threatens the essay's narrower claim to distinguish specification correction. Explicitly distinguish at least two cases: discovering an unrecognized means–end incompatibility under an accepted goal; and discovering a reason to change the comparative importance or scope of goals. If the latter is indispensable, give a case in which inquiry changes an initially reasonable ordering of preservation, usability and future repair, and explain why this is warranted reconsideration rather than merely factual learning under a comprehensive utility function. You need not deny that one can retrospectively describe any inquiry under a richer standard; explain why operative standards, rather than ideal omniscient ones, are relevant to institutions allocating authority.

A revision could also keep the simpler result: a defect-admission rule may undermine effective direction regardless of whether the discovered reason changes ends or means. That is more robust but changes the claimed contribution and title's scope.

### 2. Separate jurisdiction, ability and institutional support

A person can retain legitimate authority while temporarily lacking the knowledge or resources to exercise it well. A homeowner does not lose the right to determine a renovation because a laboratory is unavailable; an author retains editorial authority despite inability to afford an experiment. Conversely, an expert may supply all needed information without possessing authority over the decision. The draft sometimes treats loss of effective exercise as a transfer of authority to designers, which does not follow without an additional premise.

Define practical authority as a combination of decision jurisdiction and reasonable opportunities to exercise it, or distinguish those explicitly. Then explain the special problem with an arrangement that itself selectively blocks a relevant means while continuing to demand the associated judgment. Unlike ordinary scarcity, the rule makes the current standard an admission condition for examining that standard. This institutional self-insulation is the most defensible target. Avoid asserting that designers thereby become the authority in every case: authority may instead be unrealized, fragmented or held by the client.

The four-step argument otherwise risks an almost definitional conclusion: arrangements should supply means necessary for a role they claim to make practicable. Its interest lies in identifying a nonobvious necessary means and a specific way institutions obstruct it, so develop those features rather than resting on the general consistency claim.

### 3. Confront epistemic-support precedents directly

Alfrink et al., section 6.1.3, already propose sandbox exploration; section 6.1.4 includes assistance in learning and formulating challenges. Section 3 recognizes interaction between problem and solution formulation. This makes the contrast with mechanisms merely responsive to articulated complaints too weak as a characterization of that literature. The manuscript does not explicitly misattribute the restrictive view to them, but the organization can leave that impression. Cite and assess the exploration provisions directly: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11023-022-09611-z.

Veluwenkamp and Buijsman, sections 3–4, require access to reasons as well as authority, and 4.2.2 supplies training and resources. Their framework is not restricted to users who already possess challenges. Explain whether your case is an implementation failure under their requirement or establishes a further requirement that their notion of epistemic access cannot capture. A modest application or clarification is defensible; a general discovery-versus-response novelty claim is not yet supported: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s43681-025-00657-0.

Schön's problem-setting and reflective experimentation are a close primary-source lead. Search results from the book preview point to p.40, but my attempts to retrieve a full primary excerpt failed, so I do not assert a verified detailed anticipation. Consult a readable primary passage before relying on it. The essay should acknowledge the antecedent if confirmed, and locate the contribution in the normative admission condition rather than rediscovery of making as inquiry.

### 4. Test the strongest noncircular refusal

The second workshop's rule is deliberately unreasonable, so rejecting it is relatively easy. Strengthen the argument by considering a workshop that accepts an unsettled concern but demands a plausible proposed test and a reason that it could inform the project. That is not demanding the future finding in advance; it is normal allocation of finite resources. Can the author distinguish justified evidential thresholds from circular ones without saying all barriers are bad?

Propose a precise distinction: an applicant may be required to identify uncertainty, relevance, and a proportionate exploratory procedure, but not to establish the particular inadequacy that the procedure is intended to investigate. Even this is defeasible: radically novel concerns may lack an established procedure. State whether the theory provides a presumption, a protected initial exploration budget, or just a negative prohibition against one admission rule. A specific counterexample and response would add more than repeated qualifications about cost.

### 5. Address collective authority and revision without control

The craftsperson is hired by an owner, whose interests can diverge. Who may revise what counts as a successful restoration? The draft's role-relative account can answer, but make the owner–craftsperson allocation explicit. Exploration can identify reasons that the craftsperson must present to the client rather than enact independently. Accordingly, acquiring reasons and making them consequential may involve entitlement to consideration rather than final authority to change standards. Distinguish initiating inquiry, requesting revision, and deciding revision. This strengthens the already good delegation discussion.

## Significance, originality and scholarship

Significance is credible: the paper directs attention upstream of formal challenge, where an arrangement may determine which grounds can become available. The benefit is a practical test for tools and institutions that leave people nominally responsible for unsettled purposes. A realistic case would help establish prevalence, but do not invent empirical claims; the current hypothetical supports a possibility claim.

Originality remains tentative. The author commendably recognizes substantial antecedents. The best remaining candidate is a role-relative objection to requiring findings as admission tickets to exploratory work, compatible with automation and independent of blame. Develop that exact argument, not a general claim that contestability overlooks learning.

Source accuracy is broadly good for the checked claims. Bainbridge sections 1.1.1–1.1.3 support the skill/practice point (https://gwern.net/doc/sociology/technology/1983-bainbridge.pdf). Sennett chapter 8, especially printed p.220, supports reframing in skilled work (https://blogs.ncl.ac.uk/christianmieves/files/2020/04/richard-sennett-the-craftsman-8-resistance-and-ambiguity.pdf). Santoni de Sio and van den Hoven's Tracking section supports reason-responsiveness beyond direct operation and explicitly accommodates changes in reasons (https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/robotics-and-ai/articles/10.3389/frobt.2018.00015/full). These sources do not by themselves establish the new normative inference. Bibliographic metadata checked for both contestability articles matches the manuscript. No citation fabrication found in the checked surfaces; no full-source or exhaustive novelty verification is claimed.

## Readability

The opening states the thesis promptly; the example earns its place; prose is unusually clear for the topic. Preserve that economy. Clarify the sentence beginning “Call the first activity”: its antecedents are distant and readers may confuse the two workshops with the two corrections. Define the two corrections immediately before applying them. Replace recurring abstractions such as “jurisdiction” with “which decisions the person is entitled to make” on first use. The final two sections repeat the point that equal outcomes do not imply equal control; compress one recurrence and spend the space on the hardest admission-threshold objection. No decorative opening or added length target is needed.
