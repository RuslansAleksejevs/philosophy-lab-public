# Separate critique of essay 23

## Verdict and reconstruction

The draft offers a clear, defensible limited claim, but its principal argument has a much closer philosophical precedent than its literature discussion recognizes. Substantial revision should foreground that precedent and identify a narrower contribution. The strongest prospective contribution is the separation between a proponent's responsibility for restricting options and an authority's reason to accept a harmful package under pressure. That distinction needs development beyond the familiar observation that accepting an unjust demand need not vindicate its maker.

The argument is: causal additionality compares a package with what will actually happen without it; a duty bearer can deliberately make that comparison favorable by withholding restoration it independently owes; an unjustified refusal does not remove unconditional restoration from the alternatives against which its conduct must be justified; therefore causal additionality alone cannot establish permission. The authority may nevertheless reasonably accept where enforcement is unavailable. The draft rightly grants restoration its ecological value and does not infer that every offset is wrong.

## 1. The central originality problem

Barry and Cullity's “Offsetting and Risk Imposition,” *Ethics* 132(2) (2022), 352–381, §II, supplies a direct predecessor missing from the draft: independent risk-reduction duties belong in a moralized baseline, so their fulfillment cannot simply neutralize newly imposed risk. Their Business Opportunity example is structurally close to the marsh case. This is more than a general resemblance to compensation theory. [Primary article](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/full/10.1086/716871).

The author should acknowledge that the independent-duty argument is an application of an existing baseline distinction. Explain what changes when biodiversity goods, institutions controlling conservation, and third-party authorities replace individual risk imposition. A promising difference is that one causal ledger can remain appropriate for the authority's emergency choice while a different comparison applies to the proponent's justification. Develop that difference explicitly; do not merely rename moralized baselines “justified conditionality.” No brief search can establish the resulting extension's novelty.

Armstrong's “Global justice and the opportunity costs of conservation,” especially “Status quo baseline,” is another relevant predecessor: anticipated conduct and legitimate opportunities must be distinguished when assessing conservation costs. This challenges any claim that asking whether opportunity costs are justified is new. The draft could use it to identify the distributive issue it intentionally leaves unsettled. [Primary article](https://conbio.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/cobi.14018).

## 2. Existing policy theory is not merely causal accounting

The treatment of Maron et al. is too narrow. Their Figure 1 evaluates whether withdrawal from prior commitments is reasonable, distinguishing resource-poor circumstances from adequately resourced countries. Their discussion includes actual reduction in conservation investment, not just dishonesty about forecasts. The draft's sentence contrasting its concern with that literature therefore risks creating a false gap. Cite Figure 1 and “Counterfactual scenarios,” and frame the contribution as explaining the moral structure and divided responsibilities behind those distinctions. [Repository primary PDF](https://research-repository.griffith.edu.au/server/api/core/bitstreams/c13256db-73d1-583b-a34d-66a16380726e/content).

IUCN's §10.2 attribution is accurate. However, §§5–6 already require considering alternatives, and §8 permits a reference scenario better than predicted outcomes. Acknowledge that actual practice can build normative exclusions into its baseline. Define “causal additionality” as the deliberately thin criterion under examination, rather than treating all offset additionality as purely predictive. Also distinguish on-site rehabilitation from offsets: the flood-barrier example establishes a point about repair generally, not automatically about biodiversity offsets as defined in this policy. [Policy, printed pp.2–4, 6–7](https://iucn.org/sites/default/files/2022-06/iucn_biodiversity_offsets_policy_jan_29_2016_0.pdf).

## 3. What exactly is defeated?

The draft moves among an unjustified proposal, an unjustified condition, and an impermissible destructive action. These are different conclusions. Consider an agency owing marsh restoration whose recreational development independently brings substantial public benefits sufficient to justify its ecological cost. Its threat to withhold restoration remains wrongful, but development plus fulfillment of its duty might be permissible; unconditional restoration alone might be worse overall. The wrongdoing in negotiating need not contaminate every action in the resulting package.

The word “solely” in the proposed principle protects against this counterexample, but later statements should consistently retain that qualification. Say that the withheld duty cannot supply the missing justificatory reason, not that every package proposed through wrongful withholding is unjustified. Specify whether the essay concerns permission to destroy, entitlement to count a benefit, or wrongfulness of bargaining conduct. A short comparison of the agency's options—restore only, develop and restore, do neither—would clarify the distinct questions without requiring a formal model.

The consequentialist paragraph especially needs repair. Restoration alone is ecologically superior to destruction plus restoration, but is not necessarily superior all things considered because development has recreational benefits. Explicitly stipulate insufficient independent benefits in the original example, or limit the conclusion to the invalidity of deriving justification solely from the restoration gain.

## 4. “Adequate reasons” needs a sharper role

In the clear-duty case, the criterion has substance. Beyond it, asking whether an actor has adequate reasons for excluding less harmful options can become equivalent to asking whether the proposal is justified. The commercial case already assumes justified benefits, alternatives, fairness and financing; it consequently does little independent philosophical work.

Offer a modest necessary condition with a clear domain rather than a comprehensive test. One possible formulation is that permission claims must not credit fulfillment of an independently binding, presently feasible conservation duty as if it were a discretionary concession. Then treat genuinely disputed duties as an explicit boundary. If retaining the wider test, supply a case where it resolves something that ordinary proportionality and mitigation hierarchy leave unresolved.

The alleged practical circularity is also not always present. A wrongdoer may openly admit that withholding is unjustified and use it instrumentally, without purporting to justify it by the demanded permission. This is wrongful option restriction, not necessarily circular reasoning. Present circularity as one attempted defense; make responsibility for feasible alternatives the main argument.

## 5. Authority, incentives, and scope

The emergency acceptance distinction is the best place to invest revision space. Separate immediate outcome assessment from the effect of announcing a policy of acceptance. A credible rescue rule might encourage threats; a categorical refusal might foreseeably sacrifice habitat. The draft need not solve this institutional design problem, but its suggestion against precedent should acknowledge that agents respond to actual patterns of acceptance, even if authorities verbally withhold endorsement.

The author should also distinguish a conservation agency with the funds from a regulator unable to compel it. Presently the institutional arrangement is stipulated but unexplained. A sentence establishing separated control and enforcement limits would make the example plausible without a decorative narrative.

## Readability and significance

The opening makes the question intelligible and the writing is generally accessible. The marsh example earns its space. “Conditionality,” “justificatory significance” and “independence of justification” should receive plainer restatements at first use. The final repair discussion repeats an introductory distinction and can be shortened to make room for the close precedent and action-level distinctions. Avoid claiming a general test where the result is a specific limit on one inference.

The issue matters because favorable ecological comparisons can coexist with wrongful control of conservation. The draft supplies a useful account of that possibility, but currently its novelty is chiefly explanatory synthesis. A narrower institutional extension may be worthwhile if genuinely defended. This review does not certify novelty, truth, or competition readiness.
