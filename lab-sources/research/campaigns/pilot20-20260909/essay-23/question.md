# Question
Can ecological restoration discharge a duty to repair without making the original destruction permissible in advance?

Tentative proposal: restoration must be assessed along two independent dimensions: the ecological goods it restores and whether the responsible agent is entitled to treat the loss as a replaceable cost. An agent's duty to provide a substitute can arise precisely because the agent lacked authority to substitute originally. This may explain the moral asymmetry between repair and preauthorized biodiversity offsets without asserting that restored nature is intrinsically worthless. Investigate whether this merely restates familiar restoration objections, and whether a defensible criterion distinguishes exceptional justified development from permission manufactured by promised repair.
