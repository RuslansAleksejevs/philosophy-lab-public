# Question
When bodily ability fluctuates, what distinguishes merely being able to perform an action from being able to rely on that action as a practical possibility?

Provisional thesis: embodied agency includes an ability to make commitments without repeatedly reopening whether one's body will support them. Fluctuation can damage this second-order practical standing even when average first-order performance remains unchanged. The philosophical aim is to explain this loss without identifying health with bodily invisibility or treating dependence on assistance as diminished agency. A proposed test compares bodily options by the commitments they support under available, non-punitive revision arrangements.
