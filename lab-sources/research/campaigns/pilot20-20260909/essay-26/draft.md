# A Body One Can Plan With

Being able to do something today does not always make it something one can organize a life around. A person may be able to attend a meeting, teach a class, or visit a friend, yet be unable to predict whether that ability will remain available when the occasion arrives. Counting successful performances misses what is difficult here. The difficulty includes deciding now, allowing others to rely on that decision, and carrying it forward without making every later bodily sensation a possible summons to renegotiate one's place.

This essay argues that an important dimension of embodied agency is the security with which a person can enter revisable commitments. Such security need not come from a more predictable body. It can come from arrangements that make interruption compatible with continued authorship of a project. The distinction matters because a response to fluctuating ability can improve performance while leaving agency precarious, whereas another response can improve agency without improving performance at all.

The argument does not identify agency with productivity, health, bodily invisibility, or independence from assistance. Nor does it offer a general definition of disability. It identifies one specific relation among bodily fluctuation, practical commitment, and the social conditions of revision. Its central claim is that the freedom to begin sometimes depends on an assured way of stopping.

## Possibility at two times

Consider a stipulated case. Lea wants to take part in a reading group. On some evenings she has enough energy to attend; on others she does not. She cannot reliably distinguish them a week in advance. Her interest and understanding do not fluctuate with her attendance. The organizers want participants to reserve a discussion slot in advance because other people prepare around the schedule.

Under one arrangement, Lea may reserve a slot, but cancelling twice leads to exclusion from future scheduling. Under another, a cancellation activates an agreed alternative: the discussion proceeds using a short contribution she has already authorized, or another participant takes that evening while Lea keeps a later opportunity. The alternative is feasible, understood in advance, and does not make her continuing membership conditional on gratitude or apology. Assume that her bodily course, expected attendance, and others' total preparation time are identical across the arrangements.

The second arrangement gives Lea something the first lacks. She can settle on participation without accepting a standing threat that an episode beyond her control will disqualify her from future participation. This difference exists before any cancellation. It changes what her present decision exposes her to. If she attends every meeting, it was still less precarious to enter the second arrangement.

This is not adequately expressed by saying that attendance is possible in both arrangements. At the time of attendance, possibility concerns what Lea can do given her condition. At the earlier time of commitment, possibility also concerns what she may reasonably undertake before knowing that condition. An action can be available at the first time while the commitment to it is practically unavailable at the second. The missing element is not necessarily strength, confidence, information, or determination. It may be a bearable and legitimate route from a good-faith commitment to its later alteration.

Call an undertaking *securely revisable* when relevant contingencies have feasible responses that preserve the person's standing to direct the project, with burdens she can reasonably accept. Security is relative to a specified undertaking, a range of contingencies, and a background of available support. It does not require protection against every conceivable event. Revision need not mean completing exactly the same task by another means. It can include postponement, substitution, or withdrawal, provided that these responses do not make initial participation a trap.

Three qualifications prevent the definition from manufacturing security by stipulation. The alternative must actually be available, rather than promised by someone unable to provide it. The participant must have some authority over its use, rather than being replaced whenever others prefer. And the costs must include the work of invoking the alternative: repeated medical explanation, negotiation, or humiliation can make a formally permissive arrangement practically unusable. These are distinct conditions. An efficient replacement system may fail the authority condition; a generous promise may fail feasibility; a usable procedure may still impose disproportionate burdens.

## What the existing accounts already explain

The underlying phenomenon is not new. Havi Carel treats bodily doubt as a disruption of ordinary confidence in embodiment and shows how illness can reorganize the meanings through which a person inhabits a world. Her account also allows for rebuilding a form of life rather than depicting illness only as permanent catastrophe. This makes it possible to ask which arrangements support reconstruction. The present argument isolates one mechanism within that wider picture: uncertainty about bodily performance becomes uncertainty about one's continuing practical standing when commitments lack acceptable revision paths. It does not offer a replacement theory of lived illness. See Carel's discussion of altered meanings and bodily doubt in [“Pathology as a phenomenological tool,” sections 2 and 5](https://d-nb.info/1232397415/34).

Susan Wendell supplies an even closer precedent. She describes how fluctuating capacities are misread as unreliable commitment, and proposes changes in timing, teamwork, and forms of contribution. Flexible accommodation is therefore not the innovation of this essay. Its narrower task is to identify what such accommodation can secure before it is exercised, and to distinguish that benefit from increased output. The proposed concept articulates a relation already visible in her account while making its criteria and counterfactual implications explicit. See [“Unhealthy Disabled,” the section “Accommodating Chronic Illnesses,” reprint pp. 167–168](https://ieas-szeged.hu/downtherabbithole/wp-content/uploads/2018/02/Lennard-J.-Davis-ed.-The-Disability-Studies-Reader-Routledge-2014.pdf).

Nor is the claim that flexible planning sustains agency unprecedented. Piotr Tomasz Makowski argues that planning can require plasticity in unstable environments: preserving overall agency need not mean preserving every intention. His argument concerns the organization of planning and the agent's readiness to alter it. My concern is a condition that skill alone cannot supply. Lea may be an excellent, adaptable planner while lacking permission to revise without losing membership. Competence in revising a plan and an environment in which revision remains practically admissible are different goods. See [“Intention inertia and the plasticity of planning,” sections 5–6](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/306319236_Intention_inertia_and_the_plasticity_of_planning).

## Why revision security is a dimension of agency

An argument for the distinction begins with the role of commitments. Some valuable activities require a present decision to organize future conduct. One cannot participate in every cooperative activity by arriving only when already certain that one's body will sustain it. People reserve time, prepare materials, and coordinate their contributions. Even solitary projects involve earlier investments that later performance will either vindicate or interrupt.

A commitment therefore does more than express a desire for an outcome. It changes the situation in which the agent will later choose. If revision is prohibitively costly, committing now can place a person under pressure to continue even when bodily circumstances make continuation unreasonable. Anticipating that pressure, she may reasonably decline to start. A formally open opportunity can thus fail to be an undertaking she can reasonably enter.

Where an available revision arrangement reduces this exposure, it expands the set of undertakings she can enter on acceptable terms. It does so without necessarily expanding the set of performances her body can deliver. That is an agency gain because it enlarges her authority to organize her future, rather than merely producing a result she happens to favor. The inference relies on a modest premise: having acceptable ways to initiate and govern a valued activity is one dimension of agency. It does not rely on the stronger premise that more commitments always make a better life.

The distinction also shows why benevolent compensation can be insufficient. Suppose the first reading group offers Lea a gift after excluding her, chosen to compensate for her disappointment. She may reasonably prefer that offer to nothing. Yet compensation does not restore her authority to participate in the group's continuing activity. Or suppose the group guarantees that her ideas will be represented but allows the organizer to decide, without her consent, when she should stay home. Her contribution may reach the audience more reliably while her role as a participant becomes less secure. Output and agency can diverge in both directions.

A further distinction is between permission and security. “We can probably make an exception” is not equivalent to an established revision arrangement. If the exception depends on a particular organizer's goodwill, Lea must monitor not only her body but also whether she remains favorably regarded. The practical burden has changed location. What she needs for this purpose is a warranted expectation that using the arrangement will not itself count against her standing. That expectation can be supported by informal trust as well as explicit rules; institutional paperwork is neither necessary nor sufficient.

The account yields a comparative test. Hold bodily trajectories and the person's aims constant. Change the feasible and legitimate responses to interruption. Ask whether the person can now enter a valued undertaking without undertaking an otherwise unacceptable exposure, and whether she retains a say in its continuation or revision. If so, the arrangement improves this dimension of agency even if expected performance remains constant. The test is deliberately qualitative. It identifies a difference that a count of tasks or successful days cannot by itself register.

## Is this still about embodiment?

An objection is that the argument concerns ordinary risk. A storm, unreliable train service, or a precarious job can undermine future plans. Refundable tickets are useful for everyone. Why introduce a concept of embodied agency?

The comparison is correct but does not defeat the argument. A mechanism can have several applications while revealing something distinctive about one. Fluctuating bodily capacity can affect both the planned action and the resources needed to revise it. Lea may lose the energy to attend and, in the same episode, the energy to explain her absence, arrange a substitute, or contest an exclusion. An external obstacle need not have this structure. A blocked railway may leave the traveller fully able to negotiate alternatives. Bodily fluctuation can disable the very repair process on which an apparently flexible plan depends.

This yields a substantive requirement: the route of revision must remain accessible in the circumstances that make revision necessary. An accommodation that requires a long negotiation precisely when the person cannot sustain conversation fails its own practical purpose. An arrangement prepared during a period of greater capacity can transfer some of this work away from the moment of interruption. The point is not that every bodily condition affects cognition or communication. It is that a theory of agency must allow correlated loss of performance and repair capacity, rather than implicitly treating the planner as an unaffected manager of bodily resources.

Embodied experience is also involved in the anticipation of this correlation. A sign of fatigue can carry more than information about likely attendance. Under the first arrangement, it may signal an impending need to defend one's standing. Under the second, it can signal activation of an already acceptable alternative. Nothing requires the sensation itself to change. Its place within an unfolding course of action changes. This is a limited phenomenological claim about intelligible differences in practical significance, not a prediction that every person will feel relieved immediately after a rule changes. Learned distrust may persist, and some uncertainty may remain distressing despite excellent support.

The concept is therefore not exclusive to bodily cases. Its embodiment-specific contribution is to show why secure revision must be designed for the changing capacities of the reviser. The body is not merely an unreliable instrument observed by an otherwise stable executive.

## The limits of accommodation

Another objection is moral. Other people also have plans. A general permission to cancel could relocate all uncertainty onto them. If the reading group requires everyone else to remain perpetually available to cover Lea, the arrangement may protect her agency by undermining theirs.

This is a real limit, not an unfortunate detail. Secure revisability is a good to evaluate alongside other goods; it does not automatically establish an unlimited entitlement. Feasibility and reasonably acceptable burdens apply to those who provide support as well as those who receive it. An arrangement can pool uncertainty, schedule reserves, rotate coverage, or reduce the stakes of a missed session. Sometimes it cannot. The concept tells us what is lost when no acceptable arrangement exists. It does not guarantee that every loss can be avoided fairly.

The comparison should nevertheless be made at the level of the actual practice. If attendance at a particular moment is essential to the activity, rescheduling may change its nature. If punctual presence is simply customary, insisting on it may create an avoidable obstacle. Calling a requirement essential is a claim needing reasons. Conversely, labelling an alternative an accommodation does not show that it preserves what participants value. Lea may care about live conversation, so circulation of written remarks may be an inadequate substitute. Her authority includes rejecting that alleged solution.

There is also a danger in making planning the model of a worthwhile life. Some people flourish through spontaneous activity or prefer to avoid long commitments. Severe illness may leave little scope for either. Nothing here makes the security of commitments a measure of a person's worth or the total value of a life. The claim is conditional: where a person values an activity whose entry precedes knowledge of later capacity, revisability can support her agency. Freedom includes declining the opportunity and changing what one values. An intervention that pressures a person into more commitments because they are now “accessible” would defeat the reason for valuing access.

Finally, secure revisability does not restore every aspect of bodily confidence. Pain may remain, bodily attention may remain necessary, and lost possibilities may remain irreplaceable. Nor should restored confidence mean ignorance of limitation. A person can be vividly aware of bodily fluctuation while able to proceed on terms she endorses. This is why a more transparent or predictable body is not the sole route to the relevant good.

## Beginning without being trapped

A practical possibility must sometimes be assessed twice: once as an action that a body can perform, and once as an undertaking that a person can enter before knowing whether performance will be possible. The second assessment includes the consequences of interruption, the authority to revise, and the accessibility of revision when capacity declines. These features can vary while bodily performance stays fixed.

The philosophical payoff is a change in what counts as restoration. Supporting embodied agency need not mean returning the person to uninterrupted performance or training her to tolerate more uncertainty. It can mean making uncertainty compatible with commitments she has reason to endorse. A person can regain room to act because stopping, postponing, or substituting no longer threatens her standing to continue directing a life. For this dimension of agency, a body one can plan with is partly made possible by a world in which plans can be changed.
