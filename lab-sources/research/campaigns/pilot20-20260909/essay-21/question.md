# Can changing a desire compensate for its frustration?

Question: When a person loses a valued possibility and subsequently ceases to want it, what distinguishes genuine prudential compensation from merely ending an unsatisfied desire?

Tentative direction: Desire-based accounts need a distinction between the cessation of a welfare deficit and a benefit that compensates for an earlier deficit. A prospective reduction of frustrated desire cannot itself erase an already incurred loss. Explore whether an intervention comparison with a no-loss baseline can make this distinction without privileging immutable or objectively correct desires. Test voluntary adaptation, manipulated preferences, and desires whose fulfilment can occur only later.
