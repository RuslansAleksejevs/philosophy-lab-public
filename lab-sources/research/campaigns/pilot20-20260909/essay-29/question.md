# Question
When historians redescribe an earlier action through its later consequences, what distinguishes legitimate retrospective understanding from anachronistically attributing the later significance to the actor?

Tentative thesis: historical descriptions should separate the conditions that make a retrospective predicate true from the conditions that made an action intelligible to its agent. A consequential description can legitimately identify a historical object while remaining inadmissible as a premise in explaining its intentional production. The proposed contribution is a more demanding test: retrospective explanations must survive replacement of the later identifying description by an agent-available description, or explicitly identify the causal pathway by which later developments change something other than the past intention. Investigate narrative sentences, contextualism, and the worry that this excludes structural historical explanation.
