## Arthur C. Danto
“Narrative Sentences,” History and Theory 2(2) (1962): 146–179. https://paperzz.com/doc/8013133/narrative-sentences---university-of-oregon ; corroborating transcription https://abuss.narod.ru/Biblio/eng/danto_narrsentences.htm . Read opening and pp. 152–155, 159–168 in displayed transcription. Citation cover gives Arthur C. Danto; second mirror's middle initial is a transcription error.

The closest precedent is stronger than the initial question anticipated: Danto already distinguishes project descriptions from descriptions whose truth requires subsequent success. His pp. 164–165 explicitly distinguish performing a project from obtaining its outcome. Thus neither that distinction nor the idea that later consequences furnish legitimate historical descriptions is claimed as new. The proposed contribution must concern explanatory use: a target-relative test distinguishing identification, explanations of earlier production, and explanations of later historical status. ## Paul A. Roth
“Narrative Explanations: The Case of History,” History and Theory 27(1) (1988): 1–13. DOI 10.2307/2504958. Author-uploaded text: https://www.researchgate.net/publication/274436940_Narrative_Explanations_The_Case_of_History . Read pp. 1–5, 8–9 and 12–13 in displayed HTML, not an asserted full reading. The strongest nearby objection challenges any description-neutral inventory of events. The test should therefore compare claims inside a specified narrative, without presupposing an ideal chronicle.

## Scope
Focused search is not an exhaustive originality review. Danto already connects narrative with action description and references Anscombe; Anscombe's book was not independently read and is not a direct source. Skinner was an investigated lead, not an established source. No historical case study is verified; invented examples will be explicitly hypothetical. Novelty remains uncertain, especially relative to later work on narrative explanation.
