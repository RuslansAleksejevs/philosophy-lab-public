# Novelty assessment and argument development
Broad claims about nonliteral accountability and audience-sensitive commitments are already present in this literature. Do not claim their invention. The proposed narrower argument concerns a speaker's knowing appropriation of a particular uptake, where a reasonable alternative interpretation remains available. It derives an obligation to repair the practical dependency, without retrospectively defining the uptake as an assertion. The difference between interpreting initial speech and justifying subsequent use of its reception is central.

Focused searches also surfaced Elder (2024), Pragmatic Inference: Misunderstandings, Accountability, Deniability, and Geurts (2019), Communication as commitment sharing. Only snippets/bibliographic mentions were read; neither may be treated as verified primary support. This leaves a material novelty limitation, particularly against full commitment theories and conversation-analysis literature. No exhaustive novelty claim.
