# Question
When a speaker deliberately uses an ambiguous expression so that different audiences recover different contents, what determines the commitments for which the speaker can be held answerable?

Initial thesis, TENTATIVE: speaker intention and common acceptance each underdetermine public answerability. A speaker who knowingly relies on an audience's reasonable interpretation to obtain uptake incurs a defeasible obligation either to acknowledge that interpretation as a commitment or to repair the uptake before exploiting it. This is a norm of accountable communication, not a new semantic definition or an equation of foreseeable misunderstanding with assertion.

The essay will test the thesis against implicature cancellation, common-ground theories, and strategic ambiguity. The intended contribution is a precise account of why clarification sometimes counts as a retraction despite a literally available narrower reading, while innocent misunderstandings remain corrigible without admitting a prior assertion.
