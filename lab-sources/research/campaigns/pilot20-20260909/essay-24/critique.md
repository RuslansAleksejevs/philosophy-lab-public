# Separate critique of essay 24

## Verdict and reconstruction

Revise. This is a clear, disciplined conceptual essay with a defensible central distinction, but its advertised contribution currently outstrips what it establishes beyond its closest source. The most promising result is a diagnostic account of institutional change: losing an effective remedy, losing a valid remedy, changing what an official act makes effective, and abandoning the governing criterion need not coincide. Develop that result rather than presenting the elementary possibility of unanimous error as the discovery.

The argument distinguishes an announcement that reports an independently acquired title, one that confers title while violating an instruction about whom to appoint, and an unconstrained designation. Actual agreement and obedience can match across these arrangements. Therefore those data do not determine institutional status or the significance of correction. Likewise changing the arrangement need not change current officeholders. Application, settlement and amendment track different institutional effects; the account does not claim they require different acts or officials. This strongest reading is coherent and avoids confusing institutional validity with moral legitimacy.

## Most consequential revisions

1. **Confront how much Hart already supplies.** Hart VII.3 does more than separate finality from correctness. He describes adding a final scorer while preserving the scoring standard, and distinguishes this from a discretionary game. These are close predecessors of the essay's second and third arrangements and its transformations. The text is at https://deepwrite.github.io/HartConcept/book-original/chapter_07, paragraphs 164–167, page markers 142–143. The draft acknowledges Hart but then treats the three-part structure and transformation as a further payoff without clearly locating the further step. Add an explicit paragraph conceding the inherited structure and explaining the distinctive diagnostic claim. Do not claim novelty for holding outcomes fixed: a rule change that happens not to affect present cases is an immediate familiar possibility. A substantive advance would explain why a plausible way of measuring institutional continuity systematically misclassifies changes in remedies or authority, with a worked hard case.

2. **Separate a criterion of truth from evidence that the criterion persists.** The formulation “maintained in the operation of the institution” does nearly all the work at the hard boundary. Section 3 supplies evidence for easy cases, but section 5 does not answer the strongest challenge. It first supposes universal resistance to correction, then introduces private acknowledgments and continuing application elsewhere. That establishes that universal noncompliance can coexist with recognition; it does not settle universal repudiation or universal sincere misinterpretation of the priority rules. Distinguish these cases explicitly. A recently appointed committee may unanimously misunderstand a charter that the association's enduring amendment practice still protects. Alternatively, everyone may knowingly treat the charter as a historical artifact. What sustains the relevant distinction in each case? State a limited sufficient condition—such as a still-operative, separately anchored amendment procedure—rather than implying that the account supplies a full persistence criterion. Admit indeterminacy where all relevant practices conflict without a settled priority.

3. **Avoid treating all institutional change as amendment.** The principle requires an amendment power or a change in practices sustaining the application/amendment distinction. Consider a sunset clause: the appointment criterion expires automatically tomorrow, without a new act and while the community's amendment practices remain identical. Or a rule makes certification sufficient once membership reaches one thousand. The governing framework stays fixed while the operative criterion changes. This is neither an erroneous application nor an exercise of amendment power. Clarify whether “standard” means the entire conditional framework or its presently operative criterion. Restrict the principle to changes of framework, or add transitions already authorized by it. Also distinguish changing an institution's rules from replacing it with a numerically different institution: the argument establishes the former, not a general identity condition.

4. **Tighten what is held constant.** The three associations share votes, announcement and current compliance, not every institutional fact or current classification. Once certification becomes conclusive, current powers and liabilities may change even if the chair's name does not. Section 4's “No present classification changes” is too broad: the committee may now be classified as possessing final authority. Say that officeholder assignments remain fixed. Similarly, it is the stipulated rule that makes a recount declaratory or constitutive; the recount itself does not reveal its role without institutional interpretation. These qualifications preserve the useful example without suggesting a stronger observational equivalence than has been shown.

5. **Do not let relative error become equivocation.** In the second association, “Mara is chair” is true under the title-conferring rule; “Mara received most votes” is false; and the committee violated its appointment duty. These are three propositions, not one status attribution simultaneously correct and erroneous relative to unspecified standards. Define correctness for each proposition. A short explicit contrast will strengthen the central argument considerably.

## Significance and originality

The practical stake is intelligible: an appeal reform can alter authority without changing visible outcomes, and formal review can survive while losing its effect. But the draft currently asserts these stakes rather than analyzing a difficult instance. A hypothetical reform with an unchanged officeholder, surviving entitlement, obstructed remedy and later amendment could test the distinctions in one sustained case. Explain which mistaken diagnosis the account corrects and why it matters. Originality remains unestablished after a focused search; the present merit is careful synthesis and a potentially useful diagnostic extension. Search leads on constitutive rules and the Hart/anchoring connection are recorded separately, not claimed as fully read precedents.

## Scholarship and source accuracy

The checked Searle passage supports the modest attribution concerning objective institutional truths despite attitude dependence (2006, pp. 13–14, https://www.mit.edu/~shaslang/SPIG/SearleSO2006.pdf). Epstein's historical dependence discussion supports the cited role for past events (2016, pp. 153–154, https://epstein.org/wp-content/uploads/Epstein-A-Framework-for-Social-Ontology-in-Phil-of-Social-Sciences.pdf). I did not independently reread the full anchoring discussion and cannot certify every page locator.

Dörge and Holweger's concealed-heir example and indirect-recognition distinction are accurately reported (§1, https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11229-020-03010-6). Clarify that they reconstruct a position they ultimately reject; saying their category “accommodates” the case is appropriate, but avoid suggesting they endorse the heir's institutional existence in their final view. The essay sensibly declines to refute their full anti-realism. Brännmark's introductory motivation is used cautiously and accurately (abstract and §1, https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10670-018-9994-7). Hart's unofficial transcription remains an acknowledged verification limitation; secure an edition-specific reference before eventual submission.

## Readability

The opening question earns its space, the cases are easy to follow, and the prose is unusually free of decorative complexity. The title overpromises a correction-based theory that the body rejects; retitle around institutional error and changes of authority. Define “priority” as which act or evidence has which institutional effect, rather than letting it suggest one universal ranking. Replace “extensionally identical” with “assign the same people to the same offices.” Shorten the modest-realism section unless metaphysical realism becomes a defended conclusion. Use the saved space for the persistence boundary and automatic-change counterexample. No extra length is required for its own sake.
