# Question
When all competent participants misidentify who occupies an institutional role, can the institution itself still determine that they are wrong? What distinguishes an institution surviving systematic administrative error from an institution changing its constitutive rules?

Tentative direction: defend an account of institutional objectivity based on an asymmetry between ordinary status attribution and authorized correction. Shared acceptance of present classifications need not settle status when the same practice preserves a procedure capable of defeating those classifications. Investigate whether this offers more than familiar recognition, grounding/anchoring, or practice accounts, and whether it can explain the limits of error when all relevant correction structures disappear.

Scope: institutional statuses such as officeholding and membership, not a universal definition of every social entity. Examples will be hypothetical, and institutional validity will be distinguished from moral legitimacy.
