# Sources and revised direction

The source record is sources/reading-notes.md, with URLs, publication metadata, actually read locators and limits. Retrieval date: 2026-09-09 UTC.

The initially tempting claim that status survives mistaken collective attribution is familiar: Searle distinguishes epistemic objectivity from attitude dependence; Epstein separates grounds and anchors; Dörge and Holweger explicitly offer a concealed heir example. Hart additionally shows why actual appeal cannot be necessary for a judgment to be institutionally wrong. Accordingly, the essay must not propose “correctability makes institutions real.”

Revised contribution: a constrained account of institutional error under conflict. A practice must specify which acts report/apply a status criterion, which settle its practical consequences, and which change the criterion. Current consensus does not settle these roles. Test for institutional transformation by holding the classificatory outcome fixed while varying which evidence or action is allowed to defeat it. Different priority relations can change the institution even before any classifications change. Loss of remedies does not by itself erase standards; loss of standards is a different transition. The argument will make the difference operationally intelligible without treating hypothetical behavioral response as an infallible test or a general metaphysical analysis of every social entity.

Novelty remains uncertain. Proposed value is a focused consequence and a diagnostic framework combining, and constrained by, established distinctions. Anti-realism cannot be refuted merely by pointing to causal effects. No claim that institutional validity entails moral legitimacy.
