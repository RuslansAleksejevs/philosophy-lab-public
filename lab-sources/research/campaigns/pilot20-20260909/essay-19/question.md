# Question
When does a machine-checked proof warrant the ordinary mathematical theorem it is presented as proving, rather than merely a formal surrogate?

Tentative thesis: the connection needs an independently criticizable account of which variations of the mathematical situation preserve the theorem and which defeat it. A certificate of derivability cannot supply that account by itself. Contrasting nearby true and false claims can expose a formalization that passes a positive proof check while omitting the intended mathematical burden. Such contrasts are defeasible evidence of faithful representation, not a second proof or a general solution to semantic skepticism.

The essay will investigate whether this contrast requirement adds a substantive epistemic norm to familiar observations about formal verification and mathematical understanding. It will distinguish knowledge of derivability, interpretation of a formal statement, and warrant to export the result into ordinary mathematical practice. Strong objection: once definitions are transparently fixed, contrast tests add nothing to mathematical knowledge and may merely be useful engineering.
