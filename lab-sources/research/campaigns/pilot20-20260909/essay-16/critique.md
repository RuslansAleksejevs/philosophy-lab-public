# Critique: What a Second Look Leaves Unchecked

## Verdict and reconstruction

Revise. The essay is clear, mathematically correct, and offers a useful warning about what agreeing observations leave open. Its strongest result is a conditional one: agreement can simultaneously raise Pr(p) and increase the share of remaining error attributable to a cause whose odds against correctness the check does not change. Consequently, a scalar posterior does not determine which further investigation would be informative. The associated restriction on invoking a check against an identified concern is plausible. The present draft does not yet establish a sufficiently distinct philosophical contribution, and its final prohibition on using evidence to support its own discriminatory assumptions is too broad.

The argument has four steps: (1) partition error into mechanisms with different response profiles; (2) show selective removal and residual concentration under agreement; (3) distinguish overall confidence from support against a particular alternative; (4) limit reflective checking claims and reliability inferences accordingly. Steps 1–3 work within the stipulated model. Step 4 needs sharper distinctions between testing a possibility, obtaining evidence against it, and warrant to claim a test was adequate.

## 1. The nearest precedent is closer than the manuscript admits

Melchior's précis does more than make sensitivity central. Its chapter 4 overview explicitly distinguishes checking p against q from checking p against r; the relevant sensitivity conditions differ. Its chapter 3 overview distinguishes ordinary successful checking from reflective checking involving knowledge of a method's modal features. Chapter 7 already relates bootstrapping to a method that produces its favorable verdict regardless of reliability. These are the three principal conceptual moves surrounding this essay's calculation. Cite and engage the chapter 4 overview, not only the opening and chapter 3. See [Melchior, PDF pp.4–6 and 9–10](https://philpapers.org/archive/MELPOK-2.pdf).

The author need not abandon the residual-error thesis, but should present it as a development of contrastive checking and explain what it adds: a dynamic account of how successful checking changes which contrast deserves attention. Also address the difference between modal sensitivity and probabilistic discrimination. A method can discriminate in likelihood while still returning the wrong indication in the closest error world. Is the essay replacing a necessary condition on checking or merely describing graded evidential improvement? Without answering, apparent agreement with Melchior masks a substantive difference.

A second nearby literature is missing. In their own [short account, Worked Example and Conclusions](https://www.thebsps.org/short-reads/evidence-osimani-landes/), Osimani and Landes explain how assumptions about random and biased errors alter the evidential value of repeated versus varied investigations. They allow evidence to update both the target hypothesis and source reliability. This is directly relevant to the supposed distinctiveness of residual-error composition and to §5. I read their account, not the inaccessible full article; exact anticipation remains unestablished. Bovens and Hartmann's [2002 primary PDF](https://eprints.lse.ac.uk/2717/1/Bovens_Bayesian_Networks_Problem_2002.pdf) is a further relevant lead, bibliographically checked but not substantively reviewed here. A focused comparison is more valuable than adding many remote citations.

## 2. “Checked” is not identical to “confirmed by this result”

The norm shifts between a procedure's capacity and a particular agreeing result's likelihood ratio. Consider a three-outcome test A/B/C with likelihoods (.4,.3,.3) under correctness and (.4,.5,.1) under distortion. The procedure can distinguish the hypotheses, but an observed A leaves their odds unchanged. The person did perform a potentially informative test; this result did not favor correctness over distortion. Conversely, a test that actually produces evidence for distortion remains a real check although it fails to vindicate p.

Specify that your norm regulates claims that a result provides additional assurance against an error, not every true report that the error was checked. Distinguish “I tested for distortion,” “this result is evidence against distortion,” and “distortion is now sufficiently improbable.” Those claims can come apart. This repair also gives the normative discussion content beyond a restatement of the likelihood-ratio calculation.

## 3. Joint learning is not automatically illicit feedback

The final sentence of §5 forbids using a result to establish the contrastive assumptions giving that result value without further information. As written, this risks excluding ordinary joint Bayesian learning. Suppose several possible source models have independently warranted prior weight. Repeated reports may alter the posterior over those models and over p together, even though none of the model likelihoods was antecedently known. Learning the source model from the observations is not automatically treating an inferred p as a fresh observation.

The needed restriction is narrower: do not count an intermediate conclusion as independent evidence in addition to the evidence from which it was obtained. Explain whether this is all you mean, in which case acknowledge dependence on No Feedback, or supply a reason for the stronger prohibition. [Weisberg, §4.2 objection 3 and reply](https://jonathanweisberg.org/pdf/Bootstrapping%20in%20General.pdf) explicitly permits learning about faculties from what their reports themselves make probable. Your account is compatible with that allowance only when evidence can revise uncertain source assumptions without illicit amplification. Do not promise a general solution to perceptual bootstrapping from a model that stipulates the relevant conditional probabilities.

## 4. Preserve the limits already present, but move them earlier

The arithmetic is correct: .891/.9188≈.969743; .0198/.0278≈.712230; .0198/.9188≈.021550. The repeated-agreement ceiling .90/.92≈.978261 also follows given your stated conditional independence and fixed profiles. These are genuine conditional results, not empirical discoveries. The partition accommodates overlapping causes explicitly, a strength.

However, rising error share alone does not establish optimal next inquiry: likelihoods of available tests, possible outcomes, costs and the epistemic objective matter. You acknowledge some of this. Move the qualification on “more urgent” into the opening, and say that error composition is needed to assess further inquiry, not sufficient to select it. Likewise, identify the probabilities as warranted credences under a model before calling their rise an improvement in justification. Mere coherent conditionalization of unjustified priors would not establish that normative claim.

## Significance, sources, and readability

The practical significance is credible: reliable communication about checks should preserve the particular concerns they answered, especially when later common-cause evidence arrives. The originality is uncertain and currently modest: the mathematics is elementary, contrastive checking is prior work, and related Bayesian error modeling is extensive. The best revision is a carefully delimited argument about communicating the scope of evidential improvement, illustrated by the residual concentration result.

Pryor's definition at published p.519 supports the background characterization ([primary PDF](https://www.jimpryor.net/research/papers/Skeptic.pdf)). Woodard's §3 supports improvement beyond knowledge and attention to differing error sources ([publisher text](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/episteme/article/why-doublecheck/6315B213FD85E98657D2D0C93CF9E07F)). Vogel's gas-gauge setup was independently checked at p.518, but I did not fully recheck the dipstick passage. Fernández-Vargas remains abstract-only in the author's investigation, not independently verified here. No invented priority, full-book reading, or comprehensive source verification is claimed.

Readability is good: the opening example immediately earns its place, the calculation is intelligible, and caveats usually clarify rather than obscure. Reduce repetition of the same limitation across §§3–6. Define “epistemic authority” as what another person may infer from a checking report. Replace the opening assertion of greater urgency with its already-defended conditional version. A compact contrast among testing, confirming, and sufficient assurance would sharpen the essay more than further examples of lenses.

Priority revisions: engage Melchior's explicit contrastive account and the error-variety literature; separate procedure adequacy from result support; repair the overbroad ban on joint learning; narrow the contribution and qualify the opening claim. Preserve the successful numerical core.
