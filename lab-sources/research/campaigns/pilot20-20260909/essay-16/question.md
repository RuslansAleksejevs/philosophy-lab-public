# Question
When a perceiver deliberately rechecks a perceptual judgment, what makes the second look an epistemic check rather than a repetition of the first appearance? Can perceptual justification increase without independent evidence that perception is reliable?

Tentative thesis: a second look provides additional justification only relative to an error possibility that the checking procedure could discriminate. Epistemic dependence does not eliminate all checking value, but a check's evidential scope must not exceed the error mechanisms it tests. This may explain both the legitimacy of ordinary perceptual rechecking and the illegitimacy of perceptual bootstrapping without requiring antecedent justification of global perceptual reliability.
