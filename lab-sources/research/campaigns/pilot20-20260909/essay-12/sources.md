# Sources and contribution boundary
Read primary-text indexed extracts of Neil Levy, The Real Spoilers Puzzle, sections 1 and 3 and notes 5–7 (https://doi.org/10.1080/29960126.2025.2527423), and C. Thi Nguyen, Autonomy and Aesthetic Engagement, abstract/opening and manuscript p.14 (https://philpapers.org/archive/NGUAAA.pdf; final https://doi.org/10.1093/mind/fzz054). Read Nick Riggle's publisher abstract (https://doi.org/10.1111/phpr.13045). Exact coverage, dates, bibliographic ambiguity, short excerpts and failures are in sources/retrieval-notes.md. Full-paper access unavailable; novelty confidence limited.

Levy already identifies epistemic autonomy and grants special twist/puzzle losses. Nguyen already explains value in interpretive activity. The prospective contribution must therefore be narrower: distinguish a lost opportunity for work-guided, actually uncertain revision from both consent violation and a decrease in pleasure; explain why inference, prediction, voluntary disclosure and imposed disclosure can consume or realize this opportunity differently; derive only a defeasible sequencing reason for criticism. This is an extension and clarification, not a claim to originate engagement aesthetics or spoiler autonomy.

Strong objections: the value is generic puzzle achievement; imagined uncertainty suffices; an informed rereading has equal or higher value; comparative hedonic equality may mask other experiential differences; irreversibility alone confers no value. These will be addressed with a constructed case and explicit premises, not attributed to authors whose papers were inaccessible.
