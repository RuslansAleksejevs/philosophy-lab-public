# Question
When a spoiler destroys the possibility of discovering an artwork's structure for oneself, what—if anything—is aesthetically lost even when the informed experience is equally pleasurable and perceptually rich?

Tentative thesis: some artworks offer a temporally nonrenewable opportunity to exercise interpretive agency under uncertainty. Spoilers can destroy that opportunity without making the later experience worse overall or damaging the work. The relevant value belongs to an artwork–appreciator encounter and depends on how the work supports the appreciator's revision of an initially reasonable interpretation. This could explain both legitimate spoiler objections and why preserving ignorance is not invariably aesthetically preferable.

Investigate precedents in aesthetic autonomy, aesthetic understanding, and the paradox of suspense. Test whether the alleged loss collapses into generic preference satisfaction, achievement, or experiential pleasure. No novelty claim yet.
