# Critique: What a New Proof Cannot Repair

## Verdict and reconstruction

The essay defends a correct and intelligible distinction, but currently has a more persuasive diagnostic than an original philosophical result. Revise rather than discard. Its strongest prospect is a precise restriction on when alternative support can rehabilitate a challenged route, with a practical consequence for corroboration and reuse. The principal danger is that the restriction becomes true by definition: evidence that does not address an objection does not answer that objection.

The argument is: (1) endorsement of a deduction assigns supporting roles to particular transitions; (2) the correctness or warranted acceptance of its final proposition does not settle whether those transitions perform those roles; (3) therefore an acknowledged objection to a transition requires withdrawing or repairing that reliance, even when independently justified belief in the conclusion and knowledge of endpoint entailment survive. A second case extends this from actual invalidity to epistemically defeated certification of a valid proof. Logic identifies the first defect; epistemology supplies much of the directive force in both cases.

The engineer's countervaluation is correct. It clearly distinguishes an entailed conclusion from an invalid intermediate step. The prose also avoids conflating invalidity with falsity. These merits should be retained.

## Most consequential vulnerabilities

### 1. The elementary example adds no demonstrated limit to existing method norms

Evershed's principle is not required to apply only once, to the endpoints of a complete argument. Apply it to the engineer's transition from the disjunction to p: it immediately produces the intended verdict. This does not need a new normative parameter; it needs an ordinary identification of which inference was performed. The draft concedes accommodation but still describes its case as isolating a further parameter. That rhetoric can make a straightforward instantiation sound like an extension.

MacFarlane's §2 expressly recognizes proof rules and the exclusion of information outside stipulated premises, then distinguishes that project from belief revision (printed pp.4–5, https://johnmacfarlane.net/normativity_of_logic.pdf). Thus an argument that a belief-revision taxonomy does not exhaust proof norms is not yet pressure on its intended adequacy. Explain why *continuing practical reliance* falls between these projects, or describe the result as a synthesis rather than a missing bridge discovered in them.

Revision: make the engineer case a preliminary illustration, explicitly say that Evershed already diagnoses it locally, and move the burden of contribution onto what is gained by distinguishing replacement from recertification. Evershed §§4,6 (https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11229-021-03049-z) is the closest verified precedent.

### 2. Non-substitution needs a hard case in which alternative evidence really bears on the old route

The principle is persuasive when independent evidence is simply irrelevant to the challenged step. But independent success can itself provide evidence about a route. Imagine a checker that is reliable on most inputs but suspected of a particular error, and an independently calculated result agrees with its output. Depending on the error model, that agreement raises the probability that this run was correct. Repeated independent tests may restore reasonable trust without rechecking every internal step. A different proof can also establish a general equivalence theorem that certifies the old derivation wholesale.

These possibilities do not vindicate arbitrary bad proofs; they show that independence of source is different from irrelevance to route correctness. “Merely by supporting the conclusion” excludes them only if the essay supplies a criterion for the exclusion. Otherwise the thesis says little beyond “irrelevant evidence is irrelevant.”

Revision: distinguish evidence for c, evidence that r is correct, and evidence that r supplies an independent confirmation of c. Allow the same item to play the first two roles when an explanatory or probabilistic connection is independently warranted. What it cannot automatically do is the third: rehabilitating r through c's other proof may destroy the independence needed to count r as additional corroboration. Develop this dependence asymmetry rather than an unconditional ban on rehabilitation by new proofs. Clarify whether independent confirmation is meant statistically, evidentially or just as a second derivation; the current manuscript slides among these.

### 3. The proposed normative object remains ambiguous

At different points “route” means a displayed derivation, a historical inferential episode, present basing, a method reused on new inputs, and certification of an opaque artifact. These objects can come apart. An engineer might stop believing that the recorded proof works while retaining the same conclusion through a measurement. Her belief revision is then entirely ordinary at the metalevel. Or she may preserve an old derivation as a heuristic, without treating it as a proof. Neither violates the intended norm.

The encoding objection is handled sensibly, but conceding that all relevant states can be represented propositionally means the conclusion should be about the necessary *content* of an adequate account, not a new irreducible normative object. Specify present endorsement for a particular deductive purpose as the target. A historical error need not be corrected in every stored copy once endorsement stops.

### 4. Two kinds of objection require different repair conditions

An acknowledged invalid step is an objective failure of the displayed deduction. A checker warning defeats entitlement only relative to an agent's evidence. The latter can disappear through evidence about the checker even if no proof changes. Accordingly, saying that both cases require “repair” obscures the difference between revising a derivation and restoring warrant for an unchanged derivation. The essay already recognizes the distinction but should build it into the principle explicitly. Do not let the concrete invalidity example do argumentative work for disputed higher-order defeat; the stipulation gives an application, not a defense of defeat theory.

Montessori §1 provides an appropriate nearby discussion of sole-ground deduction and misleading criticism (https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10670-025-01024-5). The current non-counterexample disclaimer is accurate and worth keeping.

## Significance, originality and scholarship

The practical significance is strongest where users must decide which assumptions can be removed and whether two proofs furnish additional assurance. Develop one such consequence in detail rather than repeatedly restating that correctness does not validate a bad proof. The contribution is presently a useful synthesis of basing, invalidity norms and proof repair; originality beyond that remains uncertain.

Boghossian's connection to basing is accurately and cautiously presented (§§2–3, https://paulboghossian.com/pb/files/WhatIsInference.pdf). Hamami's publisher abstract supports the limited attribution (https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/10.1086/715137). However, the full author preprint is publicly discoverable at https://www.yacinhamami.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/12/Hamami-Proofs-Reliable-Processes-and-Justification-in-Mathematics-2020-preprint.pdf . Consult relevant portions before relying on abstract-level distance from this precedent; note its version. I did not read that full preprint. No search establishes novelty.

## Readability and revision order

The opening states the question clearly; the circuit example earns its place. Sections 3–4 repeat replacement-versus-vindication several times. Compress this repetition to make room for the counterexample about corroboration and recertification. Define “route” once in ordinary terms before extending it. “Endpoint taxonomy,” “non-substitution,” and “dependence structure” are manageable only when tied to the example; avoid multiplying these labels.

Priority order: narrow the claimed advance over Evershed; test and qualify non-substitution against evidence that bears on route correctness; specify the target as current deductive endorsement; distinguish correction from recertification; deepen one practical consequence and cut repetition. No manuscript edits made by this critic. The verdict is substantive criticism, not certification of truth, novelty or eligibility.
