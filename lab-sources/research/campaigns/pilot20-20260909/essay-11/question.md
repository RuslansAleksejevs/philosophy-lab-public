# Question: What must change when a valid inference loses its warrant?

Can logic rationally require an agent to abandon an inferential basis while permitting exactly the same premises and conclusion to remain believed? I will investigate whether bridge principles connecting consequence to permissible belief configurations omit a distinct normative object: the agent's dependence claim that these premises support this conclusion in this reasoning episode. The tentative thesis is that acknowledged deductive commitments create an obligation either to maintain an undefeated entitlement to the inference route or to withdraw reliance on that route, even when no belief revision is appropriate. This would supplement, rather than replace, familiar wide-scope constraints. Main risks: the result may already be captured by basing theories or norms of reasoning; invalid arguments can have inductive merit; epistemic defeat of an inference must not be confused with semantic invalidity.
