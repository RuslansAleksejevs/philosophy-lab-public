# Separate critique: When Only Believers Count

## Verdict and reconstruction

The essay has a clear, defensible restricted argument. It should be revised rather than set aside. Its strongest result concerns the evidential history of participation: a practice cannot justify a fresh claim of successful convergence merely by retrospectively defining successful participants as those who converge. The draft is already unusually careful about immediate religious warrant, practical persistence, and the fallibility of former participants. The main outstanding problems are the modest originality of the elementary selection argument and uncertainty about what positive evidential standing its conclusion grants.

The best reconstruction is: (1) participation may be necessary for epistemic access; (2) a further inference from the outcomes of qualified participation to the practice's reliability depends on how outcomes are selected; (3) if continued assent alone selects the reports counted, unanimity is guaranteed whenever the selected group is nonempty; (4) that guaranteed unanimity cannot itself supply the advertised additional confirmation; (5) therefore subsequent dissent alone cannot erase an initially qualified participant's relevance to this particular assessment. This does not refute the doctrine, make every dissenter an expert, or establish that the unfiltered record supports religious belief.

The genuine prospective contribution is the distinction between an access requirement and a retrospective rule of testimonial eligibility, especially applied to people whose participation subsequently changed their minds. The distinction between commitment and credence is background from Buchak; selection bias itself is background. The manuscript acknowledges both, appropriately.

## Most consequential revisions

### 1. Separate three things now grouped under evidential standing

Preserving a person's historical outcome, crediting the accuracy of their report, and assigning authority to their interpretation are different. The first follows most directly from the selection argument. The latter two require further premises. Suppose a once-qualified contemplative accurately reports a past powerful experience, but now incorrectly denies a doctrine after accepting an unrelated historical error. Their outcome belongs in the trajectory record. Their new interpretation need not receive equal or even appreciable weight about the earlier experience's nature.

The draft generally allows unequal weighting, but phrases such as former participants possessing standing outsiders lack can suggest a stronger testimonial privilege than its argument establishes. State explicitly that inclusion as a relevant outcome is not certification as a reliable retrospective narrator or interpreter. Describe what must be retained: initially established qualifications, participation undertaken, experienced result, subsequent judgment, and reasons for revising it where available. This makes the result useful without pretending to solve the whole epistemology of testimony.

### 2. Handle delayed success and attrition as the strongest competence objection

The current objection about changed standards is good but comparatively abstract. Consider a practice that antecedently predicts ten years of uninterrupted participation before the relevant capacity appears. Someone leaves after five years because nothing happens. Their experience is a result of entering the program, but is not an instance of completing the specified access procedure. Excluding them from a claim about ten-year completers can be legitimate, even if leaving reflects dissent.

Conversely, if the community cites success among completers to recommend starting the practice, attrition becomes relevant to the chance that starting delivers the promised learning. The essay can extract a stronger claim-specific result here: the appropriate denominator depends on whether the claim concerns entering, completing, or currently exercising a practice. A record can be informative for the third while misleading for the first. Introduce this example and distinguish independently specified duration/completion standards from retrospective standards that expand whenever dissent occurs. Do not assume that eligibility at entry establishes eventual competence.

### 3. Make the precise loss of confirmation explicit

The numerical example shows that a selected percentage hides variation. It does not show that every fact about the selected group is evidentially inert. Eighty affirmative reports rather than twenty may remain informative if the initial cohort size is known; the contents and independence of reports may matter too. The essay already protects individual evidence, but should identify its target as the bare unanimity statistic conditional on selection for affirmation, not the whole selected body of testimony.

Also acknowledge that full retention of dissent is only a necessary repair. Both communities might undergo the same suggestion, share one interpretive source, or be exposed to common pressure. The first community's inclusive record does not establish reliability. One direct sentence would prevent readers from taking openness as a sufficient religious epistemology.

### 4. Strengthen the account of an acceptable reason to exclude

The manuscript alternates between a reason beyond dissent and a reason identifiable without assuming the contested claim. These differ. A doctrinally internal explanation may offer rich detail while depending entirely on the conclusion supposedly being independently confirmed. Conversely, expertise often reasonably uses background claims that are themselves open to dispute.

A workable refinement is local: the exclusion must not receive all its justificatory support from the very convergence claim it helps manufacture. This permits internal criteria and ordinary background knowledge while exposing circular dependence. Supply a positive religious example, even hypothetical: a witness demonstrably practiced a different exercise or a teacher certified attention by an independently observable task before learning the witness's verdict. Contrast with a defect inferred solely from unbelief. Avoid promising a universal neutral test of religious competence.

## Originality and literature

There is a close omitted precedent in C. Thi Nguyen, “Echo Chambers and Epistemic Bubbles,” DOI https://doi.org/10.1017/epi.2018.32. I read the author penultimate draft's pp.1–5: https://objectionable.net/wp-content/uploads/2018/09/nguyen-echo-chambers-and-epistemic-bubbles.pdf. Nguyen distinguishes missing voices from actively undermined voices and treats dependence on approved insiders as a structural epistemic problem. This substantially precedes the manuscript's general institutional-discrediting diagnosis. It does not, in the passages checked, settle the exact access/retrospective-eligibility asymmetry. Add a short acknowledgment and articulate that narrower difference. Do not inflate the paper into a comprehensive echo-chamber theory.

Alston's Perceiving God is a relevant lead for practice-based justification and circularity, but I accessed only search-level contents/previews, not the needed chapters: https://www.jstor.org/stable/10.7591/j.ctt5hh24c. An author should not add detailed claims about Alston without reading. The most efficient improvement is precise engagement with the accessible Nguyen text and an explicit statement that this essay addresses a particular extra inference, not every form of practice-dependent justification. No priority or novelty verdict is established by my focused searches.

## Significance and readability

The question and stakes are clear immediately. The matched communities are effective, and terminology is mostly explained. The prose does not need decorative expansion. Sections 3–5 repeat the restriction that dissent alone cannot invalidate participation; consolidate some repetition to make space for delayed completion, the three meanings of standing, and the literature comparison. Explain “vindication” once as a further reason to regard the practice as reliable, or use the latter phrase.

The contribution would become more significant by showing how one and the same filtered record can legitimately support a narrowly conditional claim while misleadingly supporting an invitation to undertake the practice. That develops the existing argument rather than simply restating a familiar warning about self-confirmation. Preserve the concluding restraint: the argument defeats a particular inference and restores information; it neither adjudicates God's existence nor establishes the reliability of dissenters.
