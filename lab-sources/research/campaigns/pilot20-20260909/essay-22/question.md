# Question
When religious practice makes evidence available only to committed participants, what distinguishes legitimate learning through faith from manufacturing confirmation?

# Initial thesis (TENTATIVE)
A practical permission to undertake faith does not itself confer evidential authority on its products. Nevertheless, participation-dependent evidence need not be disqualified as circular. Its additional warrant depends on whether the evidence-producing practice preserves possibilities of recognizable correction: experiences may be caused by commitment yet discriminate between the religious claim and relevant alternatives. The central contribution will be an argument for an asymmetry between permission to begin and permission to close inquiry, with attention to the question whether faith can require removal of the very exit conditions that justified its experiment.

# Plan
Read James's defense of faith-created access and contemporary primary work on non-doxastic faith and epistemic circularity. Test the proposed constraint against trusting relationships, transformative religious experience, and objections about external standards. Originality unestablished.
