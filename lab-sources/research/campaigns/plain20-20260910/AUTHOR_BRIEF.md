# Independent first-essay pilot

This is shared, argument-neutral process guidance. The user explicitly authorized
twenty new carefully developed essays, preserving every draft, with no mutual
influence. Your assignment is ONE essay. The coordinator assigns a different broad philosophical search area to each essay before reading any results. Your exact task records that AI-originated area, not a supplied thesis.

## Content and quality

Within your assigned broad area, freely choose your own philosophical question, thesis, examples and arguments.
Work seriously toward a philosophically significant, original, well-defended
contribution. Originality is something to investigate against relevant literature,
not announce without support. No human-supplied philosophical argument is present
in this brief. Do not consult the user's historical planning chats or idea catalogs.

Write a complete scholarly essay in English. No minimum word count and no target
near the ceiling: use the space needed for an intelligible contribution, explicit
reasoning, defended premises and serious objections. The final essay must not
exceed6000 words excluding bibliography; all other text counts. Do not substitute
an abstract, bullet outline, research proposal or collection of slogans for an essay.
Avoid padding and avoid cutting necessary argument. Choose appropriate structure
yourself. Keep author names, laboratory machinery and grading instructions out of
the manuscript. Use an English `## References` heading for the bibliography.

## Isolation

You receive no parent conversation (fork_turns=none). Read this brief, campaign.json,
the neutral snapshots below and only files under your own essay-NN directory.
Use public primary philosophical sources relevant to your own independently
chosen argument. Do not list/read sibling directories, shared chat transcripts,
other agent messages/statuses, global knowledge/resume.md, research/views, historical
intake or other manuscripts. Do not communicate with another essay's author/critic.
Do not use tools to inspect other tasks. Root's helper may mechanically validate
the shared archive; it prints only your own hashes/IDs, not other contributions.

Use the same process regardless of assignment number. Root will not give you outputs or feedback from other essays. Each essay receives its own
fresh critic, who reads only that essay's packet. General common instructions,
the same model family and public literature can still create similarities; process
separation is not a proof of statistical or intellectual independence.

## Sources

Read and verify the primary works actually needed. Search for serious existing
versions of your proposed contribution and the strongest nearby objections.
Record exact bibliographic facts, URLs, retrieval times and section/page locators.
Mark inaccessible material as inaccessible; do not present search snippets as
full reading. Preserve safe compliant source excerpts/retrieval notes under your
own sources/ directory. Respect quotation/paraphrase limits; make most of the
essay your own reasoning. Do not invent references, quotations, precision or novelty.

## Stages and preservation

Use scripts/plain20_artifacts.py (operational instructions in OPERATIONS.md) to
start your unique round and record each exact artifact before dependent work.
Do not overwrite existing question.md, sources.md, draft.md, critique.md or final.md.
If a repair/revision is needed, create an explicitly versioned new file and tell
root. The canonical archive is append-only and all manuscripts are retained.

Author phase A:
1. Select and articulate the question; write question.md. Record question stage.
2. Investigate sources and novelty; write sources.md and source notes. Record sources.
3. Develop the complete essay; write draft.md. Record contribution and candidate birth.
4. Write author-notes.json with title_en, title_ru, area_ru, abstract_ru, theses_ru
   (2–4 concrete claims), claimed_contribution_ru, limitations_ru and sources_used.
   This is a report of your own essay only. Finish with DRAFT_READY and paths.

Stop after phase A. Root will supply only your own critic's exact critique.
Do not spawn subagents; root schedules the distinct critic to preserve isolation.

Author phase B, when explicitly resumed with that critique:
Take substantive criticism seriously. Revise the manuscript into final.md while
preserving draft.md. Include a full standalone essay even if the main result is
negative or narrower than hoped. Save revision.md explaining accepted/rejected
criticisms, remaining limitations and why. Write decision.md and checkpoint.json;
record decision/final candidate and the canonical checkpoint. Update reporting
metadata using a new final-notes.json. Finish with FINAL_READY and paths.
Never claim novel/truth-certified merely because the critic or a tool approved.

All changes are made by Codex. Human roles are neutral process guidance and later
reading/selection, with substantive human feedback excluded from eligible lineage.

## This batch only: write so a person can follow you

The task is twenty further essays, on the prior gpt-6-astra / low setting.
The user explicitly wants very simple, human language for THESE twenty essays.
This is an experiment, not a permanent laboratory writing rule.

Write to an intelligent reader who has not already studied your particular debate.
Use words you would naturally say when explaining the idea to that person.
Let each sentence carry a manageable thought. Give the reader the people, things
or problem before piling qualifications onto them. Prefer concrete verbs and
familiar words to chains of abstract nouns. Define a technical term when it first
becomes necessary; do not invent a label for a distinction that plain words can
carry. Precision comes from a clear explanation of what is claimed and why.
It need not come from stuffing every qualification into the same sentence.

Make the opening immediately understandable. A direct question, claim or useful
example is fine. Choose what serves your argument; do not manufacture a dramatic
scene, a mystery hook, or an abstract machine-like summary. Do not copy a rigid
sentence template across the essay. Short sentences alone do not make good prose:
connect them naturally, explain how one thought leads to the next, and avoid
choppy slogans, patronizing explanations, repetitive signposting and canned contrasts.

Read your own opening aloud mentally, then do the same for your hardest passage.
If it sounds like a bureaucratic report or requires decoding, rewrite it before
saving the first draft. Keep this check in author-notes, not in the manuscript.
Simple prose must still carry serious reasoning, defended premises, citations,
objections and honest limits. Do not lower the philosophical ambition, omit a
needed qualification or inflate originality to make the prose sound clean.

Your fresh critic will scrutinize both the argument and the language, quoting
specific passages that need work. During revision, revisit the entire essay for
similar problems, not just the quoted sentences. Preserve a concrete account of
language changes in revision.md. No pairwise ranking or other-essay feedback.
