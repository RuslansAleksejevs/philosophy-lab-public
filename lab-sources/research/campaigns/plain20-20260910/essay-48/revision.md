## Substantive changes

I accepted the critique's main objection that the hereditary assignment in the opening made the conclusion too easy. The final uses adults who voluntarily accepted fixed-term care jobs. The institutional wrong appears later, when managers knowingly stop training replacements and use residents' resulting dependence to extend service. This case isolates the claimed duty to maintain transfer capacity.

I narrowed the principle to institutionally allocated care tasks. The final does not claim that parenthood, love, or every role-specific duty can be transferred. It states one primary ground in equal standing: an institution may not treat a particular carer's life as its sole safeguard against harm when it can share capacity at proportionate cost. Care ethics adds attention to dependence, trust, communication, and the resident's interests.

I replaced the vague appeal to what is “reasonable” with seven factors: burden on the carer, urgency of need, the kind of continuity at stake, institutional causation, transition costs and risks, fairness to replacements, and the risk of entrenchment. The institution bears a heavier burden of justification when its own cost-saving created the bottleneck.

I separated task continuity, trust and communication continuity, and relationship continuity. The Alstott parent case now marks a limit and is not treated as direct support for occupational transfer. The final also distinguishes three duties: the carer's short-term rescue duty, the institution's immediate duty to arrange relief, and earlier managers' duty not to create avoidable sole dependence.

I accepted the nonidentity criticism. The final separates cases where the same people exist from cases where policy affects identity. In the second kind, it gives a non-comparative equal-standing argument and explicitly denies that this settles the whole nonidentity problem.

I added the critic's verified close literature. McKittrick-Sweitzer supplies a nearby argument about exploiting a caring person's presumed response. Wissenburg supplies a structural precedent about passing forward capacity to meet duties while preserving freedom. The final retains Randall only at the level of the accessible abstract and narrows the originality claim to a possible institutional synthesis. It makes no priority claim.

I added tests involving disaster scarcity, a resident who refuses replacement, a high-turnover system, and parents. I also applied the principle to a residential disability service so the practical difference between funding care and maintaining safe transfer capacity is visible.

## Language changes

I revised the whole essay for simple, natural language and made recurring terms stable. “Inheritance” is no longer the main object of the principle; the final names “durable care institutions,” “care tasks,” and “safe transfer capacity.”

Concrete examples of revision include:

- Before: “We should judge an inheritance partly by whether its duties can be shared and handed over fairly.” After: “When an institution makes one person's continued service the sole barrier against serious harm, it owes an explanation.”
- Before: “Otherwise the decision-maker can use future need to secure authority that they lack now.” After: “If the home chose not to train anyone else, it cannot justify extending a carer's term merely by saying that only she can now leave safely.”
- Before: “Where an institution has organized the dependence, it should normally take responsibility for building and maintaining the route to relief.” After: “If it created that bottleneck and can reduce it at proportionate cost, it should do so.”
- Before: “The answer is to distinguish what must continue from every activity currently attached to it.” After: the final gives separate, concrete accounts of task continuity, trust and communication continuity, and relationship continuity.
- Before: “We care for future people partly by refusing to make their goodness the only thing holding our plans together.” After: “We care for future people partly by refusing to build institutions that work only because one conscientious person cannot bear to leave someone unprotected.”

The final explains qualifications at the point where they matter rather than packing them into the thesis sentence. Paragraphs usually introduce a person or institution before moving to the abstract claim.

## Disagreements and remaining limits

I did not adopt a single numerical threshold for proportionate cost. The relevant goods differ too much for a universal ratio, and pretending to have one would add false precision. I instead specify the required factors and allocate the burden of justification.

I also did not claim that transfer capacity is always necessary for justice in the sense that every just care arrangement must permit eventual occupational exit. Extreme scarcity and some personal relationships can support stringent duties without feasible transfer. The defended duty is pro tanto and applies when institutions can reduce avoidable sole dependence at proportionate cost.

The source review remains limited. Randall's full article and relevant books were not accessed, and adjacent work may contain the same institutional proposal. The argument therefore remains a candidate contribution under criticism rather than a certified original result.
