# Decision

Outcome: **REVISE**.

The candidate is retained in revised form. The central contribution is now a narrow institutional principle: a generation that creates a durable care institution has a pro tanto duty to maintain safe transfer capacity when it can do so at proportionate cost. This includes shared skills, knowledge, trust, transition time, and authority to arrange relief. The principle rests on the equal standing of residents and carers and does not imply a general right to transfer relationships or abandon people in emergencies.

The critique's objections about the easy opening, vague scope, undefined reasonableness, emergency duties, nonidentity, and thin literature were accepted and addressed in `final.md`. The draft remains preserved. The proposal is worth continuing because it identifies a practical component of intergenerational care provision that ordinary resource measures can miss.

The result does not establish philosophical truth, priority, or competition readiness. The literature search is limited, Randall was available only through an abstract, and the standard of proportionate cost remains a structured judgment rather than an algorithm. A later review should test the principle against adjacent work on relational autonomy, non-domination, caregiver exploitation, professional continuity, and family-care justice.
