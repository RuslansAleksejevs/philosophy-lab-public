# Critique of essay 40

## Argument and verdict

The essay defends a restricted and plausible claim: choosing and achieving a reasonable replacement conservation goal does not, without further reasons, show that an earlier loss has been compensated or earlier duties discharged. Its argument distinguishes the reasons for choosing a feasible plan from the reasons for judging a particular claim satisfied. The natural-loss/industrial-loss pair usefully holds future management constant while varying the history that may ground compensation. The author allows justified substitution, innocent managers, mistaken original goals, impossible duties and finite settlement. These qualifications belong to the strongest reading and should be retained.

Verdict: revise. The central invalid inference is exposed clearly. The more ambitious contribution about authority remains underdeveloped, and some practical requirements do not yet follow from that negative result. The manuscript can become a defensible focused essay if it makes the source and bearer of obligations explicit and develops one distinctive institutional argument instead of repeating the general distinction.

## Main philosophical vulnerabilities

1. **A valuable goal is not yet a duty, and a frustrated duty is not yet a compensatory claim.** The first two premises establish continuing value or reasons, not an obligation owed by this institution. Imagine a public reserve tasked only with maximizing regional conservation benefits from its annual budget. Protecting its freshwater marsh was justified while it was efficient. After unavoidable change, another habitat offers more benefit. No promise, negligence or beneficiary-specific undertaking exists. The old population mattered, but that need not give it priority over an equally threatened population elsewhere. The draft's conditional thesis survives, but the repeated language of an account that must stay open suggests more. State three distinct possibilities: value lost; an existing duty left unmet; a further feasible duty generated by that failure. Explain which is established in the central example. A voluntary worthwhile project need not create a permanent claim on its sponsor merely by being adopted.

2. **Make the authority claim more than a change in vocabulary.** Showing that an inference is invalid establishes a need for reasons, not necessarily a separate permission, office or hearing. Suppose legislation legitimately delegates both goal revision and compensation settlement to the same agency, which applies a publicly accepted rule for transferring commitments under unavoidable change. Its new plan can embody both judgments. The essay allows one body and one calculation, correctly, but then what distinct claim about authority remains? A promising narrower claim is that a mandate to select ecological targets does not itself authorize the agency to waive claims held by people outside that mandate. Defend this using a specified mandate, claimant and proposed waiver. Distinguish lack of authority to release another party from authority exercised with poor reasons. The existing sentence about different arguments does not fully settle this distinction.

3. **Do not transfer responsibility to rescuers.** The industrial example acknowledges innocent managers, yet the practical account becomes the reserve's responsibility. Who owes the explanation, new resources or settlement: polluter, reserve, government or public? A small rescue charity with no record of past industrial harm could meet its own responsibilities without auditing every historical claim. Explain why the reporting requirement attaches to an institution asserting comprehensive success or release of liability. This is stronger than requiring every successful conservation project to produce a historical moral balance sheet. The responsible firm example is the best candidate for a developed case.

4. **The participation requirement needs a premise.** A missing justification does not by itself entail that affected people must have a procedure to contest substitution. Perhaps an expert body already applies a fair, authorized rule. Add a brief reason based on the agency's incomplete knowledge of the affected interests or the claimant's standing to challenge purported settlement. Likewise, representation of nonhuman life is an additional institutional recommendation, not a direct deduction from the inference alone. Specify who represents which interest and avoid implying that a species has interests in precisely the same sense as an individual animal.

5. **Strengthen the demanding counterexample, not only the easy one.** Viewing platforms versus a route for the originally protected species makes the old duty intuitively salient, but a harder choice is between that route and saving more animals of another equally valuable population. A general welfare mandate may require the latter; a specific entrusted commitment may favor the former. Show what your principle asks in both cases. It may demand honest acknowledgment rather than a different allocation. This would clarify its practical significance without covertly treating historical priority as decisive.

## Significance, originality and literature

Significance is credible: institutions can change what they measure and thereby obscure claims their measures never settled. The essay identifies a recognizable problem without asserting that a real reserve has committed it. Keep this conditional status.

Originality remains uncertain. The draft appropriately credits Page and Heyward. More directly relevant environmental literature was missing from the initial search. Moreno-Mateos et al.'s *The true loss caused by biodiversity offsets* (2015) already discusses neglected moral and contextual losses and the need to make losses transparent. I read its primary abstract only, so I cannot declare duplication. But the final should engage this close neighbor or explicitly limit the comparison. Its primary link is https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0006320715300665 ; author institution metadata is https://intelligent-earth.ox.ac.uk/node/3976666 . The potential contribution is a carefully specified limit on what delegated target-setting authority can settle, not the general observation that replacement benefits can miss losses.

Two additional leads, not fully read: Sandler's *The Ethics of Species*, chapter5, directly concerns changing conservation goals (publisher summary: https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/ethics-of-species/shifting-goals-and-changing-strategies/D80F91E1A4D0704693006B94397C3C5C). Simmonds et al.'s target-based ecological compensation proposal may provide a constructive comparison (https://conbio.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/conl.12695). Do not cite either as a fully reviewed opponent without further retrieval.

## Concrete language review

**Opening:** “A nature reserve may have to stop protecting the kind of place it was created to protect.” The problem is immediately understandable once the next sentence supplies the marsh. The first sentence repeats “protect” and leaves “kind of place” vague, but no manufactured drama occurs. Optional simpler rewrite: “A reserve created to protect a freshwater marsh may have to let it become a salt marsh.” Keep the explanation of cost and failure immediately after it.

**Central thesis:** “when a conservation body replaces a justified goal because it has become unattainable, achieving the replacement goal does not by itself settle obligations arising from the loss of the original object.” This packs goal, justification, feasibility, achievement and obligation into one sentence, while “original object” is unnatural. Rewrite: “Sometimes a reserve must abandon a worthwhile goal because it can no longer achieve it. Meeting its new goal does not, on its own, show that it has done everything owed to those harmed by the loss.” Then explicitly add the nonhuman case, rather than hiding it in “object.”

**Missing argument section:** “Third, an institution's permission to choose a feasible response comes from reasons about that response.” The reader cannot tell which reason grants which permission. Rewrite: “The reasons for creating a salt marsh may show that this is the best plan now. They do not yet show that anyone harmed by losing the freshwater marsh has been compensated.” Then derive the general point.

**Claimed contribution section:** “This point links a practical decision about future habitat to a demand for a separate justification of closure.” “Links,” “demand” and “closure” obscure the action. Rewrite: “Before the reserve says that nothing more is owed, it must explain how its new plan meets the duties left by the loss.” If the duty bearer is the firm, name the firm instead.

**Practical section:** “Interests of nonhuman life need representation tied to the reasons that justified protecting them.” The sentence is short but still requires decoding: who represents what? Rewrite: “Someone must also explain what the change means for the animals or species the reserve undertook to protect, and why the proposed response meets that commitment.” This makes an informational minimum clear; if stronger representation is intended, explain it separately.

**Strongest objection:** “A harder objection says that even a justified conservation goal creates no obligation after its object is gone.” This is mostly clear but “object” sounds technical. Rewrite: “A stronger objection is that nothing can be owed to a population once it is extinct. Duties must concern things we can still do.” The reply should immediately distinguish duties toward surviving animals, duties owed to people and accountability for negligence; listing them together currently masks their different bases.

**Conclusion:** “Without a separate judgment about unsettled losses, the very process that makes conservation realistic can gradually remove the grounds on which its failures remain answerable.” Abstract noun chains hide the mechanism. Rewrite: “When reports track only the new target, earlier losses can disappear from view. People may then mistake a successful new project for proof that every earlier duty has been met.” This preserves the point without suggesting that paperwork literally removes moral grounds.

Priority order: clarify the duty and its bearer; build one concrete authority case; address the offset literature; revise abstract passages throughout using named actors and actions. Avoid adding length merely to sound more scholarly.
