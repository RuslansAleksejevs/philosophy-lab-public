# Critique of Leaving Without Taking Back the Thanks

## Verdict and reconstruction

Revise, preserving the narrow central argument. The essay argues that justified departure and truthful recognition of past care are compatible; gratitude supplies a beneficiary-specific reason not to falsify that care, but does not give the benefactor control over the response. Its strongest contribution is the connection between the pressure to remain close and a temptation to invent a discrediting account of the past. Its weakest point is the move from the intelligibility of a grateful reason to a beneficiary-specific responsibility or right. Those are different claims.

The argument has four steps: care can matter after friendship ends; its value does not determine which response is appropriate; deliberate unsupported denial can wrong the giver as giver, beyond the ordinary wrong of falsehood; even a warranted complaint does not authorize any means of securing recognition. The first and fourth steps are plausible but familiar. The third needs the most defense. The second defeats unlimited control more clearly than it defeats moderate duties arising from gratitude.

## Argument and serious objections

1. **Do not let the opening grant the difficult conclusion.** The case stipulates that Leon has good enough reasons to leave. That establishes the conditional argument about how to leave, but cannot independently establish that gratitude cannot make leaving wrong. The essay mostly respects this, then sometimes slides into a stronger claim about what gratitude can justify. State that the defended conclusion is conditional. Separately defend the modest proposition that past care alone does not settle a demand for intimacy. This permits gratitude to count against leaving without making it decisive in every case.

2. **The exclusive-friendship comparison attacks an extreme.** Equal benefits need not license equally valuable or equally appropriate returns on any plausible rival account. A rival can readily accept that money for an emergency and lifelong exclusive friendship differ. The harder case is Mara asking Leon to try one month of honest, nonexclusive meetings because his settled wish might change. He has no promise or reliance-based duty, and the request is neither coerced affection nor deception. Can gratitude itself make this trial obligatory? The draft concedes reasons to attempt repair, but leaves the relation between reasons and duties unclear. A useful reply: yes, it can sometimes support a bounded duty to try; no, this creates no guarantee of restored affection and no permanent authority to demand attempts. This would strengthen the conditional position without claiming too much.

3. **The distinct reason is plausible, but partly asserted.** The equally informed witness example properly removes an epistemic advantage. It does not yet demonstrate why being the beneficiary creates a responsibility rather than merely explaining why gratitude would be fitting. A further comparison could help: Leon lies about Mara's help to an unrelated stranger, and then lies about equally well-documented help to himself. Hold his friendship with Mara, evidence, and reputational harm constant. Ask why the second is a distinct refusal of something personally offered. Explain the premise: receiving care gives one a reason to respond to its meaning for oneself, rather than treating its personal value as usable and its giver as expendable. This premise remains contestable; acknowledge it as the account's substantive commitment rather than announcing that the example proves it.

The present wording also overstates the wrong: Leon need not turn the history into something Mara was foolish to offer. Her help may remain entirely worthwhile despite his later denial. Replace this with a claim about his response, not the value or rationality of her generosity.

4. **Separate reason, duty, complaint, and right.** The thesis introduces a right to fair treatment. Later the essay alternates among a reason to acknowledge, a responsibility, a complaint, and a requirement. A reason need not establish a duty; a fitting complaint need not establish a demandable right. The public correction case is already supported by the ordinary wrong of defamatory falsehood, so it does not show that gratitude creates a further right. Explicitly distinguish: ordinary fairness can require correction; gratitude can provide an additional reason to make it and explain a further dimension of ingratitude; the argument need not establish a separately enforceable gratitude-right. This is also where the nearby Manela literature matters.

5. **Private self-protection is a stronger objection than possessive Mara.** Consider a recipient who knows the old help was sincere but can leave a subsequently harmful relationship only by temporarily avoiding all favorable recollection. They do not publicly accuse anyone. The essay permits silence and involuntary feelings, but its language about deliberate mental training might still condemn a necessary coping practice. Distinguish not rehearsing a memory, temporarily bracketing its emotional force, and cultivating a false belief about it. Then state whether severe circumstances can excuse or override even the last wrong. A defensible reason against distortion need not be exceptionless. Keep this counterexample clearly hypothetical; no empirical claim about coping is needed.

## Sources and interpretation

I independently read Aristotle IX.2–3 and relevant material surrounding it at https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.9.ix.html (HTML lines 40–59). The former-friend remainder is accurately attributed. However, Aristotle's permitted separations involve changing utility, pleasure, character, or substantial difference in development. “No longer fit together” is a broad modern gloss, not his unrestricted principle. Name one actual case and keep the essay's modern permission separate.

I independently read Seneca II.10–11 and III.1–7 at https://www.gutenberg.org/files/3794/3794-h/3794-h.htm (HTML lines 294–309, 430–459). Memory and intrusive reminding are accurately represented. III.1–2 would be more direct locators for denial and memory than only III.3–5. Seneca treats forgetfulness much more harshly than the essay does; the draft's distinction between deliberate revision and involuntary memory is its own departure and should be marked as such. II.11 supports the advertised-rescue example directly.

I independently read Macnamara §§5–6, particularly HTML lines 174–210, at https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/japp.12726. The draft fairly avoids attributing a universal intimacy-duty. But her account expressly permits weak communal concern, even toward strangers. Quiet help after friendship fits her account readily. Treat this as showing why promotion of communal ties need not mean continued intimacy. To challenge her account itself requires a case in which gratitude remains but even weak reciprocal concern is absent; the current recognition example may itself qualify as that concern. Do not manufacture a disagreement the source need not have.

## Significance, originality, and literature

The practical significance lies in permitting criticism of ingratitude without making it a veto on departure. That is worthwhile. The broad compatibility claim is already anticipated by the cited ancient sources. The more particular contribution is an account of motivated denial during separation and limits on its repair. It is an argued application and clarification, with originality unestablished.

An independent search recovered Tony Manela's “Obligations of Gratitude and Correlative Rights,” publisher abstract at https://academic.oup.com/book/32715/chapter-abstract/272200971. The abstract concerns obligations owed to benefactors alongside the apparent absence of corresponding rights. The page failed on direct opening; I read only the search-displayed publisher abstract. Do not claim to have examined his solution. The overlap shows that separating gratitude from benefactor authority is an existing problem. Either obtain the chapter and engage it or explicitly bound the essay's contribution without claiming that distinction as new. This limited search does not establish priority or exhaust relevant work.

## Concrete language review

**Opening.** “Mara helped her friend Leon through a difficult year. She brought food, listened, and made room for him when he had nowhere else to stay.” This is clear and relevant scene-setting. The reader understands the relationship. The later question, “Must he now decide that Mara's help was less generous than he once thought?” sounds psychologically odd until the next paragraph supplies the pressure. Optional shorter bridge: “Suppose he is right to leave. Does staying grateful mean he must stay friends?” Then explain why people can feel pushed to deny the past. Preserve the conditional assumption openly.

**Central thesis, introduction.** “Gratitude can survive the end of a close relationship as a reason to give the other person's care its proper place in our judgment.” “Proper place” conceals what the reader needs to know. Rewrite: “Even after a friendship ends, gratitude gives us a reason to remember the help honestly and recognize that it mattered.” Add separately that this need not involve constant remembrance.

**Why care does not settle the form.** “The second demand concerns something whose value depends on a kind of participation that a past benefit cannot supply.” The abstract placeholders make a simple thought difficult. Rewrite: “A marriage needs willing participation. Having helped someone does not give you a claim to that person's willingness to marry you.” This remains an extreme example, so add the more moderate trial-of-friendship objection discussed above.

**Gratitude after closeness.** “If every special concern for a person counts as a relationship, preserving some relationship can mean little more than preserving the concern at issue.” Clear enough upon rereading, but repetitive and abstract. Rewrite: “If caring about someone already counts as a relationship, Leon can keep that relationship without seeing Mara again.” Then explain that this is compatible with Macnamara's broad usage rather than a defect in it.

**The wrong in rewriting.** “It identifies one way an existing duty can also answer to a particular person for a particular reason.” A duty does not naturally “answer to” a person in ordinary English. Rewrite: “Leon already has a duty not to lie about Mara. Gratitude gives him another reason to tell the truth: the care he is denying was care for him.” Whether it also strengthens the duty should be argued separately.

**Strongest objection, final section.** “The recipient would be most tightly bound to the helper who valued no possible response except control over the recipient's life.” This prejudges Mara's desire for friendship as a desire for control. Rewrite: “On that view, Leon would owe more simply because Mara would accept fewer kinds of response. Her refusal of alternatives cannot by itself decide what he owes.” Mara can want friendship without wanting control. The objection is stronger when she remains sympathetic.

**Conclusion.** “It asks us to bear a past that does not conveniently endorse our present choices.” The metaphor is striking but imprecise: the past need not oppose the present choice either. Rewrite: “It asks us to admit that someone cared for us even when we have good reasons to leave.” The closing line can remain.

Priorities: clarify the strength of the central claim; distinguish duties and rights; test a modest repair request and private mental avoidance; accurately position Macnamara; replace the quoted abstract sentences across the manuscript. These revisions should deepen the reasoning without padding the essay.
