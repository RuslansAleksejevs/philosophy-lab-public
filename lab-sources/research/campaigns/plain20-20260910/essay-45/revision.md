## Substantive changes

I accepted the central criticism that the draft moved too quickly from “every contingent fact has an explanation” to a demand for contrastive explanation. The final essay now names and defends a difference-coverage premise, while conceding that it is a substantive account of completeness. It distinguishes causal completeness, fact completeness, difference completeness, and the still stronger demand for a prior sufficient reason.

I also accepted the criticism that “favoring,” probability raising, causal production, contrastive discrimination, and sufficient reason were blurred. The revision separates these relations. It adds the critic's radioactive-decay case to show that symmetry does not decide whether a stochastic law plus a token event explains the event's exact timing. It adds the symmetric-pencil case to show how a token perturbation can explain divergence even when the coarse setup is symmetric.

The agency section now presents the strongest Clarke-style reply. An agent's exercise of active power may itself be a contrastive cause even when no prior motive favors the selected option. The final argument therefore does not call such an action simply unexplained. It says more narrowly that the action may lack a prior favoring reason, which matters only to a demanding Leibnizian principle.

The conclusion about possible worlds now includes its missing inference: a reason common to several eligible worlds is compatible with each, so it cannot by itself entail or differentially support the actual world. A divine choice may still causally explain the actual world. The essay no longer presents its synthesis as established novelty; literature coverage remains insufficient for that claim.

## Language changes

The opening no longer assumes “genuine randomness.” It describes the absence of bias first. The thesis is stated through three defined kinds of completeness instead of the dense phrase about “keeping track of real differences.” The symmetry discussion now asks what plays a different explanatory role and uses concrete cases rather than grouping chances, causes, and reasons in one abstract test.

The four possible stopping responses are now written as direct questions. Dense passages about metaphysical consequences have been replaced by explicit inferences. The conclusion states exactly which argumentative move fails. I reviewed the whole essay for similar compression, especially the sections on stochastic explanation, agency, and the contested bridge from facts to contrasts.

## Remaining limits

The difference-coverage premise remains contestable, especially for theories on which a stochastic cause fully explains an exact token outcome. The essay classifies this disagreement but does not supply a general theory of causal explanation. It also does not settle whether agent causation is true or whether a basic exercise of agency satisfies fact completeness. The contemporary literature on contrastive principles of sufficient reason, stochastic explanation, and agent causation was not comprehensively checked. Lipton's article was available only through its official opening extract.
