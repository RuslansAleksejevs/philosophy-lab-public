# Critique of Being Able to Stop

Verdict: REVISE. The narrow distinction between a stopping ability and a usable warning route is soundly motivated. The proposed account of whose control external assistance supplies needs a stronger argument. In particular, the workshop comparison changes two factors at once. This is a promising clarification of control, not yet a demonstrated original theory.

## Argument and contribution

The strongest reading is task-relative. To control sanding to a marked depth, the carpenter needs both the capacity to stop and an actual dependable route by which reaching the mark can affect her. Motor control can survive the loss of this route. An external route counts as something she controls through only when she practically relies on it and can revise, suspend, or leave the arrangement. This avoids identifying agency with conscious supervision and avoids identifying control with successful outcomes.

The first result is principally a useful clarification of established attention and control concerns. The potentially distinctive contribution is the proposal that external monitoring belongs to a performer's control because it remains usable under changes of plan. The essay explains the relevance of that proposal but does not yet isolate or fully defend it.

## Most consequential objections

1. **The paired cases confound reliance with responsiveness.** The known colleague can follow a changed plan; the unknown colleague cannot. A theorist who cares only about a dependable chain across the relevant range can explain the difference without accepting the additional reliance requirement. The stipulation of equal reliability applies only to the fixed plan, while the conclusion assesses a wider range containing changed plans. Add a hidden colleague who learns of every change from the carpenter's public work order, warns about the revised limit, and always respects her decision to continue. She does not know he is there. Now the signal routes are equally responsive across the same range. Does the author still deny her control through this arrangement? If yes, defend why practical uptake is necessary; do not repeat the obsolete-plan example. If no, weaken the requirement that she must be using the particular help.

2. **Reliance may operate at a broader level than this particular helper.** A carpenter may habitually heed clear warnings from anyone while having no expectation that someone is monitoring this panel. She can inspect the mark after a warning and reject the warning when it conflicts with her revised plan. The essay already grants that the resulting stop is hers. Explain why this standing openness cannot connect her agency to the entire performance when the warning route is reliable. It may be right to distinguish her control from the workshop's protection, but the distinction needs more than the language of whose project the helper serves. The carpenter and helper can have the same project, and she can exploit an unanticipated information source.

3. **Control before and at the warning needs a consistent time scale.** Control is normally dispositional: one controls a performance partly because one would respond if the relevant event occurred. The first carpenter also does nothing with a signal before it arrives. What distinguishes her earlier control is an arrangement, not actual intervention. Explain whether the disputed attribution concerns ongoing possession of a capacity, successful exercise of that capacity, or credit for arranging the warning. Lack of credit for arranging a protection does not establish lack of control while protected. Replace any slide from “never directed or relied on” to “never controlled” with a defended premise.

4. **The third requirement is weaker than the motivating contrast suggests.** Its disjunction permits simply stopping reliance. The hidden-helper carpenter can often disregard an obsolete warning, too. Meanwhile, a fixed workshop safety alarm may be impossible to reprogram yet fully usable for her chosen task. Make clear that revisability concerns her use of help, not power over its trigger. Consider a voluntarily chosen commitment device that prevents a later impulsive change. Does it preserve execution of an earlier plan while reducing current control? That is a defensible result, but it would show that preserving control and serving a person's plans can come apart. The essay should state which it measures.

5. **Avoid turning one lapse into total loss.** The text stipulates that she will not notice the mark, which supports its example. Other formulations sound as though any lost warning means absence of control. A generally reliable performer can make a mistake while retaining a capacity. Distinguish a route that is unavailable on this occasion from a route that works imperfectly; explain the relevant normal range without pretending to supply a universal reliability threshold. The current denial of perfect reliability helps but should be connected directly to the verdict about the carpenter.

The regress response is adequate for this limited account: capacities need not all be consciously monitored. The rejection of a moral-blame conclusion is also justified. Neither should absorb space needed for the harder ownership objection.

## Concrete language review

### Opening

“A skilled carpenter can sand a wooden panel while thinking about a conversation. Her hands adjust to the surface without instructions about each movement. If asked to stop, she can stop at once. Is that enough to show that she controls whether the sanding continues?”

This is understandable immediately; the ordinary example earns its place. The phrase “controls whether” initially invites the easy answer that she plainly can stop. Introduce the task's stopping point sooner. Optional rewrite: “A carpenter sands a panel while thinking about a conversation. She can stop the moment someone asks. But can she still keep the sanding within the marked limit if she will not notice when she reaches it?” This previews the exact distinction without drama.

### Central thesis, opening section

“The help must be part of how the performer pursues a plan that she can change.”

The sentence is short but its key relation, “part of how,” is underspecified. Rewrite: “She must rely on the help in doing the job, and she must be able to change how she uses it when her plan changes.” Use this only after resolving whether reliance is a necessary condition; simple wording cannot settle the objection.

### What the ability to stop leaves open

“That conditional can be true even when noticing has become practically unavailable.”

“Conditional” and “practically unavailable” make the reader translate an easy idea. Rewrite: “She might stop if she noticed the mark, yet have no working way to notice it while she sands.” This keeps the difference between a response and the conditions that allow it.

### Two ways to receive the same warning

“It also need not enlarge her agency retrospectively to include work she never directed or relied on.”

“Enlarge her agency retrospectively” is unnecessarily academic, and the object of “relied on” is unclear: work or assistance? Rewrite: “Her decision to stop does not by itself show that she controlled the earlier sanding through the colleague's help.” This also avoids assuming that all earlier work lacked her direction.

### When help becomes a way of controlling

“It must bring an appropriate range of relevant changes to bear on the work, with enough time for a response.”

The abstract phrases hide what the help does. Rewrite: “It must alert her in time when the conditions that matter for this job change.” Follow with the existing timer example to explain why a warning can be reliable yet track the wrong condition.

### Strongest objection, Three objections

“The relevant relation is practical: the performer pursues her task through the help, and changes in her plan can change how she uses it.”

The wording announces an answer but does not explain it; the reader still needs a behavior that constitutes practical reliance. Rewrite: “She keeps herself ready for the signal because she is counting on it. If her plan changes, she can change the signal's role or stop counting on it.” Then directly address the responsive hidden-helper counterexample. Do not let simpler repetition stand in for the missing argument.

### The skill that remains

“This is why an account of the individual's stored abilities is not yet a complete account of her control on an occasion.”

“Stored abilities” sounds like machinery and “on an occasion” sounds stiff. Rewrite: “Knowing what she has learned does not tell us whether she can control this job now.” The following explanation of the setting can supply the detail.

Priorities: fix the paired-case logic and time-scale ambiguity first; then simplify these passages and scan for other expressions such as “relevant range” that need concrete explanation. Preserve the readable examples and the distinction between control, success, and blame. A focused additional objection and source comparison are more valuable than expanding the essay generally.
