# Critique of essay 44

## Verdict and reconstruction

The essay defends a plausible necessary condition for a specific person's continuing review, rather than a full theory of autonomy. If the manager must be able to reconsider a schedule for staff needs, she needs a usable opportunity to learn of those needs. When the scheduler's rejection of a need also prevents every report of it reaching her, the formal veto cannot supply that opportunity. A separate route need not be a separate product or unaided human performance; it must survive the particular error under review.

This is worth developing. The controlled theatre comparison makes the difference intelligible, and the argument avoids equating delegation with lost agency. However, the account needs a more explicit standard for a sufficient opportunity to hear a reason, a harder case for its scope, and a more demanding comparison with prior work. Recommended outcome: revise, retaining the narrow claim and resisting any claim of a newly discovered general condition of control.

## Argument: principal vulnerabilities and repairs

1. **The conclusion alternates between possibility and dependable capacity.** A separate mailbox that receives one in a million wrongly filtered requests prevents the first error from automatically blocking every response. It nevertheless supplies almost no practical review. Conversely, a route with a small chance of simultaneous failure may support adequate review even though it cannot always survive the scheduler's mistake. The draft acknowledges degrees but its proposed test is often binary: could the request still reach her? State that a meaningful practical opportunity is required under the specified failure, with reliability and timeliness appropriate to the stakes. No numerical universal threshold is needed. Show why a vanishingly unlikely rescue fails and an ordinary reliable inbox can pass. This is more than a usability qualification: it determines what the central condition actually says.

2. **State the unit of review consistently.** A manager can meaningfully audit a scheduling practice by sampling raw requests without reviewing each particular schedule. This gives reason-responsive control even though many individual omissions will never reach her. The draft explicitly allows limited review, but sometimes goes from retaining review of 'a certain kind of consideration' to requiring a route for 'each' relevant reason. Add a sampling case and distinguish review of individual outputs from review of the ongoing policy. Both can be real; they preserve different powers. The claim should match the power actually promised. Otherwise the final language about comprehensive judgment risks attacking an inflated promise rather than ordinary oversight.

3. **The scope cannot simply be declared fixed.** The provider cannot define away care needs, but the essay does not explain who can properly specify the review task. Suppose the theatre authorizes review only of contractual conflicts; a new care need is morally serious but outside the agreed role. Has the manager lost control, or is the institution exercising control over too narrow a subject? Distinguish these diagnoses. The essay can assume a justified scope for its conditional result, while saying it offers no sufficient account of what that scope should be. A stronger development would allow workers to question the scope through a general channel. Do not imply that a taxonomy agreed in advance exhausts all reasons that may deserve consideration.

4. **The relation to control needs one explicit bridge premise.** The example shows an information defect immediately. Explain why it is a defect in the manager's power rather than only a defect in the scheduler's accuracy: the retained power is to reconsider for relevant reasons, so arrangements must support the acquisition of such reasons in the ordinary exercise of that role. Present this as a defended conception of review, not a verbal definition of all control. The random-refusal paragraph helps; the proposed bridge would integrate it with the main argument.

5. **The strongest objection is partly answered but leaves an evidential problem.** Failure-relative independence avoids an infinite demand for checks, and the same-product inbox example is apt. Still, knowing that a second route survives the first error can itself be difficult. A raw inbox may share the same ingestion process and lose the request before classification. Clarify that the test diagnoses a dependency when it is present; it does not establish that designers can certify its absence by inspection. Give one concrete test: insert messages deliberately omitted from the recommendation and check whether the reviewer can find and act on them through the proposed route. This supplies evidence for a bounded claim, not proof against every common failure.

6. **One analogy can go.** Checking a locked door is not obviously independent of the lock: tugging it tests the same mechanism, and merely looking may reveal nothing. The theatre already does the work. Remove the analogy or specify a physical test against the relevant failure. This also saves room for the harder sampling example.

## Originality and significance

The contribution is a useful failure-relative diagnostic for retained review, particularly the point that corporate or product separation is neither necessary nor sufficient. The broader claims about informed challenge and nominal authority are established background. The existing source discussion is admirably restrained but too easy on the proposed contribution.

A directly relevant addition is Cohen and Suzor, 'Contesting the public interest in AI governance' (2024), Internet Policy Review 13(3), §3.3, https://policyreview.info/articles/analysis/contesting-public-interest-ai-governance . They already argue that contestability requires alternative information and criticize monopoly over the information used to evaluate an actor. Their §3.3 also recognizes unavoidable dependence on internal reporting. The essay should acknowledge this close predecessor. Its possible advance is the more local question of whether a particular error blocks the route needed to expose that error, including within one product. Explain that distinction through a case, rather than merely calling the test specific.

The publisher abstract for Zhou et al., 'Why human–AI collaboration fails: An operational contestability framework', https://journal.hep.com.cn/fem/EN/10.1007/s42524-026-6049-7 , was accessible during this review. It concerns constraints on representation, authority and intervention before execution. I did not read the full article. This is another reason to keep novelty claims modest, not evidence that it already contains the exact argument.

The significance is real but bounded: the essay explains how human approval can overstate what was retained after delegation. To become a stronger philosophical contribution, it should settle the possibility/reliability distinction and the individual-output/policy-review distinction rather than add more introductory claims about AI.

## Concrete language review

### Opening

> A manager uses a program to arrange the shifts at a small theatre. The program works well. It fills every post, observes the agreed hours, and shares unpopular shifts fairly. Before publishing a schedule, the manager can change any part of it. A large button says “Reject.”

The people and task are immediately clear. 'Observes the agreed hours' is less natural than 'keeps to the agreed hours', and the large button is a little staged, but the example is useful rather than decorative. Optional tighter version: 'A theatre manager uses a program to arrange staff shifts. It fills every post, keeps to the agreed hours, and shares unpopular shifts fairly. The manager can change or reject its schedule before publishing it.' Keep the next paragraph's missing care request: it supplies the actual problem.

### Central thesis

> For the kinds of reasons a person is expected to consider, there must be a usable way for those reasons to reach her even when the recommending tool fails to recognize them.

The front-loaded qualification and 'reasons reach her' make the sentence more abstract than the example. Rewrite: 'If the manager is meant to review staff needs, staff must have a practical way to tell her about a need that the program dismisses.' Follow with a general sentence if needed: 'The same applies to any kind of reason that her review is meant to cover.' This also makes the scope explicit.

### First argument section

> With respect to needs that the program excludes, her advertised power of review has no dependable occasion for use.

'With respect to' and 'dependable occasion' conceal the concrete absence. Rewrite: 'When the program hides a staff need, she has no reliable way to learn that she should reconsider the schedule.' This states the intended failure without suggesting she cannot review anything.

### Main argument

> The same judgment is both the subject of review and a condition of access to the review.

This is compact but a novice must reconstruct what judgment and access mean. Rewrite: 'The manager is meant to check whether the program wrongly dismissed the request. But she can see the request only if the program has already decided not to dismiss it.' The extra words explain rather than merely shorten.

### Strongest objection

> Requiring a further check on every check would lead to an endless chain.

This is already clear and should not be rewritten just to meet a quota. An optional concrete version is: 'If every source needed another source to check it, the manager could never finish checking.' More importantly, the reply needs the practical reliability qualification identified above; simple prose cannot compensate for its absence.

### Reply to the objection

> The criterion identifies a preventable form of closure.

'Criterion' and 'closure' are unexplained specialist shorthand. Rewrite: 'The test identifies one avoidable problem: the program can block the very information that would expose its mistake.' This preserves the limited conclusion and reconnects to the example.

### Delegation section

> Consent to delegation and continuing control over its particular results answer different questions.

The abstract nouns obscure a useful distinction. Rewrite: 'Choosing to let the program decide does not show that the manager can still review each decision it makes.' Keep the prior explanation that giving up this review may be an informed choice.

## Revision priorities

First settle how strong the surviving opportunity must be and distinguish case review from policy review. Second engage Cohen and Suzor and the stronger counterfactual aspect of tracking; describe the contribution as a bounded refinement. Third replace abstract shorthand throughout, remove the lock analogy if unnecessary, and retain the concrete opening. These revisions should sharpen the argument without expanding it into a general governance theory.
