# Decision

Outcome: **REVISE**.

The central distinction survives criticism: reasoning well from selected evidence does not establish an ability to assess how that evidence was selected. The final manuscript narrows the duty to educational programs that claim to have developed independent judgment in a domain. Such a claim requires developmentally suitable practice and assessment in detecting consequential limits on inquiry, including at least some checks whose results are not fixed by the original selection process.

The candidate remains worth developing because it turns a familiar concern about hidden guidance into a specific gap in evidence of educational success. It is not certified as original, true, or competition-ready. The main remaining needs are a fuller review of contemporary work on epistemic dependence, educational authority, curriculum, and indoctrination, plus empirical work on usable assessments across learner stages and high-stakes domains.
