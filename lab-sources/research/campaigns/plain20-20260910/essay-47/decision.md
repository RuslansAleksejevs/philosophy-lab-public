# Decision

Outcome: **CONTINUE**.

The candidate should remain under consideration in revised form. Its defensible contribution is narrow: when a religious community authorizes a rite as a way to decide whether to promise a specified future service, it cannot use the resulting conflict between invitation and convention as the sole ground of an enforceable claim against the participant. Depending on authority and ritual conditions, either no promise occurs or the community lacks standing to enforce it. Later belief alone does not repair either problem, although informed ratification or later conduct with a new public meaning can create or assume duties.

The Sol critique was accepted on the principal weaknesses: the missing distinction between validity and enforcement, the unspecified object of “commitment,” the absence of limiting conditions, the overly broad statement about repetition, the easy version of the wholehearted-devotion objection, and the need to acknowledge close recent literature. The final manuscript addresses each point while preserving the draft and avoiding claims of established novelty or truth.

The remaining limits are material. The essay does not provide a full account of institutional authority, sacramental validity, or promising. Its literature review is not systematic, and one close article was available only by abstract. A later review should test the argument against concrete religious traditions and fuller primary speech-act and promise literature before any submission decision.
