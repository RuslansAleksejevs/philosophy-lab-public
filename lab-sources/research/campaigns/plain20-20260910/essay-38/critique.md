# Critique of essay 38

## Verdict and reconstruction

Revise. The essay makes a plausible, useful conditional argument in unusually accessible prose. Its strongest result is that institutions cannot count a complaints procedure as adequate merely because individuals may file complaints: some grounds for challenge become visible only across accounts. The manuscript does not yet explain precisely what distinguishes its entitlement from an ordinary duty of competent, independent complaint review.

The argument is: public power owes affected people a reasonable opportunity to challenge its conduct; its own division of evidence can make that opportunity ineffective; forcing people to disclose intimate information publicly is avoidably burdensome when a reliable protected alternative exists; therefore the institution owes such an alternative. This is a duty to provide an opportunity, not a duty on victims to use it. The distinction among inquiry, procedural repair and personal punishment sensibly prevents a reason to investigate becoming a finding of guilt.

## Most consequential revisions

1. **Say what the claimant is entitled to receive.** Imagine an independent ombudsman already confidentially compares every complaint, investigates recurring harms, and publishes audited annual results. Applicants cannot contact one another and cannot see whether their own account matched another. Does this satisfy the duty? The text sometimes says yes (an independent reviewer is a possible route), but its opening and conclusion promise people a way of finding out whether their experience belongs to a pattern. Confidential institutional knowledge is not necessarily knowledge available to complainants. Choose a precise minimum: perhaps an answer to a request for cross-case review, with reasons and a means of appeal, rather than a right to see others' reports or meet them. Explain why that minimum is needed. Otherwise the essay changes subjects from collective discovery to agency competence.

2. **Defend the missing bridge from review to provision.** A public office may owe fair decisions and correction yet satisfy these through routine random audits, direct inspection or an external regulator. No pooling of voluntary accounts is required if these routes already expose the relevant problem. The draft's conditions partly accommodate this, but it should explicitly make the duty one to remedy a specific failure of challenge, with pooling required only when alternatives do not adequately fill it. More subtly, why must affected people be able to initiate scrutiny rather than simply benefit from excellent oversight? The justification paragraph gestures toward being owed reasons, but needs one argument: a reliable audit may miss the kind of harm that only affected people can identify, and a person must be able to ask the reviewer to examine that possibility. That would also give the applicant's role real content.

3. **Separate a repeated event from a pattern of unequal treatment.** Several similar reports may establish recurrence without showing that one class is singled out. The housing example moves between humiliation, repetition and selective mistreatment. Suppose ten disabled applicants report intrusive questions, while every applicant receives them. That remains objectionable, but it does not establish discrimination. For selective treatment, comparisons may require information about uncomplaining applicants. State that pooling is a means of formulating and testing a hypothesis, not automatically sufficient proof of the alleged pattern. Add the distinction between duplicated accounts and independently submitted accounts that nevertheless derive from a shared rumour or common interpretation. The existing caution about rumours is sound; show what it changes in the example.

4. **Make feasibility less forgiving of institutional evasion.** An institution can underfund independent review and then claim that protection is infeasible. Distinguish limits it may reasonably accept from limits it owes people a duty to remove. An office lacking a suitable reviewer may need to secure access to a shared external body; it cannot create its own excuse. Conversely, genuine scarcity can make even a feasible scheme unreasonable if it would displace more urgent services. A short proportionality account should use seriousness, likelihood, alternative routes and resource cost. This preserves the conditional thesis without making 'reasonable' do all the work.

5. **Keep the evidence distinction, but avoid a fixed three-level ladder.** Procedural changes can impose grave harms, and an investigation can publicly stigmatize an official more than a minor sanction. The passage already acknowledges reputational costs. Add that required evidence follows the particular decision's burdens and reversibility, not just its category. An intrusive procedure change based on anonymous allegations cannot be justified by invoking the lower category. The private interview-space example is a good low-cost illustration, not a general model of every reform.

## Significance and originality

The issue matters because privacy can protect individuals while leaving them unable to contest an institution together. The most promising contribution is a specific adequacy condition for complaint procedures grounded in the complainant's standing, rather than a new privacy technology or a general celebration of privacy's public value.

Originality remains unsettled. The essay correctly credits escrow mechanisms and Cohen's democratic account. However, identifying the institutional duty as its novelty after reading only the closest article's abstract is too weak to support a confident priority claim. My search located an author-hosted copy of Information Escrows, but could not retrieve readable pages. Search snippets suggested institution-related material; these are research leads, not grounds for asserting that the article already makes this exact argument. Retain a modest contribution claim and either inspect that source's full relevant discussion or state the remaining comparison limit in the research record. Do not add many citations decoratively. Scholarship should focus on the duty's actual foundation and its closest predecessor.

## Concrete language review

**Opening.** “A housing office asks applicants to discuss their needs in private. That seems right. A person should not have to tell the whole town about a disability, a family breakdown, or an unsafe home in order to seek help.” This is immediately understandable and supplies a real reason for privacy. No artificial scene-setting needs removal. Optional tightening: “A housing office discusses applicants' needs in private. People should not have to tell the whole town about a disability or an unsafe home to get help.” Keep the example rather than replacing it with an abstract summary.

**Central thesis.** “This duty arises when it runs a practice that seriously affects them, when separate complaints predictably conceal possible repeated harm, and when a reasonably reliable, less exposing route is feasible.” The stacked conditions are necessary but difficult to hold together. Suggested rewrite: “The duty arises when separate complaints leave people unable to challenge a possible pattern of serious mistreatment. It applies if the institution can offer a reliable way to compare their accounts without requiring public disclosure.” Adjust to the final scope after resolving alternative review routes.

**Ground of duty.** “If the agency could choose any evidential arrangement it liked, it could make its conduct practically unanswerable without declaring itself above criticism.” 'Evidential arrangement' and 'unanswerable' obscure an otherwise clear thought. “The agency could avoid answering criticism simply by choosing what evidence it would accept. It would not need to ban complaints.” The next sentence can give the exact mechanism: requiring a pattern while refusing comparisons.

**Participation section.** “My narrower point concerns access to a particular form of participation.” This tells the reader about the essay rather than the problem. “Sometimes people can challenge an office only by comparing what happened to them.” The following paragraph can state how this relates to Cohen without repeating 'participation'.

**Strongest stated objection.** “The strongest objection is that protected reporting could make accusations easy and answers impossible.” Clear and direct. 'Answers' could be more exact: “Protected reporting could let people make accusations that the accused cannot properly challenge.” The next concrete examples work well. I regard the alternative-oversight objection above as more consequential for the distinctive thesis, so avoid ranking this objection as definitively strongest.

**Trust section.** “This is not perfect transparency. It is divided scrutiny, with each access justified by what the person receiving it needs to check.” 'Divided scrutiny' introduces a label without helping the reader. “Different reviewers may need to see different records. Each should receive only what they need to check the decision.” Preserve the surrounding explanation of how the public can evaluate those reviewers; access minimization alone does not show trustworthiness.

**Conclusion.** “The central demand is simple.” This announcement is unnecessary. The final sentence itself is clear, but promises personal discovery more strongly than parts of the argument establish. After settling the entitlement, end with its concrete content: for example, “When accounts can safely be compared, a person should be able to ask for that comparison without making her private life public.” This is a proposal for precision, not a demand to reduce the thesis to a slogan.

Priority: settle the complainant's entitlement and alternatives first; repair pattern/evidence claims second; then simplify the abstract bridge passages throughout. Preserve the essay's concrete opening, voluntary-disclosure limits and careful treatment of accused persons.
