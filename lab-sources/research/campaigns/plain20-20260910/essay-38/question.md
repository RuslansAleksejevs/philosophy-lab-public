# Question
When privacy keeps experiences apart, it can also hide a pattern of injustice. Does a public institution therefore owe people a way to combine evidence without requiring them to identify themselves publicly?

Tentative thesis: where an institution controls a consequential shared practice, and isolated private reports cannot reveal or challenge a repeated harm, it has a conditional duty to provide a protected way of pooling evidence. The duty is not a general duty of victims to disclose. The argument will distinguish public knowledge of a pattern from public access to the people whose experiences establish it, while considering accountability, false reports, and cases where identities are necessary.
