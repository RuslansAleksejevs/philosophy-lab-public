## Conditions for a successful manuscript
1. Preserve the proposal's narrow conclusion. Logical compatibility does not imply equal likelihood, show that nobody excludes, or remove all probabilistic evidential support.
2. Keep explicit the score model's stipulation of deliberative reliance. Merely causal influence is not automatically acting for a reason. Especially explain the convicting judges: a consideration against conviction can enter deliberation without being a positive motivating reason for conviction. The clean result concerns complete exclusion from the deliberative basis. To infer failure of reason-guided exclusion, explain this link rather than defining every numerical influence as basing.
3. Distinguish accepting an exclusionary reason, partially responding to it, and completely excluding its target. Partial suppression may establish some control; the countermodel does not refute that weaker achievement.
4. Model A must explicitly make complete exclusion occur because of the relevant rule if it represents successful second-order responsiveness. Zero contribution alone does not identify that causal/normative connection.
5. Do not claim that unchanged individual outcomes establish successful exclusion. Nor should the manuscript claim that all empirical investigation of reasons is impossible. Process observations narrow model classes without guaranteeing uniquely correct explanations.
6. Attribute inherited insights generously. This can be a concise correction rather than an inflated new theory.

These are development requirements within the already specified proposal, not a demand to change its thesis before drafting.
