# Proposal p1: A hierarchy can need an infinite map

Status: exact proposal for independent pre-draft review; no manuscript drafted.

## Question and thesis

What does a finite diagram of kinds have to assume before it can represent the strict explanatory order of all the facts it classifies?

**Thesis:** An acyclic token grounding structure admits a finite acyclic classification that preserves every grounding edge as a strict arrow if and only if there is a uniform finite bound on the length of its grounding paths. This remains true when the classification must refine any antecedently fixed finite classification. Consequently, finite explanatory termination does not suffice for a finite, complete hierarchy of kinds. A cycle in every finite refinement can be forced by wholly founded, individually finite explanations: it is not evidence of token circularity or an infinite descending explanation. The philosophical increment is a diagnosis of an overlooked extra commitment in treating finite ontological charts as complete representations of explanatory priority, together with an exact criterion for when refinement can discharge that commitment.

This is a claim about finite charts whose nodes classify tokens, not about every possible finite linguistic description. A recursive rule can finitely describe infinitely many ranks. Nor does it refute every kind-based account of relative fundamentality: some such accounts deliberately omit token grounding edges.

## Definitions and argument

Let x R y say that x is a ground, or a component of a ground, of y. Work with an acyclic binary dependency graph. If grounding is plural, its incidence graph records only the partial dependency edges; it does not reconstruct full sufficient grounds. A classification c assigns each token exactly one of finitely many cells. Its existential quotient has an arrow C→D whenever some x in C bears R to some y in D; retain self-loops. Call a chart faithful only in the limited sense that every R-edge appears as a strict chart edge. This notion is weaker than preserving explanations or modal structure, but already demanding enough for the result.

1. If a faithful chart has m cells and is acyclic, every token path has at most m−1 edges: its image is a directed walk in the chart, and repeating a cell creates a closed walk, hence a cycle. No appeal to the transitivity of metaphysical grounding is required.
2. Conversely, suppose every R-path has at most h edges. Assign each token its maximum incoming path length r(x), between 0 and h. Whenever x R y, r(y)>r(x). Rank therefore gives an acyclic finite chart.
3. If an initial finite classification has k cells, refine each cell by r. There are at most k(h+1) cells, and every projected edge increases r. This proves a finite-refinement criterion, not just the failure of one careless grouping. The order-theoretic method is familiar from Mirsky (1971); no mathematical-priority claim is made.
4. Consider a disjoint union of finite paths, with one path of every finite length. Every token has a finite grounding ancestry; there are no infinite chains in either direction, no token cycles, and every component has a fundamental bottom. But path lengths have no uniform bound. Every finite classification, including any finite refinement of an initially natural taxonomy, has a cycle or self-loop if it preserves all edges.
5. Thus repeated failure to repair a finite chart cannot discriminate cyclic ground, descending infinitism, and entirely finite foundational structure. The inference fails for a structural reason that no further finite subdivision can remove. To infer a defect in the underlying metaphysics from that failure, one needs an independent uniform-depth assumption, not merely foundationalism.

A metaphysical realization to defend conditionally is a family of finite singleton constructions of every finite depth, grounded in distinct base objects, restricted to their existence facts. Alternatively, a family of finite nested conjunctions can realize the schematic structure given standard logical grounding assumptions. The graph result is unconditional mathematics; the examples' metaphysical interpretation is explicitly conditional. Transitive closure does not alter unbounded height or create descending infinitude in the disjoint finite components.

## Why it matters beyond an illustration

The argument distinguishes two responses to apparent type cycles: a *local misclassification*, repairable with a better finite taxonomy, and a *global representational obstruction*, which survives every finite taxonomy. A foundationalist need not promise that enough finite distinctions will remove every diagrammatic cycle. Conversely, a theorist who guarantees such repair has already imposed uniform finite depth. That is a substantive constraint on admissible metaphysical structures, not merely a preference for tidy presentation.

An honest chart may omit internal dependencies, permit non-strict same-level edges, or represent ranks by a recursive parameter. Each is legitimate, but then the absence or presence of cycles in its finite skeleton cannot by itself testify to the acyclicity of the full grounding relation. The appropriate conclusion is to separate a chart's explanatory scope from the world's alleged architecture.

## Closest comparisons and rejected versions

- **Correia, “A kind route from grounding to fundamentality” (2021), especially §5.1, PDF pp. 9–11.** Direct adverse overlap: his alternating mental/physical subkinds already show that universal cross-kind grounding can be reciprocal despite a token-level descending sequence. Therefore neither “coarse kinds can cycle” nor the two-kind alternating example is our contribution. Proposed difference: the present result uses a structure with *no infinite chains at all*, quantifies over *every finite refinement*, and identifies uniform bounded depth as precisely what makes repair possible. Correia's own foundation relation excludes reverse partial grounds; it is not our existential quotient, and our result does not refute his account.
- **Rabin and Rabern, “Well Founding Grounding Grounding” (2016; preprint 2015), §4.** They separate finite grounding, a foundation, and lower bounds. We accept the need for those distinctions. We add a separate representational condition—uniform bounded path length—and determine its exact relation to finite acyclic classifications. The word “well-founded” will not be used as if their notions were interchangeable.
- **Mirsky, “A Dual of Dilworth’s Decomposition Theorem” (1971), Theorem 2.** The finite-height/finite-antichain-cover relationship is established mathematics, and the rank construction is elementary. The claimed contribution is not that theorem, a new graph invariant, or a renamed theorem. It is the philosophical consequence for finite-refinement diagnostics of grounding cycles, including the countermodel to the assumption that finite termination guarantees finite taxonomic repair.
- **Geiger et al., “Causal Abstraction: A Theoretical Foundation for Mechanistic Interpretability” (2025), p. 7 and §2.7.** They explicitly show acyclic causal models can have cyclic abstractions. That observation is prior art, not novel here. Our question concerns when *any* finite strict-edge abstraction is possible, with no inference from causal intervention semantics to metaphysical grounding.
- **Busse, “No foundations for metaphysical coherentism” (online 2023; print 2025), §§1, 3.** He challenges purported dependence cycles through more detailed analysis. The present argument supports caution about alleged cycles but adds a limit: structural refinement can require infinitely many cells even in an entirely finite-chain structure. We do not attribute to Busse a demand for a finite diagram; the result constrains a possible strengthening of his strategy.
- **Schaffer, “On What Grounds What” (2009), §1.3.** His sorted/ordered distinction is relevant background: categories and dependency order are different structures. The present criterion says when a *finite* sorting can retain every strict arrow of an ordering. Schaffer does not assert the finite-chart guarantee criticized here.

Rejected preliminary idea: merely noticing type/token equivocation in cycles. The primary readings already supply it. Also rejected as sole contribution: generic reciprocity versus universal reciprocity; this is close to Correia's existing discussion. The retained thesis must stand or fall on the exact refinement criterion and its philosophical consequence, not on those weaker points.

## Objections and limits to address in a draft, if released

The strongest novelty objection is that the result is only Mirsky's theorem applied to grounding. The response must earn its keep through the specific no-finite-repair consequence and the difference between explaining each fact finitely and representing the entire order by finite levels. If the independent critic judges this still insufficient, revise or set aside before drafting.

The strongest substantive objection is that no serious level theorist demands preservation of every token edge. Concede the restricted target. The argument is a conditional diagnostic, not a refutation of kind fundamentality or science's levels. A meaningful application is evaluating the demand that a metaphysical cycle disappear after a sufficiently detailed but finite reclassification. The draft must not fabricate an established opponent who makes that demand.

Naturalness may preclude the sufficient rank refinement: the criterion proves existence of arbitrary cells, not natural kinds. Necessity remains valid under naturalness restrictions; sufficiency then may fail. A finite chart can also be useful while lossy. No normative demand for complete diagrams is defended.
