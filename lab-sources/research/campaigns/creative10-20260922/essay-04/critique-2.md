## 1. Does the draft deliver the approved increment?

Yes, in a bounded sense. Sections 1 and 3 identify loss of usable *unaffected* support rather than falsity, failed honesty, or the generic difficulty of reversing assertion. Section 4 argues from invited reliance, asymmetric control of repair-relevant information, foreseeable costs, and inexpensive retention. Section 6 distinguishes doing one's best after erasure from having acted responsibly before it. Together these yield the advertised counterexample to the sufficiency of initial warrant plus later honest correction.

The new application has philosophical content because it isolates an audience-facing burden and explains why successful current correction can coexist with a prior communicative failure. The criterion also disciplines the practical recommendation: retain distinctions that change correction targets, not every detail or necessarily personal identities. These are useful discriminations, although not a new theory of epistemic duty.

Much of the machinery is inherited. The paired-source construction, the importance of provenance, conservative overinclusion, and the thought that future repair can justify present records are not discoveries here. Section 2 now acknowledges that plainly. There is no unsupported first-ever claim. The draft would overstate its contribution if later described as establishing that recordkeeping obligations or diachronic testimonial responsibilities were previously unknown.

## 3. Repairs that matter

### A. Explain the transition from beneficial retention to a directed duty

Section 4 is substantially better than merely asserting usefulness, but its central move still rests on calling the transfer of burden unfair. The reader can accept that erasure is wasteful without accepting that it wrongs recipients. Sharpen why the desk's own invitation and exclusive control make these particular avoidable costs its responsibility. Distinguish this from an independent entitlement to receive the best affordable information service.

The risk-acceptance point is suggestive but not decisive. Accepting fallibility does not entail accepting avoidable spillover; equally, that logical distinction alone does not prove a right against spillover. Do not let the former stand in for the latter. Explain what makes the underlying reliance arrangement reasonable and what kind of notice, if any, could genuinely limit it. A blanket disclaimer should not automatically settle the question, especially if ordinary users cannot reasonably avoid the desk.

This is the main philosophical repair. It strengthens the approved thesis without requiring a replacement topic.

### B. Make the scope of foreseeable risk more explicit

Cheap retention and a foreseeable possibility do not automatically outweigh even small ongoing costs. The draft already includes stakes, alternatives, and competing reasons; use them to say whether the duty needs a non-negligible likelihood or expected burden during the invited reliance period. Otherwise an imaginable but extraordinarily remote defect can generate too many preservation duties.

The bridge example may stipulate a normal, recognized possibility of source-specific faults. Do not imply that the information criterion alone identifies every distinction anyone morally ought to preserve. It identifies an instrumental requirement once the relevant notices and desired selectivity have been selected.

### C. Keep the formal and practical results aligned

Section 3 properly separates original support from present overall entitlement. Keep this qualification in summaries and the final conclusion. The numerical illustration is arithmetically correct under the stated equally likely assignments: (0.99 + 0.50)/2 = 0.745. Its practical payoff needs the availability of different routes and stakes at which choosing the reliably supported one matters; it does not show erasure worsens every conceivable decision. The text mostly says this already. A short concrete decision comparison would make the importance less dependent on the reader supplying it.

For the formal condition, qualify comparisons to histories compatible with the notice as well as the retained information. Some notices can themselves reveal a source assignment or be impossible in some histories. The bridge case holds the same notice fixed and is sound; the general formulation should not accidentally require distinguishing histories that the notice already excludes.

## 4. Significance and final assessment

The manuscript offers a defensible small contribution: an analysis of how truthful relays can impose an avoidable epistemic burden by preventing future selective correction, plus a conditional norm assigning responsibility for that burden. Its significance is diagnostic and normative, not computational, and not a wholesale revision of assertion theory.

I continue to judge **PASS_RELATIVE** reasonable for the narrow contribution, after examining the actual manuscript rather than deferring to the precheck. The most important unresolved issue is strength of the directed-duty argument, not source accuracy. Repair A should receive priority. Repairs B and C prevent an attractive limited conclusion from being read as a universal preservation rule.
