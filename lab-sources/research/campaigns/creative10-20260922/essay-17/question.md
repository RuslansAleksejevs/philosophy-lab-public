# Essay 17: choosing a bounded question

Author context: /root/creative_author_17. Research date: 2026-09-22. No manuscript has been drafted.

Candidate A: Can gratitude require keeping one's gratitude undisclosed because disclosure defeats the original benefit? Rejected for this attempt: search immediately found the established distinction between expressive gratitude and benefactor-directed goodwill, including anonymous reciprocation. No defended increment yet.

Candidate B: Can regret be fitting without an all-things-considered preference for the alternative life? Rejected for this attempt: a broad familiar dispute, with no sufficiently precise new argument identified. Search returned decision-theoretic and developmental work and Bittner-related discussions; coverage is preliminary, not grounds for any general verdict about the field.

Candidate C: What does a recipient reject when she rejects a sincere apology? Selected as a narrow critical question about Oliver Hallich's 2025 argument. Existing work already separates acceptance from forgiveness and demands accurate acknowledgment. The possible contribution is specifically a counterexample to the inference that content-related rejection collapses into doubt about sincerity, and hence to the exhaustion argument for an ineliminable request for forgiveness.

A temporal alternative about withdrawing accepted apologies was briefly explored but is not selected. Process theories and literature on taking back forgiveness make a generic temporal thesis unpromising without additional work.

Selection rationale: the chosen question permits a precise, source-located correction rather than advertising a new theory of apology. The key test holds genuine wrongdoing, standing, known sincerity and the victim's forgiveness fixed while varying accurate acknowledgment of which culpable contribution occurred. Independent precheck must decide whether that correction is substantial enough. A merely new example of Smith's familiar account would not qualify.

Correction to provisional search vocabulary: one search used the unsupported surname Schaber. The target author's identity was subsequently checked directly on the publisher page: Oliver Hallich. The mistaken query is retained in the search log; no substantive attribution relies on it.
