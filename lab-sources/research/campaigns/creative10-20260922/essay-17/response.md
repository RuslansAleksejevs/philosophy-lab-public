# Responses to both independent reviews

Decision: REVISE within the exact approved thesis; retain the modest critical result. Neither review established a fatal defect. The revised final does not claim a complete semantics or that apologies cannot include a conventional forgiveness request. Original draft and reviews remain unchanged.

## Argument opponent

1. Integration versus embedded assertion — ACCEPTED. The final specifies Ada's offer as an answer to Bea's complaint about how exclusion occurred. It gives a contextual criterion for the relevant refusal: offered acknowledgment, incorporated contested content, and refusal to close the exchange as satisfactorily answered. It distinguishes recognition of the apologetic act, acceptance/reaffirmation of any forgiveness component, and acceptance of the reparative acknowledgment. It expressly leaves open whether the last is a distinct sense or contextual use of acceptance. This strengthens a local alternative without claiming a demonstrated universal semantics.

2. Normative relevance does not itself make acceptance misleading — ACCEPTED. The missing premise is now explicit: in this particular exchange unqualified acceptance would reasonably communicate satisfaction with the answer Ada offered. Qualified acceptance remains available and reasonable; neither accuracy nor concern about Cal determines all things considered acceptance. No general duty to police explanations is introduced.

3. Charitable target and force of conclusion — ACCEPTED. The target discussion now acknowledges that Hallich recognizes some alternative rejection uses. The final separates the collapse into sincerity from comparative naturalness and the universal conventional-force thesis. It replaces the broad “defeats” language with an identified gap in the exclusion of an alternative explanation. No claim is made that one case establishes the most natural usage overall.

4. Retain controls and concessions — ACCEPTED. Ada's culpability, remorse and standing stay fixed; she commits no new knowing deception. Bea's forgiveness does not change. The possibility of requesting reaffirmation and of selective acceptance is retained. The contrast with an incidental assertion is now treated as limited support, not proof of semantic individuation.

## Residual limitation

The argument supports a contextually intelligible acknowledgment-based response and contests its exclusion. It does not empirically establish ordinary-language frequency or settle whether this response individuates a distinct speech-act component. A defender may maintain a forgiveness-directed component with further argument. This remaining disagreement is part of the final's bounded outcome, not concealed as a solved general theory. The approved thesis is narrowed in precision, not replaced; no new novelty gate is asserted.
