# Candidate questions — 2026-09-22

AI-originated preliminary research directions; no manuscript yet.

1. **Policy-induced exit and democratic ratification.** Can a policy gain retrospective democratic legitimacy from the approval of those who remain when its opponents have left because of it? The promising target is an identification problem: identical survivor approval can result from persuasion or selective departure. Develop implications for what a renewal vote can establish, rather than simply recommending inclusion of affected people.
2. **Institutional apology and divided beneficiaries.** Can an institution obtain forgiveness from a representative while the remedy itself changes who is willing to be represented? Potential contribution: representation might be adequate for distributing remedies but inadequate for forgiving in others' names. Rejected provisionally because collective forgiveness literature likely already makes this distinction.
3. **Agency and the right to interrupt.** Do members of a collective agent have a stronger claim to halt an organizational action than to choose its replacement, when the action destroys their future capacity for dissent? Attractive but likely an application of democratic self-preservation and contestatory republicanism.
4. **Rotation and responsibility.** Can mandatory rotation of office undermine collective responsibility even while strengthening electoral accountability, because nobody retains authority to discharge an admitted obligation? Interesting institutional design problem, but likely resolvable by ordinary continuity of corporate obligations.

Select direction 1 for investigation. Seek a precise argument that separates the epistemic value of survivor approval from its possible independent prospective authority. Do not assume that all emigrants or former members retain permanent voting rights. Alternatives remain provisional and are not novelty-certified.
