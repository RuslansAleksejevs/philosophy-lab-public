## Judgment
**Retain the candidate; focused revision warranted.** The actual draft earns the narrow relative-originality judgment independently of the precheck. It develops a coherent argument beyond the survivor-bias example and stays within the approved proposal. It does not justify a broad priority claim concerning incompatible safeguards, plural democratic jurisdictions, or sunset accountability.

The worthwhile result is that prospectively legitimate renewal can coexist with an unfulfilled retrospective assurance to trial participants who have lost membership. Full information and proper present voting do not cure the latter defect. Former participants can retain standing to demand an answer without acquiring permanent authority over future policy. The draft makes these distinctions explicit, including an independent review that need not obtain complainants' agreement. This combination has practical significance for how an uncertain collective trial is justified before it begins.

## Main originality repair: Hirschman is more than a causal background
The draft candidly acknowledges selective exit and says its distinctive target is the combination of safeguards. But Hirschman's chapter 4 is explicitly about the difficulty of combining exit and voice. The targeted postdraft retrieval confirms arguments about exit draining the people who could activate voice, with institutional design affecting that balance. Thus “one protection disables another” is itself inherited, not the additional philosophical result.

Repair the attribution in “More than missing information” and the later contribution statements. Credit both selective depletion and the broader exit/voice interaction to Hirschman. Then locate the increment in the distinct normative question: why answerability to already exposed participants survives loss of membership even when current governance need not restore their electoral power. Do not claim that the mere diagnosis of safeguards failing to add up is new. This correction strengthens the essay without requiring a new thesis.

## Inheritance versus result
Erman: function-sensitive inclusion is correctly acknowledged as prior work. The draft does not pretend to invent different standing for different kinds of decisions. Campos, checked during preflight, provides additional background for differentiated boundaries; adding every background citation is unnecessary because no general priority claim remains.

Molloy: the manuscript properly credits the established need for effective, inclusive sunset review. Its residual claim is not simply that a fair vote is insufficient. It specifies a defect that can persist even with sincere information-rich prospective deliberation, because the competent question and the people owed an answer differ. This is compatible with Molloy's framework rather than a refutation. The distinction should remain narrow; effective review in Molloy's richer sense could already accommodate the proposed remedy.

Kuyper: the temporary-escape qualification is accurate to accessed passages. No evidence shows his own arrangements necessarily remove review standing. The draft wisely avoids claiming that. It would be misleading to present the workshop example as a demonstrated counterexample to his entire proposal.

The core duty relies on familiar continuity and assurance principles. That does not eliminate the result, but it limits its ambition. The draft's explicit admission that an ordinary business can also owe review is a strength. It establishes a constraint on one justification of democratic experimentation, not a distinct duty arising from democracy alone.

## Precision repairs
1. “A second question with adequate standing and powers is necessary” should retain the conditional scope: necessary to deliver the stronger assurance in the affected designs, not necessary for every temporary collective policy.
2. Explain briefly the difference between making redress recommendations and possessing an effective route to redress. A powerless recommendation procedure would not satisfy the draft's own definition. It is sufficient to require an identified competent decision-maker and a route for implementing warranted correction; no administrative blueprint is needed.
3. The workshop assurance speaks of assessment in light of “members' actual experience.” Clarify once that the assurance is owed to people as trial participants, without a continued-membership condition. Otherwise a reader may dispute the premise rather than the inference. The draft already argues against treating exit as automatic waiver, so this is clarification, not a new premise smuggled into the conclusion.
4. Avoid upgrading “may reasonably accept” into a general empirical claim that sunset promises cause initial consent. The existing illustrative modal language is suitable.
5. Correct the Hirschman locator to chapter 4 pp. 51–54 if this is the intended bounded passage: the accessed table of contents starts chapter 5 at p. 55. The present “chapter 4, especially pp. 51–55” is slightly imprecise.
