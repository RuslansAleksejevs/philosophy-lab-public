# Proposal p1: What a second vote cannot repair

## Exact thesis
A sunset-and-renewal procedure cannot by itself make a membership-affecting political experiment democratically reversible. When experiencing the policy selectively causes members to leave before its review, a perfectly fair renewal vote can have prospective authority for the remaining association while lacking authority to discharge the trial's accountability to former members. The institutional implication is a separation between **renewal jurisdiction** and **trial-accountability jurisdiction**, not permanent voting rights for all former members. A trial advertised as corrigible to its initial subjects must preserve their route to an answer about the trial even if they leave; the date at which the rule expires is insufficient.

Scope: voluntary associations with public or collective decision-making powers, and local institutions where membership legitimately changes. Applying this directly to citizenship requires further argument. No claim that departure establishes injustice, or that renewal normally extinguishes legal liability.

## Main argument and counterexample
1. A promised later review can help justify an uncertain trial by making the institution answerable for what its initial subjects actually experience. This is stronger than merely promising that future members may choose future rules. The argument addresses the stronger assurance only.
2. Experience can change the population entitled to exercise that review. This can occur without fraud, coercion, manipulation or objectively bad policy.
3. Consider an association of 100 members unanimously authorizing a one-year pilot on the assurance that adverse experience will receive effective review. Forty expect benefit; sixty are uncertain. At the end forty support renewal and remain. Sixty have left. Compare two histories with the same initial vote, same current membership, same sincere unanimous renewal vote, and same administrative record of departures. In A, the sixty found the trial worthwhile but left for independent reasons. In B, those sixty suffered a serious unforeseen burden, complained, then left because relief could not wait until the sunset. The renewal vote cannot distinguish these histories. Even complete transparency to the forty does not make their vote the sixty's release of the institution from answering those complaints.
4. This is not merely a statistical problem. Suppose the full history B is known. The remaining forty may have a sound claim to govern their future common activity; the departed sixty may have no continuing stake in that activity. Yet the forty cannot fulfill a promise of effective review made to the sixty merely by exercising their own future governing power. The institution's continuity preserves the obligation although membership continuity does not preserve the electorate.
5. Neither freezing the original electorate for all future decisions nor rejecting all survivor decisions follows. Distinguishing the two questions avoids that false choice: who may authorize continuation, and who is owed an answer about the trial? Answerability includes receiving complaints, a reasoned assessment by an appropriate decision-maker, and capacity for warranted correction or redress; it does not guarantee compensation or an individual veto.
6. Thus a sunset may preserve reversibility of the legal rule while losing reversibility of the accountability relation. A defensible pilot must separately specify post-exit complaint standing or an equivalent independent review with consequential powers. If such review is already preserved, the objection is met. Where exit is costless and informed, no promise of retrospective review exists, and the association simply offers different terms prospectively, the result may not apply.

## Defended increment
The numerical survivor-bias observation is elementary and not claimed as original. Hirschman's selective-loss mechanism is also inherited. The proposed contribution is the jurisdictional result obtained when that mechanism interacts with a particular justification for provisional democratic decisions: the very exit safeguard that protects people during a trial can remove them from the body supposedly empowered to assess whether that trial honored its terms. A second vote can therefore be simultaneously authoritative for continuation and inadequate as fulfillment of the original review assurance. The remedy preserves the legitimate change of electorate and allocates an enduring, issue-specific claim against the collective institution. This is a qualified limit on the democratic value of procedural reversibility, not a new name for disenfranchisement.

## Closest comparisons
- Albert O. Hirschman, *Exit, Voice, and Loyalty* (1970), chapter 4, especially pp. 51–55. His account explains selective loss of active dissatisfied members and consequent failure of recuperation. This supplies the causal mechanism; it does not in the accessed passage distinguish the powers of a renewal electorate from the institution's obligation to initial experimental subjects. The essay must acknowledge this substantial dependence.
- Jonathan W. Kuyper, “Designing institutions for global democracy: flexibility through escape clauses and sunset provisions” (2013), pp. 198–203, 205–209. He defends flexibility as facilitating cooperation, learning and escape from path dependence. My result identifies a conditional tension between exit and renewal when they are supposed jointly to secure answerability to trial participants. His temporary escape clauses do not necessarily remove membership; the objection applies to designs involving actual loss of standing, not his entire program.
- Robert E. Goodin, “Proximity Principle, Adieu” (2024), especially opening sections and its explicit separation of franchise principles from what should be settled by votes. His approach extends voting rights beyond geography according to interaction/affectedness. I do not claim to discover issue-sensitive inclusion. My inference instead permits the current electorate to remain unchanged for renewal while locating trial claims in a separate process; it relies on a specific assurance and institutional continuity rather than a universal all-affected franchise.
- Robert Farrow, “European Constitutionalism and ‘Retrospective’ Legitimacy” (2007 conference paper), pp. 10–13. He questions imposing a constitutional scheme despite current opposition in hope of retrospective democratic acceptance. My counterexample grants unanimous initial trial authorization and valid present renewal, locating the failure between those valid acts. I do not attribute Farrow's interpretation directly to Habermas without reading Habermas.
- Daniel Francis, “Exit Legitimacy” (2017), abstract only. Exit can enhance consent and autonomy. My proposal is compatible with that prospective benefit; abstract access cannot establish absence of closer arguments in the paper.

## Planned pressure tests
Distinguish ordinary membership churn from causal exclusion; distinguish review from compensation; show why initial unanimous consent does not waive expressly retained review; allow informed explicit waiver; ask whether independent representation can discharge answerability without former members' participation; concede the result is conditional on the stronger democratic assurance and does not condemn legitimate specialization of associations. Serious novelty risk: the result may be judged a familiar persistence-of-obligation principle applied to institutional experiments. Independent precheck must assess whether the two-jurisdiction resolution and counterexample add enough.
