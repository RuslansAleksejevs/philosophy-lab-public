## What the draft must establish

1. Specify the comparison behind “increase dependence.” Relative to actually hearing thin testimony, the reader might already depend completely on the critic. The defensible increase concerns losing an available route to independent evidence, or comparing later inquiry under alternative disclosure orders; it need not mean greater present credence based on testimony.
2. Separate episode evidence from an audience-general claim. One uninformed reaction can inform the latter without verifying it. Explain why losing this defeasible sample matters without making first impressions sovereign.
3. Preserve the distinction between an invalid direct inference from guided recognition and a legitimate inference aided by models, memories or comparison readers. The claim is not that informed critics cannot judge successful concealment.
4. Do not make the argument depend on an unnoticed bias. An informed reader who perfectly recognizes the limitation still lacks that particular uninformed encounter. This is the strongest way to show a result beyond merely warning against hindsight bias.
5. An “accurate explanation” must not smuggle in independently verified truth of the evaluative conclusion. Grant truth for the case while distinguishing the hearer's warrant for each premise.

These are development and attribution requirements within the approved thesis, not a request for a different proposal.
