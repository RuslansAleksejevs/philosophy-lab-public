## Verdict

Retain and revise narrowly. The actual manuscript realizes the approved modest contribution; it is not automatically accepted because precheck passed. The two inquiry orders, the separation of structural and response premises, and the perfectly calibrated informed reader jointly distinguish its result from a generic warning about spoilers. There is a defensible relative increment in analyzing the distribution of opportunities to check a critic. It should not be presented as a discovery of hindsight's evidential costs or as a challenge that overturns testimony theories.

## What survives the originality challenge

The manuscript now squarely acknowledges Fischhoff's relevance and testing results. Its strongest step is the competent reader who avoids biased inference yet lacks an observation under the relevant conditions. This takes the thesis beyond psychological error: the missed encounter is a possible epistemic cost of successful teaching itself. That cost is then located within a mixed assessment, rather than equated with inability to appreciate or understand the work.

Still, this is a synthesis and specified consequence of established distinctions, not a new general epistemology. [Fischhoff's discussion](https://web.mit.edu/curhan/www/docs/Articles/15341_Readings/Behavioral_Decision_Theory/Fischhoff_1975_Hindsight_is_not_equal_to_foresight.pdf) already makes retrospective interpretation problematic for testing. The manuscript appropriately states the inherited portion; keep those admissions and avoid priority rhetoric in later abstracts or summaries.

[Hazlett](https://philpapers.org/archive/HAZTUA.pdf) already permits understanding while dependent on a speaker; [Hills](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/nous.12344) does not claim every explanation provides autonomous judgement. The manuscript respects both points. Its difference concerns what explanation removes, relative to another sequence of inquiry, rather than the bare persistence of dependence.

The negative-evaluation example is useful: an explanation can impede testing a criticism that a narrative telegraphs its ending, without depriving anyone of a successful reveal. This narrows the overlap with [Johnson's early draft](https://www.ou.edu/content/dam/cas/philosophy-new/events/mew-2025/Johnson%20MEW.pdf). It does not show incompatibility with Johnson, and the draft correctly says her account can accommodate the loss. The same restraint is appropriate for Levy.

## Revisions worth making

1. Clarify the preservation problem in the first inquiry order. Reading the story itself eventually reveals the solution, so an unguided encounter followed by later explanation does not by itself guarantee a presently accessible independent check. Its evidential contribution must survive in sufficiently reliable episodic memory or in a contemporaneous record. The final pedagogical paragraph mentions records, but the central comparison should make this condition explicit where it first treats the episode as evidence. Otherwise an opponent can press that both completed inquiry orders end in hindsight. This is not fatal: the first can preserve evidence that the second never generated.

2. Keep the comparative dependence claim precise throughout. The draft admirably distinguishes current confidence from the opportunity for subsequent checking. But “a single successful explanation can redistribute dependence” is most securely warranted as redistribution of available checks, with actual increased testimonial reliance requiring the further condition already stated: no adequate independent replacement. Reiterate that scope briefly at the transition to the general conclusion. No need to retreat from the thesis or add another elaborate label.

3. Mark the limits of the fiction example's demonstration. The invented latch/gloves/time case illustrates the structure but does not establish the psychological claim that a particular reader would have had a worthwhile unguided sample. The explicit conditional formulation largely handles this. Preserve it; do not strengthen the conclusion into a claim about typical readers or the overall effects of explanatory teaching.

4. An optional positive-control sentence would sharpen the domain: guidance that reveals a stable structural feature need not change the conditions relevant to testing its presence. The manuscript already implies this; an explicit contrast would show why the argument does not make every instruction epistemically suspect.
