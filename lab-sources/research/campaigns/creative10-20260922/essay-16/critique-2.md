## 2. The controlled comparison has a narrower logical reach than some wording suggests
Section3 begins with an outstanding community claim and assumes an independently grounded local duty. It then adds another claimant while holding that community claim fixed. That demonstrates the irrelevance of bare additional multiplicity given the assumed independence. It does not independently prove that a local duty and unsettled community claim can coexist; someone denying precisely that possibility will challenge the starting stipulation.

This is not fatal because the essay expressly makes a conditional claim and supplies an explanatory distinction between the local acknowledgment's object and public repair. But call the comparison a conditional test of the multiplication inference, rather than letting “counterexample” suggest that every premise establishing the local duty has been defended. Explain that the justification for treating the local reason as unchanged is its specified object—accurately recognizing this person's acknowledgment of this wrong—not simply the instruction to hold reasons fixed. The important claim is absence of an inference from multiplicity alone, not general separability of all moral reasons.

## 3. Waiver: separate effectiveness, permission and obligation consistently
The garden analogy usefully distinguishes power from permissible use. Later “reasons not to permit a particular waiver” could blur that distinction again: endangering others might make exercising an effective power wrongful without making its exercise invalid. Clarify which possible objections concern valid discharge and which concern whether one should discharge. Also make explicit that waiving the address does not automatically waive every attitudinal responsibility on the victim's side. The essay need not defend Fabre's broader waiver package to criticize one scope inference.
