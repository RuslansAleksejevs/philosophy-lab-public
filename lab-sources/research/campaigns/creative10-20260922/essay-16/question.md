4. Selected after reading Colin Hay: Does the inability of a victim to settle everyone else's reparative claims undermine her authority to settle her own? More specifically, does the universal content of a commitment not to reoffend make every possible beneficiary a party to the apology addressed to one actual victim?

Provisional contribution: a limited criticism of the multipartite branch of Hay's argument against a duty to accept apologies. Distinguish who benefits from the apologizer's reform, who has actually been wronged, and whose claim a particular act of acceptance discharges. Construct a controlled comparison holding the victim's apology and burdens constant while adding a second injured claimant. Adding a claimant defeats total settlement but does not by itself defeat a local directed normative change. Identify the extra coupling premise needed for the contrary inference.

This does not prove Fabre's general duty, deny burdens, presume forgiveness, or deny community claims. Hay already distinguishes private/public repair; the proposed increment must be the counterexample and missing inference, not that familiar distinction. Primary texts and adverse evidence are being checked before a separate proposal and gate.
