# Proposed essay: One Apology Is Not Everyone's Settlement

## Exact thesis and target
A narrowly directed objection to Colin Hay's multipartite argument: the fact that one victim cannot settle the wrongdoer's relations with other people does not itself diminish her authority to settle her own claim, or defeat whatever independent pro tanto duty she might have to acknowledge adequate repair. This remains true when the content of a genuine apology includes an unrestricted commitment not to repeat the wrong against anyone. The proposed essay does not defend a general duty to accept apologies.

## Main argument
Distinguish three sets: beneficiaries of future reform; holders of claims arising from an actual wrong (including risk victims and collective claimants where justified); and persons whose claims a specific acceptance purports to settle. These sets can differ. In particular, the first is not a measure of the third.

Controlled example: a careless driver damages two independently owned bicycles in a single incident. He acknowledges both wrongs, sincerely reforms his driving toward everyone, and separately offers each owner an adequate apology. One owner accepts; the other postpones an encounter. There is no shared compensatory fund, concealment, pressure, selective remorse, or claim of public exoneration. If accepting the first owner's apology could settle her local claim in a one-owner case, the addition of an independent claimant does not by itself invalidate that settlement. She is not deputized to speak for the second owner merely because she accepts a promise of universal care. Her first-person assurance can be complete in its scope while the overall repair remains incomplete.

A duty version is deliberately conditional: assume, for argument, that she has a local pro tanto duty to acknowledge the repair. Adding a third person's unsettled claim does not supply a defeater unless a further connection is shown. The countermodel leaves her original grounds, evidence and burden unchanged. Thus public incompleteness alone cannot show that the duty was falsely generated. This inference is distinct from proving that such a duty exists.

The substantive diagnostic result is a necessary question for a successful multipartite objection: WHAT couples the separate settlements? Candidate answers include acceptance that actually purports to speak for everyone, diversion of a shared scarce remedy, a public signal that predictably licenses continued wrong, or evidence that selective reparations undermine sincerity. These can defeat or qualify local acceptance. The mere number of affected people cannot do their work. This is not a universally sufficient checklist; it isolates the missing link.

A further consequence concerns waiver: relinquishing my demand to receive an apologetic address cannot, merely because that address would include universal forbearance, waive either your first-order duty not to harm others or their directed reparative claims. Apology is not what first creates the prohibition on reoffending.

## Closest works and adverse evidence
Hay (2025), particularly “The waiving of the duty to apologize” and “From apology to reparation,” moves from outsiders' unsettled claims to limitations on the victim's waiver and acceptance, and hence to weakening a duty to accept. He already allows a private/public division of repair. The proposed increment is therefore NOT discovering that division: it is showing, through the controlled two-owner case and a coupling requirement, why his negative inference does not follow even when public incompleteness is granted. His separate victim-burden objection remains untouched.

Fabre, “The Duty to Accept Apologies” (online 2023; volume21,355–378), grounds an acceptance duty in respect for the apologizer's agency. Her author-hosted PDF p15 explicitly denies that acceptance returns an expelled person to the moral community. That passage supports the limited textual claim that communal readmission is not the necessary goal of her account. It does not establish her full theory.

Christopher Bennett (2018), “The Alteration Thesis,” already distinguishes directed obligations a victim can waive from non-directed obligations she cannot waive, and discusses acceptance without forgiveness (accepted manuscript pp12–15). This is strong adverse novelty evidence: the basic architecture is inherited, not invented here. The new work is its application to a specific later argument, identifying two illicit scope transitions and stating when the objection can instead succeed. A generic essay about local versus public forgiveness would be repetition and must not be written.

Hallich (2025) argues that apologies request forgiveness and impose problematic pressure. The present argument does not require a final resolution of that dispute: even if acceptance includes forgiveness, one person's act need not claim to forgive on behalf of another. Harrison (2026) treats apologies as extended processes. The two-owner countermodel can place the separate acceptances after warranted reform; no instantaneous-completion theory is assumed. Tobi's imperfect duties of victims concern broader epistemic justice, not the inference targeted here.

## Planned objections and limits
(1) Hay only says “diminish,” not “erase”: a lost public benefit can weaken a consequential argument, but it does not weaken unchanged directed grounds without further argument.
(2) Moral reform is global: granting global sincerity requirements does not globalize the authority of uptake.
(3) Collective injustices are indivisible: some are; these require a genuine coupling premise and are not contradicted by the separated case.
(4) Other victims supply reasons for solidarity: sometimes decisive, but not identical to lack of authority to discharge my own claim.
(5) The distinction is old: openly acknowledge Bennett; the contribution is a bounded counterexample and correction, not a novel normative-power framework.
(6) A victim may have no acceptance duty at all: compatible with the conclusion.
(7) Actual apologies create social pressure or imply exoneration: retain these as independent defeaters, not idealize them out of practical advice.
