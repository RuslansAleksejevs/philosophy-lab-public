# Argument critique: Difference without Reconstruction

Role: fresh argument opponent, essay 09. Date: 2026-09-22.
Scope: this essay's draft, question, proposal and source record, plus the campaign role/archive instructions. No other review or essay was consulted. This report evaluates the argument internally; it does not independently verify the bibliography, the interpretation of Nourbakhshi, or priority claims. No additional external sources were used.

## Charitable reconstruction

The essay addresses a restricted necessity claim: full phenomenal reconstruction, or preservation of an independently inspectable phenomenal component, is necessary for recollective evidence of original phenomenal difference. Against this it proposes a transformation of whole phenomenal types that can merge some distinct inputs while never assigning different outputs to identical inputs. Unequal outputs then entail unequal inputs, although equal outputs need not entail equal inputs. The table supplies a coherent example of this elementary relation. Since neither type identity nor a detachable preserved part appears in the inference, failure of reconstruction and dependence among phenomenal parts do not alone refute it.

The author expressly does not claim that actual memory realizes this transformation, that unequal outputs establish cognitive rather than affective difference, or that the conditional result resolves the psychological/phenomenal identification problem. Those limits matter: objections that merely repeat them would not refute the stated result.

My assessment is REVISE. The conditional entailment is valid. The main vulnerability is its philosophical and evidential significance, especially the difference between a formal mapping that can be imposed retrospectively on a pair and a warranted procedure for learning about that pair. There is also a repairable overstatement about the requirements for a particular null contrast.

## 1. Strongest objection: the extensional condition may be vacuous where the method matters

Location: “What a comparison must preserve,” especially “For the episodes covered by the comparison”; “The assumption real memories must earn”; and the conclusion's claim that the procedure can inform us about differences.

The displayed condition is true whenever the domain contains no two distinct episodes of exactly the same phenomenal type. Whole experiences may differ in an incidental sensation, mood or temporal nuance. Consequently a domain consisting of two actual episodes can satisfy the condition regardless of how their recollections were produced. This is a logical possibility, not an empirical claim about how often exact phenomenal repeats occur.

Consider two original wholes a and b that differ only in incidental discomfort. Their later memories u and v differ because one retrieval takes place under a leading cue. On this two-episode domain, the displayed condition is satisfied: no unequal recollections have been assigned to equal original types. The conclusion that the originals differed is true, but the actual recollective difference is generated by the cue and supplies no demonstrated evidential access to the original discomfort. Calling the pair a function of phenomenal type does not identify what explains the output variation.

Nothing here invalidates contraposition. If the conditional is independently known and the outputs differ, the conclusion follows. The problem is that an extensional conditional on the actual pair is equivalent, for that pair with unequal outputs, to the conclusion to be established. Merely describing it as a transformation condition does not create independent warrant. Nor does actual-domain satisfaction show that the procedure would avoid a false contrast if the original phenomenal types had matched.

The draft acknowledges associated beliefs and retrieval circumstances. That is good, but its present answer does not distinguish two importantly different grounds for the condition: accidental satisfaction in the actual sample, and stability across relevant variations that could otherwise explain the result. Its language about a “procedure” and “manufactured differences” naturally suggests the latter; its quantification supplies only the former unless the domain is specified further.

Repair: explicitly distinguish (i) the extensional condition sufficient for a conditional entailment, from (ii) the independently warranted, appropriately modal or cross-case condition needed for an evidential procedure. State which relevant matched-target and retrieval variations belong in the domain. Do not silently require a fully developed empirical memory theory. A paragraph explaining why retrospective factorization is inadequate would already improve the result. If no positive justification is supplied, keep the conclusion to compatibility and a specification of outstanding obligations. This preserves the logical point but reduces any suggestion that a practical method has been vindicated.

Severity: major but repairable. It is fatal to an attempted empirical vindication, which the draft disclaims; it is not fatal to the narrow consistency claim.

## 2. The asymmetry belongs to the chosen class of procedures

Location: section title “A difference is easier to preserve than a verdict of sameness,” its first three paragraphs, and the opening claim of “asymmetric consequences.”

The formal asymmetry is valid given the no-manufactured-differences assumption and permission to merge types. It is not a general asymmetry between positive and negative evidence. A relational procedure can instead avoid merging while allowing the same original type to yield several outputs: let a yield either u or v, and let b yield w. Equal output types then identify equal inputs, while different outputs u and v can come from the same input a. This reverse pattern is available once episode-specific variation is permitted, as the draft itself permits in its realistic discussion.

This does not defeat the model. It shows that its asymmetry is conditional on the direction of reliability granted, rather than a reason to regard positive contrasts as intrinsically easier or more credible. The existing section heading is stronger than the formal result. The text often supplies the qualification, but the presentation should consistently maintain it.

Repair: frame the result as two independent error directions, with a one-sided guarantee under a specified assumption. Briefly give the reverse pattern, or state why the selected no-false-difference class is the relevant one for this particular rebuttal. Do not claim an evidential priority for positive contrasts in noisy real memory without comparative error assumptions.

Severity: repairable scope/presentation defect. The contribution survives, but it becomes a taxonomy of conditional requirements rather than a general privilege of positive contrast.

## 3. A particular matching output does not require global injectivity

Location: “To license that converse inference, the transformation would also have to avoid erasing original differences: distinct inputs would need distinct outputs. That is injectivity.”

For a guarantee covering every possible matching output, injectivity is the correct requirement. For a particular observed match, it is stronger than necessary. Let F(a)=u, F(b)=v and F(c)=v. Two u outputs entail that both inputs were a, despite the noninjectivity of F. What is needed for that observation is a singleton inverse image of u within the relevant domain. A match at v is inconclusive.

The initial a/b and c/d table correctly makes both possible matches inconclusive. The general prose should not turn that feature of the example into a necessary condition for every individual inference from equality. This matters philosophically because the essay argues for inference-relative rather than indiscriminate standards of memory fidelity; the same discipline should apply to null contrasts.

Repair: add “to license this inference uniformly for every possible matching output” before the injectivity claim and mention the weaker local condition. Likewise distinguish global no-manufactured-differences from a guarantee specific to the contrast actually used.

Severity: precise, repairable overstatement; not a failure of the main argument.

## 4. What the table establishes about holism

Location: “Why holism does not close this route,” and the description of a procedure “preserving no independently identifiable phenomenal component.”

Disjoint input and output type labels establish failure of whole-type reproduction. They do not, by themselves, establish alteration of every part: distinct wholes could share parts. The draft largely avoids this error by calling partwise alteration compatible with the model rather than proved by it. Preserve that qualification. The table does not interpret a part relation or phenomenal dependence, so it is not on its own a fully specified formal countermodel to every substantive version of phenomenal holism.

There is nonetheless a good argument here: internal dependence does not entail a prohibition on stable cross-stage relations between wholes. An opponent cannot defeat that argument simply by renaming the preserved partition a phenomenal component. A classification of possibilities need not be a constituent of each possibility. The manuscript's response to that objection is sound.

Repair: describe the table as displaying the cross-stage structure and the following prose as arguing for compatibility with the stated internal-dependence claims. Avoid suggesting that six uninterpreted labels independently establish psychological or metaphysical possibility. No elaborate metaphysics of experience is required for the limited challenge, but the limits of the countermodel should be explicit.

Severity: minor to moderate clarification.

## Significance and recommendation

The draft's mathematical implication is secure; no fatal logical defect has been identified. It also responsibly preserves the additional burdens of identifying phenomenal difference, locating its character and explaining its cause. Those concessions should remain.

The open question is whether the philosophical target really asserts a necessity of reconstruction for any indirect evidence, rather than an objection to uncalibrated introspective substitution in a specific phenomenal-contrast method. The draft explicitly shifts from perception/recollection comparison to paired recollections and from immediate comparison to inference using a warranted procedure. That shift can be useful, but the opponent may consistently accept it while retaining the original epistemic objection. I have not independently checked the cited text, so this is a dialectical burden, not a finding of source misrepresentation.

Recommendation: REVISE, retaining the countermodel. Sharpen the extensional/epistemic distinction, make the positive/null asymmetry conditional throughout, qualify injectivity as a uniform requirement, and state exactly what the original opponent must reject for the result to constitute a disagreement. If the source supports only the weaker evidential objection, present the essay as a clarification of sufficient conditions rather than its refutation. That narrower result survives, although whether it is a substantial enough contribution remains unresolved.
