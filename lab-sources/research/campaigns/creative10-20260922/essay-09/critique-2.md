## Requested clarification

In “What a comparison must preserve,” the domain is described as the episodes covered by the comparison. If readers take this to mean only the actual pair, the condition can be satisfied vacuously whenever those targets differ. Checking the observed outputs cannot independently establish the condition, because the desired conclusion concerns the unknown inputs.

The later calibration discussion substantially anticipates this concern, so the logical result does not fail. Still, explicitly say that practical justification must concern an independently delimited range of relevant possible episodes and retrieval conditions, or otherwise independently justify the no-manufacture counterfactual. The domain cannot be selected after noticing the desired contrast. This clarifies how the condition could constrain inference rather than redescribe its success. No empirical calibration experiment is demanded for the stated limited result.

A smaller presentational improvement: “A difference is easier to preserve than a verdict of sameness” can sound like a psychological comparative claim. The established comparison concerns logically weaker requirements, not how readily human memory meets them. Adjust the heading or immediately specify that sense.
