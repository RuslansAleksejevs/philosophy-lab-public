# Difference without reconstruction

Proposed thesis (not a manuscript): failure to reproduce the phenomenal character of an earlier experience, even when combined with phenomenal holism, does not establish the impossibility of recollection-based phenomenal contrast. The logically relevant preservation requirement for a positive contrast is weaker: a suitably matched comparison procedure must not manufacture differences from phenomenally identical targets. This yields a one-sided form of evidence. Different memory outputs can support original difference under that condition; matching memory outputs cannot support original sameness unless a stronger condition is justified.

## Exact target and relative increment

Nourbakhshi (2023), sections 4, 6–8, argues that replacing perceptions with memories/imaginations introduces potentially spurious contrast. He explicitly considers partial similarity, and answers a proposed subtraction of state-mode phenomenology by appealing to phenomenal holism. Thus the bare suggestion that recollection need only preserve relevant features is already in the closest paper; it is NOT claimed as my contribution.

The proposed increment is a countermodel that accepts holistic changes and does not subtract any component, followed by a diagnostic separation of two preservation requirements with opposite evidential roles. The countermodel undermines the necessity of full reflection, including the inference from interdependence of phenomenal parts to the unavailability of a valid comparison. It does not establish that actual novice/expert judgments are reliable.

## Argument to develop

1. Distinguish the target pair of earlier perceptual episodes from the present pair of evidential states through which their difference is assessed. The present states need not themselves be substitute instances of the earlier phenomenal types.

2. Let X be a set of whole phenomenal types. Under a fixed evidential procedure, let F assign each target type a recollective phenomenal type. This is an idealized deterministic countermodel, not a psychological theory of memory. If x=y, then F(x)=F(y); by contraposition F(x)≠F(y) entails x≠y. F need not reproduce x, be invertible, be linear, preserve distances, or leave any component untouched.

3. For an explicit finite countermodel, take four holistic perceptual types a,b,c,d and two recollective types u,v, with no member common to the two sets. Set F(a)=F(b)=u and F(c)=F(d)=v. Every original type changes on recollection, and some original distinctions disappear. Nothing is removed from a composite experience and treated as an independently invariant part. Nevertheless an observed u/v difference guarantees a difference between original inputs. This remains compatible with each part of a recollective state depending on the entire original state. A proof of incompatibility would require a premise stronger than the interdependence of phenomenal parts.

4. In the same model u/u does not distinguish an a/a input from a/b. So a null contrast is strictly weaker than a positive one for this class of procedures. The relevant division is not vivid versus faint memories, or full versus partial reconstruction, but whether the procedure creates false differences and whether it erases real differences. These are separate questions. Perfectly vivid recollections could fail the former through systematic suggestion; radically transformed recollections could satisfy it.

5. Actual memory is more plausibly represented F(x,z), where z includes elapsed time, retrieval cues, episode-specific beliefs, attention and reconstruction. Holding the mechanism constant is not holding z constant. A memory of recognizing a pine tree and a memory of not recognizing it may differ because z differs even if the original perceptual phenomenology did not. Therefore observed output difference warrants original phenomenal difference only if these routes are excluded or appropriately bounded. The countermodel must not quietly turn this evidential demand into an empirical assertion.

6. The resulting philosophical conclusion has two parts. The categorical reconstruction objection fails as a logical obstruction; a conditional, empirically demanding contrast method survives holistic distortion. Yet this success is limited to establishing that some original phenomenal difference exists. It neither identifies its content nor establishes that it is perceptual rather than affective or cognitive. Any richer inference still owes its own comparison of explanatory rivals.

## Why this is more than an illustration

The intended essay changes the burden of the dispute. Showing that a memory is phenomenally unlike its target, or that state and content phenomenology interact, no longer suffices to disqualify contrast. One must show a failure of the relevant no-false-difference condition or lack of justification for it. Conversely, ordinary confidence that memory is accurate is inadequate: its errors must be assessed in the direction needed for the inference. The positive/null asymmetry follows even if all original phenomenal parts are altered and memory loses information. That result is not obtained by the subtractive response Nourbakhshi already considers.

## Closest other work and objections

Fulkerson and Cohen (2025), sections 2–4, distinguish access to a psychological difference from evidence that it is phenomenal. Their objection remains live: choosing X as phenomenal types in the countermodel must not be used to certify actual data. My contribution concerns a separate obstruction, memory's failure to duplicate its target, and explicitly retains their identification burden.

Chudnoff, Phenomenal Contrast Arguments for Cognitive Phenomenology, accessed manuscript introduction and pp. 8–11, separates phenomenal presence, irreducibility and independence. My conditional difference result establishes none of those theses about cognition. It addresses the evidence used before such explanatory conclusions.

Strong objection: the countermodel assumes precisely the reliable mapping that opponents doubt. Reply: it does assume it for a possibility result; that is sufficient against the claimed implication from non-reconstruction/holism to invalidity. It is not sufficient to vindicate a real contrast. A completed essay must preserve that narrow scope and explain why a claim of logical necessity deserves refutation even without a newly validated empirical case.

Another objection: this is merely elementary mathematics. Reply: the mathematics is elementary and not original. The proposed contribution is its use to refute a specific necessity claim while accepting that claim's holism premise, and to expose the differing evidential requirements for positive and negative phenomenal contrasts. An independent critic should reject the proposal if that dialectical correction is too slight or already made elsewhere.
