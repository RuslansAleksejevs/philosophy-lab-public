# Proposal p1: When causation does not give personal time

This is a research proposal, not a manuscript. Requested independent check: does this constitute a worthwhile precise correction to Maslen (2024), rather than a repackaging of known restrictions on causation?

## Exact thesis

The inference from causal persistence to a determinate personal temporal order needs two independent qualifications, not merely a concession about duration. First, causal links between stages of the very same individual need not all point forward in personal time. Second, even when the relevant persistence links are selected and acyclic, the resulting history need not have a uniquely determined order. This second qualification matters for Maslen's proposed extension to multiply instantiated artworks: preserving artwork identity through authorized derivation can provide a branching genealogy, not the single personal time presupposed by the comparison with external order. The conclusion is a restriction on that proposed generalization, not an objection to time travel or to causal accounts qualified by an independently justified linear history.

## Target and main argument

Maslen, 'Personal Time and Transmigration Time Travel,' Philosophies 9(4), 98 (2024), section 4, PDF pp. 6–8, explores a general definition after the problem of differently embodied entities. Her The Thinker example allows a mold sent to ancient Greece to produce a sanctioned instance of the sculpture. She then moves from the ubiquity of causal persistence to a general ordering. Her explicit limitation is missing metric and thus an inability to classify forward travel on ordering alone. Her cited Wasserman formulation is importantly narrower, requiring identity-making causation; the proposal must retain that qualification fairly.

Counterexample 1 is familiar in its components, and expressly credited to Lewis/Van Cleve rather than presented as new fiction. A young person's ordinary development produces her older stage; the older traveler teaches the young stage a fact that helps shape that development. In the causal graph restricted simply to this person's stages, there is a directed cycle. A real-valued coordinate increasing on every edge would have to be smaller than itself. Personal identity of the endpoints is therefore not the missing edge restriction. This does not refute the qualified immanent-causation account. It shows why a reading of Maslen's unrestricted formulation is untenable.

Counterexample 2 addresses the natural repair. Suppose a sanctioned mold produces two instances of the same artwork, independently, with neither downstream from the other. Each genuinely continues the artwork by the criterion the artwork example accepts. Sending one derivation route backward in external time does not make the two branches causally comparable. The graph is acyclic, so linear extensions exist, but their order of incomparable branches is arbitrary. Two extensions can disagree about whether a pair's personal order reverses external order. Nothing in identity-preserving causation settles that disagreement. This is not the familiar missing metric: it concerns before/after itself. Nor is it personal fission: the example concerns the multiple-instantiation case invoked to motivate generality.

The elementary finite-graph result makes the diagnosis exact. A strict scalar assignment increasing along every selected edge exists iff the graph is acyclic. Its relative ordering is fixed iff every pair is comparable in the transitive closure. This statement concerns finite collections of selected event/stage tokens; no claim about embedding arbitrary continua in the reals. Without comparability, a linear extension is a convention, not a recovered personal history. With a cycle, collapsing strongly connected components destroys the internal successive stages whose history we sought. These are separate failures.

Repair: distinguish a genealogy of realizations from a linear career. A causal definition of personal time can be conditionally successful for a chosen career whose constitutive links have the required structure. It should not automatically inherit the universality of causal dependence as a requirement of persistence. For an artwork type, one can instead use path-relative personal times of realization lineages, or a partial causal order; either option changes the original scalar definition's domain or output. This is a defended limit, not a proposed new universal identity criterion.

## Closest comparisons and objections

Lewis (1976), pp. 148–149, already distinguishes causally grounded continuity from coincidence and admits information loops. Van Cleve, 'No Loop in Looper,' distinguishes causal loops from a person's circular life. These supply counterexample 1; originality does not rest on discovering that distinction.

Hall (2014), 'In Defense of the Compossibility of Presentism and Time Travel,' pp. 147–149 in accessed PDF, explicitly uses the relevant immanent relation. Wasserman is quoted both there and by Maslen; his book was not directly read. The edge-selection repair belongs to this literature. The proposed further increment is that edge selection still does not establish uniquely ordered time for the multiple-instance case Maslen includes.

Ehring (1987), 'Personal Identity and Time Travel,' pp. 427–433, uses time travel against familiar inferences from simultaneous stages to different persons. The proposal neither denies such simultaneous stages nor relies on counting bodies. It asks about directional comparability of genealogical branches, after the relevant identity is granted.

Strong objection: a work's stage at a time is the aggregate of all simultaneous instances, not an individual instance. Reply to develop: this can define a global artwork history indexed by external time, but it does not recover the provenance route by which a new instance arrives before its source. The theorist can choose that individuation, but must explain how it yields the intended verdict for the mold example. Another objection: time need not be linear. Agreed; adopting a partial order is a substantive revision of what the proposed definition supplies and makes some comparisons indeterminate. No impossibility of generalized time is claimed.

## Limits and possible rejection

Modest targeted result. Graph facts, causal loops, immanent causation and branching genealogies are familiar. Worth depends on the explicit counterexample to the published universal ordering claim and the connection between that article's artwork example and its ordering proposal. Search is bounded; no absolute priority claim. Only excerpts of some nearest literature accessed. A generous reading of Maslen as already presupposing individually linear careers may defeat the criticism; independent critic should test this directly. If the artwork passage uses a different ontology that blocks branching, no successful contribution has yet been established.
