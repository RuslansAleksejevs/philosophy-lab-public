## What the manuscript actually adds

The manuscript now distinguishes a complete personal chronology from a causal partial order sufficient for a particular backward-travel verdict. It makes the realization-event interpretation explicit; admits that it is not automatically a temporal-parts ontology; considers aggregate stages, individual casts, and route-relative orders; and correctly credits the inherited loop and immanent-causation ideas. The finite-order reasoning is sound under its stated assumptions. These were the major burdens at precheck, and they have been met sufficiently to sustain a modest contribution.

The best result is an underdetermination diagnosis inside the target's permissive artwork case. Accepting two authorized realizations of one work does not force a provenance comparison between them. Deriving personal time from that relation thus needs a decision about whether the output is partial, route-relative, or supplemented. This is more than a decorative example of branching: the target itself appeals to multiple instantiation to motivate generality. But the result remains a targeted clarification, not a discovery that causality can branch or that objects can have several temporal descriptions.

## Required revisions

### 1. State underdetermination rather than nonexistence

The title, “A Work Can Have a Genealogy without Having One Personal Time,” invites the stronger claim that the work lacks a single personal time. The body establishes that the selected provenance data do not determine a unique complete chronology. It explicitly permits scalar assignments and supplementary structure. Revise the title and any compressed summary so that a reader can see this difference immediately. No change to the defended thesis is needed.

Likewise, the sentence that identity preservation establishes neither acyclicity nor comparability “simply as a matter of logic” is safe only when identity preservation is not already defined using a temporal order. Add that qualification or keep the claim explicitly about the permissive provenance criterion in this example. Otherwise a theory whose identity-making relation includes temporal structure can appear to be ruled out merely by assertion.

### 2. Add the closer multiple-times comparison found in this review

The primary manuscript *Location and Perdurance*, https://philpapers.org/archive/EAGLAP.pdf, has closer material than the draft's branching-person comparison alone. In PDF pp. 32–33 it describes one object's different personal times relative to different sameness-constituting relations. In pp. 37–38 and 43–44, ordered fusions raise arbitrary-order and overgeneration problems when no natural ordering selects among alternatives. These are relevant antecedents for the draft's route/aggregate alternatives and its warning against imposed ranks.

The accessed argument concerns composition, sortal-relative causal relations and multiply located objects; it does not in these passages supply the specific derivation-tree objection for authorized instances of one artwork. The manuscript can retain that difference. It should briefly acknowledge that plurality of personal times and arbitrary ordering of parts are established responses/problems, then distinguish its particular point: incomparability can remain within the single accepted artwork-provenance relation. Do not present route-relative or multiple personal times themselves as new solutions. Bibliographic author/date metadata should be verified before adding a formal entry; this review has preserved the primary text without guessing them.

This new evidence narrows the comparative claim but does not warrant SET_ASIDE. The difference is explicit enough to defend without an exhaustive priority proof.

### 3. Keep the target conditional throughout

The draft fairly concedes that a partial reading of Maslen yields clarification rather than refutation. That concession should govern the presentation from beginning to end. The retrieved section 4 supplies the artwork case and a general ordering claim, but does not explicitly demand an injective ranking of every realization event. Therefore do not say that Maslen demonstrably makes the extra inference. The essay shows what the proposed generality does not guarantee, not that the main account of transmigration fails.

The aggregate response is reasonable and appropriately non-exhaustive. It exposes a choice of bearer/output; it does not prove every possible aggregate theory requires independent noncausal structure. Preserve that limitation.
