## What passes
The defensible increment is a constructive nonidentifiability result: two smooth two-variable systems have equal initial-state-conditioned limiting outcomes for EVERY nonempty persistent coordinate clamp, equal baseline and equal fixed-point/qualitative stability catalogues, but a shared joint-clamp release reaches opposite stable equilibria. This is stronger than a new illustration of different transient speeds. It removes finite target coverage, hidden state, and different observed endpoints as explanations of failure. It gives a precise limit on extending a validated experimental menu.

The mathematical argument appears sound. Under x clamps only the rate of y decay changes; under y clamps the x equation is identical. The slow-k zero-crossing bound and fast-k integrated forcing bound establish different basins. The comparison argument after positive crossing must be spelled out in the manuscript. At critical clamped cubic roots, qualitative stability should include nonhyperbolic/one-sided cases or simply say the same classifications, not assert all equilibria are strictly stable/unstable.

## Closest overlap and inherited material
[Weinberger, Intervening and Letting Go](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10670-021-00463-0), especially section 5, already treats clamping and release as distinct actions and explains symmetric adequacy limitations of equilibrium models. Thus neither the headline distinction nor action-relative adequacy is new. The accessed argument compares causal orderings for a piston; it does not establish equality of an exhaustive family of clamp outcome maps for two different smooth systems with differing released attractors. The proposal fairly describes its work as an extension.

[Blom, Bongers and Mooij, Beyond Structural Causal Models](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v115/blom20a/blom20a.pdf), definitions 4 and theorem 2, already represents initial-state-sensitive equilibrium outcomes, including the empty intervention. Their construction uses the full map rather than deriving its empty-intervention part from all nonempty parts. The proposed example supplies a concrete failure of that derivation within smooth ODEs. It is not a failure of CCM expressiveness. Their lemma about sequential addition of disjoint interventions does not claim that withdrawing interventions is equivalent.

[Dash, Restructuring Dynamic Causal Systems in Equilibrium](https://proceedings.mlr.press/r5/dash05a/dash05a.pdf), theorem 2 and conclusion, already warns that manipulation and equilibration need not commute and that temporal information can be needed. The proposed experimental equivalence theorem concerns two dynamics and a richer intervention dataset, not a refutation of Dash's warning.

[Ryan/Dablander equilibrium causal models article](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/00273171.2025.2522733) distinguishes pulse and press interventions; publisher search excerpts discuss stable linear systems, while the manuscript search excerpt explicitly acknowledges attractor changes in multistable systems. Do not claim discovery of lasting pulse effects. Full publisher access failed (403).

A [public mathematical note on intervention-qualified mechanism equivalence](https://www.cordana.dev/math/interventional-mechanism-equivalence/) gives matched step responses separated by further intervention tests. Search excerpts concern finite panels and transient output signatures, not the exhaustive steady-state-clamp/final-attractor theorem here. Direct page opening failed; its dates and wider claims are not independently established.

## Boundaries for drafting
State 'nonempty persistent coordinate clamps with limiting-pair readout' wherever describing completeness. Time traces or relaxation eigenvalues identify k; unrestricted unclamped initial-state experiments directly reveal the missing map. Baseline agreement is one specified state, not arbitrary observational histories. Joint clamping prepares the exact release state; this is not an empirical claim that ideal clamps are realizable.

Keep the philosophical payoff bounded: target/value completeness does not entail protocol completeness. Do not claim that existing theorists assume that entailment or that mathematical representation cannot accommodate release. The example is a modest but nontrivial strengthening of known cautions, not a new general causal theory.
