# Question selection — 2026-09-22

Can exhaustive success in persistent intervention experiments warrant a prediction about removing those interventions?

Three candidates were explored before drafting:

1. **Robust evidence can weaken rather than strengthen confirmation.** Rejected as already directly treated by the primary work *Robustness and Independent Evidence* (repository abstract read), with its Bayesian counterexamples. A new illustration would not establish an increment.
2. **Null results after memory interventions cannot distinguish erasure from inaccessible memory.** Rejected as already a central issue in *What does it take to demonstrate memory erasure?* (2008). Only bounded search/landing-page retrieval was undertaken; not advanced as an original proposal.
3. **Intervention equivalence need not survive release.** Selected in a restricted form, after Weinberger and Blom/Bongers/Mooij made a broad 'equilibrium forgets history' claim untenable as a novelty claim. The proposed increment is an explicit pair of smooth systems agreeing under every nonempty persistent clamp, for all clamp values and initial states, and at a shared observational baseline; a joint-clamp-then-release experiment selects different final stable equilibria. Exact scope excludes arbitrary unclamped initial-state experiments, which already contain release information.

The selected question concerns evidential completeness of an experimental menu, not whether existing dynamical causal formalisms can represent the example. They can. A familiar result about unidentifiable rates is used to prove a stronger, sharply restricted experimental indistinguishability result whose failure appears even in the final equilibrium, rather than only in transient timing.

All alternatives and the selected construction are AI-originated. No manuscript has been written. Independent precheck may reject the increment as too close to existing work.
