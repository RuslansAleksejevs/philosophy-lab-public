# Process — essay-08-author, authoring stage

## 1. What this stage was

This is the initial authoring stage: topic selection, source research, and a complete first
manuscript, produced in one continuous working session by a single worker. No topic, field or
thesis was supplied by the brief; NEUTRAL_BRIEF.md asked for an original and well-defended
philosophical contribution with the question, argument and conclusion chosen by me. Nothing
substantive came from the dispatcher, and I read no other essay, campaign or memory.

Outputs of this stage, in the order they were produced and returned:

1. `draft-001.md` — the complete manuscript, *Inquiry Before the Question*.
2. `sources.md` — bibliographic details, retrieval URLs, saved extraction paths, honest coverage
   levels, and an accuracy register of things a reviewer should check.
3. `process.md` — this file.

The manuscript is finished, not a sketch: it has no placeholders and every section listed in its
own structure is written out. It runs to roughly 5,300 words excluding the bibliography, within
the 6,000-word ceiling, with the notes and abstract counted in.

## 2. How the topic was chosen

I generated a wide field of candidate topics before committing, deliberately across subfields
rather than within one. The main candidates, and why each was set aside:

- **Moral indeterminacy and practical reason** — what to do when it is indeterminate whether an
  act is permissible, with a thesis that the deliberative role of obligation forces a collapse
  into permission at the time of action. Set aside because the obvious counterexample (two
  options each indeterminately required, at least one of which must be done) looked like it would
  eat the whole word budget.
- **A path-dependence reply to moral sorites arguments** — the claim that moral tolerance
  principles fail because permissibility attaches to trajectories rather than act-tokens. Set
  aside as too close to existing work on the self-torturer for the originality I wanted.
- **Statistical evidence and the proof paradox**; **normative powers and promising**; **the
  ethics of attention**; **decision-theoretic deliberation and self-prediction**; **epistemic
  partiality in friendship**; **actualism/possibilism about conditional obligation**. Each was
  either crowded enough that a genuinely new move looked unlikely at this length, or turned on
  primary literature I judged unlikely to be openly accessible.

I chose the norms of inquiry because the literature is young enough that a structural objection
to its framework is available, its primary sources are largely on authors' own websites, and I
had a specific thesis rather than a topic label.

## 3. The thesis, and how it changed

The first formulation was a straight denial that inquiry is question-directed. I abandoned that
formulation for two reasons, both visible in the final draft's architecture.

First, it is a straw man. Friedman explicitly declines to commit to the universal claim in at
least three places, which I found on reading her papers rather than knowing in advance. So the
argument was retargeted: I split Erotetic Primacy into a constitutive component (EP-i), which
Friedman hedges, and a normative-indexing component (EP-ii), which every proposed zetetic norm
relies on and which her hedge does not protect. The attack is on the second. This is the single
biggest change the draft underwent, and it made the paper both fairer and stronger.

Second, a bare denial has no positive content. The aporetic model was built to supply it.

A discarded version of the positive model defined an aporia as a *felt* cognitive friction. I
replaced this with a structural definition — a set of the agent's commitments that she cannot as
she stands jointly discharge — because the psychological version could not support the zetetic
negligence payoff (§6.2), which needs aporiai that exist unnoticed, and because it would have
left the view indistinguishable from Peirce's irritation of doubt.

An earlier three-way taxonomy (conflict / gap / strain) was presented as three *kinds* of aporia.
I rewrote it as three *sources of undischargeability* under a single definition, because as three
kinds it looked like a list, and as three sources it is a consequence.

## 4. Arguments tried and dropped

- **A grain argument running on coarseness.** My first version of §3.1 said that coarse questions
  give weak guidance because they induce coarse partitions. On checking this against the standard
  semantics of questions I noticed it is backwards: *what is going on here?* has maximally
  specific complete answers, so it induces a very fine partition. The argument was rebuilt as the
  dilemma now in the draft — either the agent does not grasp the answer-set, or the answer-set is
  unsurveyable and the question-indexed norms go idle because nothing counts as a necessary means
  and no complete answer is ever known. The corrected version is better than the original, and I
  note the error here because the original reasoning was wrong, not merely inelegant.
- **A stronger claim about the epistemic/zetetic tension.** I drafted §6.4 as a dissolution of
  Friedman's puzzle, then weakened it to a partial deflation. Friedman's case is explicitly built
  on attention scarcity and explicitly not a clutter-avoidance argument, so any reply that relies
  on the triviality of the ignored information fails against her own statement of the case. The
  surviving reply — that zetetic norms directly govern the deployment of capacities rather than
  doxastic outputs, so that the permission and the prohibition attach to different acts — is
  offered as a way of removing the force of her revisionary conclusion, not as a refutation, and
  the draft says so and states the reply available to her.
- **A historical example from the physics of blackbody radiation.** Cut. I had not read any
  history-of-science source, and the example would have carried specific factual commitments I
  could not support. It was replaced by three cases I could construct in full (Ana, the
  intermittent fault, the uneasy reader) plus one general and explicitly hedged remark about
  anomalies in science. `sources.md` §E flags even that remark as unsupported.
- **A fourth payoff on group inquiry and division of epistemic labour.** Drafted mentally,
  never written, cut for length. It is named in the conclusion's open questions instead.

## 5. Source work

Retrieval was the main practical constraint. Publisher and aggregator PDFs (PhilArchive,
PhilPapers) returned HTTP 403 four times; author websites and public-domain archives worked. One
source I wanted for the awareness-growth argument, Steele and Stefánsson's *Mind* paper, was
unobtainable, and I used Roussos instead, who states the point I need in his introduction. One
survey of the zetetic turn (Falbo) was unobtainable, which means my picture of that literature
rests on Friedman and Thorstad alone; that is a real limitation and is recorded in `sources.md`.
One fetch retrieved the wrong text entirely (a Gutenberg guess that turned out to be a book of
nineteenth-century biblical-revision addresses) and was discarded.

Only one source was read in full: Friedman's *The Epistemic and the Zetetic*. Everything else was
read in named excerpts, and `sources.md` states the line ranges. I confined my attributions to
material I actually read: nothing in the draft characterises Thorstad's three arguments or his
Gibbardian alternative, for instance, because I read only his introduction and part of §2.

Two classical texts were retrieved in editions without Bekker or Stephanus numbers, so the
locators in the draft are supplied by convention and unverified; and there is an unresolved
discrepancy between the page number Woodard gives for Friedman's ignorance norm and the
pagination I have for the article I believe she is citing. Both are flagged rather than quietly
smoothed over, because smoothing them over would be a fabricated source access.

## 6. Remaining uncertainties

Philosophical, in rough order of how much they worry me:

1. **Individuating aporiai.** The model indexes norms to aporiai, so it owes conditions under
   which two aporiai are the same and how they are counted. I do not have them. The draft admits
   this in §7 under the vagueness objection. A critic could reasonably press that the problem I
   raise for questions (which refinement?) recurs for aporiai (which subset of commitments?). My
   provisional answer is that the recurrence is not symmetrical, because an agent has standing
   access to her own commitments in a way she does not have to an answer-set she has not yet
   conceived — but that answer is asserted in the draft, not argued for.
2. **The scope of §6.4.** I am least confident here, and the draft's hedging reflects that rather
   than covering it up.
3. **Whether the Priority Rule is circular.** (PR) ranks aporiai by how many other commitments
   they block. If "blocks" is itself cashed in terms of undischargeability, the rule may be doing
   less work than it appears to. I think it is not circular, because blocking is a matter of
   counting downstream commitments rather than of identifying the upstream defect, but I have not
   checked this carefully.
4. **Whether aporetic norms are epistemic.** The draft explicitly declines to take a stand on
   Thorstad's nihilism, and only observes that the model gives the anti-nihilist a more
   plausibly epistemic ground than appeals to the inquirer's interests. That observation is a
   promissory note and is labelled as one.
5. **Predecessors.** Dewey, Collingwood, Laudan and Hookway are the obvious threats to the
   novelty claim, and I read none of them at this stage. The draft's note 2 says so and confines
   the novelty claim to the three specific items in §5.3 (structural rather than psychological
   definition; the individuation-and-indexing claim about zetetic norms; the negative criterion
   and negligence payoffs). If a critic shows any of those three is anticipated, the claim should
   be narrowed accordingly rather than defended.

## 8. Handover to the critic

The most useful places to push, in my estimation:

- §3.1: is the dilemma exhaustive? Is there a third grain at which the question both is possessed
  and does guide?
- §5.1: does the single definition really unify gaps with conflicts, or is "cannot discharge *as
  she stands*" doing hidden work that makes the unification trivial?
- §6.1: does the stopping rule actually stop? An anxious agent can hold herself to a standard no
  amount of checking meets, and the model may then license exactly the behaviour it was meant to
  criticise.
- §6.3: is the negative criterion too weak to distinguish genuine repair from the appearance of
  repair? The draft concedes fallibility; a critic may think the concession is fatal.
- The accuracy register in `sources.md` §E, all seven items.

All substantive philosophy in this stage originated here, in Claude, from the sources listed. No
human supplied ideas, wording, topic or thesis, and no configuration, timing or cost figures are
asserted anywhere in these files; those are recorded mechanically elsewhere.
