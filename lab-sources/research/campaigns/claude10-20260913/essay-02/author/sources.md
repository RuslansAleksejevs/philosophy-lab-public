## 5. Adams, Robert Merrihew. “Middle Knowledge and the Problem of Evil.”

- Publication: *American Philosophical Quarterly* 14, no. 2 (April 1977): 109–17.
- URL: https://andrewmbailey.com/rma/1977b-Adams-Middle_knowledge_and_the_problem_of_evil.pdf
- Saved: `tasks/essay-02-author/sources/bf5c3ec316e289239a2c-25afe93d6b9e.txt`
  plus `.original.pdf`/`.json`.
- Coverage: **partial / degraded.** The PDF is a scan of the journal with an
  embedded OCR layer. `pdftotext -layout` reproduces the two-column page as
  interleaved fragments with irregular spacing and some substituted characters
  (e.g. “?” for hyphens at line breaks). I read pp. 109–112 by reflowing the
  fragments; I did not attempt pp. 113–17. No OCR tool was available to improve
  the extraction.
- Exact locations used (p. 111, section I, the Keilah discussion):
  - “But the basis thus offered for the truth of (1) and (2) is inadequate
    because it is not precisely necessitating.”
  - “A free agent may act out of character, or change his intentions, or fail to
    act on them.”
  - The substitution of probabilistic conditionals (5) and (6) — “If David stayed
    in Keilah, Saul would probably besiege the city” — which Adams says are
    “enough for David to act on” but not enough for middle knowledge.
- Reliability flag: because of the reflow, I treat these as accurate to the
  wording but would want a clean copy before relying on them in print. The
  essay's argument does not depend on the exact phrasing, only on the claim that
  psychological grounds underwrite probabilistic rather than definite
  counterfactuals.

## 6. Works cited without full-text access (no quotation taken from any of these)

- **Bradford, Gwen. *Achievement* (Oxford: Oxford University Press, 2015).**
  Coverage: **unavailable.** Paywalled at the publisher. I saw only publisher and
  search-result descriptions of the thesis (achievements are valuable in virtue

  doi:10.1111/j.1467-8519.2010.01854.x). Cited only as the locus of the “freedom
  to fall” objection to moral enhancement; nothing is quoted and no specific
  argument is reconstructed.
