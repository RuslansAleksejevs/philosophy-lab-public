# Process — essay-07-critic, critique of `draft-001.md`

## 1. What this stage was

This was the independent criticism stage. My input was the sealed output of `essay-07-author`: the manuscript `draft-001.md`, its `sources.md`, and its `process.md`. My job was to assess that one essay and its source record on argument, originality, significance, clarity and source accuracy, verifying primary sources myself wherever I could and disclosing where I could not.

I did not write or sketch a revision, and I did not rank the essay or assign it a verdict of the kind a competition judge would. Where I say what the essay "should do," I mean it as a reason plus a direction — the claim that needs defending, the case that needs running — not as drafted replacement prose.

Outputs: `critique.md`, `sources.md`, `process.md`. No other essays, campaigns, shared research or memory were read; nothing outside the assigned inputs, the sealed dependency folder, and sources I retrieved myself during this stage.

## 2. Method, and the order I worked in

I deliberately did not read the author's `process.md` self-assessment before forming my own view of the manuscript's weak points. The sequence was: task and brief → the dependency `sources.md` (which arrived first, because my initial attempts at the manuscript used the wrong filename) → `process.md` → the manuscript. That ordering was accidental rather than chosen, and it is a mild contamination: I knew the author's own list of weaknesses before I read §§4–6 of the essay. I have tried to compensate by checking each of their self-identified weaknesses against the text rather than accepting the self-report, and by noting in the critique where I think the self-assessment is *too* generous to the essay (the Agenda Thesis / D2 gap in critique §3 is not on the author's list, and I think it is worse than anything that is).

The substantive work then split in two.

**Source verification.** I re-retrieved the two papers the author read in full and read them completely myself, rather than spot-checking. This was the right call: the payoff was not catching misquotation — there is none — but noticing two things in Weltman that the essay had read past (the "mere addition account" report of Gläser, and the §5.1 concession about responsibility with its bracketing footnote). Both became central criticisms. I also re-read Reid's chapter in the same scan the author used, and verified all four quotations word by word.

**Literature probing.** I then went looking for what a specialist referee would notice. The productive move was searching on the *structure* of the essay's taxonomic claim rather than on requests: "positive second-order reasons," "reasons to act for a reason." That surfaced Sekine 2026, which the author did not have and which materially changes what can be claimed in §5. Sekine's footnotes then did double duty as a bibliography of the field, supplying Whiting, Adams, Keeling, Darwall 2010, Raz's postscript, and the correct publication details for Piller.

## 3. Lines of criticism I developed and then dropped

Recording these, because several looked promising for a while and were wrong.

- **"The dilemma equivocates between attending and replying."** My first reaction to §4 was that Permitted Inattention concerns deliberative attention while D2's costly silence concerns communication, so the contradiction is merely apparent. I dropped this as a criticism of §4 because the essay's own Agenda Thesis clause (i) is genuinely about considering, so the essay is not simply equivocating. But the observation survived in transposed form: it became critique §3, where the same gap between considering and answering shows that the *positive* thesis fails to entail D2. I think that is the better home for it, and it is a stronger objection there, because it catches the essay's own machinery rather than its polemic.

- **"Permitted Inattention is false — we are always criticisable for inattention, merely excused."** This is the objection the author anticipates in their `process.md` §6.1. I tried to run it and could not make it bite. The essay's reply (that this reading makes deliberation impossible) seems to me correct, and the principle as stated is well chosen. I say nothing against it in the critique. This is one place where I think the author's self-assessment overstates their exposure.

- **"Idleness is ill-defined for epsilon reasons under uncertainty."** I drafted a technical objection: since the deliberator does not know in advance whether the case is a tie, "reasonably takes to be idle" needs a probabilistic gloss, and any such gloss reintroduces expected-value reasoning that the epsilon theorist can exploit. I dropped it because the essay's third reply in §4 already concedes the tie-breaking point and rests its case elsewhere, and because the objection would have been a technical skirmish on ground the essay has already ceded. It also risked exactly the kind of apparatus-for-its-own-sake the author rightly rejected in their own §4.

- **A formal reconstruction of the Agenda Thesis.** I sketched a rendering with an agenda set, an answerability relation, and defeat conditions, intending to show formally that the account underdetermines D2 and has no conflict-resolution rule. I abandoned it. The points can be made in two paragraphs of English (critique §§3–4), and formalising them would have implied the account is further developed than it is — you cannot usefully axiomatise a thesis whose defeat conditions are the thing you are complaining are missing.

- **Pressing the Reid material as anachronistic.** I considered arguing that the essay leans on Reid for more than a philosopher of mind can supply. I dropped it: the essay is scrupulous about this ("Reid's point was about the philosophy of mind. Mine is its normative analogue"), and the author's `process.md` §7 says explicitly that Reid is used as a diagnosis, not an authority. The use is legitimate. I record instead the one place where Reid could be used *more* (the second-person-of-verbs passage).

- **Attacking the drowning case as question-begging.** My first draft said D5 begs the question by holding the addressee's knowledge fixed. That is wrong — holding information fixed is exactly the right control against a Weight Model, which claims the request adds something *beyond* information. I rewrote the objection as a case-selection complaint (the case is chosen where any increment is swamped) plus three counter-cases. That is the version in critique §6, and I think it is fair where the first version was not.

## 4. Where I changed my mind in the author's favour

- I began suspecting the essay had misclassified Enoch, since Enoch's view is a triggering account and triggered reasons might be thought wholly inherited from the background conditional. Weltman's §2 settles it: what the trigger renders true is a conditional "if X asks Y to Z, Y has a reason to Z," so the output is a first-order reason to Z. Classing Enoch under the Weight Model is defensible, and I say so. The author's worry in their `process.md` §5 can be partly retired.
- I expected to find quotation errors, given how much of the essay is quoted at second hand. I found none. Every checkable quotation is exact and correctly located. That deserves saying in a review, and I say it.

## 5. What I could not establish

- **Enoch and Lewis remain unread by me.** Every host returned 403. My additional evidence is Weltman's §2 rather than the originals, which is a third report agreeing with two others — better than the author had, still not reading them. Anyone with library access should check critique §2 (whether Lewis really explains discretion by non-obligatoriness rather than by low weight) and critique §8.1 first.
- **Gläser.** My most important criticism concerns a paper I could not read. I have quoted two independent published reports of his view, and I have been careful to frame the criticism as "the nearest rival is unengaged" rather than "Gläser already holds this thesis." If Gläser's obligation-based account turns out to be quite different in content, the criticism weakens from "originality risk" to "engagement gap" — but it does not disappear, because the essay's target family already has his name for it.
- **Whether anyone has theorised a reason to place something on a deliberative agenda.** I established that positive second-order reasons are a live and named category. I did not establish that the essay's specific idea — a reason bearing on deliberative uptake, created by address — has been proposed before. My searches returned nothing on point, but absence of search results is weak evidence, which is the same caution the author applies to their own originality claim and which I extend to my criticism of it.
- **Raz's text.** I could not open *Practical Reason and Norms*, so the citation chain the author flagged as most in need of checking (Piller's quotation of p. 39) is still unchecked at the source. What I could add is that the 1990 edition contains a postscript rethinking exclusionary reasons at p. 178 — which raises the stakes on the unchecked claim rather than resolving it.
- **Darwall.** I could not read him and so make no claim about what he argues. My point is only that an essay whose positive thesis is "address makes you answerable" cannot leave the *Ethics* 120:2 Darwall–Raz exchange uncited. That point does not depend on reading it.

## 6. Remaining disagreements with the essay

Stated as disagreements rather than as corrections, since some are matters on which reasonable people will differ:

- I think T1 as stated is probably false, and that the essay's best version is the weaker one: asking adds no *independent* weight, but the case-selection in D5 does not establish the universal claim, and the withdrawal and confidentiality cases look like genuine counterexamples. The author may well have replies; the essay does not contain them.
- I think T3 is the weakest of the three theses and the easiest to repair. The repair I suggest — separating the requirement to consider from the requirement to act only for an avowable reason, and conceding that only the second is Raz's missing species — costs the essay its neatest formulation. I expect the author to resist. I would rather they resisted in print, with an argument, than kept the current framing.
- I think the Eavesdrop Test should be replaced rather than patched, and that Raz's "I am not asking" case (already in the essay's own footnote 10) is the better instrument. I also think it may not deliver the verdict the author wants, which is a reason to run it, not to avoid it.
- I am *more* impressed than the author seems to be by §6.4, the argument from what we build defences against. Their `process.md` treats it as an objection-reply; I think it is the strongest independent evidence in the paper and should be promoted.

## 7. On the author's self-documentation

One discrepancy worth recording as a process matter rather than a philosophical one: the author's `sources.md` (item 7) and `process.md` both state that the essay mentions Gläser as holding that requests obligate rather than add reasons. The manuscript contains no such sentence; Gläser appears once, in a list of theorists said to hold that requests contribute to the first-order balance — which is the opposite of the view the source record attributes to him. The record and the manuscript have drifted. I flag it in `critique.md` §8.1 because provenance accuracy is part of what is being assessed, and because the drift points at the substantive gap.
