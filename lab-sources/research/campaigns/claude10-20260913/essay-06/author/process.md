This file describes what I did at this stage, what I tried and set aside and why, what

## 1. What this stage was

The task assigned no topic, field or thesis. It required one original philosophical
essay taken from source research through to a finished initial manuscript, at most 6,000
words excluding bibliography, for a separate fresh Claude critic to read next.

I read the three assigned inputs (`task.md`, `NEUTRAL_BRIEF.md`, `RULES.md`), chose a
topic, ran eight web searches and eleven fetch attempts, read the separately saved
extractions for the retrievals that succeeded, and wrote the manuscript. Order of work:
topic selection → priority checks → source retrieval and reading → case construction →
argument → drafting → a citation audit that produced the correction list in `sources.md`.

## 2. Topic selection, and the candidates dropped

I generated a wide candidate list first and pruned it for two reasons: excessive existing
coverage, or no clear route to a defensible original claim rather than merely original
phrasing. Dropped, with reasons:

- **Transformative experience and anti-paternalism.** If L. A. Paul is right that one
  cannot rationally evaluate transformative options ex ante, then the epistemic argument
  against paternalism weakens exactly where autonomy seems to matter most. A real
  tension, but the connection is already drawn in the literature.
- **A preface paradox for moral belief** (I am confident some of my moral beliefs are
  badly wrong but cannot say which). Dropped: hard to get action-guidance out of, and
  adjacent to a large moral-uncertainty literature.
- **Standing to blame; aesthetic testimony; moral encroachment; trust versus reliance;
  Frankfurt cases; self-effacing theories; "deliberation crowds out prediction";
  Razian exclusionary reasons.** All dropped as crowded relative to what I could add.

I selected the eventual topic because it had a constructible case, a mechanism I could
state precisely, canonical texts to engage, and a practical payoff.

## 3. Framings tried and abandoned within the chosen topic

This is where most of the work went. The final essay is the fourth framing.

**(a) "Consent laundering" via a manufactured evaluative standpoint.** My first idea was
that A wrongs B by engineering the very will whose authorisation A then invokes: B's
later consents are valid but issue from an evaluative standpoint that is A's artifact.
**Abandoned.** It collapses into the adaptive-preference and manipulation literatures,
and it requires a criterion separating benign from vicious value-shaping — a known
quagmire, since all of us have our values shaped by others. Replacing the *will* with
the *option set* as the thing A captures solved both problems at once: B's preferences,
reasoning and character can be stipulated untouched, which makes the case far harder to
dismiss and lets me say cleanly that this is not manipulation.

**(b) "Consent is not closed under conjunction", on the model of the preface paradox.**
A tempting headline, and I drafted the essay under it. **Abandoned as the organising
frame.** The preface analogy implies accumulation of small risks or small errors, which
is not the mechanism here; the mechanism is endogenous reservation values, and the
sequence can be short. The closure point survives, demoted, as Observation 1 in §4.

**(c) A sorites-shaped case with many small steps.** My first version of the case had a
long series of tiny concessions. **Abandoned deliberately.** It invited two cheap
dismissals — that this is a variant of Quinn's self-torturer, and that it is a vagueness
problem needing a threshold. I rebuilt the case with four large, discrete, perceptible
episodes precisely to block both, and said so in §4 and §8c. This was the single most
useful revision.

**(d) An intention-based diagnosis:** the wrong lies in A's *designing* the sequence.
**Abandoned.** It makes the account hostage to the Thomson/Scanlon dispute about whether
intentions bear on permissibility, and, worse, it excludes the cases I most want to
capture: platform lock-in and single-sponsor visa regimes, where nobody designs anything.
Replaced by a criterion stated in terms of what a course of dealing predictably does and
what a party obtains by it, which yields the deliberately awkward result that there can
be blameless ratcheting (§8d).

**(e) A normative power to authorise trajectories.** I drafted the remedy as a power in
B: alongside step-consent, a power to consent to a *path*, with a corresponding duty in A
to disclose the path. **Downgraded.** The argument shows the fix cannot be more consent
from B, because what B would be authorising at step (1) is A's unexercised discretion,
which is not yet a fact. The duty is therefore to *bind*, not to *inform*. That became
the Commitment Condition (§7), which I now regard as the most distinctive claim in the
essay; the trajectory-consent idea survives only as the thing CC replaces.

**(f) An exploitation/unconscionability framing.** **Abandoned** because exploitation
theory indexes the wrong to unfair terms in a transaction, and my case is built so that
every transaction is competitively priced at the time it is made. This became the §3
acquittal and the §8g reply about compensation.

**(g) A fuller formal model.** I considered an explicit bargaining game with endogenous
reservation values and a proof that A's captured share rises monotonically. **Cut.** The
observation needed is simple, and within the word limit the apparatus would have crowded
out the normative argument. §4 keeps only enough notation to make the structure visible.

**(h) A Kantian version of the whole essay.** I read Pallikkathayil's paper intending to
build the argument as a challenge to the possible-consent reading of the Formula of
Humanity. **Abandoned:** it adds a heavy interpretive burden for no argumentative gain,
and the essay's core claim is about justification, not about the derivation of duties.
One paragraph survives in §6, using Korsgaard's criterion as a foil — actual consent
procured by reshaping what the agent can possibly refuse ought to worry the Kantian, and
the criterion as stated does not catch it.

**(i) Mill's Humboldt passage.** I read and set aside Mill's discussion of engagements
involving personal relations and the liberty of retractation (Gutenberg edn pp. 196–97).
It points toward a different thesis, about the revocability of long-term commitments,
and would have diluted §6.

**(j) A second worked case.** I drafted an intimate-relationship case — accommodations
that are each generous and each reduce the accommodating partner's independent standing,
adding up to something recognisable as coercive control with no coercive act. **Cut to a
clause** in §8d/§9, both for length and because the employment case lets me stipulate
away confounders (love, self-deception, fear) that the intimate case cannot.

## 4. What the finished essay claims

Four theses: Historicity of consent; Comparative Triviality; Inverse Reliance; and the
Commitment Condition. The load-bearing move is the shift from *will-capture* to
*baseline capture*, which lets me stipulate a fully rational, informed, unchanged,
unpressured consenter and still generate the failure. The conclusion is a burden shift
rather than a verdict: baseline capture strips A of the consent defence and requires A
to justify on terms benchmarked to a baseline it did not author.

The two places where the essay stakes most on literature engagement are Zimmerman's
interference-sensitive baseline (my case satisfies no interference condition — A only
gives) and Mill's slavery passage (I argue Mill's own evidential premise yields a
gradient, not a threshold, and is exploitable by anyone who can raise the cost of
leaving).

Length: estimated at roughly 5,300 words excluding the bibliography. No shell or word
count tool was available to me, so this is an estimate from section-by-section counting,
not a machine measurement. It is comfortably inside the 6,000-word cap on any plausible
counting convention, but the critic should not treat the figure as measured.

## 5. Remaining uncertainties and open risks

Ordered by how much they worry me.

1. **Priority risk I could not close.** Arto Laitinen's "Normative Powers, Agency, and
   Time" is the closest-sounding published work I identified, and PhilArchive returned
   403 on both the archive URL and a second attempt. I cannot rule out overlap with my
   §7 principles. Recorded in `sources.md` §D.
2. **The mechanism is not new even if the normative claim is.** I should say plainly that
   the underlying structure — specific investment, lock-in, ex post expropriation — is
   recognisably the hold-up problem from the economics of relational contracting. I did
   not research that literature and did not cite it, which is a gap. My originality claim
   is about what this does to *consent's justificatory force*, not about the mechanism.
   A critic should press here, and a reviser should probably acknowledge the economic
   antecedent explicitly rather than leave it implicit.
3. **Is the burden shift enough?** §5 concludes that A loses the consent defence, not that
   A acts wrongly. That is deliberately modest, but it may be too modest to sustain the
   subtitle's talk of wrongs without wrongful parts. There is a real question whether the
   essay's headline and its conclusion are pitched at the same strength.
4. **Validity without justificatory force.** I insist Mara's consent is valid but idle.
   A critic could reasonably argue that this is unstable — that a consent which cannot
   justify is simply invalid, and that I have relabelled rather than solved. I think the
   distinction is defensible (the power really is exercised; no battery occurs) but I did
   not defend it at length.
5. **Does the conjunctive criterion in §7 draw the line correctly?** Condition (ii) —
   A uses the lowered reservation value to obtain better terms — may catch cases it
   should not. A firm that has specialised a worker and then cuts wages because it is
   failing satisfies the letter of (ii). I do not have a settled answer.
6. **The counterfactual baseline.** The burden-shifted justification appeals to what B's
   alternatives would have been absent A's course of dealing. I claim in §9 that this is
   an implementation problem, citing the analogy to reliance damages, but I have not
   shown that.
7. **Whether ratcheting generalises to other normative powers.** §9 conjectures that
   promising, authorising and forgiving are similarly vulnerable. That is a conjecture,
   flagged as such, and unargued.
8. **Citation defects I found in my own audit.** Listed in full in `sources.md` §F. The
   two that matter: a quotation attributed to Allen Wood in §3 that is in fact a search
   summary's paraphrase and must be de-quoted, and a Cohen page reference in §8f that
   should be p. 10 rather than p. 9. Three SEP section numbers and several bibliographic
   entries are unverified. I chose to leave the draft as written and report the defects
   rather than silently patch a file already saved, since preserving versions is required
   and corrections belong to the revision stage.
