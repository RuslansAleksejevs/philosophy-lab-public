# Process — essay-06-critic, critique stage

This file records what I did at this stage, the approaches I tried and set aside and why,
what remains unresolved, and the version lineage. It records visible work products only.
No configuration, timing, cost or model metadata appears here; those are collected
mechanically elsewhere. I did not simulate the revision stage or write any part of a revised
essay.

---

## 1. What this stage was

I was assigned one dependency essay — `draft-001.md` from essay-06-author — together with its
`sources.md` and `process.md`, and asked to assess it independently: strongest argument,
defended premises, serious objections, originality uncertainty, significance, clarity, and
source accuracy, verifying primary sources myself where accessible and disclosing gaps.

Order of work:

1. Read the three assigned inputs (`task.md`, `NEUTRAL_BRIEF.md`, `RULES.md`).
2. Read the dependency's `sources.md` and `process.md`, then the complete manuscript.
3. Formed a first-pass philosophical assessment from the manuscript alone, before opening any
   source, so that the source audit would not drive the philosophical verdict.
4. Ran eight independent retrievals and seven web searches; read the separately saved
   extractions, not the fetch summaries.
5. Revised the assessment in light of what the sources said — which changed it substantially
   in two places (see §3).
6. Wrote `critique.md`, then `sources.md` as an audit of my own retrievals, then this file.

One procedural note. My first attempt to open the manuscript used the filename `essay.md` and
was refused; the dependency's manuscript is `draft-001.md`. I did not retry the refused path
and did not treat the refusal as a blocking failure.

## 2. How I chose what to verify

The author's `sources.md` already listed several self-identified defects. I decided early not
to spend the stage confirming the author's own confessions, since that adds little, but to
allocate effort by a different rule: **check the things the essay's originality claim depends
on**, and check the author's confessions only where confirming or overturning one changes the
revision advice.

That produced this priority order:

1. The four *SEP* entries the essay uses to establish that "the toolkit acquits" — because if
   any of those entries contains the resource the essay says is missing, §3 fails and with it
   the novelty claim. This is where the two most consequential findings came from.
2. The *SEP* "Exploitation" entry, which the essay does **not** cite. I judged it a serious
   omission on the face of the manuscript and retrieved it on that suspicion. It proved to be
   the single most productive retrieval.
3. Mill and Cohen, the two primary texts the essay quotes at length, for quotation and
   pagination accuracy.
4. Pallikkathayil, for the Korsgaard quotation.
5. Priority searches.

I verified the author's flagged Cohen page error because a critic who cannot independently
confirm a reported error is not much use, and because the correct fix turned out to be
different from the obvious one (the p. 9 page carries a sentence that says the same thing, so
the quotation can be swapped rather than the citation renumbered). I did **not** attempt to
re-verify every bibliographic detail the author already marked as unverified; I say so in
`sources.md` §D rather than leaving it implicit.

## 3. Lines of criticism tried and abandoned

Most of the work at this stage went into discarding objections that looked good and were not.

**(a) "The consent is simply invalid."** My first instinct was the objection the author
anticipates in their `process.md` §5.4 — that a consent which cannot justify is just an
invalid consent, and the validity/force distinction is relabelling. **Abandoned.** The
distinction is defensible and the essay is right about it: the normative power really is
exercised, no battery occurs, and "valid but idle" is a coherent position with analogues
(a valid but inadmissible confession; a genuine but worthless piece of testimony). Pressing
this would have been the predictable objection and the wrong one. What survives is a
different and I think better complaint: not that the distinction is unstable, but that the
essay never identifies the *act* for which the burden-shifted justification is owed. That is
`critique.md` §4.

**(b) "This is a sorites in disguise" / "this is just the self-torturer."** Both **abandoned
immediately**. The manuscript blocks them explicitly and correctly, and the author's
`process.md` §3c records rebuilding the case for exactly this purpose. Raising them would have
been a failure to read.

**(c) A purely economic deflation: "this is the hold-up problem and there is nothing new."**
**Abandoned** as stated, because it is unfair — the essay's claim is about consent's
justificatory force, not about the mechanism, and the author concedes the economic antecedent
in their process file. What survives is the narrower point that the concession belongs in the
essay rather than only in the process record, since an unacknowledged antecedent reads as an
unnoticed one.

**(d) A distributive objection: "if she was paid for the lock-in, there is no complaint."**
**Abandoned as a headline.** §8g anticipates it. But working through it produced something the
essay does not address: the §5 remedy pays Mara a counterfactual wage while she keeps
firm-specific capital Aurum financed, so there is an unresolved question about who owns the
specific investment. That became the second half of `critique.md` §4.

**(e) An attack on the electoral analogy as merely rhetorical.** I nearly wrote this as a
complaint about style. **Recast.** The analogy is not decorative; it is doing argumentative
work, and the right criticism is that it has the wrong causal structure (the ballot-rigger
acts unilaterally, Aurum needs Mara's four acceptances). That became `critique.md` §2, which I
now regard as the central philosophical objection.

**(f) Counting the ways §7's criterion overgeneralises.** I drafted a long list of
counterexamples and **cut it to three**, chosen to make different points: the internal labour
market (shows the conclusion is more radical than §9 admits), the failing firm (shows the
criterion needs an explanatory or intentional element), and the marriage (shows
"characteristically" is not a criterion). More examples would not have added an argument.

**(g) A full assessment of the §9 policy proposals.** **Cut.** Whether non-compete bans and
data portability actually follow is a separate question, the essay is appropriately modest
about it, and adjudicating it would have crowded out the source audit.

**(h) Writing a corrected §3 for the author.** **Refused by design.** The task forbids writing
the revised essay, and there is an independent reason: the correct repair of §3 depends on how
the author chooses to answer the mediation problem in §2, which is theirs to decide. I state
what the entries say and what the repair must accommodate, and stop there.

**(i) Treating the essay as a competition submission and grading it.** **Refused.** The task
is explicit, and in any case a grade would displace the actionable material.

## 4. What changed my assessment, and when

Worth recording because it bears on how much weight to put on the critique.

Before opening sources, my draft assessment was roughly: strong core idea, weak discriminating
criterion, under-argued Commitment Condition, overstated subtitle — and a mild note that §3
was doing a lot of work on thin citations. I expected the source audit to produce a short list
of page-number corrections.

Two retrievals changed that.

- **The manipulation entry's §4.1.** I opened it looking for the copyright block and found a
  whole section on situational and environmental manipulation, including Riker's "structuring
  the world so you can win", which is close to a one-line description of the essay's own case.
  This converted "thin citation" into "the section states something the cited source
  contradicts", and it is why `critique.md` calls §3 the weakest part rather than merely
  underdeveloped.
- **The *Exploitation* entry's §2.2.2.2.** Steiner's account — A gains more and B less than
  they would have but for a prior injustice, illustrated by A having deprived B of alternative
  sources of labour — is close enough to the essay's structure that finding it uncited was the
  most serious moment of the stage. On reflection it is *good* news for the essay, because
  Aurum commits no injustice and so falls outside Steiner's condition; but the essay currently
  claims novelty against a description of the literature that this entry falsifies.

One retrieval changed the assessment in the author's favour: the *Coercion* copyright block
confirmed Scott Anderson and 2023, closing a flagged uncertainty.

One finding was serendipitous. I read past the Mill passage the author quoted, into the
Humboldt material they had set aside, and found the reliance sentence immediately after it.
That is the most useful single thing I can give the reviser, and I found it only because I
read sixty lines rather than the fifteen I needed.

## 5. Remaining uncertainties and open risks

Ordered by how much they qualify what I have written.

1. **My literature claims are *SEP*-mediated.** I read six encyclopaedia entries and three
   primary texts. Everything I say about Steiner, Vrousalis, Goodin, Zwolinski, Riker,
   Barnhill, Mills, Garnett, Pettit, Olsaretti, Feinberg, Millum and Garnett, and Miller and
   Wertheimer is a report of a report. The entries are reliable reporters, but a reviser who
   acts on my §8 should read Steiner 1984 and Garnett 2018 directly before restating the
   originality claim. I could not do that here.
2. **Laitinen remains unread.** PhilArchive returned 403 to me as it did to the author. My
   downgrade of that priority risk rests on catalogue and search summaries, which are not a
   source. The risk is reduced, not closed.
3. **My word-count figure is an estimate, by the same method as the author's.** I got roughly
   5,900–6,300 words excluding the bibliography against the author's ~5,300. Two estimates
   that disagree by a thousand words are evidence that neither should be trusted, which is
   precisely why I flagged it: the disagreement, not my number, is the finding.
4. **I may be wrong that §7's condition (ii) needs an intentional element.** There is a
   structural reading on which occupying the ratchet position generates the duty regardless of
   what explains A's terms, and the essay's fiduciary sentence gestures at it. I recommend
   developing that line rather than asserting that the intentional reading is forced.
5. **My §2 objection may be answerable more cheaply than I allow.** If the essay's point is
   purely evidential — that a self-serving comparison is worthless however it came about — then
   the mediation through Mara's choices may simply not matter. I think that answer costs the
   essay its ability to discriminate, but I am not certain the cost is fatal, and I have tried
   to present the objection as a demand for an argument rather than as a refutation.
6. **I did not test the essay's §9 conjecture about other normative powers.** Promising,
   authorising and forgiving may or may not be vulnerable to analogous capture. The essay
   flags it as a conjecture; I have not assessed it, and my silence is not endorsement.
7. **Disagreement I am leaving on the record rather than resolving.** The author's `process.md`
   §5.3 and my `critique.md` §6 reach the same worry about the subtitle from opposite
   directions — the author asks whether the conclusion is too modest for the headline, I say
   the headline presupposes a wrong the argument does not deliver. We agree there is a
   mismatch and disagree about which end should move. I recommend moving the headline; the
   author may reasonably prefer to strengthen the conclusion.
8. **Sections I did not read.** *Coercion* §§2.1–2.3 and 3.4; *Dynamic Choice* §§2.1–2.4 and
   3–4 (including the resolute-choice material I cite only as a pointer); *Ethics of
   Manipulation* §§1–3 except two paragraphs; *Informed Consent* §§2, 3, 5.1–5.2, 7–10;
   *Exploitation* §§1, 2.1.3, 2.2.1.1, 2.3.2–2.3.3, 4. Any of these could contain material
   bearing on the essay that I have missed. `sources.md` §A gives the exact ranges read.
