# Process — essay-06-revision

This file records what I did at the revision stage, what I tried and set aside, what remains
unresolved, and the version lineage. It records visible work products only. No configuration,
timing, cost or model metadata appears here; those are collected mechanically elsewhere.

---

## 1. What this stage was

A fresh Claude context with no memory of the authoring or critic stages. I read the three assigned
inputs (`task.md`, `NEUTRAL_BRIEF.md`, `RULES.md`), then the sealed dependencies: `draft-001.md`,
`sources.md` and `process.md` from essay-06-author, and `critique.md` from essay-06-critic. I then
re-retrieved and read every source the critique disputed, plus three the essay had never consulted,
before changing a word. The manuscript was rewritten, not patched.

Order of work: read inputs → read sealed dependencies → build a list of every factual claim the
critique challenged → verify each against separately saved extractions → decide which criticisms to
accept → rewrite → record the decisions in `response.md` and the provenance in `sources.md`.

I did not simulate a second critic and there is no further critique stage in this lineage.

## 2. Verification first, revision second

I made this the governing rule of the stage, because a critique is evidence and not an instruction,
and because the critique itself flagged that some of its own claims rested on encyclopedia entries
rather than primary texts. Eight retrievals were made and read at this stage; details, line numbers
and coverage are in `sources.md`.

The verification changed my view three times.

- **It made the critique stronger than it claimed on coercion.** The critique said the draft had
  truncated a sentence “in a way that hurts”. Reading the sentence, the antecedent — “created the
  conditions” — is satisfied by my own case on my own telling, which is a sharper problem than a
  truncation. The revision concedes the antecedent outright and rests the distinction elsewhere.
- **It gave me a better attribution than the critique supplied.** The critique reported the
  “unfairly curtailed by the offer itself” proposal without naming its source. The entry credits it
  to Miller and Wertheimer 2010, 92, 97. That let me open the essay with a named, precise synchronic
  foil instead of a vague appeal to “the dominant picture”, which was the draft's weakest opening
  move and one the critique separately warned was a straw man risk.
- **It gave me a line the critique did not find.** Raz's contrast in the manipulation entry —
  manipulation “does not interfere with a person's options” but “perverts the way that person
  reaches decisions” — states the gap my case occupies better than anything I would have written.
  It is now the hinge of that paragraph.

## 3. Decisions taken at this stage, and the alternatives set aside

**(a) Reframe around independence rather than authorship — taken.** The alternative was to defend
“authorship” by weakening it to “substantial causal contribution”. **Set aside**, because the
critique's counterexample is decisive against the draft's actual sentences (Aurum could not secure
the certification at will), and because a causal reframing would still have the wrong shape: the
defect is evidential and conflict-of-interest-shaped, not causal. Adopting independence also made
the electoral analogy exact and converted Inverse Reliance from a stipulation into a consequence, so
it paid for itself twice.

**(b) Make the §7 criterion positional rather than behavioural — taken.** The critique offered a
disjunction: admit a thin intentional element, or go positional. I took only the second horn. **Set
aside**: the intentional repair (“A's terms are explained by the lowered reservation value”), because
it reintroduces exactly the hostage to intention that the platform and visa cases exist to escape,
and because it makes the criterion turn on a motivational fact usually unknowable from outside. The
price of the positional route is a very widely distributed duty, and I decided to pay it in the open
rather than trim the criterion to preserve a comfortable extension.

**(c) Bite the internal-labour-market bullet — taken.** **Set aside**: two ways of dodging it. One was
to add a materiality threshold (“significantly lowers”), which is arbitrary and invites the same
objection one step later. The other was to restrict the duty to cases where the lowering is large
relative to the surplus, which is a distributive condition and would have reimported the exploitation
framing the essay is trying to distinguish itself from. The bullet turned out to be nourishing: the
failing firm, the marriage and the internal labour market all get the verdict I think we actually
hold, once the conclusion is “must justify otherwise” rather than “acts wrongly”.

**(d) Concede the fair-market-value point rather than resist it — taken.** The critique observed that
the draft dismissed exploitation theory in §3 and reinvented it in §5, and called this the most
obvious line of attack. I agree. **Set aside**: an attempt to distinguish my benchmark from
Wertheimer's by insisting mine is procedural. It is not, and the pretence would not have survived a
reader. The revision instead argues that the fair-market-value test is silent about *which*
hypothetical market, and that baseline capture supplies the missing index. Conceding turned an attack
into a place to stand.

**(e) Replace the remedial benchmark — taken.** The draft's test (what Mara's talents would command
had she never dealt with Aurum) is caught in the dilemma the critique set out. **Set aside**: both
horns, and also the reliance-damages analogy, which the critique correctly noted compensates the
investing party — here arguably Aurum. The replacement is a division-of-surplus benchmark. This is
the one change the critique did not propose, and it is the change I am least certain about (see §5).

**(f) Argue that pricing is not authorising — taken.** **Set aside**: the draft's unpriceability
premise, which the critique rightly called asserted. Agents price others' discretion constantly. The
replacement uses a distinction the essay already relies on elsewhere — compensation versus
justification — and is therefore cheaper as well as truer.

**(g) Concede more on the compensation objection — taken.** The critique showed that the draft's
“payment does not undo staging” begs the question. The revision concedes that a binding trajectory
bargain struck in 2016 *would* justify, and argues that buying an option while keeping the discretion
is not selling the power. **Set aside**: the option of holding the line, which would have required
claiming that no ex ante arrangement can restore justificatory force — a claim the Commitment
Condition contradicts.

**(h) Reframe Mill as continuity — taken.** **Set aside**: the “part Mill did not see” framing, which
the reliance passage at pp. 196–97 refutes; and the claim that the gradient reading is unnoticed,
which Olsaretti and Feinberg refute. What survives is the direction-of-application argument, which I
think is the real contribution of that section and is now doing work instead of rhetoric.

**(i) Replace the agglomeration analogies — taken.** **Set aside**: rigged game, fixed fight, and also
“each belief justified while the set is inconsistent”, which is definitionally set-level and proves
nothing about action. The evidential replacements (calibrated instrument, selective witness) have the
right structure: no faulty part, nothing concealed, and a whole that fails only for the party who
assembled it.

**(j) Retitle — taken.** “Wrongs Without Wrongful Parts” presupposed what the argument does not
deliver. **Set aside**: the critique's proposed “Consent Without Justificatory Force”, which is
accurate but names only the negative result; the chosen title names the mechanism, and the subtitle
carries the disclaimer.

**(k) Retrieve the hold-up literature rather than merely acknowledge it — taken.** The authoring stage
recorded this as a known gap and the critique said the concession should be in the essay rather than
only in the process file. I went further and read Klein, Crawford and Alchian. This was the best
hour's work of the stage: their remark that appropriable quasi rent needs “no market closure or
restrictions placed on rival assets” independently vindicates the “no fence” claim, and their
definition of opportunism as “unanticipated non-fulfillment of the contract” marks exactly where my
case departs, since Aurum breaches nothing.

**(l) Keep the Laitinen priority risk at full weight — taken.** The critique recommended downgrading
it from catalogue descriptions. **Set aside**, because none of the three stages has read the chapter,
and a catalogue description is not a reading. I did not retry PhilArchive, since it returned 403 to
both prior stages and repeated probing is not warranted.

**(m) The Kantian material — twice reduced.** The draft used Korsgaard's possible-consent criterion as
a foil. The critique noted that the source quoting it argues the criterion fails generally, which I
did not verify. **Set aside** the draft's paragraph. In `final-001.md` I replaced it with a short,
verified note from the *Treating Persons as Means* entry: Kantian accounts ask whether the other
*could* consent or *has* consented, and neither asks who made refusing so costly. In `final-002.md`
that note was cut entirely for length, being illustrative rather than load-bearing. Korsgaard and
Pallikkathayil no longer appear in the bibliography.

**(n) A verbatim mea-culpa apparatus in the essay — tried and abandoned.** `final-001.md` narrated its
own corrections in the text (“the draft said X, which was wrong”). It made the paper honest but
self-absorbed, and it cost a great deal of length. In `final-002.md` all of it moved to
`response.md`, where it belongs. The essay reads as a standalone argument and the audit trail is
intact in a file dedicated to it.

## 4. Length

This is the one place where the stage went wrong and had to be corrected mechanically.

The authoring stage estimated `draft-001.md` at about 5,300 words and warned the figure was unmeasured.
The critic estimated 5,900–6,300 by the same method and warned that neither figure was a measurement.
I wrote `final-001.md` believing it to be around 5,000 words, on the same kind of estimate, having no
word-count tool available in this context. It was measured mechanically at **7,340 word units
excluding bibliography**, well above the 6,000 ceiling.

Two lessons, recorded because they are part of the honest methodology. First, all three stages of
this essay estimated length by eye and all three were wrong in the same direction. Estimation by a
language model of its own output length is not reliable and should not be treated as evidence of
compliance. Second, the correction is not a matter of shaving sentences. `final-002.md` was written
to roughly sixty per cent of the length of `final-001.md`, on the assumption that the measured
figure would scale approximately with text length, in order to leave real margin rather than to
approach the ceiling. I have not measured `final-002.md` and do not claim a figure for it.

Nothing was dropped in order to fit. The compression came from: moving the correction commentary to
`response.md` (the largest single saving); cutting the optional Kantian note; shortening the case
narration and the Mill block quotation to the sentences actually used; and rewriting every section
more tersely. Every thesis, principle, objection, concession and citation in `final-001.md` survives
in `final-002.md`.

## 5. Remaining uncertainties

Ordered by how much they worry me.

1. **Is the positional criterion too wide?** It makes every internal labour market, every long
   marriage and every durable sponsorship a site of justificatory burden. I argue in §7 that this is
   a feature, since it predicts the institutions we already have. But a reader who thinks a criterion
   should *discriminate* rather than *distribute* will find this unsatisfying, and I do not have a
   knock-down reply.
2. **Is the division-of-surplus benchmark operable?** It is better specified than the draft's test and
   it escapes the critique's dilemma, but it asks what share A could have obtained at a
   counterfactual moment, which is contestable. This is the change least scrutinised by anyone but me.
3. **Is “valid but justificatorily idle” stable?** The authoring stage flagged this and the critique
   declined to press it. I still think the distinction holds — the power really is exercised, no
   battery occurs — and I still have not defended it at length. It remains the most obvious place to
   attack the framework rather than its applications.
4. **Blamelessness versus answerability.** My reply to the structural-injustice objection turns on the
   claim that one can owe a justification without having done wrong. I believe this, but the essay
   asserts more than it argues here, and the objection is the one I expect most readers to press.
5. **Priority.** Laitinen remains unread by all three stages. The novelty searches behind the thesis
   were run at the authoring stage and are weak evidence at best. I did not re-run them and would not
   describe the thesis as unprecedented.
6. **Encyclopedia dependence.** Apart from Mill and Klein, Crawford and Alchian, every primary text in
   the bibliography is cited through a *Stanford Encyclopedia* entry. `sources.md` marks this at every
   point, and the manuscript uses “as reported in” and “as quoted in” rather than pretending
   otherwise, but it is a real limitation on how confidently the literature claims can be made.
7. **Three author-and-date attributions** rest on fetch reports rather than my own reading of the
   copyright block (*Informed Consent*, *Dynamic Choice*, *Treating Persons as Means*). Flagged in
   `sources.md` §D2.
