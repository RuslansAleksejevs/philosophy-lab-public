## Stage narrative

**Topic selection.** No topic or thesis was supplied. I generated candidates and discarded most quickly; the discarded set is listed below. I settled on the moral testimony / moral deference debate because it has a sharply defined dialectic and because two scoping searches suggested an unoccupied structural position: searches for collective-level or public-good treatments of moral deference surfaced only individual-level work (Hills, Hopkins, Nickel, Sliwa, Enoch, Howell, Fletcher, and Wiland's joint-agent account). That gap became the paper.

**Thesis.** The pessimists' intuition is sound and their location of it is wrong. Granting the optimists the entire individual-level case, there remains an ecological cost: moral norms need a distributed stock of applicative competence to stay in repair; that stock is produced by the activity deference replaces; so deference draws on it without replenishing it. The essay names the structural feature (substitution-coupling), argues that morality lacks the exogenous error signal which makes deference safe in medicine, argues that dispersal matters because it decorrelates error, blocks assimilation to bad generalisation arguments by moving the unit of assessment from acts to policies, and offers three Replenishment Conditions plus a plurality constraint.

## Approaches set aside, and why

**Other topics considered and dropped.** Sunk-cost rationality reconstrued as option-individuation (risk of reinventing Kelly's work on acting for the sake of the past); standing to blame and hypocrisy (crowded, and my angle was a variant of existing answerability accounts); ex ante contractualism and the reference-class problem (the individuation worry is already in Frick); advice as a normative category distinct from assertion and command (attractive, but Wiland's book occupies much of that ground); attention as a distinct locus of moral failure (real, but I could not make the argument crisp enough to defend in the space).

**Framings within the chosen topic that were tried and abandoned.**

1. *Rescuing pessimism.* My first instinct was to offer a better individual-level account of what the deferrer lacks. Abandoned: every version reproduced the familiar dilemma of overshooting into benign cases or shrinking to a pro tanto cost that Enoch-style risk considerations swamp. Conceding the individual case entirely turned out to be a strength, since it insulates the thesis from the whole existing dispute.

2. *Running the argument as a tragedy of the commons.* Dropped because the fit is poor, and the poor fit is informative. The stock is not depleted by rivalrous consumption; my asking you costs no one anything. Substitution-coupling - consumption and production competing inside a single decision - is more accurate and does more work.

3. *Leaning on response-dependence.* An earlier plan made the argument partly constitutive: outsource application and there ceases to be a determinate answer. This buys a stronger conclusion at the price of a contested metaethics. Demoted to a parenthesis in section 9 and explicitly set aside.

4. *Arguing that individual deferrers act wrongly.* Tempting as a headline, but it walks into the aggregation objections and is false besides. The essay keeps the conclusion structural and relocates the normative burden onto advisers and institutions, which also yields the more useful practical upshot.

5. *Deploying epistemic-democracy results.* I considered supporting the decorrelation claim with Condorcet-style or Hong-Page-style material. Dropped: I could not access primary sources for it in this stage, and importing formal results I had not read would have been exactly the unverified apparatus the brief warns against. The point is made informally instead, which is all the argument needs.

6. *Foregrounding AI moral advisers.* Rejected as a frame. The argument is independent of the technology and is stronger stated so. The application survives as a short, clearly marked coda.

7. *A fourth Replenishment Condition.* Plurality was drafted as R4 and then moved out of the list, because it is a property of an advisory ecosystem rather than of a deference episode, and four conditions diluted the criterion.

## Remaining uncertainties and disagreements

- **The empirical hostage.** The thesis assumes applicative competence is built mainly by exercise, so that policies of deference leave a population with less of it. Section 9 states this openly and says what would falsify it. I did not support it with psychological literature; that is the most obvious place for the paper to be attacked or extended.
- **Whether the position is genuinely unoccupied.** My originality check was two targeted searches, not a systematic review, and I could not read the full texts of Hills, Hopkins, Enoch, Howell, Fletcher or Wiland. One of them may make a version of the ecological point in a passage I could not see. Wiland is the likeliest near neighbour, since his joint-agent account also moves past the solitary deferrer, though toward a two-person unit rather than a population-level capacity. I flag this rather than claim priority.
- **Strength of the science disanalogy.** Section 5 claims morality has no error signal exogenous to moral response at the level of application. A strongly naturalistic metaethics could deny this. I restricted the claim to application rather than truth, which I think is defensible, but it is the premise most likely to be contested.
- **The unread ORA source.** An unidentified paper on moral testimony pessimism sits downloaded and unread in `sources/`. Its argument is unknown to me and might bear on section 9's treatment of authenticity.
- **Hart at one remove.** Every Hart claim is quoted from the encyclopedia entry, not from *The Concept of Law*. That is a careful source and I attribute accordingly, but it is second-hand and marked as such in `sources.md`.
