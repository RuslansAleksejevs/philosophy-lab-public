# Process notes — essay-04-author, generation stage

## What this stage was

One worker, one pass, from topic selection through source research to a finished manuscript. This is the generation-only condition: there is no critic and no revision stage, and I did not simulate one. `final-001.md` is the first and only version produced at this stage.

## Order of work

1. Read the three assigned inputs (task.md, NEUTRAL_BRIEF.md, RULES.md). No other files, essays, or prior campaign material were read.
2. Generated and triaged candidate topics (below).
3. Attempted source discovery. Web search was unavailable for the whole session (rate limit hit on the first two queries), so all retrieval proceeded from URLs I could name directly. This is the single largest methodological limitation of the stage and it shaped the bibliography: encyclopedia entries and a public-domain legal text, rather than current journal articles.
4. Retrieved and read sources; the local hook archived originals and extracted text separately from the fetch summaries, and I read the archived text for every source on which a substantive claim depends.
5. Drafted the argument as a numbered skeleton, stress-tested it against objections, then wrote the manuscript in one pass.

## Topics considered and set aside

Recorded because the brief asks for abandoned approaches and reasons, not only for the survivor.

- **Moral zetetic norms: duties of attention and the bootstrapping problem** (what you ought to attend to depends on what matters to you, which depends on what you attend to). Set aside because the circularity is genuine but I could not find a resolution crisp enough to defend inside the length limit, and because attention ethics is currently well populated.
- **Transformative experience and the rationality of deference.** Set aside as heavily worked over, and because my angle collapsed into a familiar point about changing standards of assessment.
- **Degrees of moral status and the argument from vagueness.** Set aside because the decisive move needed metaethical commitments larger than the essay could carry.
- **Rational non-reconsideration modelled as trust in one’s past self.** Set aside as too close to existing work on resolute choice and self-trust; novel wording would not have been enough.
- **Obligations to the dead.** Set aside; interesting but the literature is mature and my contribution would have been a refinement.

## Framings of the chosen topic that were abandoned

- **Bernard Williams’s Gauguin and retrospective justification as the central foil.** This was the original plan: the essay would have been organized around denying that success retrospectively justifies. Abandoned after fetching the SEP “Moral Luck” entry and finding that the portions I read do not discuss the Gauguin case at all, and after failing to obtain Williams’s text. Building a central foil out of an author I could not read would have meant unverifiable exegesis. Williams is now not cited anywhere.
- **An argument from the open future.** I drafted a line of thought on which, if future contingents lack truth value, there is at the time of action no fact about whether consent will later be given, so retroactive justification has nothing to be grounded in. Abandoned because it makes an ethical thesis hostage to contested metaphysics, and because the retroactivist can simply deny the open-future premise. It was replaced by the bypass argument (§4.2), which does not depend on any view about future contingents.
- **Stating the thesis as a supervenience claim** — that deontic status supervenes only on facts at or before the time of action. Abandoned because it is false as stated: objective oughts can plainly depend on later facts (you ought to bet on the horse that will win). This discovery forced the reformulation that became §6.1, distinguishing a future fact functioning as a *condition* from a future act functioning as a *giving*. I regard this as the most important correction made during drafting: the earlier formulation would have sunk the paper.
- **Framing the whole essay as an anti-moral-luck argument.** Set aside because it would have imported a different debate and made the thesis look like a corollary of the Control Principle, which it is not; the parity argument (§4.3) is now presented as one consideration among three, not as the foundation.
- **A section on Ulysses contracts and self-binding.** Cut for length, and because on my own thesis prior consent is the easy case; a paragraph in §7.2 does the needed work.

## Things I decided to keep despite discomfort

- The *Ballot* case does heavy lifting against instrumentalism about consent, and I have conceded in §8 that it is an intuition pump rather than a refutation. I considered strengthening it into an argument and could not do so without begging the question.
- §7.2 touches an ethically charged area (later re-description of past consent). I kept it because the framework genuinely clarifies it, and wrote it to state a structural distinction — exercise of power versus finding of fact — with explicit acknowledgement that later testimony is often the best evidence about the past. I did not want the thesis to be quotable as something it is not.

## Remaining uncertainties

1. **Whether the remedial layer is fully waivable.** My Corollary 2 says only that the remedial layer is where a power *could* operate. Critics of normative-power accounts of forgiveness argue it often does not. If they are right, retrospective endorsement does even less than I allow. I have not adjudicated this.
2. **Liberto’s actual argument.** I have read only the SEP characterization of her “backward reach” claim. My reply in §6.2 addresses the reported structure. If her cases involve conferral of authority rather than late identification of a claim-holder, my reply needs more work.
3. **Legal relation-back.** My claim that such doctrines are hedged in favour of intervening third parties is an unsourced conjecture, presented as such.
4. **Ratification without prior delegation** (§8, fourth concession) is the place where the thesis is most exposed.
5. **Third-party standing.** I assert that the victim’s release does not touch it, and support this with the review-committee observation. An opponent could say the committee is enforcing institutional, not moral, standards, which would weaken the test.
6. **The grounding argument (§4.1)** depends on interest-based accounts of normative powers. A non-interest-based account might motivate backward reach differently.
