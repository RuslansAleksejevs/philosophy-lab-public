## Novelty assessment

The broad starting proposal is substantially anticipated. Preservation duties, practical contestability, fair allocation of evidential prejudice, and favorable presumptions after missing evidence are existing ideas. It would be inaccurate to present their combination as the discovery of evidentiary rights. The draft should acknowledge this directly.

The narrower proposed contribution is an argument about continued authority: when an institution itself disables a fair challenge, its loss of entitlement to impose the ordinary appellant's burden concerns present maintenance of power. It need not entail that the original decision was wrong, that destruction supplies evidence of guilt, or that the challenger receives the disputed substantive status. Develop a rule for withdrawing reliance on a default, and explain why remedy may require renewed assessment or interim protection rather than certification of the claimant's factual assertions. Originality of that exact account remains unestablished.

Searches included combinations of “contestatory democracy”, “burden proof”, “culpable ignorance”, “spoliation”, “epistemic gaps”, “right to contest”, and “evidence destroy”. These found serious nearby work and constrained the proposed claim. Forst's 1999/2010 justification articles were located through publisher metadata, but full text was not read. His accessible manuscript was located but is not used. Search results on colonial epistemic reparations were considered for relevance, not treated as a read literature base.
