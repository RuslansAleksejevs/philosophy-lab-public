## Accepted criticisms and resulting changes

1. **A prohibition is not a reserved permission.** Accepted. Removed the argument that a compensatory system necessarily lets the institution buy permission to breach. Section 2 now states and defends a fair-procedural-demand premise: the ordinary allocation of evidential responsibility cannot justify itself after the institution breaches a materially enabling part of that allocation. A delivery/nonresponse analogy isolates the altered normative significance of the claimant's nonperformance. This remains a contestable philosophical premise, not a conceptual truth deduced from any duty.

2. **Define the result, and resolve the fixed high-accuracy case.** Accepted. The defended result is loss of reliance on the ordinary default plus a right to effective independent remedial reassessment, not presumptive full waiver. Section 3 expressly permits collection with an adequate independent remedy in the stipulated 99.9-percent case. High probability does not restore review but can support a new allocation under uncertainty. This is a substantive concession relative to the draft's stronger rhetoric.

3. **Separate restoration, new grounds, and override.** Accepted. The final distinguishes actual restoration of assessability, remedial reassessment while the original question remains unresolved, and authorization of a different prospective decision such as a safety qualification. Weighty competing interests can override a procedural claim without vindicating the original result; a bare reference to revenue does not establish such weight.

4. **Compensation and the £10 example.** Accepted. Section 3 explicitly rejects both automatic expected-value adequacy and automatic full waiver. Compensation assessed by a genuinely empowered independent reviewer can be adequate. The essay supplies no universal valuation of procedural standing. It also expressly declines to infer a general suspension rule from the more limited right to reassessment.

5. **Information-discovery function of access.** Accepted. Section 4 supplies a second trigger: eligibility for a justified inspection, culpable loss of the designated material, and resulting informational disability, even without an identified substantive error. This gives access to a procedural remedy, not automatic qualification for the underlying benefit. The Osborn §88 comparison is added.

6. **Bad faith, innocent loss, and causal boundaries.** Accepted. The final distinguishes moral entitlement from an administrable presumption that can mistakenly benefit a dishonest claimant. It does not treat a known false exemption assertion as morally vindicated. It explains the additional reason supplied by fault without allocating innocent loss automatically to the claimant. It explicitly assumes that compliance would have maintained the relevant opportunity and brackets redundant-cause cases.

7. **Scholarship and novelty.** Accepted. Re-read Adams p. 161; added primary passages from Carey and Osborn, saved in sources/revision-*.json and described in sources-v2.md. Acknowledged that Stewart already distinguishes appeal triggers from factual findings and automatic reversal. Engaged Rule 37(e)'s prohibition on disguised severe sanctions: changing the description of a waiver does not settle its proportionality. Removed the exact O'Neill lecture date from the final reference because it is unnecessary to the argument and the critic did not verify it from the transcript.

## Not adopted as conclusions

The critique presents a stronger presumption of substantive relief as one possible path. I do not defend that path: the missing premise for automatic waiver is not supplied merely by the intrinsic value of procedure. I retain the narrower right to an effective reassessment capable of changing treatment. The possibility of unchanged substantive outcomes does not reduce that right to a duty to improve official phrasing: it changes the claimant's threshold for obtaining review and the institution's task in justifying the residual burden.

I also do not claim that the three categories of surviving justification exhaust every institutional setting or establish a complete remedial theory. They distinguish the key mechanisms in the examples; exact proportionality and the design of general procedural guarantees remain open.

## Remaining limitations and reopen triggers

- Further challenge to the fair-procedural-demand premise may show that the default can survive more breaches than the present account permits.
- The independent assessment still requires substantive standards of acceptable remedial allocation; the essay identifies constraints rather than a general decision algorithm.
- The role of preexisting strict guarantees, innocent loss, and redundant causation is explicitly limited.
- The account's precise originality remains unestablished. Solum and Marzen is a serious unexamined lead; the relevant books and legal scholarship have not been exhaustively surveyed.
- The final revision has not received another separate critique. This is one completed author–critic–revision cycle, not independent certification of the revised argument.

Reopen if a closer source already supplies the same default-defeat argument, if an independently compensated rival can preserve the default without denying effective remedial standing, or if a counterexample shows that the proposed demand itself allocates responsibility unfairly.
