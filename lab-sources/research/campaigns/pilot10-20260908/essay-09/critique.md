# Critique of “When Restrictions Sustain Their Own Reasons”

Independent AI critic: /root/critic_09. 2026-09-08 UTC.

Assessment: REVISE. The core distinction is defensible and the writing is clear. The present draft has a consequential normative gap and an important originality gap. Neither requires abandoning the project. The strongest revision would defend a bounded obligation attached to control over another person's exit from a restrictive regime, explicitly compare that account with existing rehabilitation jurisprudence, and separate opening access from paying for positive assistance.

## 1. Strongest reconstruction

The essay grants a legitimate standard, a warranted original suspension and accurate present assessments. It therefore avoids the easy conclusion that an institution should correct a false or biased verdict. Its claim is that continuing restrictive authority can bring an additional responsibility: if the regime itself obstructs ordinary ways of meeting a release criterion, and the institution can reasonably reopen one, then it must enable the opportunity or supply an adequate reason for refusal.

The argument has four steps. First, immediate safety can justify continued exclusion. Second, exclusion plus assisted preparation is an alternative to exclusion without preparation. Third, present incapacity alone does not establish that the second course is permissible when the first is available. Fourth, because the institution maintains both the restriction and the obstacle, it bears responsibility for enabling the better arrangement. The distinctive practical object can be development of competence, not merely evidence of competence already possessed.

The first three steps establish that accuracy does not exhaust the relevant justification. The fourth is the substantive moral step. It cannot be obtained from the logical insufficiency of one reason alone. A reason can fail to justify refusing assistance without there being an institutional duty to supply assistance. The paper recognizes this, but its explicit principle remains more secure in the access case than in the provision case.

Preserve the true-from-the-start case, the difference between permission to restrict and responsibility to improve the arrangement, the prospective comparison, and the refusal to guarantee a favorable verdict. These are the paper's most useful features.

## 2. The most consequential originality problem: close rehabilitation precedents

A close precedent exists outside the sources currently discussed. In James, Wells and Lee v. the United Kingdom (2012), §§206–209 connect preventive detention with access to rehabilitation, including the difference between reducing danger and demonstrating its reduction. In Haney and related appeals, [2014] UKSC 66, §§36–39 distinguish an obligation to enable reasonable rehabilitation and demonstration of safety from an entitlement to immediate release; the reasoning gives a separate place to facilities preceding review. The court's reliance on Article 5 and rehabilitative purposes matters. These decisions do not establish the proposed general moral duty, but they substantially anticipate its central practical distinction. [Official ECtHR judgment](https://hudoc.echr.coe.int/app/conversion/pdf/?TID=rekkwokewm&filename=001-113127.pdf&id=001-113127&library=ECHR); [official UK Supreme Court judgment](https://supremecourt.uk/uploads/uksc_2014_0036_judgment_9a6017c043.pdf).

The draft should not merely add a citation and continue unchanged. It needs to explain what philosophical work remains once a recognized instance of this structure is on the table. A potentially worthwhile answer is a general account of why control over release conditions creates duties even where no rehabilitative purpose was part of the institution's initial mandate. That account could explain when the detention analogy extends to licensing and when it fails. It would also show that the argument depends on a relation of authority, rather than on the particularly strong value of bodily liberty alone.

The relevant contrast is therefore not principally with people who think that only false beliefs can be wrong. It is with a defender of limited administrative mandates who accepts reasonable rehabilitation duties in detention yet denies that this tells us anything comparable about licensing bodies. The latter opponent will say that detention is an exceptional exercise of coercion, often governed by additional purposes and undertakings. A licensing authority may be authorized to protect third parties without being a provider of vocational training. Address that opponent explicitly.

Neither the newly found precedent nor the recourse literature proves that there can be no original contribution. They do make the current contribution claim too underdeveloped for an essay whose importance rests on identifying a distinctive duty. Describe the result as a proposed general justification and boundary for a familiar kind of enabling responsibility. Do not claim discovery of the basic coexistence of justified exclusion and owed assistance. The literature search remains incomplete.

## 3. The duty to justify is not yet the duty to provide

The first premise in §4 says that conditional restrictive authority must justify avoidable burdens. This is plausible. The difficult inference is from that premise to a directed positive duty to provide opportunities that consume resources. The authority can accept that it must justify its training policy yet deny that it must finance Mara's participation. The draft moves among removing an exclusion rule, arranging instruction and bearing the cost of administering power. Those are distinguishable responsibilities.

Consider a variation. The authority removes the rule excluding uncertified operators from the simulator, gives Mara a fair place in its queue, and charges her the same reasonable fee as new applicants. The fee covers real supervision costs. Mara cannot afford it. A general vocational assistance body can fund one trainee, with several applicants whose needs are as serious. The original exclusion materially closed Mara's only route; the licensing body controls a technically feasible intervention; training is safe. Does reopening require the licensing body's budget to pay, a transfer from the assistance body, merely fair access to a grant, or a subsidy that recognizes an extra claim arising from suspension? The draft's qualifications about scarcity do not identify which responsibility it has actually defended.

This is not a demand for a complete allocation theory. It is a demand to identify the content of the central directed duty. If every cost-allocation dispute is delegated to unspecified further moral facts, the theory has established a duty to reconsider obstruction, with a conditional prospect of assistance. That is a useful but weaker result than an institution owing provision whenever costs are proportionate.

A precise revision could distinguish three claims: an obligation to remove an unjustified eligibility barrier; an obligation to arrange accessible preparation where the regime has made independent access unavailable; and a separate, defeasible obligation to bear some costs. Defend the second through the fact that the institution continues to exercise avoidable control over the person's exit. Say explicitly how much of the third follows. If it does not follow, stop short of asserting that assigning costs elsewhere is necessarily evasion.

Give the moral bridge a direct defense. For example, someone may concede temporary restrictive authority without granting the institution unilateral power to retain avoidable obstacles to termination. Explain why a fair distribution of burdens of that authority must include enabling reasonable termination, rather than merely periodically testing for it. This would articulate a substantive condition of legitimate administration. The present claim that extra control has not been justified points in that direction, but mostly restates the problem.

## 4. The stipulated example makes the extension look easier than it is

The opening stipulates an authority that owns the only useful equipment, excludes precisely the people who need training, and knows a safe route is promising. Removing that rule seems compelling. But it is unclear how much work is done by continued conditional restriction and how much by a gratuitously exclusionary rule governing an essential public resource.

Compare Nara, a first-time applicant seeking the same profession. She has the same deficit and cannot safely gain the missing competence outside the authority's facility. The facility admits only certified operators. Nara has never been suspended. Does the authority owe her access? If yes, explain what the special restriction-based duty adds to an already general obligation to keep a qualification system practicable. If no, explain why Mara's prior inclusion changes the claim even where Nara has equal need, aptitude and reliance on this career. The fact of an ongoing suspension may produce additional responsibilities, but it does not by itself establish their scope or priority.

The case also needs a criterion for what counts as a restriction. Every licensing denial can be represented as a continuing prohibition that terminates on qualification; every benefit denial can be redescribed as conditional exclusion. Without a boundary, the claim to avoid an unlimited right to be made qualified depends on a verbal choice about whether a denial is an intervention. That issue cannot be resolved by saying Mara is already subject to an ongoing arrangement, since applicants are also subject to the licensing prohibition.

One defensible route is to limit the principle to significant exercises of authority over access to a liberty, livelihood or similarly weighty interest, with independent justification for counting the institution as responsible for the relevant obstruction. Another is to accept a broader implication for first applicants but state that the objection is to institutionally blocked access to qualification, whether or not preceded by suspension. Either choice would improve the argument; the author must decide which its reasons support.

Test the boundary with a voluntary club requiring advanced skill for participation and offering no remedial instruction. Mere control over its own facilities plus an unmet condition should not automatically make it a remedial provider. The explanation should identify differences of coercive reach, public responsibility, prior undertaking or independent opportunities. This is a stress test for the proposed basis, not a claim that every club is exempt from duties.

## 5. The diagnostic question needs replacement

The proposed diagnostic in §5 asks whether a refusal reason would apply with equal force to someone for whom training is safe and likely to succeed. That is not a reliable test of relevance to reopening. A fair queue rule or a reason to preserve the simulator for an urgent safety exercise can apply with equal force to exactly such a person. Both may justify postponement even though they do not vary with training efficacy. Your later discussion already grants these possibilities.

Even the phrase in §4 about a condition that distinguishes none of the courses is too broad if taken as a general principle of reasons. A fact shared across possible actions can affect their comparative value because the actions respond differently to that fact. Current incapacity is common to both courses, yet its severity can determine whether training is productive or whether close supervision is needed. The draft partly handles this in its unsafe-simulator example; state the modest point consistently.

Replace the counterfactual-person test with a contrast between feasible policies: identify the proposed alternative, then ask whether the supplied considerations explain why it is permissible to maintain the identified barrier instead. Facts about current incapacity are relevant when joined to evidence about the alternative's risks or prospects. The conclusion should concern the insufficiency of bare present disqualification, not the logical irrelevance of facts common to alternatives.

This repair preserves the paper's best insight: answering one practical question does not discharge the burden of answering another. It removes a test that creates false alarms for precisely the resource-allocation reasons the essay accepts.

## 6. Institutional scope and policy feasibility

“Controls a practicable means” can be too restrictive or too permissive depending on what institutional unit is meant. A licensing office might administer suspensions while a separate public department operates simulators. Both can tell Mara that the other body owns the relevant decision. Conversely, a clerk may physically be able to grant access while lacking legitimate authority to allocate the facility.

You need not develop a full theory of collective obligation, but distinguish the body giving the adverse verdict, the body able to change the obstruction, and the governing arrangement that allocates powers between them. A duty to initiate referral, seek authorization or secure coordination can exist even when the verdict-maker cannot provide training alone. Otherwise administrative fragmentation supplies an easy escape from the proposed principle. Your statement that other bodies may assist does not yet establish who must begin coordination or what happens when the arrangement itself withholds the necessary authority.

Assess practicability at the level of a fair policy, not just a favorable isolated intervention. One free simulator place may be affordable for Mara while providing equivalent opportunities to a large class is not. Conversely, an institution should not manufacture an exemption by underfunding the very process its restrictive regime foreseeably requires. Both possibilities matter to the claim that a practicable route exists. Add a short account of reasonable capacity planning and staged access, without claiming that present budget labels settle the moral limit.

## 8. Significance, clarity and revision priorities

The significance is real but conditional on defending the positive-duty step. A review procedure can repeatedly reach true conclusions while its surrounding policy wrongly prevents change. That identifies a practical target of evaluation that cannot be reduced to improving classification. As drafted, however, some of the apparent depth comes from redescribing an obviously unnecessary training exclusion. The principle becomes more interesting when it can distinguish assistance genuinely owed through a restrictive relationship from assistance supported by general distributive duties.

Clarity is strong. The seven sections build a coherent argument, and the examples avoid fabricated empirical claims. Repetition in §§2, 4, 5 and 7 could be compressed to make room for the harder work. “Continued suspension with supervised practice can nevertheless be safer for the public” is unnecessary unless a public-safety benefit beyond indefinite removal is specified. The decisive stipulated comparison needs comparable safety and lower burdens; it need not claim superior safety.

Priority order for revision:

1. Acknowledge the close rehabilitation precedents and identify the general philosophical result that remains to be defended.
2. Separate opening access, arranging provision and paying for it; defend the directed institutional obligation rather than deriving it from an unanswered justificatory question.
3. Address the first-applicant case and state the scope of qualifying restrictive authority.
4. Replace the diagnostic test with an explicit comparison of policies and relevant reasons.
5. Clarify coordinated responsibility and policy-level feasibility, then correct the Ortmann locator.

A narrower successful conclusion would be: significant conditional restrictive authority creates a presumptive duty to keep practicable routes toward termination accessible, with further duties of provision where the institution's continuing control makes independent access unavailable. Cost responsibility remains constrained by a defensible allocation rather than guaranteed by causation. That is a candidate repair, not an instruction to adopt my wording or a conclusion established by this critique.
