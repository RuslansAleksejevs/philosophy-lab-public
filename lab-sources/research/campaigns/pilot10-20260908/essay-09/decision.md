# Decision — essay 09

Outcome: CONTINUE. Position: TENTATIVE. Argument: REVISED. Retain final.md as the complete revised manuscript for this round; it supersedes draft.md for reading while preserving the original candidate and every original file. This decision is neither a truth certificate nor a submission, publication or competition-readiness authorization.

The separate critic's principal objections were warranted. The original draft had insufficiently distinguished a duty to justify an obstacle from a duty to provide and fund assistance; it also underestimated close rehabilitation precedents and drew a faulty diagnostic contrast. The revised essay acknowledges the Haney precedent and claims a more limited general moral explanation. It grounds the duty in the difference between imposing a legitimate qualification and retaining control over permissible means of meeting it. Access, arrangement and ordinary training costs are distinguished. First applicants receive the same basic claim under the same authority. Administrative coordination and feasibility across comparable cases are made explicit. Ortmann's citation is corrected to §6.3.

The retained contribution is a bounded reopening principle: significant conditional restrictive authority over an interest to which people have a claim on justifiable terms cannot retain unjustified institutional obstacles to satisfying or demonstrating its terminating criterion. If the institution reasonably retains control that forecloses independent access, it owes reasonable arrangement of a substitute unless safety, feasibility or fair allocation justify withholding it. This does not establish universal subsidy of ordinary qualification costs.

Reasons to retain the candidate: the distinction between accurate verdicts and obligations concerning permissible routes is coherent and practically discriminating; the revision answers concrete allocation and scope cases rather than treating every adverse decision as requiring training. The original suspension can remain justified, and refusal of reopening can be the first wrong.

Reasons for caution: the general moral bridge remains contestable, and the result may be an application of existing proportionality, rehabilitation and recourse arguments rather than a major original contribution. The text assumes independent standing to fair access, does not settle general distributive justice, and relies on stipulated cases. Relevant primary works were read selectively; newly accessing later de Bruin passages does not imply an exhaustive novelty audit. There is no second independent review of final.md.

Next step: preserve for later user selection. If selected, obtain a further isolated critique of the authority-based moral bridge and compare directly against broader rehabilitation, administrative-justice and recourse accounts. Reopen or set aside if a prior account already provides the same general justification and cost boundary, or if that bridge cannot support arranging duties without additional assumptions that do all of the work.
