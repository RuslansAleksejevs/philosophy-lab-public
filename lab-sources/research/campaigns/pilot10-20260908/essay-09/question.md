# Question — essay 09

When an institution's initially defeasible judgment helps produce the very behavior later cited to confirm that judgment, what can the resulting evidence justify? In particular, can an accurate assessment nevertheless fail to justify the continuation of the treatment that made it accurate?

Provisional approach: distinguish evidence that a present condition obtains, evidence about the warrant for an earlier intervention, and authority to keep imposing the intervention. Investigate whether an institution owes a fair route to disconfirmation when its own intervention suppresses the possible counterevidence on which appeal depends. Avoid a blanket ban on endogenous evidence: teaching, rehabilitation and legitimate protective intervention often make good evidence possible. The hoped-for contribution is a non-circular account of reassessment that identifies when evidential adequacy and institutional answerability come apart.
