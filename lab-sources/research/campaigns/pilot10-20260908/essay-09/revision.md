## Accepted criticisms and concrete changes

1. **Close prior rehabilitation argument.** Accepted. The author independently read the relevant official Haney judgment, especially §§36–39 and 60. Final §2 directly acknowledges that reasonable enabling opportunities, development versus demonstration, and justified continued exclusion alongside an enabling duty are already present in a historical institutional argument. The final contribution is a proposed general explanation and boundary, not discovery of this coexistence. The limited-mandate objection now receives an explicit response in §3; §5 specifies why the analogy extends selectively.

2. **Justification does not automatically entail positive provision.** Accepted. The original account moved too quickly between removing a barrier, arranging a route and paying all associated costs. Final §3 supplies an explicit moral bridge: authority to enforce an important conditional restriction does not include discretionary authority to foreclose permissible means of satisfying its terminating condition. Administrative cooperation and reasonable arrangement are defended as conditions on continuing to retain that control. This remains a substantive moral premise, not a logical consequence of bare descriptive facts.

3. **Payment and provision differ.** Accepted, with an actual narrowing. Final §4 distinguishes access, arrangement and payment. It answers the fair-fee/scarce-grant case: reopening alone does not give Mara full subsidy, a transfer from a separate assistance fund, or priority over equally placed applicants. The authority can discharge the obligation attributable to its eligibility barrier while general distributive needs remain. Ordinary expenses of qualification are not all costs of repairing an institution's wrong. Necessary administrative work cannot be replaced by purely nominal permission.

4. **First applicants and the restriction boundary.** Accepted; the author chose the broader implication for applicants rather than privileging prior membership. Final §5 grants Nara the same basic claim when she falls under the same authority and obstruction. Prior certification can support additional reliance claims but is not the source of the basic duty. Scope is tied to significant authority over a substantial interest with an independently grounded claim to fair access. A voluntary chess club ordinarily lacks the relevant jurisdiction and obstruction of independent preparation. Labels such as benefit denial and private club do not settle the moral facts.

5. **Defective diagnostic test.** Accepted. The counterfactual-person test was removed. The overbroad claim that a fact shared between options cannot bear on their comparative value was also removed. Final §6 instead compares specified policies. Present deficit matters when connected to evidence about training risks, prospects, costs or supervision; fair queues can be relevant regardless of likely success.

6. **Fragmented responsibility and policy-level feasibility.** Accepted. Final §5 distinguishes referral and pursuit by review offices, assessment by bodies controlling relevant rules, and coordination responsibility at the governing level. A clerk's physical capacity is not administrative authority. Final §6 evaluates a fair policy for similarly situated people, including scheduling, independent providers and reasonable capacity planning, rather than one exceptional place.

7. **Source locator and safety comparison.** Accepted. Ortmann's three-option argument is cited at §6.3, verified by the author. The unsupported suggestion that suspension plus preparation is safer for the public than continued exclusion has been replaced with a comparison allowing equivalent protection and lower burdens on the restricted person.

## Qualifications and departures from proposed repairs

- The general duty is not grounded exclusively in prior suspension; the critic's first-applicant comparison supports a broader scope when the same morally significant authority is present.
- The final does not claim a blanket duty to restore a former person's abilities or subsidize all reasonable training. That stronger interpretation was withdrawn.
- The author did not introduce a comprehensive theory of social funding or collective agency. It gives a definite limited answer to the fee case and an allocation of coordination responsibilities, while leaving wider distribution open.
- The main text uses the directly consulted Haney judgment as its close antecedent. The author's fresh ECtHR PDF attempts failed; it does not cite James as independently reread or make claims about current law. The critic's earlier successful access remains separately preserved.
- The revised text does not present its generalization as established by judicial authority. The court's domain-specific reasoning identifies prior art; the moral extension is separately argued.

## Remaining limitations and reopening conditions

The contribution is narrower than the initial draft implied. Its importance depends on whether the condition on restrictive authority yields more than a useful specification of proportionality and recourse. The moral bridge, the independently grounded standing to fair access, and reasonable institutional provision remain contestable. Cost allocation is only partly determined.
