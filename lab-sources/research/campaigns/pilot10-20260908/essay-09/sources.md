# Sources and contribution boundary — essay 09

Status: TENTATIVE. Exact retrieval notes and URLs are in sources/readings.md, part of this source packet. The sources stage does not establish originality, truth, or complete literature coverage.

## Refined question

Can an institution owe a person an opportunity to change an accurately assessed condition when its own initially justified restriction helps prevent that change? The target is a restriction that preserves the factual basis of its own renewal. The difficult case grants both the original warrant and the accuracy of subsequent assessments. A bare criticism of false prejudice or misleading validation does not resolve it.

## Intended contribution after research

Develop a defeasible institutional duty of reopening: where an institution administers a continuing conditional restriction, materially blocks ordinary routes to satisfying its release criterion, and controls a proportionate means of reopening such a route, it must provide that route or justify refusal on grounds that independently address the proposed intervention. The present disqualifying condition alone cannot decide whether to provide a means of overcoming it. This can call for restoration of opportunity or competence, not just another test. The institution need not pretend the person is currently qualified or expose others to an unacceptable risk.

The route must respect a legitimate criterion, offer a reasonable prospect rather than a guaranteed favorable verdict, and address significant institutionally imposed obstacles. The normative foundation will be bounded authority and non-evasion of the burden of continued restriction, supported by a comparison of feasible courses of action over time. The essay must defend these premises rather than treating “non-circularity” as a magic source of duties.

## Relation to primary literature

- Merton (1948) already explains induced confirmation and institutional exclusion. Not the proposed discovery.
- King and Mertens (2023) already explain reflexivity, absent error signals, and accountability problems, including aware participants. Not the proposed discovery.
- De Bruin (2025) explicitly covers credibility deficits made accurate; only his full abstract and an introductory excerpt were accessible. Full novelty comparison remains incomplete.
- Venkatasubramanian and Alfano (2020) already ground recourse in agency, address individual burdens and institutional reform. The proposed addition is a particular source and allocation of a duty when the restricting body closes its own exit conditions, even if its assessments contain no mistake.
- Vredenburgh (2022) supplies a relevant account of informed self-advocacy and distinguishes information from material opportunity. The proposed result is compatible with and more specific than a general material complement.
- Ortmann (2025) treats the unavoidable effects of continued or altered model use. The proposed principle will compare admissible alternatives, without assuming a causally pristine baseline.

## Main risks to test in the draft and separate critique

1. The proposed duty may simply be an application of familiar proportionality or recourse, rather than a significant new contribution. State a limited claim: a diagnostic principle and an argument fixing its distinctive trigger, burden bearer and practical object.
2. Present accuracy might justify immediate restriction and long-term continuation if restoration is unsafe, futile or disproportionate. Preserve the distinction between present permission and provision of a route.
3. Causation alone does not entail a duty to compensate. Ground the duty in an ongoing restrictive authority with control over the relevant exit, not in a general causal principle.
4. The original judgment need not have been false; restoration must not be sold as discovering an innocent pre-institutional self.
5. Scarce resources, other claimants, voluntary refusal and uncertainty can defeat or qualify a specific reopening demand. Explain what independent justification means rather than granting automatic priority.
