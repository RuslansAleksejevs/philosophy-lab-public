## Accepted substantive criticisms

1. **Merits assessment does not exhaust agency in release.** Accepted. The former rule was too broad if it treated every operative block to complaint-related reasoning as defeating release. The final essay explicitly contrasts Merits Block and Settlement Block, and develops the critic's Unconditional Release and delegated-settlement objections. An impaired assessment of excuses can coexist with competent disposition of an acknowledged claim. The new controlling condition concerns the route that actually governs the settlement, not mere causal influence or actual operation anywhere in deliberation.

2. **Wrongful origin and present defect are different.** Accepted. The core defect is a failure of the holder's exercise of the relevant power. It persists if an unrelated party or an unforeseeable side effect produces the same severe override. Conversely, a wrongdoer may receive a competent generous release. The clinic's own wrongdoing supplies additional wrongs and possible repair duties, not a general disability to benefit from forgiveness. Reliance and blame are separated from actual efficacy.

3. **Present refusal needs two versions.** Accepted. The final manuscript separately states (a) competent refusal of reversal can coexist with defective release, and (b) if present decisional control is also impaired, the weaker conclusion is that the impairment does not by itself authorize further intervention. It no longer presents the latter as a demonstrated valid veto.

4. **Prior permission is not automatically advance waiver.** Accepted. Permission for Closing, advance release of the £1,000 claim and delegation of settlement are explicitly distinguished. The earlier broad exception has been qualified. Valid prior permission to induce an impairment alone need not make a later declaration effective.

5. **Repair and cessation are distinct.** Accepted. The main construction now specifies a durable effect of a completed procedure. A separate paragraph distinguishes stopping continuing unauthorized suppression from undertaking a new corrective operation. Restoring settlement control while retaining Opening is presented as a candidate remedy if appropriately authorized and proportionate; it is neither automatically required by the refund duty nor automatically permissible.

6. **Choose one claim and avoid normative conflations.** Accepted. The central claim is a contractually stipulated £1,000 refund for an unauthorized addition, acknowledged by the clinic. The question is full discharge of that obligation. Forgiveness, blame, original permissibility and other compensation duties are expressly left separate. This prevents changes in the object of ratification from doing unacknowledged argumentative work.

7. **Engage close frameworks and reduce novelty claims.** Accepted. Independently read the specific Enoch 2024 and Bajaj–Tomlin 2024 passages suggested by the critic; source-note supplement was archived before final drafting. Their accounts are treated as compatible frameworks, not as defeated rivals. The final distinction identifies a concrete invalid inference from inability to adjudicate to inability to release. The accomplishment claimed is a bounded refinement and application; originality of a new fundamental principle is not claimed.

## Qualifications and decisions not to extend the revision

The critic suggested a list of possible minimal capacities for release. I adopted a narrower two-part necessary condition—adequate understanding of the disposition and a governing route through deliberation, policy or authorized delegation—rather than announce a complete sufficient test. Mixed/overdetermined control remains unresolved. No extra empirical or clinical claim was added. The final does not settle the metaphysics of retrospective permissibility, compare every autonomy theory, or claim full coverage of inaccessible literature. These are scope decisions, not dismissals of objections as unimportant.

## What remains vulnerable

The main severe case is intentionally clear and artificial; it does not provide a clinical diagnostic criterion. The boundary between a newly governing commitment and displaced control needs further analysis in mixed cases. The account depends on a minimal agency condition for full exercise of a normative power, defended by the function of releasable claims and contrasts, rather than a complete theory of free agency. The final revision has not received a second fresh critique. Full primary Enoch 2020, Magnusson, Kasachkoff and Christman remain reading gaps. Independent judgement of significance and originality is still needed.
