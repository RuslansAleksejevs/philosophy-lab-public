# Research question — essay 03

Can a person's later, informed endorsement of an intervention authorize that intervention retrospectively when the intervention itself made rejection psychologically unavailable?

I will investigate whether there is a distinct condition on the justificatory use of retrospective endorsement: a procedure may not treat later approval as settling its permissibility when the procedure disables the subject's ability to withhold that approval. The question concerns the authority of ratification, not whether every causally shaped preference is inauthentic or whether beneficial preference change is generally wrong.

Initial hypothesis (EXPLORING; argument DRAFT): ratification can supply a reason that a prior intervention respected the person only insofar as negative ratification remained an effective possibility. If an intervention removes the capacity to sustain objections specifically to that intervention, subsequent sincere, informed endorsement may bear on welfare and present wishes while failing to exercise the normative power needed to validate the original act. The project will test whether this separates a procedural defect in ratification from familiar historical autonomy, manipulation and adaptive-preference accounts; its originality is unestablished.

Planned stress tests: ordinary education and psychotherapy; voluntarily chosen preference change; global versus selective loss of critical capacities; present requests by a person whose preferences were altered; independent grounds for justified emergency intervention. The intended contribution, if defensible, is a limited argument about what later approval can authorize, rather than a theory of authentic desires or a general prohibition of transformative interventions.
