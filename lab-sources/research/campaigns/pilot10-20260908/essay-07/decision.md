## Accepted substantive criticisms

1. **Ambiguity about burdens and remedy.** Accepted. The final separately identifies the practical charge, the claimant's production requirement for reopening historical facts, and institutional responsibility for justifying continuation. The primary obligation is admission and adequate assessment of a procedural challenge to the terms of continuation. It is not an automatic shift in persuasion, hearing format or historical verdict. A prospectively justified closure/finality package expressly discharges the obligation if still applicable.

2. **Missing normative premise and staircase analogy.** Accepted. The analogy has been removed. The final restricts the argument to cases where effective correction is part of the adequate justification for continuation. It defends the relevance of the safeguard by distinguishing initial reliability from the severity, duration and distribution of possible wrongful treatment. An institution's mere declaration that review matters is not enough: the dependence concerns adequate normative grounds. This narrowing sacrifices the draft's broader thesis rather than hiding the critic's objection.

3. **New-evidence confound.** Accepted. The opening hypothetical no longer introduces a new interpretive method. It compares Limited Finality, Continuing Review and Unreviewable Continuation and stipulates that deletion does not change present confidence in the retained report. Future opportunities for correction differ, and this difference is explicitly acknowledged. The numerical Bayesian example has been cut; the epistemic point remains a short distinction in section 5.

4. **Control, forced deletion, fire and claimant-requested deletion.** Accepted. Present responsibility follows from continuing power and dependence on the lost safeguard, not from operating the delete command. Deliberate closure adds responsibility for advance planning and the joint package when choices are available. Required deletion and accidental loss can produce the same present need for assessment. An informed claimant's participation can help justify a finite settlement, without automatically waiving every future claim.

5. **Close legal antecedents.** Accepted after direct author retrieval. The final acknowledges the close antecedence of Blackmun's Youngblood dissent and Stevens's concurrence, carefully separating them from the holding. It does not claim novelty for replacing impossible proof of contents with a showing concerning evidence type and substitutes. Elfarissi is used to distinguish production about good/bad faith from the final's procedural claim about continuation. Rule 37(e) is removed from the final bibliography because the closer antecedents do the needed work; its earlier use remains in the retained draft and source archive.

6. **Grounding procedural value.** Accepted in bounded form. Dworkin's response to Bone was directly read at pp. 1084–1085. The final acknowledges both rights-based and instrumental grounds, and makes a conditional argument compatible with either. It does not claim to solve the general nature of procedural rights or to have read the older Principle, Policy, Procedure.

7. **Sophia counterexample.** Accepted. The destructive-test refutation is withdrawn. The final explicitly states that a surviving report may itself preserve part of the evidential base and distinguishes the preprint's constitutive epistemic target from the normative question defended here. The preprint remains an acknowledged close lead, not a proxy for the literature or a verified priority claim.

8. **Surviving negative label and concrete decision difference.** Accepted. Section 4 develops an honestly retained classification that preserves support for the official conclusion while losing an effective means of correction. It supplies a case in which adequate reasons support a three-year final charge but not indefinite continuation: once the correction-dependent justification fails, a paid three-year charge must cease. This conclusion is conditional on the stated normative facts and does not infer historical innocence.

## Limits and choices not adopted

The final does not defend a universal right to factual reconsideration, a requirement to preserve all evidence, or a categorical claim that permissible destruction destroys authority. Nor does it adopt automatic exoneration as a response. These broader possibilities are not established by the available argument. The critic's proposed narrower route was accepted because it addresses the strongest objection, not because separate criticism certifies the resulting thesis.

The final remains less ambitious than the draft's opening. Its contribution is the distinction between a factual challenge and a procedural challenge to an unacknowledged conversion into finality, and the related joint assessment of record lifetimes and the continuing uses of classifications. This is an extension and clarification of established concerns; originality remains uncertain. The manuscript's concrete consequence depends on a case-level judgement about which final term is justified, rather than deriving a universal deadline.
