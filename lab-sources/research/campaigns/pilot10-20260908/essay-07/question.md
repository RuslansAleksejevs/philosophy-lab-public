# Question: justified decisions and the future possibility of correction

Status: EXPLORING. Argument status: DRAFT.

When an institution is presently justified in rejecting a challenge to its decision, may it destroy or make inaccessible the evidence by which that rejection might later be overturned? The central question is whether current justified belief can authorize the removal of its own future defeaters when others remain subject to the resulting decision.

Provisional thesis to investigate: a continuing claim to authority incurs a conditional duty to preserve a feasible route of correction, even when the verdict is currently epistemically justified. This duty is grounded in the relation between the authority and those subject to it, rather than in an unrestricted duty to maximize future knowledge. A decision-maker cannot use the lack of surviving counterevidence as an additional vindication when its own discretionary choice produced that lack. The essay will ask when this restriction merely concerns later justification and when it creates an earlier duty of preservation.

Proposed original work: distinguish a rational present verdict from an entitlement to shape the future evidential situation; develop paired cases which hold present evidence and expected accuracy fixed while changing who can extinguish a challenge; formulate and defend a limited principle connecting continuing authority, control over evidential access, and future standing to contest. Check whether this is already captured by work on epistemic injustice, epistemic paternalism, democratic contestability, and epistemic duties to future inquirers.

Serious obstacles: the principle might merely restate procedural fairness; preserving all rejected challenges can be expensive or harmful; every inquiry destroys some information; no known defeater need exist; present authorities cannot guarantee rational future review; privacy and emancipatory forgetting can justify destruction. These objections will determine the scope rather than be treated as exceptions after the argument.

Examples will be philosophical hypotheticals, not claims about law or actual institutions. No conclusion of originality, truth, or contest readiness is established by this question selection.
