# Sources and novelty investigation

Status: TENTATIVE. Source accuracy and philosophical conclusions are assessed separately.

The question was selected before this search. Searches covered contestatory democracy; archives and epistemic injustice; evidence preservation/destruction and epistemic duties; social evidence tampering; transparency as surveillance; forgetting and agency; and spoliation, evidential closure, and burdens of proof. The sequence found a very close June 2026 preprint, which rules out presenting the broad auditability thesis as an unanticipated contribution.

The question is retained, but the intended contribution is narrower and partly critical: justified evidential closure need not extinguish epistemic or institutional authority. It can instead require changing who must justify the continuation of a burden. The central proposed distinction is between a permissible ending of inquiry, a rational surviving belief, and a legitimate requirement that the burdened person produce evidence the institution has made unavailable. Possible remedies include a preserved route of challenge, a fresh justification of the continuing burden, or termination of its continuing effect. None entails inferring guilt from innocent destruction. This exact normative argument remains to be developed, and its originality remains uncertain, particularly relative to evidence law and procedural justice.

# Philip Pettit (2005)
Two-dimensional Democracy, National and International. IILJ Working Paper 2005/8, History and Theory of International Law Series. Version read: working paper, not the later journal pagination.
URL: https://iilj.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/08/Petit-Two-Dimensional-Democracy-National-and-International-2005.pdf
Read: title pages; section 2, “The editorial aspect,” printed pp. 8–13, especially pp. 9 and 12. Web extraction PDF pages 9–14.
Finding: contestatory power includes available appeals and institutional arrangements that anticipate complaints; the reasons supporting decisions must be publicly assessable. Preservation alone is therefore not a new theory of contestability.
Short excerpt, printed p. 9: “put on record the reasons allegedly grounding their choices.”

# Karl Landström (2021)
Archives, Epistemic Injustice and Knowing the Past. Ethics and Social Welfare 15(4): 379–394. DOI: 10.1080/17496535.2021.1961004.
URL: https://irep.ntu.ac.uk/id/eprint/48233/1/1726254_Landstrom.pdf
Read: “Archives”; “Archives and epistemic injustice”; discussion of migrated archives and denial; “Backward-looking epistemic responsibility as the way forward.” PDF printed pagination is an early-publication 1–16 layout.
Finding: archival selection and destruction can perpetuate epistemic injustice and obstruct redress; archives themselves need critical interpretation. This defeats novelty for the broad observation that lost records undermine correction.
Short excerpt, printed p. 8: “destruction and sanitisation of the archival material.”

# Keith Raymond Harris (2024)
Social Evidence Tampering and the Epistemology of Content Moderation. Topoi 43: 1421–1431. DOI: 10.1007/s11245-024-10101-3.
URL: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11245-024-10101-3
Read: publisher full-text §§3–6, especially §5; metadata. Section numbering in introductory cross-references is inconsistent, so use displayed heading “5 Some Epistemic Consequences of the Removal of Misinformation.”
Finding: filtering may improve evidence but adds dependence on its controllers; manufactured consensus can lose evidential force. The essay must distinguish this epistemic issue from its normative allocation of review burdens.
Excerpt, §5: “deliverers, recipients, and third-party controllers of information.”

# C. Thi Nguyen (2022; first online 2021)
Transparency is Surveillance. Philosophy and Phenomenological Research 105(2): 331–361. DOI: 10.1111/phpr.12823.
URL read: https://r.jordan.im/download/epistemology/nguyen2022.pdf
Publisher metadata: https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/pdf/10.1111/phpr.12823
Read: abstract, early-view pp. 5–6, 21–22 and 24–27, especially §6. The accessible PDF uses pp. 1–31; the final journal year/pages were separately checked.
Finding: public demands for intelligibility can distort expert and intimate reasons; expert review reduces but does not eliminate this cost. Preserve a competent route of challenge without requiring all reasoning to be publicly legible.
Excerpt, early-view p. 26: “a more constrained form of surveillance, by relevant experts.”

# Lowry Pressly (2024)
The Right to Be Forgotten and the Value of an Open Future. Ethics 135(1). DOI: 10.1086/731431. Page span not verified; section locators used instead.
URL: https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/full/10.1086/731431
Read: publisher full-text introduction, §§I, III, IV and V (including public-interest limits and delisting); conclusion.
Finding: limiting searchable access to personal history can support confidence in agency and the possibility of new relationships. This provides a serious objection to indiscriminate retention and an opening for distinguishing public access from review access.
Excerpt, §III: “the conditions necessary for such confidence to be reasonably available.”

# Franny Philos Sophia (2026)
The Epistemic Discontinuity: Why Record Tampering Dissolves the Ground of Delegated Authority. Preprint, June 2026; ResearchGate upload June 28. Publication and peer review status beyond this upload unverified.

# Federal Rules of Civil Procedure, Rule 37(e)
Primary rule text reproduced by Cornell Legal Information Institute; read 2026-09-08. The page identifies amendment effective December 1, 2015. No legal advice or survey of case law is intended.
URL: https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/frcp/rule_37
Read: subdivision (e), web lines 85–94.
Finding: the rule distinguishes measures addressing prejudice from specified stronger responses requiring intent to deprive; its trigger includes a failure to take reasonable preservation steps. This is an acknowledged procedural analogue, not evidence of originality. The philosophical argument will concern closure that was permissible when chosen.
