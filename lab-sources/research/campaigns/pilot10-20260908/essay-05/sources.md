# Source and novelty stage

Status: TENTATIVE. Question retained, contribution narrowed.

Searches on 2026-09-08 combined culpable ignorance/tracing, evidential damage, uncertainty and burden of proof, institutional succession/reparations, and epistemic reparations. Specific counter-searches combined evidential damage or burden shifting with innocent parties, third parties, and rival claimants. Searches revealed substantial prior work. They do not establish exhaustive coverage or uniqueness.

The proposed essay will not claim novelty for liability arising from destroyed evidence, burden shifting, the separation of institutional duties from personal blame, or the relational character of epistemic repair. Its proposed contribution is a constraint on the scope of reparative presumptions: a reason to relax a claimant's proof burden against a responsible institution does not, without further justification, provide a reason to downgrade a competing innocent claimant's standing or certify the repaired claim as historically true. The same constraint can require the institution to surrender unilateral closure while withholding a verdict between claimants. This synthesis and its supporting paired cases require independent criticism; novelty remains unconfirmed.

The essay will distinguish what successors may believe, what they are blameworthy for, what the institution may decide, and against whom a remedial presumption may be used. It will defend a narrow dependency condition for a proof requirement, then apply it to ordinary institutional resources and an indivisible asset claimed by more than one person. The no-export constraint concerns the justificatory force of evidential fault, not a prohibition on every indirect material cost to innocents.

No claim about operative law, a named historical institution, or an actual restitution controversy is planned. The cases are explicitly hypothetical. No novel proposition is attributed to any source below. All philosophical development beyond the attributed literature is the author's proposed argument.

## Porat and Stein
Ariel Porat and Alex Stein, “Liability for Uncertainty: Making Evidential Damage Actionable,” Cardozo Law Review 18 (1997): 1891 onward. Consulted version: 1999 Berkeley seminar/book-chapter draft, deposited June 29, 1999, introductory letter dated March 25. Repository metadata lists Porat; the manuscript names both authors. Bibliography will cite the accessed 1999 version rather than silently equating it with the published article.
URL: https://escholarship.org/content/qt2db3b43m/qt2db3b43m.pdf
Read: front matter; passages at draft pp. 21–23, 43–46, 50, 60, 71–77; especially §VI(b)(1–2). The PDF has additional preliminary pages: use printed draft pagination, not viewer index.
Exact excerpt, draft p. 50: “The latter should not be allowed to derive any benefit from his own wrong.”
Finding: evidential damage, burden reversal, corrective justice and error-risk allocation are established proposals. Institutional defendants and limits of burden reversal are already discussed. These cannot be claimed as this essay's discoveries.

## Rosen
Gideon Rosen, “Responsibility and Moral Ignorance,” notes for ODFF, September 1999.
URL: https://www.princeton.edu/~grosen/ODFF/ODFF.html
Read: introduction and §III, especially paragraphs about Ed's trespass and derivative responsibility. These are preliminary notes, not the 2004 publication.
Exact excerpt: “You are morally responsible for your ignorance only if it derives from a failure to do what is morally required” (excerpt stops mid-sentence).
Finding: personal culpability for ignorance turns on the agent's earlier conduct under the tracing proposal. No claim that this notes-version is established consensus.

## Harman
Elizabeth Harman, “Does Moral Ignorance Exculpate?” Ratio 24(4) (2011): 443–468.
URL: https://www.princeton.edu/~eharman/DoesMoralIgnoranceExculpate.pdf
Read: abstract; §§1–2, pp. 443–453; extracted later objection at p. 461 was noticed but not studied as a full argument.
Exact excerpt, abstract: “Non-moral ignorance can exculpate”.
Finding: disputes sweeping exculpation from moral ignorance. The essay will stipulate noncomplicit successors and avoid inferring innocence simply from conscientious belief.
