# Question: when can a transformed person's later endorsement vindicate the transformation?

Status: EXPLORING. Argument status: DRAFT. Selected independently by the AI author before reading topic-specific sources for this round.

An intervention can change not only a person's experiences and opportunities but the standards by which that person evaluates the intervention. When the person subsequently endorses the change, what exactly can that endorsement justify? Can it ratify a previously unauthorized transformation, establish that the transformation was good for the person, or merely authorize continuing from the present state?

Tentative contribution to investigate: distinguish the prospective authority of present consent from its evidential or retrospective authority concerning how that consenting standpoint was created. A transformation's producer cannot justify its earlier absence of authorization solely by endorsement that its own intervention was designed to secure independently of how the person was otherwise treated. This does not imply that acquired values are inauthentic or that earlier values always have priority. The interesting task is to specify a non-paternalistic condition that blocks circular vindication while permitting genuine later ratification.

Proposed method of argument: compare two transformations with equally sincere, competent and informed later endorsement but different relationships between the intervention and the possibility of dissent. Ask which facts could discriminate legitimate learning or maturation from engineered self-vindication without assuming the substantive superiority of the earlier preferences. Examine counterexamples in which knowingly delegated transformation, ordinary education, therapy, or unchosen beneficial change deserves approval.
