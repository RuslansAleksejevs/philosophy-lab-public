# Independent critique: When a Remedy Becomes Evidence

Essay: 08. Critic: `/root/critic_08`. Date: 2026-09-08 UTC. Work state recommended: REVISE. This is an AI contribution to this essay only. It does not certify truth, novelty, eligibility, or judge fidelity. The manuscript has not been edited.

## Overall assessment

The draft defends a coherent and useful distinction: an institution may create a fully real remedial entitlement without thereby establishing the historical allegation that ordinarily supports such an entitlement. Its strongest insight is that an adequate initial decision can later become misleading through changes of audience and use. The examples distinguish epistemic authority from practical closure clearly, and the treatment of selection effects, indiscriminate destruction, privacy, and third-party risk prevents several easy objections.

The central philosophical gap concerns the claimed *reparative* character of the continuing duty. The draft convincingly establishes that institutions should avoid foreseeably misleading uses of their records. It does not yet establish what additional duty follows from their earlier wrongful destruction, beyond a general obligation already generated by issuing consequential information. A second substantial problem is that preserving a limit on the institution's own findings can slide into endorsing the institution's authority to declare the historical question unresolved for everyone. A third is incomplete engagement with the directly relevant philosophical literature on epistemic reparation. These are repairable vulnerabilities, but they affect the contribution, not merely presentation.

## Strongest reconstruction

1. By requiring evidence that it exclusively controls, the fund undertakes a directed obligation to preserve a claimant's means of substantiating her claim.
2. Its breach impairs both adjudication and the claimant's ability to contest subsequent representations of the dispute.
3. An appropriate monetary award can establish an entitlement while leaving the original allegation undetermined by the proceeding.
4. The resulting record predictably substitutes for inquiry by later recipients, and its institutional authority can amplify an inference the actual decision does not support.
5. The institution retains some control over the record's design, circulation, and correction.
6. Therefore it owes reasonable continuing measures to prevent its remedial outcome from being mistaken for an independently supported historical finding.

Premises 1–5 support a substantial communicative duty. To make the conclusion specifically reparative, the author needs a defended bridge from the impairment in premise 2 to the kind, strength, bearer, or beneficiary of the continuing obligation. The manuscript gestures toward all four without settling which supplies its distinctive result.

## 1. Make the reparative explanation do identifiable work

Consider two funds with identical present evidence, identical awards, and identical powers over their records. One deliberately deleted the photographs; the other lost them to an unforeseeable disaster despite adequate precautions. Both foresee that a payment code will be used as a factual finding. Both should attach the same qualification. Institutional succession does not separate the cases either: an innocent successor inherits ordinary communicative responsibilities in both.

The draft acknowledges this objection in its last substantive paragraph, but saying that one duty is additionally owed to Mara largely labels the difference. Why does a claimant lack comparable directed standing against an innocent institution that negligently misrepresents her through its current publication? And if she possesses that standing in both cases, what does the original breach add?

There are two defensible ways forward. The modest route is to claim a new application and explanation of familiar duties, explicitly declining to establish a new class of required action. The stronger route is to defend a residual duty to supply a substitute for a claimant's impaired capacity to contest the record, even after the issuer has met ordinary standards for accurate initial communication.

For the stronger route, use a harder case. The fund gives a receiving office an intelligible, appropriately qualified record. Years later the recipient's database migration drops the explanation and creates a damaging classification. The fund did not cause that later error and no longer controls the recipient, but can cheaply authenticate the original basis and fund a correction request. Mara cannot afford to reconstruct it. Does the fund's original wrongful loss give her a claim to this assistance that would not follow merely from its already fulfilled transmission obligations? Defend an answer, including reasonable causal scope and costs. If the answer is yes, the draft's restriction to authorized or controllable reuse may be too narrow. If no, explain what is philosophically distinctive about the reparative account within that restriction.

A possible bridge, offered for evaluation rather than automatic adoption, is: when a breach foreseeably reduces someone's capacity to resist consequential misdescription, a responsible agent owes proportionate substitute support for that capacity, where doing so does not violate others' rights. This would explain why writing one accurate letter may fail to finish the repair, while limiting the duty to identifiable disadvantages attributable to the breach. It still needs defense; the thought that wrongdoers should bear every later correction cost would be too broad.

## 2. Preserve the decision's limits without silencing the claimant

The three propositions are correctly separated. But the draft sometimes moves from “the fund did not establish the allegation” to “the historical question remains unanswered.” Those are different claims. Mara may have firsthand knowledge; neighbors may have testimony; an outside investigator may reasonably credit evidence that the fund's formal photograph requirement excludes. An institution's evidential rules do not themselves determine what anyone can know.

The opening can stipulate that all reasonable substitute inquiries have been exhausted. It should not achieve uncertainty simply by equating missing official photographs with the absence of adequate evidence. The strongest potential counterexample is an institution that destroyed corroboration, then insists on marking every later reference “unresolved” despite credible claimant testimony. Such a qualifier could perpetuate the epistemic subordination the proposed repair is supposed to undo.

Revise the principle to preserve the scope of *this determination* and the grounds on which *its issuer* acted. It should not confer a continuing monopoly on declaring the allegation epistemically open. A qualification such as “this payment decision did not determine contractor liability” can be correct even where “there is no adequate basis for believing the contractor responsible” is false. Later evidence includes testimony and changed assessments of existing evidence, not only replacement photographs. The accompanying record should neither certify Mara's account through the payment nor discredit it through the lack of certification.

This revision would also sharpen the contribution: a person may be entitled to institutional acknowledgment of the limits of an official determination without being entitled to make everyone suspend judgment about the underlying event. The duty protects the boundary of institutional authority, while leaving ordinary inquiry and testimonial agency intact.

## 3. Engage the actual difficult case of binding factual determinations

Nance is accurately identified as a close antecedent, but his proposal does more than separate sanctions from factual findings. He favors issue preclusion that directs facts to be taken as true or false within the litigation, and he explicitly notices downstream malpractice effects. His criticism of concealing evidence-management choices is at pp.1113–1114. This makes him a source of pressure as well as support. [Nance, pp.1110–1114](https://www.bu.edu/law/journals-archive/bulr/documents/nance.pdf).

The manuscript's entitlement example makes finality comparatively easy: everyone can agree that money is owed while setting aside history. A harder case arises when a remedy properly requires an institution to treat a factual issue as fixed for defined procedural purposes. A later benefits office might be obliged to honor that determination without independently believing the historical allegation. This is permissible normative reliance on a binding act, and need not be a disguised evidential inference.

Distinguish three later uses: evidence about what happened; recognition of a newly created entitlement; and application of a legitimately binding procedural determination. The third is not always reducible to the second, especially where a sanction precludes contesting a particular issue. Explain whether the proposed duty allows the factual issue to remain binding for specified decisions while requiring its remedial basis to remain visible outside that scope. Otherwise, describing the requirement as a constraint on “permissible inferences” risks condemning warranted procedural reliance.

The audience test should therefore specify a *historical evidential inference*, not any conclusion a fully informed recipient would assess differently. The draft is right that normative power cannot make a contractor have damaged a house; it also needs to explain what normative power can legitimately settle about how that allegation may be used.

## 4. Separate the claimant's repair from third parties' protection

The principle says the institution owes “those affected” continuing measures, whereas the directed preservation undertaking in the argument is owed to Mara. The contractor's protection against unsupported blame is plausible, but the same derivation does not automatically establish a reparative debt to the contractor. Perhaps the fund's preservation duty also protected contractors. If so, say and defend this. Otherwise, distinguish Mara-directed repair from independent duties of fair and accurate treatment toward the contractor and later recipients.

This matters for the suggested possibility of a settlement releasing some continuing duties. Mara may release a claim to individualized assistance, but she cannot release another person's claim against being misleadingly accused or authorize misleading testimony to third parties. Specify which obligations are releasable. The distinction prevents the attractive bilateral settlement discussion from quietly erasing the essay's two-sided protection.

## 5. Clarify the role of a completed remedy

The announced principle applies when a *remedy* depends on a wrongfully created deficit. Yet the main rationale applies even if the institution refuses to offer any remedy and circulates a bare denial. Conversely, in the unsuccessful-claim example, the denial code is not itself the compensation for lost evidence.

This is not a logical counterexample to a sufficient condition. It is a structural ambiguity about what gives rise to the obligation. Clarify that wrongful impairment plus consequential institutional representation grounds the duty, and that its survival after fair payment is the surprising application. Alternatively, explicitly limit the thesis to the content required of complete reparation and explain why a representation of the denied underlying claim counts among the relevant outputs. Avoid suggesting that paying compensation is what first creates this obligation.

## Scholarship and originality

The draft responsibly disclaims discovery of evidential-damage remedies and the belief/action distinction. Its literature search nevertheless misses a directly relevant philosophical conversation.

Flowerree argues that successful epistemic reparation requires audience uptake and a reparative response, so merely supplying information is insufficient. This directly challenges any broad claim that repair's dependence on subsequent social reception is new. The discussion read is §§1–3 of “Epistemic reparations as social epistemic achievement,” Philosophical Studies 183 (2026): 839–856, online 21 November 2025. [Flowerree](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11098-025-02452-3).

Altanian argues that epistemic reparation must address harmful epistemic practices and recognize victims as contributors, beyond giving them information. This is relevant to both the continuing-duty thesis and the danger of an institution preserving its own privileged uncertainty. See especially §4 of “Rethinking the right to know and the case for restorative epistemic reparation,” Journal of Social Philosophy 55(4) (2024): 728–745, first online 2022. [Altanian](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/josp.12492).

These works concern different central cases and do not, on my reading, duplicate the essay's precise account of remedial outcomes as misleading factual proxies. The author should situate the proposal within that conversation, not claim to overturn it. A promising narrower result concerns duties where the institution cannot now warrant the original historical finding, yet can warrant the evidential breach, the entitlement, and the limits of its own procedure. The contribution would explain the particular form that public repair should take under that combination. Do not redescribe all epistemic-reparation scholarship as requiring full reconstruction of every historical fact; the checked texts do not warrant that generalization.

Nissenbaum's contextual-integrity framework is a secondary priority: it already makes the propriety of information flows depend on context and attends to transformations of data. A brief acknowledgment could clarify that this essay specifies a reparative ground for preserving a decision's evidential basis, rather than discovering context-sensitive information obligations. [Nissenbaum, pp.225–226 and 234–235](https://nissenbaum.tech.cornell.edu/papers/Contextual%20Integrity%20Up%20and%20Down.pdf).

Jennifer Lackey's primary paper was found as a relevant lead but its PDF returned 403 Forbidden. I have not independently read it. The author should retrieve it before attributing a detailed view to it; reports in the works above are not substitutes for primary reading. The present bounded search establishes an engagement gap, not absence or presence of exact priority elsewhere.

## Revision priorities and remaining disagreement

1. Decide whether the new result is a specifically grounded application of general communicative duties, or a stronger residual duty to support the claimant's impaired capacity to correct later representations. Defend the relevant bridge with a discriminating case.
2. Replace global declarations of historical irresolution with precise limits on what this proceeding established; explicitly allow independent testimony, inquiry, and changes in evidence.
3. Confront legitimate procedural reliance on remedially fixed factual issues, and distinguish it from epistemic reliance.
4. Add focused engagement with Flowerree and Altanian, and narrow the originality claim accordingly. Pursue Lackey only with actual access.
5. Clarify beneficiaries, release of duties, and the relation between the obligation and the existence of a completed remedy.

I endorse further development, not the manuscript's present explanation that its existing examples alone establish a distinctively reparative duty. A final version may defensibly reject the stronger residual-duty proposal, but should then present the more modest result explicitly. The result also must not give the evidence-destroying institution authority to impose universal suspension of judgment.
