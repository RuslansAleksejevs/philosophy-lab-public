# Independent critique: The Objection That Outlived Its Evidence

Critic: /root/critic_01. Date: 2026-09-08 UTC. Manuscript reviewed: draft.md only. This is an AI-originated critique for essay 01, not a verdict by an actual competition judge. I read the common neutral briefs and this essay's question, source investigation, seven source notes and draft. I did not inspect other essays, historical laboratory material or other agents' work. My independent retrieval notes are preserved in sources/critic/.

## Verdict

REVISE. The central distinction is intelligible, defensible and potentially useful: the institution's present evidence about a policy's merits does not exhaust what it owes a person who already initiated a challenge. The manuscript also does unusually well at separating that claim from blame, treating innocent successors fairly, and allowing real closure. Its main weakness is an unresolved gap between this relatively modest persistence claim and its stronger practical conclusions about reconstruction, assistance and substitute responses. The essay needs to specify what protected procedural position actually survives and why retaining it constrains institutional conduct beyond acknowledging history.

The argument should be sharpened rather than replaced. A valuable narrower result is available: established initiation creates a default assignment of the next procedural task that custodial loss cannot itself cancel; departures must be justified through a process that does not leave their adequacy wholly to the challenged institution. The second clause needs defense and institutional specification. This is a proposed repair, not a result the draft has already secured.

## Strongest reconstruction

The argument is conditional on a practice in which affected people are entitled to challenge an exercise of authority and a sufficiently supported objection initiates a response duty. It does not establish that every institution owes every person such a hearing.

1. A qualifying submission completes an initiating task and changes the parties' procedural positions, independently of whether its allegation is true.
2. The challenged decision and the responsible office persist; no adequate response or independently justified termination has yet occurred.
3. A loss of information in the institution's handling of the submission concerns its capacity to perform. It does not, merely as an informational event, revoke the claimant's prior exercise of standing.
4. Automatically resetting the claimant's task would make that exercise vulnerable to the addressee's internal arrangements, threatening effective contestation across time.
5. Therefore the institution still owes the next procedurally appropriate response, subject to feasibility and justified limitations. It cannot treat information loss alone as proof that the claimant never qualified.

This is not an argument for an adverse inference on the merits, compulsory policy suspension, indefinite preservation of every document, or guilt inherited by new officers. Those disclaimers are substantively supported by the examples, not merely appended caveats. The closed-objection case and fresh-investigation case are particularly effective because they show that historical initiation and historical discharge receive parallel treatment.

The present contribution is a temporal specification of answerability and its implications for information custody. The fact that obligations can survive forgetting, that accountability involves answering, and that archives affect political standing are background. The paper's prospects depend on making its temporal specification precise enough to add something to those familiar claims.

## 1. The gap between persistent status and a concrete surviving duty

The most consequential inference occurs in the introduction and §4: if the original answer is impossible, the institution owes the nearest adequate feasible response, potentially including reconstruction, assistance, accommodation or policy review. The fact that a performance was owed does not generally entail that whichever substitute is now feasible is owed. If a unique object innocently perishes before delivery, a duty to explain the failure might remain, but a duty to supply compensation or a substitute requires further grounds. That analogy does not settle this case; it identifies the missing premise.

The manuscript says the obligation is continuing yet allows that an explanation of comparative cost can justify no further investigation. That can be correct. But two distinct theses are then in play:

- A historical status survives: the submission was qualifying and was not answered.
- A present practical entitlement survives: someone must undertake specified further work or provide an adequate alternative.

The first does not by itself determine the second's content. If a board can meet the principle by saying that the file was lost, recovery is too costly and the policy therefore continues, the result could be practically indistinguishable from the reset the essay condemns. Calling the situation an acknowledged pending challenge rather than a failed new submission would still have expressive value, but the paper presently suggests more.

Precise revision: identify a minimal enforceable procedural difference. Possibilities include preserving the original filing date and place in a review queue, avoiding a fresh filing charge, crediting the fact that initial admissibility was satisfied, providing a reasoned disposition addressed to the actual issue, and allowing review of a claimed impossibility or disproportionate cost. Choose only those consequences the argument can defend. Then explain separately why additional remedial assistance may be due. Do not make substantive compensation follow automatically from a surviving right to an answer.

The phrase “loss alone” also needs care. The institution's strongest defense will almost always be that loss has made performance impossible or expensive. If every such downstream consequence counts as an independent reason, the principle may prohibit only the absurd claim that an event never happened. Distinguish the occurrence of loss from a demonstrable feasibility constraint, and explain what a defensible response to that constraint must preserve. The draft already recognizes resource limits; the missing element is a non-evasive criterion for their application.

## 2. Custody supports an allocation, but does not yet justify the proposed default

Section 3's first consideration describes the initiating burden as completed. A skeptic can grant completion at the original time while holding that later informational needs justify renewed duties of cooperation. The paper permits some such duties but has not shown where cooperation ends and prohibited requalification begins. Its second consideration identifies a suggestive asymmetry between durable authority and fragile contestation. But durable powers do not automatically carry every historically associated burden, especially when the present policy has an independent justification. Its third consideration supplies a good reason to prevent manipulation; it does not by itself explain every burden allocation after an uncontrollable accident.

Consider two variants. In the first, the musicians retain a legible copy that takes thirty seconds to resend, while institutional reconstruction would take weeks. In the second, their copy is also gone and reproducing the submission would require another month of unpaid work. A reasonable procedure should treat these differently. In the first, the trust can permissibly make cooperation a condition of progressing its investigation. In the second, requiring the same effort may defeat the point of having a completed initiating task. Both involve information loss in institutional custody and continuing authority.

The draft's distinction between assistance and requalification points in the right direction, but a label cannot settle these cases. Specify which completed achievements are credited while cooperation is requested, which consequences may follow unreasonable refusal, and which burdens cannot be reimposed merely to repair the office's loss. The protected achievement is not necessarily possession of all the evidence needed for adjudication.

The strongest justification available seems to be a division-of-labor argument: an institution that makes delivery to its office the recognized route to initiating review thereby assumes responsibility for managing the subsequent process, including a fair allocation of continuity risks. This is a normative premise about the terms of the practice; control over records makes it plausible but does not entail it. Defend it explicitly. A fair allocation may assign some tasks to the cheapest available source while keeping procedural management with the institution. Public resources and other claimants matter even when no incumbent deserves blame.

Also remove the explicit undertaking from one version of the opening case. The existing promise to investigate makes survival unsurprising as ordinary persistence of an undertaking. A parallel case in which an objectively qualifying submission was properly delivered, but officials made no personal promise, would test the asserted basis in institutional answerability itself. Acknowledge that an undertaking is sufficient evidence in the main example, without letting it do all the work for the broader conclusion.

## 3. The challenged institution should not be the sole judge of its own substitute

The objection to unilateral expiration applies to purported substitutes as well as to retention practices. A board could preserve every pending entry, repeatedly issue formulaic cost explanations and record each as a proportionate substitute. If that suffices, procedural continuity could coexist with indefinite avoidance of actual review. Indeed, the board would now possess an impeccably maintained record of having done so.

The draft rejects unexplained closure labels and says limitations are answerable. It should spell out what that answerability requires. The same board may be best placed to disclose the loss and initiate recovery, while a different office, forum or public process may be responsible for evaluating a disputed refusal. The duty bearer, information custodian and authority to determine discharge need not be identical.

This is a close connection with Philip Pettit's argument for contestatory institutions and reduced contestatory burdens, including hearing bodies distinct from the authorities whose decisions are challenged. See “Depoliticizing Democracy” (2004), §§2.2–2.3, printed pp. 61–63, in the [author-hosted PDF](https://ppettit.scholar.princeton.edu/document/1616). The essay need not endorse his whole institutional program, but should confront the self-assessment problem his discussion makes explicit.

Precise revision: add one paragraph specifying that a disputed substitute or termination must be assessable under publicly defensible criteria, with proportionate access to a forum not wholly controlled by the addressee when stakes warrant it. Avoid pretending that every small trust needs an elaborate tribunal. The philosophical point is that preservation of status must not become an institution's unilateral power to declare itself discharged.

## 4. Separate actual qualification, acknowledged qualification and evidence of either

The draft is commendably careful about the completely forgotten case. It does not assert that identical current evidence must produce different reasonable decisions. Its criterion of objective qualification also avoids giving obstructive officials the power to prevent obligations by refusing to acknowledge submissions.

There remains a hard middle case. A register says that a submission qualified, but the institution later discovers that a clerk habitually stamped submissions admissible without reading them. Was the initiating burden actually met? Is the acknowledgment itself a procedural commitment? May its acceptance be reopened? The fire has removed the best evidence by which to decide. The draft currently treats the register as establishing both a historical act of acceptance and the actual satisfaction of the appropriate standard, which need not coincide.

This does not refute non-regression when actual qualification is established. It does affect the practical preservation recommendation: a compact record of a decision is not automatically reliable evidence that the decision was sound, and preservation of actual standing differs from finality accorded to an official acceptance.

Precise revision: keep the main claim explicitly conditional on reliably established procedural status. Say whether bona fide correction of erroneous admission is allowed under independent standards, and distinguish that correction from reopening an admission solely because its supporting evidence was lost. If official acceptance has its own binding force, explain its source and limits instead of treating it as identical to objective qualification. The essay can bracket fully disputed histories, but should state exactly what is bracketed.

## 5. Closure is handled well, but continued authority needs a tighter scope

I agree with the treatment of an adequate answer whose record is subsequently lost: loss does not undo discharge. I also agree that a fresh investigation can answer an old question. These are reasons to retain the manuscript's symmetrical handling of history.

However, the relevant kind of continuity needs a sharper statement. The same policy text may now be supported by a different mechanism, apply to different people or no longer reproduce the alleged defect. Conversely, an unanswered demand for an account may survive repeal of a policy. Continuing the same authority is therefore a useful sufficient context for the argument, not a general necessary condition for answerability.

Precise revision: distinguish the continuing duty to account for a previous proceeding from present reasons to revise or suspend an operative policy. State that the essay addresses the strongest overlapping case: the responsible office continues a materially relevant decision toward affected claimants. Where the mechanism or decision changes, the institution can explain the change as part of a disposition; it should not need to reconstruct an obsolete dispute merely to preserve a label. The paper need not solve corporate succession or historical redress, and is right not to attempt that here.

## Significance

The essay identifies a recognizable procedural wrong that favorable outcomes and present good faith may fail to capture. It also explains why a usable record of an unfinished task can matter independently of predictive usefulness. These are real advantages. The practical significance will increase if the revision identifies a concrete entitlement protected in the fire case and shows how the proposed rule differentiates a defensible response from bureaucratic postponement. At present the main case is so favorable to an owed answer that much of the result feels built into it; the cheap-resubmission, impossible-performance and disputed-substitute cases would make the defended boundary more informative.

## Originality and engagement with literature

The source investigation honestly disclaims exhaustive novelty and treats institutional forgetting as established nearby work. That restraint should remain. But the chosen comparators leave a gap around complaint processes themselves.

Sara Ahmed's [“Opening the File”](https://feministkilljoys.com/2018/02/11/opening-the-file/) (11 February 2018), especially the paragraphs on the journeys of files and complaint folders, discusses a disappearing inquiry file and the labor of making and preserving complaints. The material was accessed through extensive indexed author text; I did not read her full book Complaint!. This is a closer precedent than a generic memory comparison. The possible difference is the essay's normative treatment of innocent loss and preserved procedural achievement; that difference should be articulated, not assumed to establish priority.

Pettit supplies a closer conceptual baseline for contestation, while archival-power scholarship supplies a baseline for selective recordkeeping. The new claim is therefore best presented as an argued temporal constraint within existing accounts of answerability. A new term for non-regression does not itself establish a new contribution. The manuscript might eventually earn a stronger originality claim by defending a precise procedural floor or a distinction among kinds of institutional discharge. My bounded search neither found nor excluded an existing equivalent.

I would shorten the memory epistemology and tracing discussion to make space for these closer comparisons and the missing argument. It is useful to distinguish the topic from epistemic justification and blame, but those contrasts cannot themselves make the institutional thesis original.

- [Huemer (1999)](https://spot.colorado.edu/~huemer/papers/memory.htm), §§2–4: the relevant acquisition/retention distinction is correctly attributed. Clarify that his dualistic account is not simple preservationism; the juxtaposition with Lackey could otherwise suggest that it is.
- [Lackey (2005)](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1933-1592.2005.tb00418.x), abstract: the modest generativity attribution is supported. Her detailed argument was not independently read.
- [Vargas (2005)](https://repository.usfca.edu/phil/3/), abstract: the description of tracing and its knowledge-condition problem is supported. No full-text assessment was possible from the surface used.
- [Bovens (2007)](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1468-0386.2007.00378.x), abstract: the actor/forum characterization is accurate. The Utrecht full-text attempt failed.
- [Forst (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1017/9781108868143.010), printed pp. 125 and 127–129: the participation/standing comparison is supported by the university-hosted text. It is a foundation, not a source establishing the essay's particular rule.
- [Basu (2022)](https://doi.org/10.1017/epi.2022.36), §1 and §§2.5–2.7: the taxonomy and privacy-related use are supported. Her explicit recognition of defeasible forgetting duties makes the draft's balancing approach reasonable.

## Revision priorities and remaining disagreement

First, distinguish surviving procedural status from the grounds for specific recovery or substitute duties. Second, define the practical floor that prevents the principle from collapsing into a requirement to acknowledge a lost file. Third, defend the institutional risk-allocation premise and test cheap cooperation against burdensome requalification. Fourth, constrain unilateral declarations of adequate substitution and clarify disputed admissions. Fifth, engage the closer Pettit and Ahmed materials while retaining modest novelty language and the successful closure discussion.

My residual disagreement is with the suggestion that the bare persistence argument already yields a general obligation to arrange an adequate substitute. It establishes at least a reason for an accountable disposition, but the type and burden of further remedial performance still require substantive justification. A revision that preserves this disagreement and argues for a bounded result would be stronger than one that merely adds examples of possible accommodations.
