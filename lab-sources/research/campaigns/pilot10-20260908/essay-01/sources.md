## Nearby work and precise proposed advance

The broad assertion that institutional forgetting undermines accountability is established nearby work; the thesis must not claim this as a discovery. The memory-justification dispute also predates this essay. Forst already connects justice with standing to demand justification. These are foundations and constraints.

The proposed contribution is the following combination, to be defended rather than presumed: once a qualifying objection is taken up under an institution's continuing authority, the claimant's completed burden of presenting it does not revert merely because information held by the institution is lost. The remaining obligation concerns a response, not an inference that the objection was true; it can pass to innocent officeholders. Its practical consequence is a duty to preserve transferable response capacity, or to bear a proportionate burden of reconstruction, rather than to keep every datum indefinitely. The strongest tests are an innocent archival accident, a fully answered objection whose record is lost, a fresh evidential basis for the policy, and costly or abusive challenges.

This is a candidate refinement at the intersection of epistemology and political accountability, with modest and uncertain originality. Searches did not establish priority or exclude an existing equivalent. Scholarship on dialogical burden-shifting, institutional succession and archives could further narrow the contribution.
