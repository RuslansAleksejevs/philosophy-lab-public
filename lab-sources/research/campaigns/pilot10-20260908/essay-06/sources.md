# Sources and novelty investigation

Status: primary-source investigation sufficient for a bounded first draft; novelty unestablished. Author selected the topic and all content independently.

## Development since the question stage

The opening suspicion about stopping procedures was too broad. Bayesian stopping-rule independence can be valid when the observed sequence is retained and the relevant independence assumptions hold. The draft will not criticize optional stopping as such.

A more specific problem emerged. A summary may preserve every present degree of confidence relevant to a settled question while destroying distinctions that determine how later evidence should revise the answer. Thus an information item can be currently irrelevant to the target proposition but relevant conditional on a foreseeable audit. The central candidate is a prospective norm of retaining such distinctions when a conclusion remains in use, the relevant challenge is foreseeable, retention is cheap, and there is no offsetting reason. This norm concerns an action at the time of compression; it does not condemn a later belief merely for failing to respond to inaccessible history.

The mathematical illustration will use two exchangeable information sources, each initially 80% likely to report a target bit perfectly and otherwise to randomize. One source is selected at random. A positive report supports the target with probability 0.9 regardless of source identity. The identity can therefore be discarded without changing present confidence. If an anticipated audit later reveals which sources were reliable, the lost identity matters. No current confidence distortion, wishful belief, or pre-existing unheeded defeater is needed.

This was not an exhaustive systematic review. Bayesian conditional independence, statistical sufficiency, the value of retained information, the need for richer states in belief revision, and the distinction between belief evaluation and harmful forgetting are established ideas. None is claimed as a discovery. The candidate contribution is the conjunction of a precise future-revision criterion with a defended, limited norm for the maintenance of settled conclusions: current evidential sufficiency does not itself license irreversible loss of the capacity to respond to foreseeable source criticism. Serious overlap with prior work remains possible.

## Objections to address in the draft

- A complete Bayesian epistemic state already retains all relevant conditional relationships: the target is a marginal or local summary, not Bayesianism.
- Rational updating after forgetting remains possible; the loss is preventable epistemic opportunity, not automatic irrationality of the resulting credence.
- No obligation to retain everything follows: the norm is relative to foreseeable challenges, feasible summaries and competing ends.
- Choosing a stopping rule does not automatically corrupt the evidence. Information can be innocuous relative to one question or evidential state and important relative to another.
- A familiar observation with new terminology is not an original contribution. The precise criterion and the post-inquiry normative application must do the argumentative work.
