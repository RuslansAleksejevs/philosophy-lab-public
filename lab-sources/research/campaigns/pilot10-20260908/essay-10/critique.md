# Independent critique: Why Innocence Does Not Close the Question

AI critic context: `/root/critic_10`. Prepared 2026-09-08 UTC from this essay's question, source packet and draft, plus independent primary-source retrieval. This is criticism of essay 10 only. It does not claim to impersonate a competition judge or certify philosophical truth, originality or eligibility. Retrieval scope and failures are preserved in `sources/critic/retrieval-notes.md`.

## Verdict and strongest reconstruction

**REVISE.** The draft presents a clear, serious and substantially defensible account of a limited preservation duty. It handles privacy, proportionality and the difference between preservation and disclosure well. Its strongest feature is its focus on the conditions for ending an accountability relation after successful performance. But its central argument contains an important inference gap, and its proposed two-part test overgeneralizes from closure to evidential replacement. Newly located procedural-justice literature also makes the originality claim more demanding than the existing bibliography suggests.

The strongest argument is this:

1. Some holders of claims subjected to consequential discretionary administration have an entitlement to an adequate opportunity to question the administration of those claims.
2. That entitlement is not conditional on a substantive violation having occurred; it persists until an independently justified endpoint.
3. The administrator controls reasonably preservable, indispensable means of exercising that entitlement.
4. Absent a sufficient competing reason, an administrator may not deliberately disable an entitlement's indispensable means before its endpoint.
5. Therefore actual substantive compliance does not alone permit the administrator to destroy those means.

This argument is sound in structure once premises 1 and 2 are established. Premise 4 is plausible and the example supports premise 3. The main philosophical work lies in 1–2, especially the merits-independent persistence of the opportunity. A separate conclusion concerns who may authorize closure or an inquiry-limiting substitute. That requires further premises about authority; it does not follow merely from the fact that evidence supports participation.

The draft is strongest when it treats this as a relational extension of procedural respect. It is weakest when it suggests that the distinction between performing and closing, on its own, establishes a specific allocation of powers.

## 1. The innocence-exception objection is not fully answered

The most consequential passage is in §4: if correct treatment extinguished the review claim, the committee's first-order conclusion would settle its second-order authority, leaving review available only at its discretion. That implication does not follow.

An opponent can propose an **objective exemption**: the review entitlement expires when, as a matter of fact, all substantive duties have been satisfied. Whether the committee believes it complied would be irrelevant. A mistaken or dishonest committee would have no permission to destroy evidence, however confidently it asserted innocence. The truth-dependent permission could exist without the committee possessing authority to confer it on itself. This is precisely why the draft correctly acknowledges that permissions can depend on facts observers do not know—but its subsequent response slides back from the fact of compliance to the committee's own verdict.

An analogy clarifies the logical point without deciding the substantive issue. A debt can cease to exist because the creditor received payment, independently of whether the payer has authority to adjudicate a dispute about payment. Likewise, a right might have an objectively specified termination condition without granting anyone a power of unilateral normative closure. The debt example in §2 establishes that the two questions are distinguishable; it does not establish that the second entitlement survives the first entitlement's satisfaction.

**Required revision:** Explicitly concede that the objective-exemption proposal is coherent. Then reject its proposed condition on moral grounds: explain why this particular entitlement protects an opportunity to participate in assessment, whose value is not exhausted by securing compliance. The defense should rest on the participants' status, not on an assertion that accepting the exemption logically transfers authority to the executor.

A concise route is available. Explain that the town asks applicants to let the committee settle how their claims fare while applicants cannot directly follow its discretionary judgments. Reciprocal standing means that the committee may issue an answer, but cannot make its own answer the sole remaining possible contribution to the assessment. The applicants' procedural position is partly constituted by an opportunity to introduce a challenge and obtain a responsive assessment. Whether their possible contribution would be vindicated concerns its success, not their title to contribute. This is a substantive moral argument, not a deduction from delegation alone. An instrumentalist can still reject it. The essay should preserve that disagreement.

Do not merely add another sentence asserting that standing persists. Restructure §4 around the objective exemption so the reader can see exactly which normative premise defeats it.

## 2. Practical obstruction is not itself normative closure

The draft sometimes treats destroying evidence as taking authority to close an account, sometimes as extinguishing standing, and sometimes as making its exercise ineffective. These are different phenomena.

In the allocator case the applicants might retain every normative right they previously held. They may still demand an account, complain to another institution, require an apology or seek whatever remedy is justified. The committee's destruction can fail to close the account in any legitimate sense; indeed, it can create a fresh accountability claim. Its misconduct is disabling an existing claim's effective exercise, not successfully acquiring a normative power to extinguish that claim. The final discussion of remedies already implicitly recognizes this.

Nor does destruction always express a claim of closure authority. A negligent archivist can disable inquiry while making no normative claim at all. Conversely, an official can falsely declare an inquiry closed while retaining every record. Authority over finality and control over practical means can come apart.

**Required revision:** Introduce a precise distinction between (a) legitimate termination of an entitlement and (b) a factual foreclosure of its exercise. Describe the committee as acting *as though entitled to foreclose review* or *pre-empting a decision about closure*, while making clear that its action does not extinguish the applicants' rights. Keep the conditional non-obstruction argument as the foundation. Use the language of usurped closure only where the actor actually purports to settle a matter beyond its mandate.

This improves both the ontology of the wrong and the remedy discussion. It also prevents the title's useful metaphor from carrying more argumentative weight than it can bear.

## 3. The two-part test needs a stronger independence argument

The distinction between evidential adequacy and legitimate finality is promising. But the claim that adequate evidential substitutes must be accepted by an arrangement entitled to settle the terms is too broad unless authorization includes ordinary custodial discretion. If it includes every routine custodial act, the second requirement risks becoming trivial.

**Counterexample: exact migration.** The committee moves the complete evidential record to a secure new format, with provenance, accessibility and every relevant investigative capability preserved, then deletes the obsolete copy. Nobody has authorized final adjudication or accepted a narrower inquiry. No account has been closed and no entitlement has been reduced. This looks permissible under the original duty of care over evidence. It does not require the authority needed to terminate review.

**Counterexample: an unsolicited complete audit.** An expert examines everything applicants are entitled to question and produces a fully inspectable, accurate report. Its evidential contribution can be excellent even if nobody appointed the expert to issue a binding settlement. Applicants can use the report while remaining entitled to their proper hearing. Whether the expert's conclusion has evidential force is distinct from whether it ends the matter.

The draft's own contrasting example—a technically excellent audit confined to a criterion selected to exclude relevant claims—does not yet demonstrate independent failure of authority with functional adequacy held fixed. If adequacy means adequacy for all the inquiry actually owed, that audit is functionally inadequate too. If adequacy is relative to whatever question the committee selected, the first requirement has been weakened until almost any selective report can pass it.

**Required revision:** Fix the legitimate scope of inquiry before testing adequacy. Then distinguish three acts: maintaining equivalent evidence; reducing the available scope or forms of inquiry; and rendering a binding final determination. Only the latter two necessarily raise the additional authority issue. Use the complete-audit example to show that evidential success is insufficient for binding finality while expressly allowing equivalent evidential maintenance without a new closure decision.

The resulting thesis is narrower and sharper: technical adequacy does not itself confer authority to reduce or terminate another party's procedural opportunity. It avoids suggesting that every adequate evidential replacement is a miniature adjudication.

## 4. The retained-standing premise requires boundaries, not just qualifiers

Section 2 commendably states its normative cost. It is not simply a hidden circularity. Yet “consequential discretion over another's claim” plus difficult supervision does not itself determine the scope of review owed. A final adjudicator, emergency decision-maker, or professional selected for confidential judgment can exercise exactly that kind of power. The draft allows justified finality, but its criteria currently say little about why an institution's ordinary mandate sometimes contains finality and sometimes does not.

The statement that separate power needs separate justification is plausible. Separate justification need not mean separate explicit authorization, separate personnel, or a separate temporal step. The same institution can sometimes possess both powers for different reasons. Section 5 allows general schedules, but the earlier rhetoric can sound as though an executor is disqualified simply because it is the executor.

**Recommended revision:** Make the town case more concrete about its background authority. The committee may decide initial allocations, but the public arrangement leaves applicants a reasonable opportunity to ask an appropriate forum for reconsideration; no explicit *record-retention* clause is needed. This does not remove all philosophical interest: the essay would derive an unexpressed evidential duty from a defensible procedural relation and establish why innocence does not excuse its violation. But it would abandon a stronger derivation of review rights from bare discretionary power.

Alternatively, retain the broader ambition and devote real argument to the specific relational ground of review. Say what distinguishes the grant allocator from a legitimately final decision-maker without using “retained standing” as the distinguishing label itself. The present draft has space for either repair; it need not attempt both at length.

One additional clarification matters: a request to preserve an opportunity to check need not be an accusation, but a right to an opportunity need not entail an immediate full evidential hearing for every unsupported demand. State a modest triggering threshold, or explain that the essay concerns retention through a reasonable challenge period, leaving the threshold for commencing review open.

## 5. Significance and originality

The question is philosophically worthwhile. It identifies a frequent justificatory shortcut—treating a favorable substantive assessment as sufficient reason to dismantle its conditions of examination—and asks what right this can violate even without further losses. Its practical significance depends on giving administrators a usable distinction between harmless evidential maintenance and inquiry-limiting action. Revision of the two-part test could supply that distinction.

However, the core opposition between correct outcomes and procedural rights is substantially established. **Conor Crummey's “Why Fair Procedures Always Make a Difference”** argues that unchanged correct outcomes do not by themselves justify denying fair procedure, invoking participatory citizenship and communicative respect. The closest sections are “The narrow instrumental view of fair procedures” and “Fair Procedures and Participatory Citizenship.” The draft differs by stipulating an initially impeccable decision process and then examining destruction of future review capacity. It should explain why that temporal extension adds something beyond applying a familiar principle. [Crummey 2020, *Modern Law Review* 83(6): 1221–1245](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/1468-2230.12556).

**Lawrence Solum's “Procedural Justice”** is particularly close to the authority/finality aspect. His university-hosted abstract identifies reasonable participation as a usual condition of legitimate final binding, and distinguishes a participation principle from an accuracy principle. That makes the draft's contrast between correct assessment and authority to settle visibly antecedented. I accessed the abstract and metadata, not the article's full text; a detailed claim of equivalence would exceed that evidence. [Solum 2004, *Southern California Law Review* 78: 181–321, repository abstract describing parts V–VI](https://scholarship.law.georgetown.edu/facpub/881/).

Bovens's actor/forum definition also makes questioning and judgment central to accountability. The draft's thought that another party has a role in assessment is therefore not a new account of answerability. Only the publisher abstract was accessible to this critic. [Bovens 2007, abstract](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/pdf/10.1111/j.1468-0386.2007.00378.x).

These antecedents do not show that the entire essay already exists. They do show that Sturino is not the only close literature. The defensible contribution is an application and refinement concerning the preservation, substitution and foreclosure of evidence after compliant performance. Originality remains uncertain; the limited search cannot establish priority. Do not inflate it by moving established participation/finality arguments into a recordkeeping vocabulary.

## 6. Scholarship and source accuracy

I found no material misquotation or false attribution in the checked draft citations. The manuscript is mostly paraphrase and carefully acknowledges antecedents. The literature is relevant, but some citations supply background rather than premises supporting the central moral inference.

- **Waldron:** §5, printed pp. 14–15 supports dignitary participation, evidence and responsive adjudication. His legal account does not establish a review right in every serious discretionary relation. The draft mostly marks that extension appropriately. Later PDF text is degraded and the attempted screenshot failed. [Primary PDF](https://philarchive.org/archive/WALTRO-67v1).
- **Pettit:** pp. 302–304 and 308–310 support unexercised contestatory control and recorded reasons. Extending this to post-performance retention with anticipatory benefits removed is this essay's further step. [Author-hosted PDF](https://ppettit.scholar.princeton.edu/sites/g/files/toruqf7426/files/documents/Democracy%20National%20and%20International.pdf).
- **Kroll:** §3.3 supports contextual retention and intermediaries; §3.5 expressly discusses both system design and governance enabling audit. The draft could acknowledge that closer antecedent to its technical/normative distinction. [Primary text](https://arxiv.org/html/2101.09385v1).
- **Cobbe, Lee and Singh:** §§3.1–3.2 and §5 support process-sensitive records, actor/forum variation and the drawbacks of indiscriminate recording. Attribution is sound. [Primary text](https://arxiv.org/html/2102.04201v1).
- **Cen and Alur:** §5.2 supports the distinction between presumption and needed evidence. This is an analytic analogy, not a justification of a directed moral duty. Keep that limitation explicit. [Primary text](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.04772v1).
- **Sturino:** §5 supports the claims about erasure, evasion and incentives; the draft fairly identifies its different case. [Primary text](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/papa.12284).
- **O’Neill:** transcript pp. 5–6 supports the warning about accountability burdens and the value of informed independent judgment. [BBC transcript](https://downloads.bbc.co.uk/rmhttp/radio4/transcripts/20020417_reith.pdf).
- **Watson et al.:** “Epistemic Rights as Human Rights” supports the broad characterization. Its inclusion helps avoid a false contrast, although it is peripheral to the proof. [Primary chapter](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-031-45976-4_14).

Do not expand the bibliography indiscriminately. Direct engagement with Crummey, and cautious investigation of Solum where accessible, is more valuable than additional broad accountability citations. No current legal conclusion should be inferred from the historical legal literature discussed here.

## 7. Precise revision priorities and residual disagreement

1. Repair the fact/judgment equivocation in the innocence-exception reply. Defend merits-independent procedural standing directly.
2. Separate practical foreclosure from legitimate extinction of a right. Preserve the applicant's continuing normative claim after wrongful destruction.
3. Narrow the authority requirement to reductions of inquiry and binding closure. Add an equivalent-evidence case and a complete-but-nonbinding audit case.
4. State the legitimate scope of inquiry independently of the committee's chosen test, so functional adequacy has a stable target.
5. Situate the argument against Crummey and the accessible Solum material. State a limited contribution concerning post-performance evidential conditions.
6. Give one concrete rule for when the allocator's review opportunity remains open, with general closure rules possible and ordinary custodial discretion intact.

The “no further harm” case should remain. It is not defeated merely because procedural capacity itself counts as an interest. But eliminating downstream errors and deterrence does not establish that every possible consequentialist explanation is false; the essay need only defend its directed procedural account, not claim exclusive explanatory status.

My considered disagreement is limited but real: I accept a defeasible non-obstruction duty where a justified review opportunity remains open. I do not think the present text has shown that bare consequential discretion normally entails this opportunity, nor that every evidential substitute requires closure authority. The narrower conclusion is strong enough to preserve while those claims are revised. Completion of that revision would improve the argument; it would not establish novelty or contest readiness by itself.
