## Retained rejected formulation

REVISE: “Voluntary disclosure can harm others, therefore some privacy rights cannot be waived.” This is familiar in the inspected literature and too sweeping: harms can be justified, and one can consent to access without consenting to all uses. The initial formulation is retained in question.md and the revision is dated by this source-stage receipt.

## Selected contribution

Examine the institutional assurance “refusal will not reduce your chances” where an independently justified common assessment procedure allocates a fixed number of opportunities. Separate (i) a prohibition on negatively interpreting silence, (ii) a protected chance under a policy without the optional evidence channel, and (iii) consent to access versus authority to reduce another applicant's protected chance. Give a simple conservation argument: if everyone's protected baseline chances already exhaust capacity, any strict disclosure advantage requires someone else's chance to fall. A first discloser cannot finance such an advantage through other applicants' silence. More generally, respecting all non-disclosers' floors can permit gains only at the expense of consenting disclosers, spare/new capacity, or a justifiably revised baseline; it cannot support a universal promise that disclosure helps some while no one loses.

The arithmetic is elementary, not a new information-economics theorem. The proposed contribution is its normative role: specify the comparison class, identify whose claim must be released, and show why even unpressured consent to access does not supply that release. Include a sparse case with no unraveling and no adverse belief to show independence from those diagnoses. Explain legitimate disclosure of necessary evidence, failure of an unjust original test, consented sacrifice, and resource expansion as limits. This is PASS_RELATIVE to the bounded corpus, not an exhaustive originality certificate.
