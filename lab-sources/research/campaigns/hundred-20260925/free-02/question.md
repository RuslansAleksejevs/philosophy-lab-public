# Initial question and alternatives

AI author, free-02. This is a question-selection document, not a novelty verdict.

1. **Selected for investigation:** Can a person validly waive a privacy protection when the resulting practice makes other people's refusal a decisive adverse signal? The familiar economic unraveling of voluntary disclosure is likely already established. The potentially worthwhile philosophical issue is the structure of the right: distinguish permission to acquire a fact, permission to use the fact in allocating a protected opportunity, and permission to treat non-disclosure as inferior. Explore whether an institution's promise to leave people free to withhold includes a duty to preserve a decision route that is actually adequate without disclosure. Determine when this would illegitimately suppress relevant evidence or impose another person's privacy cost on willing disclosers.

2. **Alternative not pursued at this point:** A duty to preserve conditions of justified dissent, rather than merely provide a correct explanation. Objection: likely restates familiar democratic contestability and epistemic-injustice accounts without a sufficiently distinct result.

3. **Alternative not pursued at this point:** The ethics of promises to forget painful information. Objection: important questions about memory control, but likely too dependent on impossible capacities or familiar distinctions between remembering and using information.

Tentative test case: equally eligible applicants for a basic opportunity may supply optional private evidence of a trait. Strong applicants disclose, producing an increasingly adverse interpretation of silence. A formal right to refuse persists while its practical meaning changes. The investigation must not assume that every competitive disadvantage is wrongful, or that all statistical inference is illegitimate. It must explain why a duty falls on this institution in this relationship.

Working hypothesis, subject to revision after source reading: some institutional non-disclosure protections concern the terms of an offer, so one person's permission to disclose cannot authorize the institution to make another person's basic opportunity conditional on disclosure. This may support a duty governing institutional use without supporting a duty to believe against the evidence. The contribution cannot be merely that privacy has externalities; that fact is expected to be familiar.

Planned source search: classic disclosure/unraveling models; primary philosophical accounts of privacy as control and privacy as contextual or social practice; work on voluntary disclosure's coercive pressure and limits of waiver. Test the hypothesis against evidence-sensitive allocation and expressive voluntary disclosure. Reject or narrow it if it is only a renamed claim already defended in the nearest work.

Method limit: one author context; self-examination only, no separate critic. No sibling essay or earlier laboratory research is an input.
