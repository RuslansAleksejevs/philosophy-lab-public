# Initial question and alternatives

Selected question: What must a correction restore when a discredited source has produced the appearance of independent agreement?

Working thesis to investigate, not yet accepted: once a source has manufactured apparent corroboration, a successful public correction must sometimes restore information about dependence, not merely replace the false first-order claim. Selectively deleting the source can leave rational recipients with stronger misleading reasons than a correction that preserves a bounded, intelligible provenance record. This may ground a limited duty to maintain retrievable dependence information even when the original claim should no longer be circulated as live testimony.

The hoped-for contribution is a criterion for adequacy of correction tied to the inferential situation that the distributor itself helped create. It must avoid simply renaming the familiar injunction not to count dependent evidence twice. A promising difference is between undoing a false assertion and undoing an institutionally created entitlement to treat several assertions as separate evidence. The latter can outlast every original error in the active database.

Alternatives considered by this author in this context:
1. An explanation owed after an error-free administrative decision, grounded in the subject's capacity to reject it. Set aside: proximity to established public-justification and relational-equality arguments; novelty would require extensive literature work before a distinct inference was apparent.
2. Duties not to demand testimony whose disclosure would injure the source. Set aside: likely proximity to epistemic exploitation and testimonial smothering; a new label alone would add little.
3. A challenge to the value of information when evidence changes future agency or memory. Set aside: the counterexample would probably exploit familiar failures of the value-of-information theorem's fixed-option assumptions rather than reveal a new philosophical result.
4. The selected correction/dependence question. Retained provisionally because a small transparent case can separate truth of the remaining assertions, their informational ancestry, recipients' warranted reliance, and the institution's corrective duty.

Tests before selection for a manuscript:
- Locate primary arguments on expert dependence, source independence, evidence laundering/citation networks, retractions and continued influence.
- Determine whether the positive duty is already explicit in the nearest literature. If it is, revise toward an additional defensible condition or reject.
- Show a nontrivial claim that survives the objection that ordinary Bayesian updating already resolves everything.
- Bound the duty against privacy, harmful repetition, endless archival work and agents who retain genuinely independent evidence.
- Do not infer a general obligation to preserve every original or to disclose identifying details.
