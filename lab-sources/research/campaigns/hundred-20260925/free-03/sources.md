## Reading and closest arguments

- **Katherine Dormandy, “Epistemic Authority: Preemption or Proper Basing?” (2018), Erkenntnis 83:773–791, DOI 10.1007/s10670-017-9913-3.** Read tool-rendered introduction, central arguments in §§2–4, and portions of §§5–6. She criticizes total preemption, including discarding reasons supporting the expert, and distinguishes genuine authority from lesser superiority. The present essay must not portray all defenses of deference as unconditional or all contra-reasons as automatically probative. Her treatment concerns how available reasons support a verdict. My target is also the process making reasons available across time. The original Zagzebski book was not read; do not attribute detailed interpretations to it firsthand.
- **C. Thi Nguyen, “Echo Chambers and Epistemic Bubbles” (2020), Episteme 17(2):141–161, DOI 10.1017/epi.2018.32.** Read the Cambridge primary text's introduction and §§1–2 (rendered lines 483–565). It explicitly discusses discounting agreement produced by selection, trust manipulation, disagreement reinforcement, rational dependence and cases where outsider distrust is appropriate. Those general points are established antecedents, not this essay's novelty. Later sections were not read in full.
- **Stewart Cohen, “Why Basic Knowledge Is Easy Knowledge” (2005), Philosophy and Phenomenological Research 70(2):417–430.** Read the bootstrapping discussion, especially printed pp.425–427 / rendered lines 292–360, plus selected surrounding discussion. Cohen distinguishes illegitimate bootstrapping from the inevitable partial use of perception in supporting perception. No claim to solve the general bootstrapping debate is licensed.
- **Patrick Bondy, “Arguments from Ignorance, Coverage, and Bubbles” (2026), Informal Logic 46(1):101–115.** Read the returned article text across two overlapping retrievals, including §§1–6. His threatening-information example is a very close antecedent: silence from a system that would suppress a report does not show the report false. Merely applying coverage reliability to embarrassing evidence is therefore rejected as a contribution. His articulation permits a narrower investigation of the time-dependent warrant for continued delegation and of the difference between exclusion, uninformative silence and negatively informative silence. The essay must explicitly credit Bondy.

The essay will investigate a limitation of specifying response to recognized defeaters while leaving the production of recognizable defeaters unspecified. This is a supplement to a defeasible account, not a claim that Lange favors blind trust. Later applied sections have not been read in full.

## Change of direction

The initial demand for a separately supported channel is too broad. A source may justifiably be relied upon for a short fully closed interval. The generic point that filters hide defeaters is already familiar. The defensible project now distinguishes warrant for **using** an authority from warrant for **renewing** that reliance, explains the evidential effect of a quiet review channel using explicit conditional probabilities, and derives a limited obligation to reassess when a known temporal basis expires. This avoids both universal individual rechecking and a merely verbal willingness to change one's mind.

## Scope of proposed contribution

The contribution is a connected argument and discriminating cases, not a new Bayesian theorem or discovery of epistemic circularity. A particularly informative case is that a perfectly clean institutional record can count against reliability when candid competent systems report occasional small errors and compromised systems suppress them. By contrast, closure alone is neither a defeater nor evidence of corruption. What matters is how the observations vary with the particular failure that must be excluded. Temporal reassessment is conditional on warranted expectations of change, not an arbitrary deadline on all testimony.
