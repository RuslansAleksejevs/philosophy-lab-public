# Question and alternatives

## Scope
Independent AI-selected investigation; no other essays or prior lab research supplied. One author performs self-examination; no independent critic is planned.

## Alternatives considered before source work
1. **Duties created by unwanted disclosure.** Can a speaker wrong someone by giving information that makes an expensive rescue obligatory? Promising link between epistemic and practical burdens, but likely entangled with familiar accounts of manipulation and a difficult baseline problem. Set aside without claiming refutation.
2. **Public corrections and residual reliance.** Does an institution retain obligations after formally withdrawing a claim that others still rely upon? Significant, but the immediate thesis appears too close to familiar duties to correct misinformation. Set aside as insufficiently differentiated.
3. **Delegated judgment and control of defeaters.** If an expert legitimately decides which first-order evidence deserves attention, can that same delegation justify letting the expert decide which challenges to its own continuing reliability deserve attention? This has a precise epistemological structure, engages the regress of expertise and echo chambers, and permits nontrivial counterexamples to an overstrong openness requirement. Selected for investigation.

## Selected question
When does warranted reliance on an epistemic authority also warrant accepting that authority's exclusion of apparent evidence against its continuing reliability?

## Provisional hypothesis, not a settled result
Warrant to defer about a subject need not include warrant to delegate every judgment about whether that deference remains warranted. The interesting restriction cannot be that one must assess all criticism oneself: that defeats much worthwhile expertise. Instead, some routes by which loss of competence or good faith could undermine reliance must remain supported without depending entirely on the very continuation claim under review.

Test whether this can be made more precise than familiar anti-circularity or anti-dogmatism. Distinguish (a) evidence against a particular answer, (b) evidence against competence or honesty, and (c) authority over which evidence is admitted. Consider justified highly closed systems, impossibility of fully independent checks, unreliable outsiders, changing expertise, Bayesian selection effects, and authorities correct by luck. A useful contribution would concern the structure and temporal limits of a warrant, not the invention of a slogan for trusting less.

## Research decision
Proceed to targeted primary-source work on epistemic authority, echo chambers, bootstrapping and expert selection. Revise or reject the provisional contribution if the closest literature already establishes it or a strong counterexample defeats it.
