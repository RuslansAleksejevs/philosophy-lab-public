**Verdict: specific revision.** The narrow conditional argument is sound within the stipulated case, and the source attributions checked are broadly accurate. The major unresolved issue is whether this is a sufficiently informative philosophical contribution rather than a carefully expressed application of ordinary conditional reasoning to a deliberately bad staffing proposal. A second, related issue is the opening suggestion of a tension within James: the closest passages in James and Hayward anticipate more of the proposed picture than the manuscript acknowledges. ## What the argument establishes

The central inference can be reconstructed charitably as follows. Confidence can help produce a true belief through the actions it supports. This may supply a reason for a person's confidence within a particular arrangement. A recommendation to expand a practical expression of that confidence changes the arrangement. If that change removes inquiry indispensable to the very truth-producing effect invoked, the original reason does not carry over to the changed arrangement. A recommendation still relying on that reason must preserve the inquiry, substitute for it, or narrow its scope.

The manuscript correctly distinguishes a permission from a requirement to universalize it (lines 48–52), inquiry from disbelief, and removing a truth-producing activity from removing grounds for an assessment (66–68). It explicitly allows all participants to believe the same proposition while performing different tasks. It also allows alternative grounds for a recommendation and denies that every check must continue forever. These qualifications block several tempting but unfair objections. The thesis is not that faith always requires dissenters, that a useful attitude must work under uniform universal adoption, or that one must maximize inquiry.

The reference-work example demonstrates the limited conclusion cleanly. Its force comes from the stipulated dependence of accuracy on a checker. Ada's permission is granted for argument's sake, so objections to voluntarism or Jamesian non-evidential belief do not defeat the conditional result. The problem is the result's explanatory and argumentative reach, not an invalid inference in that result.

## R1 — Major: the change of work does nearly all the argumentative work

Relevant manuscript locations: lines 29–52, 78–92, 98–100.

Chen can already possess exactly the same confident belief as Ada. The bad recommendation adds an instruction to abandon checking. Consequently, the contrast does not by itself establish a limit on extending belief to more people; it establishes a limit on recommending a different activity profile under cover of a previously successful belief. The manuscript recognizes this, and I am not treating that recognition as an overlooked contradiction. But once it is recognized, the main novelty objection becomes stronger: why is this more than a restriction on transferring an instrumental reason when a necessary condition is deliberately removed?

A useful diagnostic is to hold every belief fixed and change only the role instruction. Everyone confidently performing the needed role yields accuracy; everyone confidently producing entries does not. The work assignment explains the difference. The same pattern would remain if the lost indispensable activity were typesetting or maintaining the project server. This does not make epistemic applications illegitimate. It does mean that inquiry's special role, and the philosophical payoff of focusing on faith, require an additional explanation.

The current first and third objection replies acknowledge ordinary instrumental rationality and convergence with good collective reasoning, then restate that the constraint is internal to the truth-making defence. That establishes where the ordinary constraint applies. It does not yet establish why its application yields a substantial result which a competent advocate of that defence would resist or miss. Likewise, describing two questions as “often compressed into one” (line 92) needs an instance or a more precise target; the toy advocate's plainly self-defeating instruction is not evidence of that wider diagnosis.

**Required repair:** identify the exact additional result being claimed beyond contextual qualification of a causal reason. This could be a defended, non-obvious consequence for a recognizable practice of inviting confidence, or a precise criticism of a source's actual inference. Alternatively, recast the contribution explicitly as an applied diagnostic and justify its value without suggesting a newly discovered tension or a new general limit on belief. A toy case is legitimate; an empirical case is not compulsory. What is needed is a reason the conceptual lesson matters beyond the fact that the stipulated indispensable task remains indispensable. If no stronger result is available, preserving a modest but accurate essay is preferable to inventing novelty.

## R2 — Material historical issue: engage James's and Hayward's more direct anticipations

Relevant manuscript locations: lines 9, 25, 78–82 and 90–100.

The historical exposition correctly reports James's conditions, action-related examples and tolerance, and Clifford's probabilistic action and socially sustained authority. However, the claim that the essay illuminates a tension *within* James is not yet supported by a passage in which James's commitments pull in opposing directions.

Three James passages should be addressed together. In the Preface, printed pp. x–xi, he expressly differentiates audiences that need criticism and caution from academic audiences that need encouragement to believe. In §VIII, printed p. 21, the useful investigator combines commitment with sensitivity to possible deception, and verification is an organized practice. In §IX, printed p. 24, cooperation proceeds through each member doing their own duty while trusting others to do theirs. These passages do not state the manuscript's exact conditional about public uptake. But they make audience sensitivity, confidence with inquiry, and differentiated cooperative work native features of James's view. They are more pertinent than a general acknowledgment that he permits continued inquiry.

Hayward supplies an equally serious comparator. §3.2, printed p. 25, starts from the best collective outcome and each person's part in achieving it. §3.3, pp. 27–29, qualifies optimism through evidence about feasibility, partners and altruism, and says it must remain informed by investigation and deliberation. The manuscript accurately reports these qualifications. Yet the subsequent warning against representing everyone merely as supporters or opponents is not shown to identify a defect in Hayward's account. His team framework has room for heterogeneous activities from the start; it does not have to acquire that capacity from this essay.

**Required repair:** either substantiate an internal tension, or present the proposal as a specific implication or development consistent with these texts. Make clear whether the maintenance question is a missing normative constraint, an underdeveloped implementation issue, or an application of an already accepted constraint. A fresh emphasis can be worthwhile, but an emphasis is not automatically a new principle. This is a challenge to framing and demonstrated contribution, not a finding that the cited authors say the opposite of the manuscript's limited conclusion.

## R3 — Useful development: make the warrant-only case earn its place

Relevant manuscript locations: lines 66–68 and 84–86.

The distinction between inquiry producing a good and inquiry supporting its advocate's assessment could give the essay more epistemic specificity. At present, however, only the productive case receives a worked example. The warrant case says the conclusion follows where continuing justification depends on the inquiry withdrawn. That is a correct conditional, but largely builds the important epistemic premise into its antecedent.

Stopping an inquiry does not ordinarily erase the evidence it previously supplied. The manuscript acknowledges stable conditions and alternative grounds, so this is not an accusation that it overlooks evidential retention altogether. The remaining task is to specify a case in which the assessment concerns a condition liable to change and further checking is needed to sustain the particular ongoing appeal. The author should distinguish a justified warning that grounds may lapse from actual loss of those grounds, and neither should be equated with the venture's failure.

**Suggested repair:** add one bounded example and state what makes the dependence continuing. If this distinction is not to bear argumentative weight, reduce its prominence instead. This is a development requirement if the author uses the warrant case to answer R1, rather than a separate fatal defect in the present productive case.

## R4 — Smaller precision points

The formal display at lines 58–60 moves from a reason for adoption to “should.” Even with a matching activity profile, a pro tanto reason does not by itself establish an all-things-considered ought. The surrounding text mostly treats the issue correctly as whether this particular reason survives. Use the same normative strength across the three steps so the relevant failure is changing conditions, not an additional, independent reason-to-ought gap.

Clifford §I, lines 12–14 of the saved text, gives an already convinced person an action-level duty to investigate, but also argues that conviction prevents inquiry from being as fair and complete as genuine doubt. The manuscript already denies reconciling him with James. A brief acknowledgment would nevertheless make clear that its confident checker is not straightforwardly endorsed by every part of Clifford's position. One can use Clifford's account of authority without adopting this stronger psychology.

No repair is required merely because the hypothetical project is simplified, because Jamesian permission is provisionally granted, or because no mathematical model is supplied. No numerical or executable model claim in the manuscript needed testing.
