# Revised proposal: Choosing Without Ranking Lives

**Decision: NARROW.**

## Narrow thesis

A justified selection among a finite set of feasible interventions does not, by itself, logically entail a complete ordering of the affected recipients' lives as wholes. This claim holds only where the allocation reasons are independently defensible, the stated context includes the distributive facts those reasons make relevant, and the resulting strict-priority relation is acyclic. A unique maximal intervention can then be selected without asserting a complete-life comparison. If several maxima remain, the action is justified only if a separate tie procedure is independently defensible; otherwise the result is underdetermination.

This is a claim about what an action justification need not entail. It is not a general allocation rule, a defense of incomplete orderings as morally superior, or a claim that every allocation can avoid whole-life comparisons. A local relation may conceal an all-things-considered life ranking; the proponent must show that the actual reasons do not rely on that hidden bridge.

## Argument earned by the probe

1. An ordering over interventions and an ordering of recipients' complete lives have different objects.
2. The assigned formal excerpts do not establish a moral rule requiring complete-life comparison whenever an action is selected. Arrow's excerpt presents completeness as an axiom for an ordering; Sen's short excerpt distinguishes choice rules from orderings but is incomplete.
3. A finite acyclic intervention-priority graph has at least one maximal option. If there is exactly one maximal option, a rule that selects undominated options returns a determinate action. This mathematical fact does not justify the edges or their moral interpretation.
4. The clinic example can produce A>B under the stipulated severity-then-corrective-access rule without encoding a complete-life ranking. But a rival severity-first/access-first weighting can reverse a verdict, and an explicit cycle defeats the existence of a maximal option.
5. Therefore action selection does not logically entail a whole-life ranking, while a particular allocation still needs independent substantive and procedural justification.

## Limits

The proof obligation now rests on the policy's reasons: explain why its priority rule is justified, how histories and current needs enter, and why a tie procedure is fair. The finite model demonstrates only the behavior of one stipulated rule on the included data. It does not establish the clinic verdict's justice, general coherence, novelty, or applicability to every decision. The closest-source investigation was bounded; originality remains unknown.
