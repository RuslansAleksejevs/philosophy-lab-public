## Bounded finding

P1's broad inferential-privacy idea is not new as stated in these sources.
Privacy duties can extend to inferences from public information, and a limited
claim to inferential privacy has been defended. P4's proposed duty of
co-maintainers is not established by these readings: Hart's account may support
reciprocity within a joint enterprise, but it also makes the practice's terms
and who benefits important; Rawls's practice framework does not itself prove
that those terms are morally justified. Singer makes a general duty of easy
assistance a serious alternative.

No read passage establishes the distinctive conclusion that a co-maintainer
owes a truthful cover refusal when protocol redesign is unavailable. No read
passage rules it out either. Because Hardimon's primary article was unavailable
and relevant privacy literature remains only partly read, novelty and the
special-duty premise both remain open.

Not tested at this stage: response-rate posteriors, side information, repeated
rounds, actual observer behavior, displacement of suspicion, or the
counterexamples. Those belong to the later assigned argument-probe stage.
