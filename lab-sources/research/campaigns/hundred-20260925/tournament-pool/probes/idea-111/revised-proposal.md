# Optional Signals, Unequal Burdens: a redesign-first governance claim

**Decision: NARROW.**

Where a person knowingly accepts responsibility for designing or administering
an optional-disclosure procedure, represents or invites reliance on protection
for legitimate confidential exceptions, and can foresee that the procedure's
ordinary response pattern will identify an exception, that person has a
defeasible reason to assess the leakage and prefer a feasible, minimally
restrictive redesign. This is a conditional claim about responsible governance;
it is not established as a distinctive special-duty principle by the bounded
sources reviewed here.

Redesign means, where practicable, private collection, identity-free aggregate
release, delay, or another arrangement that serves the legitimate purpose
without exposing individual responses. A cover refusal is not presumed owed.
It is at most a freely chosen fallback if it is truthful, does not create a
misleading implication, does not interfere with another person's stronger
right or duty to speak, and is expected to reduce net risk under an explicit
threat model without shifting suspicion to a more vulnerable person. No one
gets a general veto over another person's chosen self-disclosure.

Mere membership, administrative title, equal capacity to help, or one-time
compliance is not enough to establish the role condition. The special-duty
claim is defeated if no relevant responsibility was accepted, the secrecy
interest is unjustified, the disclosure is needed for safety or public
accountability, the proposed design is ineffective or materially burdensome,
or a stronger reporting, expressive, legal, or reliance obligation applies.

This narrowed position preserves a practice-level concern: formal permission
to refuse can fail when a predictable response pattern makes refusal
informative. The broad inferential-privacy concern is present in assigned
primary-source excerpts. The narrower governance condition is not shown there
to be new or already established; novelty remains **UNKNOWN**. The evidence
supports investigating this conditional application, not presenting the
cover-refusal thesis as a proved or original principle.
