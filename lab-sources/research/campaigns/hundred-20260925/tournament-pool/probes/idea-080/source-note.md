# Source note: idea-080, sources-001

## Scope

This stage performed a bounded primary-source investigation for the three
assigned source questions: (1) whether justice or directed duties can persist
after a claimant's death without requiring posthumous welfare; (2) whether
corrective or reparative justice can mitigate a wrong to a past person; and (3)
whether an institution can remain answerable and acknowledge wrongdoing after
its original members and the person wronged have died. It was limited to the
sealed idea-080 packet and the campaign instructions bound to `sources-001`.
Exact searches, access outcomes, and reading windows are in `search-log.json`.

## Primary-source coverage

Zofia Stemplowska's accepted manuscript “Duties to the Dead: Is Posthumous
Mitigation of Injustice Possible?” was obtained from Oxford's repository and
read in full (manuscript pages 1–25). It directly argues that death does not
make all mitigation of injustice impossible and that living people can sometimes
owe mitigation to people who are dead, without relying on a posthumous change in
their well-being. Its central route concerns surviving preferences and projects.
It distinguishes a permissive view of mitigation as improving a victim's
position from a narrower view of direct or partial rectification, and it notes a
corrective-justice route even where preferences are not themselves the currency
of justice. The source also imposes limits concerning the victim's actual or
reasonably inferred preferences, consent, permissibility, and competing claims.
Exact sections and the original PDF hash are recorded in
`sources/stemplowska-reading-notes.md`.

The search returned a short indexed passage from Joel Feinberg's “Harm and
Self-Interest,” at page 308. The passage was saved exactly in
`sources/feinberg-1977-indexed-excerpt.md`. Direct access to the PDF returned
403, so the rest of that source remains unread.

For institutional responsibility, sections 1, 3, 6, 8, and 9 of Andrew I.
Cohen and Jennifer A. Samp's “On the Possibility of Corporate Apologies” were
read in an author-content full-text display. The selected passages treat
organizational continuity, group-level fault where responsible members have
left, authorized spokespeople, and limits on what the paper establishes. A
bounded exact excerpt and the read spans are preserved in
`sources/cohen-samp-2013-excerpt.md`.

Angela M. Smith's “Institutional Apologies and Forgiveness” is a particularly
close source lead for historical wrongdoing where original victims and
perpetrators are dead. Only the Oxford publisher's citation and abstract were
available, so the source is recorded as metadata, not as a read argument. The
chapter's full text was not accessed; see `sources/smith-2021-metadata.md`.

## Coverage limits

The source work found a published argument for posthumous mitigation, a
posthumous-harm excerpt, and primary institutional-apology work. It did not
search exhaustively for “posthumous retraction,” “official vindication,” or
equivalent terms; it does not establish originality or literature-wide absence.
Some potentially relevant works surfaced only as publisher metadata, search
snippets, or rehost listings and were not treated as full readings. No argument
defeat, matched-case analysis, or survival decision was performed at this stage.

The saved sources and notes distinguish full text, bounded excerpt, and
metadata-only coverage. They record retrieval timing at the precision the tools
exposed; no missing timestamps or configuration observations were inferred.
