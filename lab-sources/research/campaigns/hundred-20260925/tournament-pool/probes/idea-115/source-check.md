# Source and nearest-neighbor check — idea-115

## Central discriminator

Question from the assigned remaining-tests file: does a primary theory independently and non-selectively ground future-directed interest, the welfare bearer, cross-temporal aggregation, and permission to impose suffering?

## Result

The retrieved Wendler article gives a close, explicit account of compensation between a victim and later beneficiary. It requires enough psychological similarity across the two times; it also allows a whole-life comparison in principle while holding that an extreme episode may destroy the sufferer’s capacity to remain connected to or appreciate that future. This supplies a principled candidate rule for psychological eligibility and a separate constraint on imposing severe suffering. It weakens the proposal’s suggestion that the field may leave those relations uncalibrated, though Wendler’s threshold for sufficient connection remains open and his argument is about animal-research policy.

Harman directly challenges a time-relative interest account with same-structure near/far cases. Her cases make future suffering, not later pleasure, the benefit/offset at issue. They show a close precedent for testing a theory’s treatment of temporal distance and permission, but do not by themselves answer whether a positive life-total compensates an earlier painful stage.

The sources do not settle the stipulated Mica example. Wendler’s approach appears to reject compensation if there is no adequate psychological connection between victim and beneficiary; the proposal’s alternative could instead posit a distinct welfare-bearer rule. That alternative needs its own primary defense. The search did not establish that a principled two-ground account is absent, nor that the proposed sign-reversal procedure is novel.
