## What the closest sources show

The closest retrieved primary argument is David Wendler’s 2022 article on compensation in animal research. Its sections 4 and 5 already ask when a later beneficiary is sufficiently psychologically connected to the individual who suffered, distinguish whole-life better-off calculations from decisions made during severe suffering, and argue that extreme prolonged suffering can block the connection needed for compensation. This is direct overlap with the selected proposal’s central P/W/K calibration problem. It means the proposal cannot claim a new discovery of that general tension. Elizabeth Harman’s primary chapter is a nearby argument against McMahan’s time-relative interests view. Harman gives near- and far-future treatment cases in which current surgery imposes pain to prevent later suffering; she argues that the view as she reconstructs it permits the near-future case but cannot explain the far-future case on the same grounds. This is relevant to T1, T4, and temporal calibration, but it concerns preventing future suffering rather than offsetting earlier suffering with later pleasure.
