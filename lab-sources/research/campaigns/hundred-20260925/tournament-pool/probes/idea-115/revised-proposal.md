# REJECT — idea-115 as a standalone new contribution

The proposal asks whether a theory can use weak psychological continuity to reduce a stage’s future-directed stake while still counting later pleasure as compensation for suffering in the same life. The logical answer is yes, if a theory independently and consistently defends its welfare bearer, aggregation rule, and permission principle. That answer is already largely present in the closest retrieved source: Wendler distinguishes the harmed individual from the later beneficiary, requires sufficient psychological connection for compensation, allows whole-life comparison, and argues that severe prolonged suffering can defeat the attempt to compensate. Harman separately gives a near/far temporal challenge to a time-relative interests account.

The proposal therefore does not survive as a new inconsistency claim or as a novel general calibration test. Its remaining question—whether a particular theory applies distinct stage-interest, life-welfare, and permission rules consistently—is worth asking when attached to an exact target text. The assigned packet does not show a target theory that fails this test or a substantively distinct test that has not already been anticipated. This question is retained only as a future research prompt. Novelty is unknown, with substantial direct overlap; no originality claim is made.
