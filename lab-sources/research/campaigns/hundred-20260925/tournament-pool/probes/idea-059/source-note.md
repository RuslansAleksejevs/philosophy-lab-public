## Sources consulted

**Sally Haslanger, “Gender and Race: (What) Are They? (What) Do We Want Them to
Be?”** The selected passages distinguish descriptive, conceptual, and analytical
projects; ask what practical work concepts should perform; and discuss the
political effects of changing terms for social identities. The article also
describes identity terms as contestable and collective resources, while stressing
that a proposed terminological shift must be assessed in its context. Read excerpts
from journal pages 33–34, 36–37, 47–49, and 52. The public Wiley page verified the
bibliography but exposed metadata rather than the article body; the saved excerpt
comes from a publicly accessible PDF mirror.

**David Plunkett and Tim Sundell, “Metalinguistic Negotiation and Speaker Error.”**
The selected sections distinguish speakers’ apparent object-level concerns from
a theorist’s metalinguistic analysis. The authors discuss a familiar objection:
speakers may treat a proposed definition change as irrelevant to the worldly issue.
They reply that a dispute about concept use can also express a dispute about
object-level stakes, illustrated with examples about “athlete” and “torture.” Read
excerpts from the PDF’s pages 1–3, 6–8, 12–13, and 23–24 (the journal version is
Inquiry 64(1–2), 142–167). This is an author-hosted copy of a primary article.

**Miranda Fricker, “Evolving Concepts of Epistemic Injustice.”** The selected
passages explain hermeneutical marginalization as unequal opportunity to make an
experience intelligible to oneself and others, and distinguish interpersonal
listening from the structural conditions that keep exclusion in place. A later
example concerns patients whose non-expert descriptions are not accepted into
clinical decision-making. Read excerpts from pages 1–5 and 13–14 of the
author-produced accepted-version PDF (18 PDF pages); the published chapter is
pages 53–60, and the pagination does not map page-for-page. The White Rose record
confirms the version and bibliographic details.

**Iris Marion Young, “Communication and the Other: Beyond Deliberative Democracy.”**
A search-index excerpt from the opening critique says that narrowly restricting
democratic discussion to argument can silence or devalue groups; the chapter then
proposes a broader communicative model. The primary PDF opened, but the web tool
returned no extractable text lines. I read only the short opening excerpt returned
by a targeted search; its exact printed page is unverified. No further claims about
the chapter are made here.
