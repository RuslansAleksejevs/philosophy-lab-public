# A causal effect can outgrow its target practice

## Status and question

This is an expanded argument proposal, not a finished essay. It develops idea-103 as an explicit descendant. No primary source or literature search was performed in this stage, and originality remains unknown.

The question is: when does a randomized intervention support a claim about a social practice, rather than only a claim about the package researchers assigned?

## Revised claim

Random assignment can identify the effect of an assigned intervention package while leaving open whether that package tested the social kind named in the research question. A kind-specific interpretation therefore needs a defensible link between the assigned conditions and the target practice. That link may take either of two forms: the target practice is sufficiently preserved across the relevant conditions, or the study explicitly treats a change in the practice's status as part of the causal question.

This revises the original proposal's apparent demand for “stability.” Experiments need not preserve a social kind in every arm. They may study how an intervention transforms a practice. The narrower requirement is interpretive: researchers must say whether they are estimating a package effect, an effect within a named practice, or an effect that includes a transformation of that practice. Randomization alone does not choose among those targets.

## Argument

Let assignment be `Z`, with `Z=0` and `Z=1` assigning two specified packages. Let `Y(z)` be the outcome under package `z`, and let `K(z)` indicate whether the resulting act or relationship counts as the target kind under a stated rule. Under the usual assumptions for the design, random assignment can identify the average package contrast, `E[Y(1) - Y(0)]`. This is already a real causal result. The proposal does not dispute it.

The additional conclusion “the intervention changed the outcome within practice K” says more. It treats the two packages as conditions on, or versions of, the same target practice. That conclusion needs a further premise: the practice described by the research question is instantiated in both arms, or the theory explicitly includes a transition in kind as part of the target effect. Without that premise, the contrast can combine a change in the outcome with a change in what the activity is.

This is not ordinary confounding. Random assignment may balance background causes perfectly and still leave the target interpretation unsettled. Randomization controls how assignment is related to units; it does not establish that the assigned conditions bear the intended relation to a socially constituted practice. Nor does better outcome measurement by itself resolve the question if the intervention changes the social relation being measured.

The need for this extra premise is conditional on the claim. If researchers ask only whether a guaranteed reward changes the amount of helping, the package contrast can answer that question. If they ask whether gratitude sustains gifts, they need a reason to treat the rewarded and unrewarded activities as gifts in the relevant sense. If they ask whether guaranteed rewards transform gifts into exchanges, the possible change of kind is not a defect: it is part of the target.

The inference is therefore:

1. A randomized design can support a causal contrast between its assigned packages, subject to its ordinary design and measurement assumptions.
2. A claim about the effect of an intervention within a social kind adds a claim that the packages instantiate that kind in the relevant respects, or that any change of kind is included in the estimand.
3. Social kinds can depend on relations such as obligation, recognition, or reciprocal expectation; an intervention may alter those relations rather than merely alter an external measurement of them.
4. Randomization establishes no premise about whether those constitutive relations remain in place.
5. Therefore, a kind-specific conclusion requires a separate, outcome-independent warrant for the target link, or a revised claim that makes the transformation itself part of the target.

The phrase “outcome-independent” matters. A researcher should specify the target and the conditions for applying its category without first seeing whether the predicted outcome occurred. This does not make the category true or non-arbitrary. It makes the proposed interpretation answerable to evidence rather than a retrospective way to save a theory.

## Worked case: rewarded help described as a gift

Consider a hypothetical experiment asking whether gratitude sustains gift-giving. One group receives help with no promised return. The other is guaranteed a reward for providing the same help. Suppose the study defines its target, before seeing results, as voluntary help that is not governed by a guaranteed reciprocal return. Suppose also that participants in the second condition understand the guaranteed reward as such a return. These are stipulations for the example, not claims about actual participants or a universal definition of gifts.

If the reward condition produces more help, randomization can support the claim that the assigned reward package increased the measured helping outcome. It does not, on those stipulations alone, show that gratitude sustains gifts: the rewarded activity falls outside the stated target in a way that changes the social relation under study. The result may be about a transition from gift to exchange, about help across two different practices, or about a broader category that includes both. The data and estimand must determine which interpretation is defensible; the label “gift” cannot settle it by itself.

The study could instead ask whether reward changes the status participants give the activity, treating that status as an outcome. Or it could choose a gratitude manipulation designed to leave the no-guaranteed-return condition intact, then check and justify that target relation independently of the measured helping result. If the study conditions on only those participants who still call the act a gift after treatment, it must explain the resulting selection problem: post-assignment classification need not preserve the balance created by randomization. A design might need a more explicit estimand and additional assumptions to support a claim about acts remaining in K under both conditions.

These alternatives show why the proposal does not prescribe one preferred experimental design. It separates three questions that are easy to conflate: what causal package was assigned, what kind of practice was instantiated, and which of those is the object of the claim.

## Strong objection and answer

The strongest objection is that this is only construct validity or the familiar problem of treatment versions. If a variable does not measure the intended construct, researchers already owe a validity argument. Calling the construct a social kind adds no new inference rule. Worse, social categories are contested and may change through the very interventions being studied. A demand to fix their constitutive conditions before an experiment could freeze a contested practice and let a theorist reject any unwelcome result as a change of kind.

The objection is serious. The proposal does not yet establish that its distinction is original or irreducible to established accounts of construct validity, consistency, treatment versions, or social ontology. Its provisional answer is narrower: sometimes the intervention may change the relation that makes the practice count as the target, rather than merely provide a noisy indicator of an otherwise unchanged construct. In that case the question is not just whether a measure tracks K; it is whether the treatment contrast has K as its object at all. Whether current frameworks already make this distinction fully is an explicit source test, not a conclusion of this expansion.

The anti-immunization objection also forces a revision. A universal rule that the kind must remain stable would be too strong. It could be used after the fact to shield a theory from a failed prediction. The revised proposal requires the researcher to declare the target relation and the observations that would count against it before using the outcome to defend the theory. A predeclared criterion can still be wrong or contestable, but a result that violates it narrows the claim rather than licensing a silent redefinition. If no independently defensible criterion can be given, the kind-specific claim should be suspended or replaced by the package-level claim.

The Astra assessment supplied the distinct challenge that a substantive stability rule might merely redescribe construct validity. The Sol report declares no separate assessor contribution; it independently highlights the risk that outcome-sensitive conditions could immunize a theory. The proposed answer accepts both as open constraints. It narrows the original stability demand to a conditional target-link warrant and leaves novelty unresolved.

## What could defeat or narrow the proposal

The argument would be substantially narrowed if primary-source comparison showed that established construct-validity or treatment-version accounts already require exactly this target-link analysis and adequately handle interventions that change constitutive relations. In that case the example might remain useful, but the claimed philosophical contribution would be an application rather than a new distinction.

The claim would also need narrowing if the target kind can be specified without ambiguity as a property of the intervention package itself. Then the package contrast may already be the right kind-specific estimand. Conversely, if plausible criteria for K cannot be fixed without encoding the theory's desired outcome, the proposal has not solved the immunization problem and should be rejected as an interpretive safeguard.

A counterexample would be a well-specified study in which the intervention changes participants' recognition or reciprocal expectations, yet an independently justified theory shows that this change is irrelevant to the identity of K and the original kind-specific conclusion remains warranted. Such a case would show that the proposed bridge is unnecessary for at least that class of claim.

## Significance and next research question

If correct, the proposal would clarify the reach of experimental conclusions without denying the causal power of randomization. A study may have a sound package-level estimate and still overstate what it has learned about a named social practice. Making the target link explicit could also let experiments study social transformation directly rather than treating every change in category membership as a methodological failure.

The central unresolved question is whether this distinction adds anything to existing work on construct validity, treatment versions, causal estimands, and the constitution of social kinds. No answer is claimed here. The next probe should compare those primary-source frameworks against the gift case, formalize which estimands are identified when `K(0)` and `K(1)` can differ, and test whether the proposed outcome-independent criterion prevents post-hoc rescue without imposing a fixed essence on social practices.
