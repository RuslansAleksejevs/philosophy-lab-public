# Focused source check of D1

## Finding

The central historical claims in D1 are supported by the authorized source packet and by targeted direct checks of Cavendish and Hooke. I found no clear quotation or attribution error in the claims central to D1. Its main four-part task test is the author's analytic reconstruction; it should not be presented as Cavendish's own formal method. D1 usually keeps that distinction clear.

## Decisive checks

Cavendish's Part I, chapter III expressly says she cannot give a solid technical judgment because she has neither studied nor practised micrography. She immediately raises the question whether it represents exterior shapes exactly, lists ways light, shadow, refraction, reflection, medium and position can alter appearances, and explicitly allows that a glass may sometimes present a true picture. The needle/knife argument is conditional: if the natural figure really were as shown, the knife would not cut and the blunt needle would not pierce as it does. The passage then shifts to the alleged costs of microscopy and asks whether experimental philosophers might find more beneficial arts, including nourishment and architecture. D1 accurately separates the evidential role of the tool example from its broader assessment of research priorities. The separation is interpretive, not an explicit division signalled by Cavendish; D1 appropriately recognizes the balance as open.

Hooke's Preface supports D1's account of his stated aim. He says he examined objects in several lights and positions before drawing, because apparent form could differ with lighting and posture; he names the difficulty of distinguishing a prominence from a depression and a shadow from a stain. This is more than mere repetition to show stability: Hooke says it was a way to discover what he took to be the true form. D1 represents that ambition fairly and notes that the procedure does not guarantee success.

In Observation I, Hooke says the needle pierces softer bodies but appears under magnification to have a broad, blunt and irregular end. In Observation II, he describes a razor as irregular, pitted, thickened in places and rugged, then asks conditionally what it could do if truly as represented. D1's distinction between a task-defeating geometry and roughness compatible with cutting is a defensible analytic reconstruction of those observations; the texts do not themselves spell out its modern four-condition test. D1 does not claim the roughness is absent.

Observation XXV describes pressing a nettle bristle, seeing liquid rise and subside, and relating penetration to burning pain. D1 accurately calls this intervention evidence for a proposed mechanism, while limiting the force of the account because the passage reports no controls for all alternative causes.

The bounded secondary excerpts support D1's stated limits: Lawson identifies the shared Cavendish-Hooke example and observer/object reading; Killeen makes a stronger metaphysical context relevant to the surface/interior language; Boettcher treats the material participation of the instrument; Clucas's abstract marks the nearby rhetoric/probabilism debate; Wilkins's abstract supports caution against a blanket anti-experimentalist picture. These are not complete works. Lawson is a relevant online post rather than a peer-reviewed article. Nothing in the checked excerpts establishes field-wide novelty.

## Source-related cautions for the independent critic

1. The historical record supports the conditional and the sequence from practical example to social criticism. D1's distinction between two arguments is plausible and useful, but remains an interpretation; it should not suggest Cavendish herself articulated the task-relative bridge in D1's contemporary terminology.
2. D1's use of the dossier as a source of literature context should be translated into reader-facing citations to the named works. A private dossier is not evidence for readers. Secondary-source coverage is limited to the exact excerpts and abstracts listed in `reading-coverage-002.json`.
3. The direct Hooke and Cavendish web checks were excerpt-only. The failed first Hooke URL was an incorrect path; the exact URL from the prepared source manifest then succeeded. The web tool did not provide a fresh origin timestamp, only cached crawl-age metadata.

## Methods and limits

I read all permitted source-packet files in full as local excerpts/summaries, then checked the primary passages with targeted web open/find results. I did not consult any earlier source-check or critique. I did not make a new secondary literature search and did not read complete secondary works or plate images. This confirms attribution and passage fit within the bounded corpus; it does not establish historical completeness or originality.
