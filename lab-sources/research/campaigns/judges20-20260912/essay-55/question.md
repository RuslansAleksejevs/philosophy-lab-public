# Question and initial contribution plan

Chosen question: When public instructions admit two reasonable interpretations, can an authoritative, sincere clarification remove all commitments incurred under the earlier wording?

Initial hypothesis: A public invitation to coordinate can generate a determinate commitment about how ambiguity will be resolved even when it fails to establish one determinate shared instruction. Where the issuer controls the wording, foreseeably invites costly reliance, and subsequently selects an interpretation, it owes a protected route through the correction to audiences that reasonably relied before clarification. This need not make their interpretation semantically correct or give them authority to veto the correction. The intended philosophical contribution is a defended distinction between authority over clarified content and authority to shift the costs of its retrospective application, together with a treatment of commitments under incompatible reasonable readings.

The hypothesis is provisional. I will test whether this merely redescribes familiar promissory obligation or general fairness, whether common-ground and conversational-score accounts already accommodate it, and whether there are genuinely linguistic grounds for attributing a resolution commitment rather than simply an external moral duty. The essay must state the narrower result if that is all the argument supports. No priority claim will be made from a bounded literature search.

Candidate examples to develop: a public access notice interpreted by two audiences; later clarification accepted by everyone; prior reasonable reliance that cannot be undone without cost. Vary issuer knowledge, audience reasonableness, opportunity for cheap clarification, the character of the invited action, and whether the issuer made an explicit promise. Distinguish reliance privately chosen from reliance that the communication invited.

Rejected initial angles:

1. A general claim that public meaning belongs to audiences rather than speakers. This risks an undefended transfer from moral accountability to semantic correctness and cannot handle incompatible audience readings.
2. A general attack on dog-whistle disclaimers. The literature is large, the initial idea insufficiently distinct, and the scope would require more political examples than the argument needs.
3. A rule that every reasonable interpretation binds its speaker. Incompatible readings can demand impossible conduct, and responsibility depends on the character of the speech situation.
4. A novel label for ambiguity-related harms. A label alone would not establish an original philosophical contribution.

Planned primary-source inquiry: Grice on utterer meaning and intention; Stalnaker on presupposition and common ground; Lewis on conversational score and accommodation; and primary work on assurance, linguistic commitments, or conversational obligations if accessible. Search will also check the adjacent ideas of reliance, correction, public interpretation, and ambiguity.
