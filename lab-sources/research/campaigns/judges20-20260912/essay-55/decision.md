## Shared objection 1: eligibility, blame and remedial duties

Both critics correctly identified that the draft’s appeal to responsiveness and desert did not answer an issuer who distinguishes the suitability of a work from the entrant’s culpability. Accepted. The final explicitly grants that the narrow restriction may constitute a legitimate artistic project, and that a grey photograph can remain ineligible even if its maker acted reasonably. The earlier suggestion that defective guidance by itself suspends a criterion is not defended.

The replacement premise is a defeasible opportunity-sensitive reliance duty: an issuer that solicits effort through foreseeably inadequate guidance owes reasonable measures to protect the invited opportunity; where a measure is available at modest cost without defeating the legitimate purpose or comparably important claims, rejecting it needs a reason. The rationale is that the invitation recruited effort toward participation, not merely spending to be refunded.

The final gives determinate answers to the reviewers’ requested cases. When the centre can offer an adequate further preparation period, preserve its narrow rule, notify all, keep submissions sealed, permit all to revise and reasonably cover additional expenses, it should do so before making exclusion final. The original grey work need never become eligible, and refusing the effective further opportunity can leave its eventual exclusion justified. By contrast, exclusion can proceed where an immutable installation and timing make the work unsuitable and no reasonable opportunity-preserving measure remains; responsibility and duties to repair the loss survive. The distinction concerns feasible protection of the invited opportunity, not a judgment of blame. A common-design-challenge purpose gives still weaker grounds for accepting disparities created by inadequate guidance.

Qualified disagreement: no complete account of institutional authority is needed to defend this conditional result. The final nevertheless abandons an unrestricted inference from defective notice to invalid eligibility and does not treat the existence of an original rule as a universal answer to remedial claims.

## Shared objection 2: comparative interest and baseline

Accepted. The final identifies the protected interest as an effective opportunity to prepare work under a sufficiently communicated common eligibility condition, not equal odds, identical resources or the best work each could imagine under any rule. It uses the requested contrast with a printmaker who knowingly chooses a private restriction under a clearly broad announcement, holding work and expenses fixed. That person lacks the guidance-induced claim concerning a supposedly common restriction.

The final also distinguishes a printmaker whose choice of work was materially affected from one whose work was unchanged. Both can have a procedural complaint against a late waiver of an actual narrow common condition; only the former has the additional production-related claim. Under a legitimate narrow offer, neither has a right to submit an abandoned grey design. The relevant complaint is about changing the common field after reliance, not merely a counterfactual improvement in artistic quality.

The worked remedy uses the actual legitimate narrow offer as its baseline and gives all entrants a sufficient informed preparation opportunity. Residual advantages from prior effort do not alone defeat it because the protected interest is not total resource equality. Independent remaining harms may require further repair. If the actual offer was broad, the appropriate baseline can instead allow grey and provide revision opportunities for those who unnecessarily restricted themselves. Unresolved actual content can leave the baseline unsettled; the final acknowledges that limit.

## First critic objection 3 and second critic objection 2: diagnostic scope

Accepted. The draft’s suggestion to drop the word “correct” while assessing a remedy was unstable. It has been removed. The final replaces it with an explicit controlled comparison: keep earlier audience evidence and actions fixed while varying actual content through features unavailable at the earlier time. Historical reasonableness stays fixed; present remedial claims can change with the actual offer. This targets the illicit transfer from eventual semantic vindication to earlier adequate opportunity without assuming that content is irrelevant to repair.

This comparison is conditional on a coherent content-fixing theory permitting the stipulated difference. The essay does not purport to settle such a theory and separately explains the case where no unique content is established.

## Shared originality objection

Accepted. No defensible verdict separating the result from a fully opportunity-sensitive and relationally informed reliance theory has been established. The final therefore says explicitly that it specifies an application of that account, not a new basic normative principle. It credits Kolodny and Wallace’s discussion as richer than expenditure reimbursement. The distinctive analytical proposal is that clarified content can be irrelevant to a change in historical warrant yet relevant to selecting a common remedial baseline. This exposes opposed mistakes: treating the losing reading as unreasonable, and treating reasonable reliance as a right to have that reading fulfilled.

The distinction between clarification retaining a direction and retraction withdrawing its central output remains. The charitable treatment of Grice, Stalnaker and Lewis remains. These are supported distinctions, not evidence of comprehensive novelty. Earlier abandoned broad novelty claims remain in question.md and sources.md.

## Limits and reopen triggers

The final is a conditional normative argument using stipulated cases. It supplies no universal remedy algorithm, exhaustive priority search, general theory of institutional legitimacy or empirical measure of what audiences reasonably understand. Borderline costs, competing interests and adequate preparation periods require further judgment. The remaining originality is analytical and local; the final has not received another independent review after this revision.

Reopen if a critic shows that the proposed opportunity-preserving duty licenses inadequate remedies despite the stated conditions, that its common-condition interest is not plausibly induced by the invitation, that the controlled content comparison is incoherent for all relevant accounts, or that the same analysis is already fully developed in adjacent work.
