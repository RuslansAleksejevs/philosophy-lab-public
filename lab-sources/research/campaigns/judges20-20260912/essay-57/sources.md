## Direction after reading, before drafting

The initial danger must be stated carefully: subtracting a correction does NOT necessarily eliminate evidence for or against its governing theory. A correction fixed independently of the readings may preserve exactly the discrepancy a rival predicts. The strongest candidate is therefore a contrast among fixed correction, adaptive fitting, and retaining the fitted correction itself. The argument should place a precise limit on inference from corrected agreement; show how accurate measurement of a reference-condition quantity can be evidentially blind to the response to conditions; and locate explanatory evidence in a retained correction record where appropriate. It should permit limited-purpose measurement and compressed sufficient records, not demand total archival preservation. Boyd and Fisher supply important antecedents that must be acknowledged.
