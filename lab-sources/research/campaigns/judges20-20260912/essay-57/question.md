# Essay 57 — question and initial contribution plan

## Question

When scientists correct a measurement to remove an influence, what must remain observable for that measurement to support an explanation of the influence itself? Can a perfectly calibrated number become inadequate evidence precisely because its correction is successful?

## Initial plan

Investigate the difference between a measurement's success at reporting its stated quantity and its ability to support an explanatory comparison. The candidate contribution is a condition on explanatory use: a correction may standardize reported values, but the evidential record must preserve the contrasts needed to distinguish the explanations under consideration. Try to derive a practical minimum stronger than the vague instruction to save raw data and weaker than a demand never to correct measurements.

Use a simple worked case of clocks whose outputs are adjusted for gravitational or environmental frequency shifts if official metrological sources support it. Investigate the VIM distinctions among measurand, calibration, correction and adjustment. Test whether the proposed condition merely restates familiar theory-ladenness, model dependence, underdetermination, or data-processing objections. The strongest anticipated rival is that correctly specified measurands settle the matter: an explanation requiring other quantities simply needs other measurements. The essay must show what, if anything, follows for the design and warrant of a measurement practice beyond this observation.

Provisional argument, not yet adopted: suitability for explanation belongs to a retained record relative to a contrast, and cannot be inferred from accuracy of its headline value. Some high precision practices can preserve a quantity while erasing evidence relevant to how it behaves. A defensible norm should identify which discarded information matters and allow reasonable limits on future-oriented preservation.

## Rejected initial angles

1. A general attack on operational definitions. Rejected because familiar, overbroad, and apt to confuse useful conventional standards with denial of underlying reality.
2. The claim that all correction is circular because it assumes a theory. Rejected because independent tests and independently measured corrections can warrant model use; theory involvement alone does not establish a vicious circle.
3. A general injunction to preserve all raw data. Rejected as an inadequate philosophical contribution and often impractical. The open issue is contrast-specific preservation and what it authorizes one to infer.
4. A social-metric case about standardized test scores. Set aside to keep the first investigation on physical measurement, where correct operational performance and explanatory loss can be sharply separated.
