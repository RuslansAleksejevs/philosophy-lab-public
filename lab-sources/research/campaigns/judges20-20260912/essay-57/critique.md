## Reconstruction and assessment

The essay distinguishes accurate reporting of a reference-condition quantity from evidence discriminating explanations of environmental responsiveness. A fixed correction can preserve a rival's residual; a correction fitted to the observed difference can eliminate that residual while reporting the reference value correctly. Retaining the fitted correction may restore the explanatory contrast. Consequently, inferential authority depends on the full procedure and the question being asked, rather than on the precision of its headline result.

The deterministic argument at lines 19–35 is sound under its assumptions. Its strongest merit is the explicit comparison of fixed and fitted correction. That comparison prevents a familiar but mistaken inference from theory involvement to circularity. The treatment of obligations at lines 53–57 also deserves preservation: a limited-purpose measurement need not anticipate every future question. The prose is clear, and the author fairly acknowledges close antecedents. I recommend revision, principally to sharpen the evidential claim and establish why its particular development matters. I found no decisive refutation of the central example.

## Three located concerns

### 1. Keep three different evidential achievements separate

Locations: lines 5–7, 33–43 and 67.

Line 7 says investigators must establish that processing would not also have produced agreement under the rival. Read literally, this excludes ordinary probabilistic evidence: agreement could occur under both explanations while being much likelier under one. Line 39 explicitly repairs this, so the problem is an inconsistent opening formulation, not ignorance of likelihoods.

A further distinction matters. Different distributions make discrimination possible somewhere; they do not guarantee that the outcome actually observed discriminates. For example, let explanation A assign probabilities (1/2, 1/2, 0) to outcomes (a, b, c), and B assign (1/2, 0, 1/2). The distributions differ, but observing a gives neither explanation a likelihood advantage. Finally, preserving some discrimination is weaker than statistical sufficiency: the former permits substantial information loss. The discussion at lines 41–43 should not suggest equivalence to Fisher's stronger preservation criterion.

Revision test: distinguish the procedure's capacity to discriminate, the actual observation's likelihood contribution, and preservation of all information relevant to the contrast. State explicitly that the probabilities are conditional on the same available background evidence and protocol. The essay already calls its requirement necessary rather than sufficient; make the opening and practical prescription equally precise. No elaborate mathematical apparatus is needed.

Judge grounding: Easwaran's *Dr. Truthlove*, opening pp. 816–819, carefully separates underlying epistemic states from their formal representation and identifies idealizations. Applying that discipline here is my inference from the documented method; these particular examples and objections are mine.

### 2. The control extension needs a stated causal boundary

Locations: lines 49–51 and 65–67.

The bath example usefully shows that preserving earlier temperature readings need not preserve the evidence one needs. However, changing recorded numbers and physically holding temperature fixed are different operations. Cooling can change the process whose behavior is being investigated. Two heat-producing mechanisms might release identical heat at the controlled temperature while differing dramatically as temperature rises. Their controller histories could therefore agree even though their uncontrolled behavior differs. This does not contradict the stipulated example, which assumes different heat releases; it limits what the example establishes.

The phrase about a defensible account of other heat exchanges acknowledges part of the problem, but intervention on the source itself is a distinct issue. A correction amount can reveal heat released under control without revealing what would have happened without control.

Revision test: specify whether the explanatory target is heat release under the maintained conditions or the source's counterfactual behavior without cooling. Show how the rival predictions pass through the intervention in each case, or restrict the extension to the first target. Present this as a condition of the analogy, not as an objection to control experiments generally.

Judge grounding: the demand to separate an idealized result from its application is supported by the supplied formal-epistemology lens. The causal objection is generic philosophical assessment, not a documented Easwaran position on calorimetry.

### 3. The remaining originality burden is substantial

Locations: lines 41–47, 53–57 and 71.

The essay is commendably candid about Fisher, Boyd and its limited reading. Nevertheless, a valid application of familiar information-loss reasoning does not yet establish a significant new philosophical contribution. Boyd's directly available definition already includes processing history, and her abstract explicitly concerns reuse across epistemic contexts and constraints on competing theories. Merely changing the inferential target is therefore not enough to distinguish this essay from that project. The fixed/fitted comparison and the conditional allocation of responsibility are the more promising distinctive developments.

Revision test: identify one precise inference, preservation decision or distribution of responsibilities that the essay resolves beyond the general demand for enriched evidence. For example, test whether the relevant evidence must be delivered by the measurement service or may remain reliably accessible through another investigator. Lines 63 and 65 already permit trustworthy reports and identifying evidence; make the unit of assessment consistently the available evidential arrangement, rather than an arbitrarily bounded file. This could sharpen the practical contribution without demanding universal archives. A broader novelty claim should await closer reading of the nearest literature.

Judge grounding: Easwaran's Hedden review, pp. 108–110, directly questions unexplained boundaries around epistemic agents and requests engagement with neglected literature. Applying that concern to distributed measurement evidence is an extrapolation. His appreciation of ambitious exploration at p. 105 also counsels developing the promising consequence rather than merely accumulating caveats.
