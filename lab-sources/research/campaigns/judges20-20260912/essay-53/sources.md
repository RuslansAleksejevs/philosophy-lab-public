## Argument boundary before drafting

The broad idea that role fidelity can coexist with wrongful organizational participation is established territory. So is the distinction between answerability and liability, and now the search also shows a direct prior separation of review closure from epistemic finality. The proposed contribution must therefore be a limited argument about the preservation and transfer of the standing of a previously surfaced serious objection. It must identify what an individual actually does when closing a process, without attributing the whole organizational act to every member.

The strongest rival is that the entire proposal is merely an instance of ordinary negligence or a duty not to mislead. The draft should grant its compatibility with negligence, test a case where no one is misled, and explain the specific duty that makes the closure negligent or otherwise wrongful. A second rival holds that institutional design duties belong only to management; the draft should distinguish authority over a local signal from responsibility for designing the whole system.

Rejected after reading: treating the procedural/epistemic authority distinction itself as new; alleging that Mortimer's passive mode automatically excuses every knowing harmful local action; presenting a named recipient as enough to transfer an objection without attention to their competence, remit and capacity; equating keeping an objection active with indefinitely halting a project.

Status: interpretations bounded by the passages above; philosophical validity and originality are unestablished and await separate criticism. Mandatory source receipt must precede draft composition.
