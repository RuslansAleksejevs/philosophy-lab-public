## Responses to reviewer 1

1. **Moral demand, institutional transition and individual duty. Accepted.** The draft sometimes spoke as if a completion could extinguish an external moral requirement, although its own argument implied that the requirement survived. final.md now explicitly separates the continuing moral requirement, institutional clearance, and individual responsibility for supplying clearance. The employee changes institutional eligibility to proceed; she cannot abolish morality's demand. The single-planner argument is limited to showing that division of work does not itself answer the objection. A further premise of reasonable care in knowingly exercising authorizing power supplies the bridge to the employee. The locked-gate comparison supports that premise; the measurement-provider comparison explains the different application without claiming that authorization creates a unique fundamental duty or always greater blame. The two-agency test is now explicit: removing a formal concern block and activating a process that never had one are distinct transitions, but both can involve an unjustified knowing authorization. Proposed design improvements are separately identified near the end.

2. **No answer versus a bad answer. Accepted as a substantive correction to the central diagnosis.** The original common-knowledge agency already endorses an assignment-based policy; describing it as having no identifiable decision was too strong. The opening now calls it a bad policy and analyzes the employee's knowing authorization under it. The four-response comparison treats silence, explicit assignment-based refusal, an inadequate selfish reason, and justified action under uncertainty separately. The explicit assignment-based refusal and selfish reason receive the same assessment when their inadequacy is reasonably apparent: neither supplies an adequate basis for the employee's unqualified clearance, and the agency's moral requirement remains unsatisfied. A bad answer can locate a decision without vindicating it. The employee can discharge the narrow duty if her own clearance is genuinely qualified and a manager uses separate authority to override; merely recording the manager's bad answer while continuing to authorize unconditionally does not discharge it. This revises the draft's permissive suggestion about the selfish supervisor. The template test distinguishes adequate policy application, repetition of a bad policy and recording an actual override. Wording or sincerity alone decides none of these.

3. **Nondecisive contributions. Accepted the need for a ground; retained a bounded extension.** The eight-of-ten example now defines the power supplied by an operative approval: a standing conditional permission usable with seven other valid approvals. The first and eighth approvals exercise that power; the tenth also can if it remains operative, for example as a replacement after another approval is withdrawn. If the tenth entry instead arrives after irrevocable authorization and is retained only as a specialist record, this closure duty does not apply. A qualification of one operative approval limits what may be done on that person's authority; it need not preserve an organization-wide block. I therefore abandon the broader language that treated every such act as controlling closure or withdrawing the same requirement. I retain the point that a veto is not necessary: others' possible authorizations do not justify my defective operative authorization. Purely expressive, ineffective endorsement is left outside the claim rather than smuggled into it.

**Other reviewer-1 points.** The unsupported frequency claim that organizations “often” couple completion and clearance has been removed; the invented institution supplies a conditional example. The Kahl paragraph now acknowledges identifiable procedural authority and outstanding-risk decisions as well as reopenability, and denies that merely distinguishing closure from finality establishes novelty. The bounded source acknowledgments, transfer/transmission distinction, timing example, and protections against scapegoating remain. No real catastrophe or extra length was added merely for effect.

## Responses to reviewer 2

1. **Ground of the duty and comparison with negligence. Accepted.** The moral/institutional distinction and the extra reasonable-care premise answer the same central gap identified by reviewer 1. The information-versus-authorization comparison now explicitly allows equal warning duties when costs and opportunities are equal; it does not assume a greater obligation merely from the label “clearance.” The additional requirement is to attend to what one's operative authorization makes available. Accurate warnings cannot by themselves repair an unqualified authorization when all the facts are already shared. The safe-outcome comparison now expressly establishes independence from actual injury, not a difference from negligence, and does not purport to establish a free-standing right to a preferred procedure. The final presents a specification of care whose value lies in its object, conditions and permitted responses.

2. **Discharge by responsible treatment versus decision attribution. Accepted.** The final no longer treats naming an answerer as an adequate substitute for treatment. The four responses are discussed in prose. Silence leaves the basis deficient; both explicit bad reasons leave it deficient where the employee can reasonably recognize the defect; justified treatment can support reliance. A separate, actually exercised managerial override can relocate authorization while leaving the moral requirement unsatisfied, provided the employee does not keep furnishing unqualified clearance. This is discharge of one local duty, not a certificate that her remaining conduct is permissible. The distinction between an adequately supported authorization, an identified wrongful authorization, and the employee's own qualified contribution is maintained throughout the revision. Kiener's account is not characterized as a blanket declaration covering everything an organization does; the final explicitly leaves his broader account intact.

3. **Actual authorization rather than available information or inert endorsement. Accepted and bounded.** The operative-permission test applies consistently to first, eighth and tenth contributions. Redundancy is distinguished from irrelevance. The test is what the practice permits actors to do on the contribution's authority, including whether it can replace another current approval, not the document's name, an employee's expressive attitude, or bare causal contribution. An entry retained only as data after irrevocable authorization receives no closure-duty verdict. Other duties may still apply. This retains a limited nondecisive case without claiming to resolve every issue of collective participation.

**Other reviewer-2 points.** The transfer's connection to the decision remains central, including the Monday/Friday assessment and Tuesday implementation. Reasonable knowledge, ability and cost remain conditions on the practical requirement and blame. Source coverage is unchanged and explicitly partial. Neither reviewer performed an expanded priority search; the revision therefore does not upgrade originality claims.

## Defended choices and rejected revisions

I retain a normative principle about reasonable care in authorizing, rather than claim a new basic moral category. Compatibility with negligence is not a defeat, but it is also not evidence of originality. I retain a strictly functional extension to nondecisive operative permissions because the authority exercised can be the same despite a different marginal effect. I decline to require each employee to guarantee an organization-wide continuing block; she can control the scope of her own permission while others act on independent authority. That limitation prevents the proposal from pretending to solve all organizational wrongdoing.

Rejected alternatives now preserved by this decision: retaining the claim that an internal act extinguishes a moral demand; treating an explicit selfish answer as adequate for unqualified clearance; treating an assignment-based refusal as uniquely not a decision; equating every completion, signature or data entry with operative authorization; requiring a unilateral veto; using safe outcomes to establish a sui generis procedural wrong; claiming novelty merely against later reopenability. draft.md remains unchanged as the original record of those weaker formulations.
