# Question and initial contribution plan — essay 53

AI-originated research question: When does a person who may complete or close an organizational procedure acquire responsibility for a serious objection that falls outside their assigned expertise or authority?

Provisional contribution: defend a limited duty attached to closing a process whose completion licenses organizational action. A person need not decide every substantive concern, but may not knowingly allow a decision-relevant, credible, unresolved concern already raised within the process to disappear merely because nobody's assigned job includes answering it. An authorization to close may carry a duty to preserve the concern's practical standing, secure an explicit handoff, or qualify the completion signal. This could locate a responsibility before the bad outcome without pretending that every participant controls the outcome or has unlimited expertise.

The difficult version is not dishonest certification. Each local completion report may be literally accurate, and every report may describe only a narrow role; nonetheless their conventionally authorized combination may extinguish an unresolved objection. The essay must determine which participant, if any, has an obligation in that case, when honest reliance is permissible, and whether the claim adds anything to established accounts of complicity, collective responsibility, role morality, and the problem of many hands.

Initial argument plan: use controlled invented organizational examples; distinguish substantive authority from authority over a completion signal; identify the normative basis of an objection's continuing standing; formulate a bounded closure principle; assess rival explanations through ordinary negligence, undertaking, complicity, collective agency and institutional design; delimit blame in conditions of coercion, unclear conventions and reasonable ignorance.

Rejected initial angles (preserved, not treated as findings):
1. Every member of a harmful organization shares responsibility simply by participating. Too sweeping; it obscures knowledge, influence and the specific division of labour.
2. Each participant must retain a unilateral veto over wrongdoing. Often infeasible, and conflates a right to qualify one's contribution with power to settle the organization’s action.
3. Institutions should maintain better concern registers. Potentially useful engineering, but a record-keeping proposal is not itself a philosophical contribution.
4. A new general theory of collective blame. Too broad for the evidence and argument that this essay can support.

Reopen triggers: established literature already develops the same limited duty; plausible cases make all narrow-role workers responsible for all risks; the principle reduces without remainder to a ban on knowingly false assertions; or credible concerns cannot be bounded without undermining reasonable coordination.
