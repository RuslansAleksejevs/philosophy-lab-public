## Primary philosophical and legal-theoretical sources

1. **Thomas Christiano, “Reply to Critics of The Constitution of Equality” (2011), Journal of Ethics and Social Philosophy 5(3), DOI 10.26556/jesp.v5i3.117.** Exact PDF and extracted text saved as sources/christiano2011.pdf and .txt. Read printed pp. 1–4 and 12–14 (PDF pp. 2–5 and 13–15): public equality, conditional authority, the limits of judicial review, and persistent minorities. URL: https://www.jesp.org/pdf/36346f73-90e0-819f-97d2-cda0dceac43a. The publisher's landing page says 1–15 while the body ends at printed p. 14; the draft will cite specific printed pages without inventing a reconciliation. His 2008 book was discovered in publisher metadata but not read. This reply, rather than the unexamined book, supports the manuscript's attribution.

2. **Philip Pettit, “Depoliticizing Democracy” (2004), Ratio Juris 17(1): 52–65.** Read the retrieved primary text, concentrating on §2.2–2.3, printed pp. 60–63; the earlier extract also exposed much of pp. 52–59. Actual web-extracted source text is saved in sources/pettit2004-initial-web-extract.txt and sources/pettit2004-contestation-web-extract.txt. URL: https://ppettit.scholar.princeton.edu/document/1616. Exact PDF download returned 403; no retained PDF is claimed. The source already supplies individual contestation, protection against contestatory burdens, and advance institutional safeguards. The initial idea is not original at that level.

3. **Gabriel L. Levine, “Democratically Durable Regulation” (2023), American Journal of Law and Equality 3: 283–318, DOI 10.1162/ajle_a_00059.** Read substantial indexed excerpts from the publisher, especially Part II.B, Principles 4 and 5, plus introduction and conclusion; saved in sources/levine2023-web-extract.txt. URL: https://direct.mit.edu/ajle/article/doi/10.1162/ajle_a_00059/117629/DEMOCRATICALLY-DURABLE-REGULATION. Direct HTML retrieval returned 403; web search exposed primary text with gaps. This is excerpt coverage, not full reading. Levine already advocates evaluating policy's democratic feedback and building review/revision triggers into regulation. Those general proposals cannot be offered as this essay's contribution.

4. **Jeremy Waldron, “The Core of the Case Against Judicial Review” (2006), Yale Law Journal 115: 1346–1406.** Exact 61-page scan downloaded from the university-hosted copy: https://www.humanities.mcmaster.ca/~walucho/3Q3/Waldron.Core%20Case%20Judicial%20Review%20Yale%20LJ.pdf. Read visually the complete printed pp. 1359–1361 and 1399–1402 (PDF pp. 14–16 and 54–57). These page PNGs are preserved. Text extraction recovered only headers/copyright notices and is explicitly unsuitable for reading the article body. The passages establish the conditional character of the argument, the persistence of disagreement about democracy's conditions, and the need for comparative institutional judgment. No full-paper reading is claimed.

5. **Daryl J. Levinson and Benjamin I. Sachs, “Political Entrenchment and Public Law” (2015), Yale Law Journal 125: 400–482.** Exact publisher HTML and derived text saved as sources/levinson-sachs2015.html and .txt. URL: https://yalelawjournal.org/article/political-entrenchment-and-public-law. Read the introduction/search excerpts, Part III.A.2–3 discussion of baselines, mixed motives, prediction and enforcement, Part III.B.2, and conclusion; exact derived-text reads were lines 2640–2940 and 3160–3460, alongside the saved search extract. Not read in full. They already identify functional entrenchment through substantive policy and emphasize that preserving the prior distribution of political power is no neutral standard.

## Search scope and rejected claims after reading

Web searches included: democratic legitimacy/political decisions/future contestation/losers/political equality; Pettit/contestatory democracy; democracy/equality over time/irreversible decisions; Waldron/core case; policy feedback/normative democracy; democratic self-correction; democratically durable regulation; political entrenchment/public law; democracy/remedies/self-undermining; democracy/contestation/circular/policy. Primary publisher or author-hosted evidence was prioritized. Search output used for the final overlap check is retained as sources/originality-search-web.txt. Unrelated search hits are retrieval context, not evidence for the argument.

The claim that policy can weaken its opponents is old. So are continuing political equality, individual contestation, preemptive safeguards, functional entrenchment and review triggers. The proposed draft will therefore concentrate on a more limited argument about the adequacy of a remedy offered as justification: whether that remedy still operates under the disputed failure scenario, and whether the need to prove a political injury can itself depend on capacities exposed to that injury. Its contribution must be assessed as a proposed specification and defense of an existing democratic commitment, not the discovery of policy feedback or contestability.

The search was not exhaustive. John Hart Ely, Michael Klarman, Richard Lazarus, Elisabeth Ellis, K. Sabeel Rahman and additional work cited in these sources were encountered only through citations or snippets and were not independently read. No positions will be attributed to them as independently established findings. A future critic may identify closer antecedents. Institutional examples in the draft will be stipulated hypotheticals, not empirical claims about actual transport policy or a jurisdiction's current law. No historical or current legal proposition is needed to establish the central argument.
