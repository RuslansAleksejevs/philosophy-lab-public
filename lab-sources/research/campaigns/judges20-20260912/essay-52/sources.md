## Primary reading

### Richard Holton, “Intention and Weakness of Will” (1999)

Publication: Journal of Philosophy 96(5): 241–262. Author-hosted manuscript: https://web.mit.edu/holton/www/pubs/Weakness.pdf. Retrieved successfully by curl after an initial sandbox DNS failure. Saved as sources/holton-1999.pdf and .txt. Read manuscript pp. 1–8 and the beginning of p. 9, especially §§1–2 and §3(ii). Status: excerpt, not the full seventeen-page manuscript. Exact relevant locators: manuscript p. 3 on coordination and limited deliberative resources; pp. 5–6 on reconsideration and exception clauses; p. 6, four rules of thumb; pp. 7–8 on contrary-inclination-defeating intentions and the boundary with stubbornness. Journal metadata corroborated by Holton's own bibliography, saved through web retrieval records.

Use: the paper already rejects unqualified persistence, allows reasonable revision without antecedently specified conditional content, and evaluates tendencies to reconsider. These are starting points, not this essay's innovations. The health example used in the preliminary question also resembles a case in Holton's later paper; the essay should develop a different central case and explicit new inferential step.

### Richard Holton, “Rational Resolve” (2004)

Publication: Philosophical Review 113(4): 507–535. Author-hosted prepublication manuscript: https://web.mit.edu/holton/www/pubs/rational.resolve.a4.pdf. Saved as sources/holton-2004.pdf and .txt. Read main text pp. 1–20 across web and local extraction; references pp. 21–22 also inspected. A combined tool output truncated part of pp. 13–14; p. 14 was read again separately, and the surrounding relevant argument was available in the untruncated source and local reads. Conservatively the manifest marks extensive excerpt coverage, rather than certify a flawless full-paper pass. Core locators fully read: pp. 7–8, “Second strategy: the two-tier account of resolutions,” especially p. 8 and notes 14–15; pp. 9–11, “The problem of akratic resolution”; pp. 16–17, “When is resolution rational?”

Use: resisting reconsideration can preserve rational action without actual contrary all-things-considered judgment. Monitoring and thresholds are explicit, including sensitivity to new information; conflicting reconsideration principles require learned discrimination. Any contribution here must go beyond “monitor your resolutions.” No claim in the draft will rely on treating the empirical work reported in 2004 as currently established psychology.

### Manuela M. Veloso, Martha E. Pollack and Michael T. Cox, “Rationale-Based Monitoring for Planning in Dynamic Environments” (1998)

Publication: AIPS-98 proceedings, pp. 171–179. Official proceedings PDF: https://cdn.aaai.org/AIPS/1998/AIPS98-021.pdf. Downloaded original and saved layout text. Read pp. 171–173 and opening portions of p. 174; remainder downloaded but not read. Status: excerpt. Core locators: p. 172, “Rationale-Based Monitoring” and three stages of monitoring; p. 173, “Plan-Based and Alternative-Based Monitors.” OCR and two-column extraction have some defects but the cited conceptual distinction is legible.

Use: selecting monitors from the reasons for planning decisions, including rejected alternatives, is established technical work. This essay does not introduce rationale-based monitoring or claim a new planning architecture. Its normative question is whether a method of persisting can retain a justification that depends on a safeguard after predictably dismantling that safeguard.

### Alfonso Anaya, “‘Absent Contrary Indication’: On a Pernicious Form of Epistemic Luck, and its Epistemic Agency Antidote” (2023; online 2021)

Publication: Erkenntnis 88: 2341–2364. DOI https://doi.org/10.1007/s10670-021-00455-0. Read the accessible publisher's complete main text, §§1–5, and the displayed notes, through successive web opens. Saved exact returned text in sources/epistemic-defeaters-reading.json, sources/epistemic-defeaters-reading-2.json and sources/epistemic-defeaters-reading-3.json. Status: full main text; not claiming examination of every linked reference. Core locators: §2.1 on kinds of defeaters; §§3.1–3.2 on lucky absence of defeat; §4 on responsiveness, monitoring, demands and regress; §5 on epistemic agency.

Use: an important close antecedent. Mere actual absence of defeaters differs from readiness to address them, and the latter can require appropriate strategies without constant checking. This substantially limits novelty. The draft will distinguish its practical, justification-relative constraint and its consequence for repairing an intention's information arrangements from Anaya's necessary condition for knowledge. It need not assume his anti-luck argument or transfer standards for knowledge directly to action.
