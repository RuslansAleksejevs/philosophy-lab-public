# First review: When an Institution Must Stop Speaking for a Claim

Reviewer: independent AI context /root/reviewer1_58. Assigned lens: Daniel Greco profile v1, used as a bounded methodological simulation, not a prediction of his judgment. Review date: 2026-09-24. Recommendation: REVISE.

## Reconstruction and merits

The essay distinguishes preserving a past assertion, retaining a justified belief, and offering an answer under continuing expert oversight. Its central argument is conditional: inviting audiences to delegate some inquiry requires arrangements capable of doing that work; therefore an institution whose correction route disappears must restore the route or narrow its invitation. This need not require retracting the proposition or lowering first-order confidence. The museum case makes the distinction accessible, and the refusal to equate institutional failure with individual culpability is valuable. The essay also correctly limits its ambition: it neither supplies a general account of group knowledge nor requires every assertion to receive perpetual monitoring.

The strongest contribution is the prospective timing of the requirement. An institution can misdescribe the inquiry it provides before receiving any contrary evidence. That point survives even if the manuscript's stronger language about a distinct epistemic entitlement needs narrowing. Three issues deserve priority.

## 1. What fixes the offered role?

Locations: the paragraphs beginning “What is distinctive about maintaining an answer,” “This does not make every current assertion,” and “Reasonable is doing substantive work here.”

The argument works most easily when the institution explicitly offers continuing oversight. But then the conclusion nearly follows from the description of its service. In more interesting cases, a current catalogue, expert reputation, and readers' reliance do not settle which activities the institution offers. A reader might reasonably expect the museum to answer a submitted objection while having no grounds to expect active discovery of objections. Another might treat the catalogue merely as the best extant report. The manuscript moves among recognition, receipt, assessment, and monitoring without fixing which of these the opening institution undertakes.

This is an ambiguity rather than a contradiction. Nonetheless, allowing the “advertised role” to determine reasonable arrangements, while identifying the role through reasonable reliance, threatens circularity. The principle could fit every verdict by adjusting its antecedent.

Revision test: contrast two institutions with the same displayed attribution and evidence but specified differences in public practices. Explain which observable difference licenses delegation of which task. Then test an ambiguous case where audience expectations exceed the institution's public undertaking. State what adjudicates that mismatch independently of the desired verdict. This objection is profile-grounded: Greco's documented concern about context-dependent applications motivates asking what selects the relevant context rather than merely allowing context to select a result.

## 2. Is a standing route necessary, or only a way to provide the service?

Locations: the opening missing-link case, “The continuity requirement has three connected parts,” and “Temporary interruption is compatible with that capacity.”

The opening establishes that no office is responsible for transmission; it does not yet establish that serious challenges cannot reach someone able to act. Informal escalation could reliably repair gaps when needed. Conversely, formally assigned routes can exist without reliably delivering competent assessment. The later insistence on a “functioning route” improves the claim but leaves its counterfactual test unspecified.

Consider a museum whose director can and reliably will commission an outside specialist upon receiving a credible objection, although it maintains neither a specialist committee nor a prearranged referral chain. Is this a functioning route, or a change in arrangements that reveals present failure? The distinction between sleeping personnel and missing structure does not decide. Reconstitution on demand may be an ordinary arrangement. If any dependable disposition to improvise counts as a route, say so; the contribution then concerns response capacity, not necessarily institutional ownership of a standing connection.

Revision test: specify how the failed museum behaves when a serious challenge actually arrives, holding ordinary opportunities for escalation fixed. Compare it with a successfully improvising museum and a formally organized but ineffective one. Defend the relevant level of reliability without requiring a numerical threshold. This is the most direct profile-grounded concern: the supplied Greco profile recommends testing how a thought experiment's stipulated capacities manifest in behavior. It does not establish that Greco endorses any particular institutional criterion.

## 3. What makes the conclusion epistemic and philosophically additional?

Locations: “The second objection is that this is merely an ethical duty,” its reply, and the paragraph identifying the advance over Kukla.

The reply shows that the represented activity is inquiry. It does not yet show that an independently epistemic norm, rather than a general norm against misrepresenting services, explains the duty. A transport service can retain accurate historical journey times while misleading people about whether it still operates. No explicit promise is necessary there either. Thus dispensing with promises does not defeat the strongest deflationary rival. This is an unsupported explanatory step, not a refutation of the practical conclusion.

Revision test: explain whether the proposed epistemic requirement is distinct in its grounding or simply a useful application of a general representational obligation. Either answer is defensible. If the latter, locate the contribution in the neglected distinction between evidence preservation and inquiry delegation, and explain what that distinction predicts beyond ordinary disclosure duties. Avoid treating “anticipatory” alone as proof of novelty: ordinary duties to disclose unavailable services also arise before customers suffer failure. This is principally generic philosophical assessment, supported more loosely by the profile's separation of framework merit from application success.
