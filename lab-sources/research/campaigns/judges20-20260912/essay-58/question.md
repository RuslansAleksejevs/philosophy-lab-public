# Essay 58: question and initial contribution plan

Date: 2026-09-12 UTC. Role: independent AI author, /root/author_58. Human contribution: neutral field and style allocation only.

## Question

What distinguishes a collective's continuing epistemic entitlement to endorse a claim from the entitlement it had when the claim was first accepted? In particular, can individually reasonable reliance practices leave a group continuing to speak with epistemic authority while nobody is responsible for noticing and conveying the defeat of the evidence on which its claim rests?

## Proposed contribution to test

Develop a conditional account of continuing collective endorsement. An institution that invites others to treat a claim as its current considered view may acquire a limited obligation to preserve a route by which relevant defeaters could change that endorsement. This would concern ownership and continuity of correction, rather than requiring every member to possess the original evidence, endless inquiry, or exhaustive preservation. The philosophical target is the relationship between testimonial warrant, the temporal character of endorsement, and distributed epistemic responsibility. A possible distinctive argument is that no individual violation is necessary for a collective to misrepresent the current epistemic standing of an answer.

This is an initial hypothesis, not an originality claim. Research will test it against epistemic dependence, assurance accounts of testimony, group epistemology, and forgotten-evidence objections. The draft may narrow or revise the hypothesis in response to sources.

## Cases to develop

A standing reference report whose authors depart; a claim copied through independently reasonable departments; a one-time historical assertion whose issuer undertakes no continuing endorsement. Separate the truth of a claim, the rationality of its users, and the responsibility of the body still speaking for it.

## Rejected initial angles

1. "Consensus is not independent evidence." True and important but too close to familiar dependence and double-counting problems; it does not itself yield a distinctive philosophical contribution.
2. A universal duty to retain all evidence supporting an institutional claim. Excessively demanding and vulnerable to the legitimate preservation of knowledge after the reasons for acquisition have been forgotten.
3. A general recommendation that science improve correction systems. Too easily becomes an administrative proposal without a defended normative distinction.

## Planned research scope

Read accessible primary texts on the indispensability of epistemic dependence and the responsibility involved in giving testimony; inspect source-grounded work on collective epistemology and testimony/retraction. Save exact retrieved bytes and distinguish full, excerpt, and metadata reading. Use a bounded adjacent-literature search; do not represent it as proving novelty or completeness.
