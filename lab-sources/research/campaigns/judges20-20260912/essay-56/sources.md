## Refinement and rejected extensions after reading

The initial proposal that a responsible critic must specify a definite stopping rule in advance is set aside. Callard's discussion and cases of delayed aesthetic rewards show why such a rule can be too demanding. The draft will instead defend revisability of personalized recommendations: unsuccessful attempts require some responsive basis for renewing the demand, not a fixed number of minutes or a predicted pleasure. I also reject the stronger claims that all art must be readily accessible, that novice disappointment by itself refutes artistic value, and that a failed route warrants diagnosing an enduring personal incapacity. These rejected extensions remain part of the record.

The forthcoming examples are invented cases, not unrecorded observations of artworks or experimental findings. No empirical claim about typical audience behavior is supported by this small philosophical corpus.
