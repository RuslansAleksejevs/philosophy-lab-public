# Essay 51: initial question and contribution plan

## Question

When may someone stop investigating because they expect other people to correct an error? More specifically: can a group possess permission to stop through a circle of such expectations, when nobody has an independently supported reason to remain capable of discovering a mistake?

## Proposed contribution to investigate

An absence of warnings can look like reassurance even when the decision to stop looking is what prevents warnings from being produced. The proposed essay will distinguish evidence for an answer from the justification for ceasing inquiry. It will test a constraint on the latter: permission to stop that relies on future correction must be supported by a correction route whose operation does not depend wholly on the very stopping permissions being justified. Call this provisional idea the correction-route condition; terminology is dispensable.

The main case will compare two groups with the same present evidence and sincere mutual trust. In one, a designated observer will continue to receive diagnostic information after others stop. In the other, each person assumes someone else remains responsible, and this assumption causes everybody to stop. The intended philosophical work is to explain why the second group's inference fails without saying that all reliance on another person's silence is illegitimate, that everybody must investigate everything, or that sufficiently strong first-order evidence disappears merely because their arrangements are poor.

The contribution is not established. It may turn out to be an application of familiar work on testimony, epistemic circularity, social expectations, or the norms of inquiry. The literature search must test those overlaps and narrow the claim. A promising distinctive result would be a criterion separating benign distributed inquiry from self-disabling mutual reassurance, together with an account of what changes when a person learns that their correction route is absent. The strongest rival is that ordinary evidential assessment already explains everything: once the agent includes evidence about the warning process, there is no need for an additional norm of inquiry. The draft should grant this rival its strongest version and identify whether the proposed condition is a derivation, an independent principle, or merely a useful diagnostic.

## Intended argument and limits

1. Construct a controlled case in which current support for an answer is held fixed while arrangements for obtaining corrective evidence differ.
2. Distinguish three things: current confidence, permission to cease active searching, and entitlement to treat later silence as confirming evidence.
3. Explain the specific circularity, if any, in allowing each participant's reason to stop to be supplied by another participant's similarly dependent reason.
4. Give a limited positive condition which permits bounded, inexpensive, intermittent, and delegated correction; it must not entail perpetual inquiry or universal monitoring.
5. Test against independent strong evidence, common deadlines, probabilistic monitoring, chains and cycles of competent experts, unknown division of labour, and obligations arising only from practical stakes.

No claim will be made that the new condition is sufficient for justified belief, a decision procedure for all inquiry, or a demonstrated historical first. Philosophical significance would come from explaining a failure that can persist in groups of honest competent people with justified current answers, and from locating the repair in the allocation of future evidence acquisition rather than in compulsory immediate doubt.

## Rejected initial angles

- **A general thesis that choosing not to investigate defeats an otherwise supported belief.** Too strong: it risks confusing reasons to acquire evidence with evidence for the proposition and unfairly penalizing ordinary finite inquiry.
- **A general objection to selective stopping in statistical inquiry.** Bayesian treatments can make the stopping rule irrelevant given appropriately specified exact data. Without careful technical qualifications this would be misleading, and the familiar selection-bias diagnosis alone is not a new philosophical contribution.
- **A general duty to preserve possible defeaters.** Too broad and potentially demanding. Finite agents routinely lose access to evidence permissibly. The narrower target is a stopping justification that itself appeals to continuing correction.
- **A novel label for an echo chamber.** A new name would add little. The chosen case should work without deliberate exclusion, distrust of outsiders, or motivated reasoning, and the source review will test its overlap with existing social epistemology.
