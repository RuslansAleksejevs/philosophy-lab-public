## Substantive changes

The revision accepts the critic's central objection. The draft stated a “demand” on historical writing but did not fully derive it. The final now identifies the error as a transfer of warrant: support for a coarse fact is made to appear as support for a causal or motivational link that distinguishes two rivals. It argues that evidence for a fact shared by the rivals cannot by itself support the feature on which they differ. The claimed contribution is narrowed to a diagnostic bridge and practical response, not a new evidential norm.

The revision replaces broad talk of a process that “removed the difference” with a four-part test: two rival accounts, a production model, specified surviving features, and shared facts held conditional. It adds the counterexample in which signatures remain informative even though speeches are suppressed. It also treats imperfect copying as graded weakening rather than complete neutrality.

The evidential and presentational duties are now separate. The evidential duty always forbids counting a neutralized feature as comparative support. Public disclosure is required when a rival preserves the supported facts, has non-negligible plausibility from evidence or background knowledge, and differs on a link central to the explanation. The response scales with the importance of that link and the strength of the production evidence.

The final engages Malaterre's sections 5–7 directly and acknowledges that his discussion of local underdetermination in connected narratives is close to this essay. It states the narrower proposed addition: a diagnosis of warrant transfer and a response aimed at explanatory links. Trouillot's stages are corrected to fact creation, fact assembly, fact retrieval, and retrospective significance.

The Venetian case is expanded from a brief mention into a worked, though still schematic, application. It identifies the shared official continuity, the rival interpretations of calm and suppressed conflict, the weakening of official calm as evidence, and the role of non-governmental sources as an evidential route outside the suppression process. The final also states that the application does not independently verify the underlying archival history.

I accept all six prioritized revisions. I reject only a possible stronger interpretation of the request for a “real application”: the available source record supports a careful application based on the article's introduction and conclusion, but not a new empirical reconstruction of the 1569 revolt. The final marks this boundary instead of pretending to have read the archival materials or the article's middle sections.

## Language changes

The opening changes “A town council agrees to raise a tax” to “A town council votes to raise a tax,” avoiding an early suggestion of voluntary unity. “Sign after threats” becomes “sign under threat.”

The thesis changes from the abstract sentence about a process that “removed the difference” to a concrete conditional: the same specified surviving features would result under either serious account. The final then says directly what follows from those features.

The draft's “passage from understanding a record's limits to writing a historical explanation” becomes the concrete problem of support for a decision seeming to cover an unsupported motive. “Keep the final decision ... fixed” becomes “Assume that both versions end with the same decision and signatures.” The dense sentence about whether “an actual feature provides a reason” becomes two plain questions about how a clue entered the record and why it is more likely under one account.

Vague uses of “difference,” “transition,” “connection,” and “demand” were replaced across the manuscript with the particular item at stake: official wording, an omitted attitude, a causal link, comparative support, or a duty to disclose uncertainty. The last line now refers to what a recording process “was capable of preserving,” avoiding an implication that all preservation was consciously designed.

The entire essay was checked for the same language problems, not only the passages quoted by the critic. Definitions now follow examples, paragraphs carry one main step, and technical ideas such as comparative support and graded suppression are explained before their consequences are drawn.

## Remaining limitations

The novelty search was limited. The full works by Lange and Trouillot, the full *Silence of the Archive*, and a broad literature on narrative explanation were not read. The bridge may already have a standard formulation. The council example remains invented, and the Venetian application relies on selected sections recorded in the source notes. Judgments about serious alternatives and explanatory centrality remain contextual. The proposed disclosure threshold is defended as a workable norm, not a complete theory of historical writing.
