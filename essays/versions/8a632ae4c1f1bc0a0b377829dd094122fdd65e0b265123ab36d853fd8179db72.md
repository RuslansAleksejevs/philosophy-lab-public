## Decisions for the revision

1. Accept the critic's nearby-owner diagnostic, A-C1-N1. The general complaint exists even without prior permission. Do not assert an additional quantum of wrong solely because Rowan lent the pump. Present the proposal as a restricted application and specification of responsibility for created dependence. The permission relation determines the undertaking, communicated allocation and protected practical option; it need not provide a unique foundation for the duty.

2. Make that specification do argumentative work. An openly terminable loan does not by definition guarantee a morally permissible exit. Yet the organizer of the continuing project controls the cheap precaution and gets the discretionary benefit of discarding it. The giver has declined a continuing commitment. Under these conditions, the project must presumptively bear its preventable continuity costs. Permission makes this allocation identifiable; it does not immunize the giver from all independent reasons. The case is defeasible and no general theory banning all foreseeable disadvantage is assumed.

3. Replace the ambiguous substantial-new-moral-burden formula. Distinguish the strength of a reason against withdrawal, the personal cost of compliance, and the significance/duration of the option lost. A strong rescue duty can be cheap to obey. In this case the moral reason is weighty, while the relevant setback is that a one-day choice has been turned into two weeks without a permissible exit. The significance is not inferred simply from obligation strength. Cheap compliance weakens the demand for expensive precautions; it does not nullify a claim to modest precautions for a meaningful option.

4. Use the critic's proposed compensation/restoration test, A-C1-N2, as a clarification, not a refutation of Spafford. Reimbursement of ordinary costs does not by itself restore a still-blocked option or authorize the organizer to purchase it. A measure of advantage that includes control may reach the same result. The paper supplies a reason to include and sometimes restore that particular interest. No claim that restoration always overrides all costs is defensible.

5. Narrow the contribution statement. This is a consent-specific account of timing, baseline and remedy assembled from familiar ideas. The important payoff is that valid withdrawal, nonmanipulative intentions, continued willing participation, and subsequent cost payments can all coexist with a prior wrong. No new universal moral-tyranny principle or historical priority is claimed.

## Rejected repairs and remaining limits

- Adding a promise to keep the reserve would evade the no-promise default problem; rejected.
- Claiming prior permission uniquely grounds the whole wrong would fail the nearby-owner comparison; rejected.
- Presenting Spafford as only concerned with money or welfare would ignore his open currency of advantage; rejected.
- Discharging the giver's rescue duty because the need arose wrongfully would sacrifice innocent residents and contradict the manuscript's sound distinction; rejected.
- A universal prohibition on foreseeable discretionary cost imposition would import broader disputed commitments. The limited responsibility argument does not need it; rejected.
- Mere one-day wording is not conclusive if the disclosed project necessarily requires a safe transition. Baseline facts remain morally assessed, not read off a phrase.

The account still relies on substantive judgments about control, fair project responsibility, significance and reasonable precautions. It gives no numerical tradeoff rule. Full Goodin and full O’Brien were not read; other relevant literature may overlap. A valid archival record is not a verdict of philosophical originality or quality.
