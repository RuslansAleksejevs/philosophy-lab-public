## Substantive changes

I accepted the critic's main objection that the draft moved too freely among felt attraction, desire, welfare, preference, and judgment. The final essay now limits its norm to an all-things-considered comparative judgment under concerns the agent continues to endorse. A new section, “What exactly is being judged?”, separates this target from passing feelings and created desires.

I accepted the objection to the draft's durability test. The final now distinguishes two questions: whether a new aversion reveals an option's importance under existing concerns, and whether the aversion itself creates a new practical reason. It states that the argument answers only the first. Persistence is relevant to future experience but neither necessary nor sufficient for authority.

I accepted the critic's proposal to state a defeater norm. Known unequal attention defeats treating the change itself as evidence of improvement unless the focus tracked importance or corrected a limitation. This replaces the weaker demand that Mara must identify and explain an improvement. The revision allows tacit practical understanding and gives fallible evidence for answering the defeater: analogous experience, stability under modest reframing, matched prompts, and correction of a specific earlier omission.

I accepted the need to separate discovery, threshold failure, global ranking, and action. The safety-engineer example shows that a one-sided check may properly change action through a veto without supporting an overall ranking. The deadline discussion also makes clear that an act can be rational because of risk or option value while the ranking remains unsettled.

I accepted the originality criticism. The final presents the proposal as a precise extension of process-sensitive views and says that the limited search does not establish novelty. It connects Holton's discussion of valuation directly to the created-desire boundary.

I did not attempt the critic's suggested broader literature review in this revision. The available source record contains only limited search results for adjacent work, not enough close reading to add responsible citations. The manuscript therefore narrows its originality claim instead of implying that the wider literature has been ruled out.

## Language changes

I revised the entire manuscript for simple, natural English while retaining the philosophical qualifications.

- “Have you therefore improved it?” became “Does that mean your new judgment is better?” This fixes the unclear pronoun and identifies the object at issue.
- The abstract thesis about a “one-sided task,” “grounds,” and “priority” became a concrete rule about unequal attention, concerns the agent still accepts, and whether the focus tracked what matters.
- “The preference for Birch inherits the justification of the check” became: “I was right to check Cedar for problems. That check made Birch feel better. So I must now have good reason to judge Birch better overall.” The transfer is now stated as ordinary reasoning.
- “Later judgments have no automatic priority that defeats such evidence” became: “A judgment is not better merely because it came later.” The reasons that can favor a later judgment follow in separate sentences.
- The draft's demand that Mara “explain why this is a better appreciation” became a less intellectualized test: she may have tacit understanding, while concrete signs can answer a specific worry about the process.
- “Reliance on a changed ranking as a better assessment” became two direct questions about evidence under existing concerns and whether a newly created desire changes practical reasons.

I also shortened abstract transitions, defined salience in ordinary language, placed examples beside each distinction, and removed several noun-heavy phrases such as “transfer of authority” and “better-supported procedure.” I kept the closing thought but made the last sentence less personifying: doubt need not settle what to do next.

## Remaining limits

The essay does not provide a general theory of which constructed preferences should guide action. Its indicators of improved acquaintance are fallible, and no empirical claim is made about how often attention-driven reversals occur. The literature search is not comprehensive, so the exact norm's novelty remains unestablished. The proposal also does not deliver a mechanical rule for choices in which broad but shallow and narrow but deep comparisons remain hard to weigh.
