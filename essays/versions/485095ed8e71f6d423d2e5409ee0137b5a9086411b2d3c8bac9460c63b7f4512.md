## Substantive changes

I accepted the critic's central objection that the draft moved too quickly from an assessment gap to a teacher's duty. The final essay now separates three claims: what performance within supplied evidence can establish; what kind of practice can develop selection-assessment; and what a program that claims to cultivate domain-level independence owes. It grounds the duty in a conditional principle about the content of that program's claim, avoidable hidden control, and choices that can alter a warranted conclusion or its scope.

I accepted the request to define “consequential” and “proportionate.” The final gives three conditions for requiring disclosure and checking: the inquiry licenses a broader conclusion; hidden selection is reasonably likely to change that conclusion or its warranted scope; and students can use a check at acceptable cost and risk. Physics and literature examples show why strong influence alone does not trigger the full duty.

I accepted the objection that the draft treated one teacher as the wrong unit of control. The final speaks of a selection process or pathway, which can include a teacher, textbook, exam board, search ranking, or institution. It distributes responsibility across teacher, school, and curriculum body. An independent check is defined functionally: the original pathway cannot determine whether relevant counterevidence appears.

I accepted the medical-emergency counterexample. The final permits temporary opacity when immediate checking would hinder safe early performance. Delay is legitimate only if tied to a checkable later module, competence threshold, or assessment, and it must occur before certification of independent judgment.

I partly accepted the literature objection. The final explicitly places the broad problem among established debates and narrows the contribution to an assessment gap and practical test. I did not add substantive claims from nearby secondary works that were not fully read in the author phase. The critic's search confirms the neighboring fields but does not establish priority. The final relies only on the verified scope of Rousseau, Dewey, and Atkinson and states the access limit on Atkinson.

I rejected any demand that each lesson provide an uncontrolled check. That would ignore development, cost, and high-stakes instruction. The defended duty applies to a program before it claims success in producing independent judgment in a domain.

## Language changes

The opening changed from “A student can reach her own answer and still be kept from thinking independently” to “A student can reason for herself and still depend on her teacher in a hidden way.” This preserves the student's real first-order achievement and states the narrower problem immediately.

The central sentence changed from “When a teacher's selection materially affects what students can conclude, students need a usable way to question the selection” to explicit conditions concerning a conclusion the inquiry licenses, likely change to that conclusion or its scope, learner readiness, cost, and risk. This removes the undefined phrase “materially affects” and distinguishes a benefit from a duty.

The abstract normative sentence beginning “where an educator can reasonably help” was replaced by a stated principle: a course cannot claim domain-level independent judgment while hiding avoidable choices that may change justified conclusions. The following paragraphs defend each limit rather than packing them into “reasonably.”

“Some checks should reach materials, people, or procedures whose relevant answers the teacher cannot simply determine” became “Some checks must be able to uncover evidence that the original selection process did not control.” The revision removes awkward phrasing and shifts attention from a person to the functional source of control.

The vague timing phrase “At some point” became a criterion: before certification, students should face at least one suitable case whose result the original selection process has not fixed. The medical example then shows why that need not happen in every early lesson.

Across the essay, I shortened abstract chains, used concrete verbs such as “find,” “check,” “change,” and “narrow,” and kept the town example as the main thread. I also revised the entire discussion to use “selection-assessment” consistently while defining it once in ordinary words. The label earns its place by keeping the three claims precise; it is not presented as a new theory or proof of originality.

## Remaining limitations

The essay offers a philosophical criterion, not an empirically validated developmental scale. It does not give a complete division of institutional responsibility or settle difficult questions about what counts as an acceptable educational cost. The literature search was limited, and priority is unknown. An uncontrolled assessment can still be badly designed, inaccessible, or captured by another shared selection process.
