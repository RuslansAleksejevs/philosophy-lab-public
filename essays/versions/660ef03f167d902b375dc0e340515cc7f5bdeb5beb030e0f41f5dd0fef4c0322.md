## Argument decisions

### 1. What consent adds

The strongest criticism concerns explanatory status, not validity. A general duty against the avoidable imposition of rescue burdens can explain the central complaint even without a loan. Critic contribution `B-C1-A1`, from session `01a0dbc9-fc23-73b1-857a-7807d5e08fcb`, proposes Ellis using and then selling Ellis's own working pump, foreseeably making the nearby Rowan responsible for rescue. I accept this diagnostic comparison. It is not in D0 and must remain attributed to this critic in the response and history.

The first defensible answer offered in the critique is preferable: revocable permission specifies the option at issue and the baseline against which reliance and new responsibility must be assessed. I will not claim an additional, uniquely consent-based wrong that survives a comparison holding every general imposition consideration fixed. D0 already describes a limited connection, so this clarifies and narrows its explanatory commitment without abandoning its thesis.

The result still matters. Consent identifies what use was authorized, which commitment was withheld, and what transitions could fairly be expected. A fixed-term commitment changes these facts. An expressly revocable loan does not on its own settle every later duty, but it makes a difference to the complaint that an organizer used a grant for present use as grounds for expecting continued provision. This is an application of a general fairness constraint under a particular allocation of responsibility, not a derivation from the bare validity conditions of consent.

Rejected repair: assert that merely accepting revocable permission creates an absolute additional right against any new moral duty. This would outrun the argument and reproduce the gap the critic identified. Rejected repair: remove the consent framework entirely. That would discard the useful application and the distinction between ending the original permission and restoring morally permissible cessation.

### 2. What a substantial burden means

Critic articulation `B-C1-A2` distinguishes compliance cost from loss of a protected option. I accept it. Low present cost of extending a loan is compatible with a material restriction of control; nevertheless, the fact that refusal would be wrong cannot itself make every extension substantial.

The revision will explicitly treat the length and practical scope of the restriction, and reasonable interests in using the resource differently, as relevant. In the stipulated example the difference is between a one-day transition and possible two-week dependence. Rowan has a reasonable interest in responsiveness to changing plans even if no present competing use defeats the residents' rescue claim. A trivial delay with no significant control interest would fall outside the claimed substantial-burden principle. This condition also limits the remedy before anyone has actually wanted to withdraw.

Rejected repair: make Rowan's continuation very costly merely to remove the verbal tension. That would undermine the case's assumed genuine rescue duty and avoid the distinction rather than explain it. Rejected repair: identify a material control loss with emotional guilt, subjective reluctance or the wrongness of any refusal. The claim concerns actual duties and reasonable interests, not every felt pressure.

### 3. Reliance and Goodin

The preserved publisher abstract supports all three claims: reasonable detrimental reliance gives a pro tanto reason against withdrawal; it can support compensation; and uncompensatable reliance provides a pro tanto reason for irrevocability. D0 states the first two accurately, but the third is a nearby stronger position worth acknowledging.

The revision will acknowledge the third without attributing an unseen theory of reasonable reliance to the full chapter. The present argument is conditional on control having been retained. Its defense of the pump case also challenges whether Ellis's additional dependence is reasonably imposed, given the available reserve and disclosed one-day notice. This does not settle every hard case of reasonable, uncompensatable reliance. That unresolved limit will remain explicit.

Rejected repair: claim Goodin endorses the pump sale, or that the pump example refutes his whole account. Neither follows from the abstract. Rejected repair: claim the prior rescue duty becomes unreal because Ellis imposed it unfairly. Keller's checked argument and the manuscript's own distinction rule that out.
