## Concrete critical test

Target: Ryan Doody, “The Sunk Cost ‘Fallacy’ Is Not a Fallacy,” Ergo 6(40):1153–1190, DOI 10.3998/ergo.12405314.0006.040. Bibliographic indexes say 2019; author's research page and PDF say 2020. Relevant evidence is in search-11/12.json, especially printed p.1175 and section 5. Full PDF retrieval repeatedly failed.

Candidate inference reconstructed for testing: social coordination gives agents reason to be predictable; a coherent flattering account of past behavior makes them desirable collaborators; persistence can conceal earlier misfortune; thus some sunk-cost honoring can have a reasonable narrative rationale. This is a charitable reconstruction of one route, not an exhaustive summary of Doody.

Counterexample: an agent bought expensive training for a kind of work she now dislikes. Persisting through an optional demonstration conceals the mistaken purchase. A partner takes that persistence as evidence of lasting enthusiasm and entrusts her with the same work next year. She would work reliably on a different task if she admitted the change now. Thus the appearance of successful continuity may obstruct, rather than support, accurate coordination.

The test isolates a real distinction: ease of fitting actions to a flattering story is not equivalent to accuracy of the model used to assign later tasks. However, it does not yet provide a worthwhile new objection. In section 5 Doody expressly limits an instrumental rationale when its object is no longer a means to the end; he also considers a different defense of the desire. The counterexample falls within the admitted instrumental limit. Moreover, basic signaling literature already distinguishes individual reputational gains from information transmission. Search-10.json records adjacent primary signaling work, including Wolton's 2024 conflict model and Gryglewicz's 2022 dynamic signaling paper; these were consulted at excerpt/abstract level, not adopted as direct philosophical refutations. No equilibrium result or new explanation is established here.

Decision: do not market this as a refutation, and do not manufacture a positive gate from a new scenario. A later investigation could ask a more sharply specified question, but that would require new argument and retrieval.
