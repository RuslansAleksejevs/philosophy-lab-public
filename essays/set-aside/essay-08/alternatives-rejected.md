# Further independent directions and negative assessment

2026-09-22. Public research rationale, not manuscript. These directions were considered after rejecting P1. No novelty claim is certified by mere absence of search hits.

## P2. Destructive replication and the last specimen

Thesis considered: a successful replication may strengthen a particular finding while worsening the epistemic position of an inquiry because it consumes the only material on which a more discriminating test could have been run. Example: two hypotheses both predict a chemical signature, but differ in a microscopic feature; a second chemical assay destroys the last fragment. The act increases reliability about the shared signature while eliminating the opportunity to choose between the hypotheses.

Potential increment: distinguish the evidential benefit of a result from the epistemic effect of the act producing it, and qualify the claim that replication is always epistemically beneficial.

Adverse evidence: *Epistemic Functions of Replicability in Experimental Sciences: Defending the Orthodox View*, doi:10.1007/s10699-023-09901-4, section 6.2 (retrieval-08.json, web lines 210–221) expressly recognizes opportunity costs and case-by-case comparison against other research; section 6.1 distinguishes purposes and designs. Haponava et al., *Towards Responsible Destructive Analysis*, Internet Archaeology 69, https://www.intarch.ac.uk/journal/issue69/4/full-text.html, identifies destructive-sampling constraints and documentation. The Nature 2021 guidelines *Ethics of DNA research on human remains: five globally applicable guidelines*, doi:10.1038/s41586-021-04008-x, as returned in retrieval-07.json, already require minimizing sampled material and preserving molecular products for replication.

Assessment: the toy case is coherent but instantiates acknowledged opportunity cost. Reading the orthodox paper's 'always epistemically beneficial' as excluding costs would ignore its explicit qualification. A new tangible example does not yield a worthwhile increment. REJECT.

## P3. Retraction without erasing scientific credit

Thesis considered: scientific assessment should preserve credit for useful methods, data, and responsible correction even when a claim is withdrawn; coupling credit to an indivisible published result suppresses rational self-correction.

Adverse evidence: Ribeiro, Kalichman and Vasconcelos, *Scientists should get credit for correcting the literature*, doi:10.1038/s41562-022-01415-6, directly proposes assessment credit for good-faith self-retractions and net contributions (substantive search-returned text retrieval-07.json; direct landing page retrieval-08.json). *Self-correction prevents withdrawal syndrome*, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6745497/, excerpt in retrieval-07.json, explicitly connects retraction, lost credit, career risk, and differentiated corrective actions.

A possible objection was considered: rewarding corrections can reward the production of avoidable errors. But a simple example with two otherwise identical careers only refutes an unqualified correction bonus; clawbacks and net-contribution assessment are obvious responses already consistent with the proposal. I have no nontrivial impossibility result or independently justified alternative credit rule. REJECT rather than misrepresent the literature.

## P4. Protect skills rather than disagreement

Thesis considered: a community can justifiably keep an unfavoured research practice alive despite agreement that its theory is unlikely, because otherwise tacit competence may disappear irreversibly. Maintaining skilled capacity could preserve future inquiry without artificially preserving false belief. The intended argument distinguished diversity of practice, diversity of belief, and diversity of capabilities.

Adverse evidence: *How should we promote transient diversity in science?*, doi:10.1007/s11229-023-04037-1, sections 4.1–4.2, directly supports funding unpopular exploratory work without requiring belief heterogeneity or harmful communication restrictions. Its examples and cited mechanisms are extensively represented in retrieval-06.json and retrieval-08.json, especially web lines 165–198. The direction from belief diversity to institutionally supported practice diversity is therefore already occupied.

Tacit-skill extinction could change optimal investment compared with reversible multi-armed-bandit exploration, but I have not supplied a defended model, qualitative inference unavailable from ordinary option value, or an actual case distinguishing the proposal. Claiming novelty because the phrase 'skill extinction' is not in the retrieved text would be unsound. REJECT AS UNDEVELOPED, not as proven wholly anticipated.
