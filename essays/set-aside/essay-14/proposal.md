Status: SET_ASIDE. Date: 2026-09-22.

Strongest explored thesis: an expert can improve a hearer's understanding of a particular event while making that event less antecedently expected. A hearer who ascribed an event to an almost deterministic process can learn that the actual mechanism was stochastic and the realized branch rare. Mechanistic understanding can improve even though the appropriate pre-event probability declines.

Attempted argument: if a model treats increased derivability/expectability as necessary for understanding, an accurately understood stochastic mechanism supplies a counterexample. The explanation identifies the process and teaches its distribution without making the actual branch likely. Conditionalizing on the already observed event would make its probability one and is therefore irrelevant to the explanatory comparison. The claim concerns understanding how this event occurred, not an unavailable sufficient reason why this random branch occurred instead of every alternative.

Why this is NOT submitted as a novel contribution: Salmon's account explicitly discusses improbable outcomes being understood as well as probable ones, endorses Railton's mechanism-based account, and already distinguishes questions and the circularity of taking occurrence as an explanatory premise. The proposed testimony scenario adds no clear inference beyond that established framework. Malfatti is a possible new target, but pointing an inherited objection at a different account is not by itself enough here. The initial coherence variant fares no better: her note 4 records the nearby challenge, and her section 3.1 already permits revision prompted by testimony.
