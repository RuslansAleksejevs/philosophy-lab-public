# Essay 05: rejected proposals, 2026-09-22

Status: SET_ASIDE. This file is a public research record, not a manuscript and not a passing novelty claim. The sealed question.md remains the original choice record.

## A. Destructive information acquisition

Proposed thesis: equal immediate evidence and equal direct acquisition costs do not suffice for equal epistemic permissions when investigations differently affect later access to evidence.

Exact illustrative argument: let X and Y be independent fair binary historical facts. Procedure A reveals X and destroys the only record of Y. Procedure B reveals X while preserving that record. Both have the same immediate likelihoods, posterior, direct costs, reliability, and duration. After B the agent can discover Y too; after A it cannot. At a common later assessment time, the best policy starting with B attains zero Brier inaccuracy on both propositions, while the best policy starting with A has residual inaccuracy 1/4 on Y. Swap the procedures' physical consequences and their ranking reverses. Therefore the immediate signal description cannot determine their complete epistemic value.

Why rejected: this is an illustration of a familiar missing-state-variable problem in sequential decision making. Represent whole policies, the environmental transitions and epistemic terminal rewards, and the result follows immediately. The contemplated difference from familiar opportunity-cost arguments—no financial cost, perfect evidence, fixed target truths—does not produce a new philosophical inference. Epistemic costs already need not be financial. Neither a counterexample to Blackwell's theorem nor a refutation of teleological inquiry follows.

Closest comparisons: Pettigrew's general teleological account of inquiry; Begby's epistemic resource-allocation argument; Whitmeyer and Williams' dynamic comparisons and their cited Greenshtein antecedent. The latter's restricted action-independent model is not itself the exact destructive-test model, so I do not claim their theorem explicitly contains this example. The broader planning structure, however, plainly supplies the proposed explanation. Full Greenshtein/Pettigrew primary texts were not obtained; that prevents asserting exact duplication, but does not create a positive increment.

## B. Preserving each answer is not preserving joint inquiry

Proposed refinement: an intervention might preserve separate routes to learn X and Y while removing every route to learn both; preservation should then concern compatible investigation sequences rather than merely a list of separately answerable questions.

Argument sketch: a reusable source permits two binary queries. A one-use source permits either but not both. Losing reusability need not remove any individually listed question while it removes their jointly executable inquiry. Generalize the description to a one-use oracle answering any yes/no question: each binary question remains answerable, but no guaranteed method identifies one among four equiprobable states with one answer.

Why rejected: the distinction between separately available and jointly executable plans supplies this conclusion with no further argument. Hoshi's protocol-sensitive epistemic dynamics is an established framework directly concerned with permissible sequences of informational events. Search also located earlier discussions of joint inquiry and knowability. I found no developed increment beyond translating a familiar modal/planning distinction into preservation language. The one-query variant also changes the question type when it compares binary questions with a four-way question; that is not an original paradox.

## C. Risk aversion and the choice of accuracy scale

A materially different candidate: refusing information may reflect the adopted accuracy scale rather than an independently specified aversion to epistemic risk. Proposed direction: show that conclusions about rational information avoidance cannot hold fixed an ordinary scoring rule while freely varying the decision rule, without further constraints.

Why rejected before full development: Campbell-Moore and Salow already explicitly adjust propriety to a risk-sensitive decision rule, discuss alternative treatments of accuracy measures, and derive evidence-avoidance results. Their retrieved manuscript section 5 and the author's research description address the proposed inferential move. No stronger result was established in this attempt. Salow's more recent work also compares danger of learning across scoring rules. Changing labels would not create a contribution.

## D. Agreement after loss of independent sources

Revisited the original alternative: testimony may converge when independent evidential ancestry is lost, so apparent agreement can become less probative. Actual follow-up search located Pilditch, Hahn, and Lagnado's work on dependence and evidential support. A selective-preservation example would be an application of existing dependence analysis. No substantive increment was developed.

## Result

No proposal in this attempt has a defended nontrivial increment. This does not prove every nearby idea unoriginal. It records failure to establish a suitable contribution here. Do not issue a PASS_RELATIVE gate or use this packet to release a manuscript. A replacement attempt should start from a genuinely different problem rather than treating the rejected claims as established new results.
