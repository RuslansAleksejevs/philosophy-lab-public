# Response to both archived reviews

This response is written before the clean revision. Both reviews and the complete draft, including its bibliography, have been read. All 88 assigned inputs passed SHA256 verification. The revision below is one editorial pass, not a further review cycle. No claim that R1 is better than D0 is implied.

## Overall decision

I accept the central criticism shared by the reviews. D0's strongest example establishes a distinction between formation and persistence, but does not refute Clapp's restricted deniability rule. The revised thesis will be narrower: **Clapp's pretzel case does not establish that permissible denial terminates the performance claim his account says the original promise created. Its evidence of conversational benefit does not discriminate discharge from other ways of avoiding accountability. His constitutive-score assumption states a bridge, but its temporal interpretation and its support remain in question.**

This is an explanatory objection, not proof that all force-open promises survive denial. I do not supply a new case alleged independently to satisfy Clapp's admissibility conditions while conclusively falsifying his rule. That deficit remains and limits the result. R1 will explicitly allow a practice to give permissible denial a constitutive cancellation power.

## Review A

### M1: Settlement may lie outside the disputed class

Accepted. The workshop exchange contains confirmation that can close the promise/prediction alternative. Clapp also adopts Camp's restriction by prior conversational commitments. D0 therefore cannot treat the workshop as a decisive counterexample and then describe the contextual restriction as a new concession forced on Clapp.

R1 will make Clapp's own pretzel case the main case. For the purpose of assessing its discharge inference, I grant its stipulated initial promise, retained force-openness, permissible denial and continued epistemic standing concerning the father's original intention. I then ask whether its reported outcome supports cancellation of the original claim. The workshop survives only as a comparison showing why sentence-level openness and the admissibility of a later reinterpretation are different questions. Uncertainty about punctuality is removed as a supposed answer to the force-openness objection.

### M2: Clapp already supplies an additional rule

Accepted. LDS is a proposed score-update rule; §4 expressly adds a constitutive equivalence between performing an act and its registration. It was misleading to suggest that Clapp simply lacked a bridge. The objection concerns the evidence for that bridge and its time-indexing, not its absence.

R1 will directly address note 18. Leo's verbal challenge shows that *that reaction* remains possible and appropriate within the case's stipulations. It does not show that insisting on purchase, prolonging a quarrel or recruiting others is worthwhile. A cheap challenge and a costly pursuit of performance can rationally come apart. This does not automatically establish Dinges–Zakkou's technical untouchability condition: if that condition quantifies over every pertinent reaction, Leo's challenge prevents applying it without qualification. The narrower point is that practical non-pursuit has not been excluded. Leo's acceptance of a lesser alternative may also be negotiation or release rather than evidence of the father's unilateral power. Neither interpretation is asserted as the uniquely correct description of the vignette.

This gives reasons for rejecting the *evidential sufficiency* of the example, while leaving its rival constitutive explanation possible. No appeal to the moral unattractiveness of the father's behavior will carry the argument.

### M3: The uncertainty and recording arguments need temporal precision

Accepted. R1 will separate four questions: historical performance, a particular current claim, permission to make a particular demand or criticism, and the practical prospects of pursuing it. It will distinguish removing an accepted proposition from accepting its negation.

The workshop timeline will be explicit: formation on Thursday, denial on Friday, promised performance on Saturday, recording recovered on Sunday. The recording can confirm Thursday's promise without defeating a valid Friday cancellation. If denial instead follows Saturday's breach, liability for that breach requires its own termination rule. Neither temporal sequence licenses automatic resurrection or erasure.

The uncertainty diagnostic will require knowledge that the denial meets the restricted rule's conditions and has successfully effected the update, as well as knowledge of the proposed discharge bridge. Only then does the bridge entail knowledge that its specified current claim has ended. Disputes about admissibility, tacit acceptance, prior breach, reliance or another duty do not contradict that prediction. R1 will not treat the existence of ordinary disputes as independent proof that the restricted rule is false.

### m1: Acknowledgment can remove a genuine denial-dependent trigger

Accepted, with a reason to retain the diagnostic. The revised version is an analyst's comparison, not an actual new utterance that might renew an undertaking. It holds renewed commitment fixed. If acknowledging past formation removes a denial-dependent cancellation trigger, the test fails even though cancellation can be valid. Failure identifies precisely where independent support for such a power is needed; it does not disprove that power. Evidential correction is excluded from this comparison because acknowledgment would remove the grounds for correction.

I therefore reject only the inference that these limitations make the diagnostic useless. They make it a map of the needed justification, not a test deciding validity by itself.

### m2: A recipient's release power is not exclusive

Accepted. A promisee's power can coexist with a promisor's qualified exit power. R1 will not derive exclusivity from directedness. It will also separate waiving a particular claim from rescinding an entire joint undertaking. Gilbert's contrary discussion is retained rather than treated as unqualified support for D0's account of recipient authority.

### m3: Different speech acts have different persistence rules

Accepted. The developed argument concerns a promise's performance claim. Assertion and permission appear only as contrasts: retracting an assertion need not falsify its historical occurrence, and withdrawing permission can end future permission without making yesterday's use unauthorized. No common rule preserving every speech-generated status is proposed.

### Review A's dissent and possible defeat conditions

I preserve the formation/persistence distinction, the correction/discharge distinction and the rejection of hearer sovereignty. I agree that the absence of an algorithm is not itself a defect in this limited project. Evidence that admissible denial independently constitutes cancellation in the relevant promise practice would answer the principal objection. Evidence that only present claimability changes would instead narrow Clapp's explanatory result. These possibilities remain open.

## Review B

### 1. Attribution and bibliography audit

Accepted. The consulted Clapp passages support a strong responsibility reading, but also use the language of appropriate accountability. R1 will present three interpretations rather than attribute one unqualified thesis to him: historical reclassification, prospective claim termination, and a gate on present accountability. His fundamental assumption is stated explicitly, and his LDS/FIG restrictions are included.

McDonald remains distinct from a demonstrated post-completion veto. Her §III gives collaborative formation conditions; §IV.3 discusses absent agreement, residual wrongdoing and possible tacit agreement despite overt denial. I do not infer a general later annulment power from those passages.

Dinges–Zakkou and Peet are resources, not targets accused of overlooking non-epistemic escape. Peet's opening explicitly separates the non-epistemic phenomenon. Clapp's article identifier, e-2024-0060, has been checked in the assigned publisher HTML metadata and will be added. The final publisher pagination of McDonald and Dinges–Zakkou remains unverified; section citations refer to consulted preprint material. There is no basis for calling those inherited bibliographic entries fabricated.

### 2A–C. Carassa–Colombetti, Gilbert and Camp are substantial predecessors

Accepted. Freshly consulted primary passages in *Joint Meaning*, §§2.2–2.3 and 4.2, directly reject dissolution by changed mental states and make accepted construal normatively consequential. Their invitation example substantially anticipates the settlement/repudiation distinction. Their allowance for some commitments to end through violation is also preserved. R1 will not attribute universal indestructibility to them.

Gilbert's article in the 2015 issue, printed pp. 21–22, explicitly distinguishes psychological process from normative product and gives default non-unilateral rescission with background exceptions. The assigned primary excerpts from the 2013 collected chapter include the future-tense assurance and the qualification about promisee rescission. Fresh access to that chapter failed. I will use the retained bounded evidence, mark its limits, and not claim that 2013 is the original date of every argument.

Camp's printed p. 59 substantially anticipates the record/liability distinction and the recording example. R1 will explicitly credit both, not offer them as a new discovery. Pages 60–63 supply qualifications about deniability, blurry boundaries and the common ground of well-formed conversations; these prevent recruiting Camp as the author of an unrestricted persistence principle.

The remaining contribution is an application and refinement directed at Clapp's explanatory transition. The uncertainty argument and acknowledgment comparison are not certified novel.

### 2D. Cousens is genuine counterpressure

Accepted. Fresh consultation of §4 confirms force accumulation and also reveals explicit discussion of transitions between predictions, pledges and promises, and undoing or suspending some enacted obligations. This is stronger counterpressure than a force-addition-only gloss. R1 will preserve it and ask which prior claim changes under which norm, not declare later force change impossible. It does not supply an independent resolution of Leo's original performance claim.

### 3–4. Target engagement and contribution boundary

All six prioritized changes are addressed to the extent described above. I adopt the burden-of-justification route rather than claiming an independently established force-open counterexample. I retain the distinction between historical occurrence and current responsibility, engage the pretzel case and note 18, qualify release authority, and restrict the diagnostics. I do not accept a conclusion that the entire project must be abandoned merely because the general distinctions have predecessors: applying them to a specific proposed explanation can expose a substantive ambiguity. Whether that application is valuable enough is not settled by this editorial pass.
