# Response to the two assigned reviews

Both reviews identify the same defensible core: perfect response to retained information can coexist with an avoidable loss of future credal accuracy. I retain that core, but narrow the normative and historical conclusions. These responses are written before producing the clean manuscript. Section references below specify the planned R1 organization; they are not claims that another review cycle has occurred.

## Review A

### A1. The normative bridge — partly accepted; stronger conclusion withdrawn

The review is right that a probability identity does not establish an epistemic action norm. Removing monetary and social benefits also does not eliminate an instrumental explanation. R1 §4 therefore separates three questions: which credences fit present evidence; how good a policy is for truthful inquiry; and whether choosing that policy violates a fundamental requirement of rationality.

I defend a local bridge for the second question. Degrees of confidence can be better or worse in their relation to truth even when both are evidentially permissible in their respective information states. A foreseeable, costless improvement in that relation gives a reason **in respect of the inquiry's epistemic quality** to prefer the better retention method. The proposed reason does not entail a desire-independent duty, a fundamental epistemic action requirement, or a duty to maximize accuracy across unrelated propositions. A strictly proper squared-error measure makes the comparison explicit; §4 explains both its rationale and its limitations.

An instrumentalist can explain the voluntary choice through the agent's current aims and still accept this distinction in policy merit. That is a permitted response, not a defeated rival. Someone who reserves “epistemic reason” for fundamental requirements on factual attitudes can instead call the retention reason instrumental. The formal separation survives; a dispute about the proper classification and grounding of the reason remains. R1 states that consequence rather than presenting terminological accommodation as a complete normative victory.

### A2. Fit-only reduction versus Hedden's overall position — accepted

R1 §1 states the target as a completeness claim: policies equally successful at fitting the information they themselves leave available have equal epistemic merit for the ongoing inquiry. No cited author is asserted to endorse this exhaustive claim for voluntary retention policies.

I reread the assigned primary extraction of Hedden's §4, including notes 13–15. Fresh retrieval of the original linked PDF failed twice; that failure is retained. His instrumental-action route and his alternative account of evidence now appear alongside the derivative-fit proposal and truth-grounding alternative. R1 §5 treats the example as a limit on one explanatory component, not a refutation of his whole approach. A fit-plus-instrumental theory remains a live solution. It need not introduce fundamental norms over temporal intervals.

### A3. Independence of B's errors — accepted and repaired

R1 §2 explicitly fixes B's report distribution conditional on both the token and A's operating condition: its reliability remains 0.9 in either condition. Sensor selection is independent of those variables. This removes the under-specification without changing the intended example.

The coupled countermodel is not suppressed. R1 §2 explains that if both sensors are perfect when A is sound and random when A is faulty, the label ceases to matter and both expected squared losses are 0.05. This is a real counterexample to the draft's derivation from marginal reliability alone, not a counterexample to the now fully specified existential construction. A new exact enumeration preserves both results.

### A4. Categorical-answer tie — accepted as a scope restriction

R1 §§2 and 4 state that the inquiry is concerned with degrees of confidence, evaluated by expected squared error. If only a best color classification matters, both policies achieve expected correctness 0.9. The numerical example therefore does not supply a strict retention reason under every possible task standard. I reject the stronger demand that an existential counterexample must work for every standard: one independently defensible dimension of epistemic merit is enough to challenge the specified completeness claim. The value of fine-grained credal accuracy nevertheless remains an explicit normative commitment, not a theorem.

### A5. External-record variant — accepted

R1 §6 assigns the retention choice to the archive manager. The recipient of the shortened record is not described as irrationally forgetting something she never knew. The variant isolates information quality and supports a channel comparison; it does not settle theories of historical evidence for an agent who once possessed the label. The main counterexample remains conditional on genuine loss of epistemically available information.

### A6. Disclosure norm — accepted and separated

R1 §6 grounds limitation-reporting in an additional premise: a record should not invite a use that its information cannot support. The squared-error identity does not entail that premise. Erase already acknowledges its loss, so disclosure is not a repair of the original policy. It can prevent a further misrepresentation without recovering the lost distinction. No generic warning or arbitrary reduction from the correct current credence of 0.9 is required.

## Review B

### B1. Pettigrew as a direct predecessor — accepted

I consulted [“The cost of remembering”](https://richardpettigrew.substack.com/p/the-cost-of-remembering), especially “Evaluating forgettings,” “The value of forgetting,” and “An example.” R1 §§3–4 acknowledge his accuracy-based assessment of forgetting, his conditioning on currently possessed evidence, and retention costs. The essay no longer treats their coexistence as distinctive. Its narrower use is the delayed loss for a fixed marginal, deployed against an appeal to accidentally successful fit. His longer inquiry manuscript was not newly retrieved or read; the assigned review's failures are preserved, and its complete overlap remains unresolved.

### B2. Aevyra's overlap extends to limitation-reporting — accepted

New reading of §§4.1–4.3 and 6.2–6.4 confirms the overlap. R1 §3 explicitly maps equal-report histories with different sources onto the condition for recovering future targets. R1 §6 also acknowledges the antecedent discussion of narrower promises and explicit degradation. I make no priority claim for provenance retention, revision sufficiency, or honest reporting of compression limits. The local contribution is their application to the restricted explanatory question.

### B3. Alvarez and Schwarz — accepted with limits

Alvarez's §9 is retained as a related mathematical comparison, not support for the normative bridge. His technical theorems are not independently audited. I newly read Schwarz's introduction and §8's information-preservation and resource-limit discussion. R1 §5 contrasts his challenge to evidential proportionality with this essay's decision to grant proportionality and assess retention separately. This does not establish that Schwarz anticipated the sensor argument.

### B4. Attribution, availability, and bibliography — accepted

The manuscript retains Dogramaci's narrow use of epistemic evaluation; it does not purport to refute his semantic account by identifying a cognitive defect. Assigned primary excerpts of Dogramaci, Hedden, Podgorski, and Weatherson were consulted within recorded bounds.

An accessible [Cambridge publisher record](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/canadian-journal-of-philosophy/article/abs/memory-belief-and-time/26B195FBE6FBAAF29FDCCD7EE5690D5F) now verifies Weatherson's issue as December 2015 and pages 692–715. R1 cites that issue year while using section locators for the consulted author manuscript. The record's separate Cambridge online date is 1 January 2020; it is not silently treated as the original publication date. The earlier repository's reported 2016 date remains in inherited provenance. Schwarz is cited as the June 2025 author manuscript actually consulted; a blocked publisher page is not treated as full-text access.

### B5–B6. Core inference and contribution boundary — accepted

The revised case explicitly deletes recoverable source-sensitive conditional information, not merely a displayed label. All positive-probability retained states receive their supported conditional distribution. Equal initial credences concern only the marginal for the token, not source identity or every joint proposition. The expected-accuracy comparison uses a common model and is not pointwise dominance.

The conclusion is now conditional and local: a fit-only account of policy merit misses an accuracy difference **if** that difference is admitted as part of the inquiry's epistemic quality. It is not a new theorem, a new general retention norm, a general defeat of time-slice epistemology, or a certified priority result.

## Residual issues and evidence limits

- The grounding and classification of action reasons remain disputed; R1 does not establish a fundamental epistemic retention obligation.
- No explicit exhaustive fit-only doctrine covering these policies has been established as Hedden's own position. The historical conclusion is consequently a diagnostic limit, not an overall refutation.
- Whether irretrievable historical information remains evidence is not settled. A theory answering yes may reject the main case's account of fit while accepting the channel comparison.
- Strict benefit is established for the stated credal task, not every inquiry standard or real-world memory system.
- Full predecessor coverage and the exact priority of the combined construction are unresolved. Only recorded passages were read; no finite search warrants an exhaustive novelty claim.
- Failed fresh retrievals and inherited failures remain in sources/. Captures are tool-returned passages, not publisher-original bytes.

No claim is made that R1 must be better than D0. This is the single assigned revision, not a further selection or eligibility decision.
