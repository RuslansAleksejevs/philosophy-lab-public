A mayor keeps commissioning citizens' assemblies until one approves his policy, then presents that panel's report as the consultation's answer. Every panel may have been fairly sampled and free to deliberate. The essay asks what justifies giving the conveniently favorable report that special status.

[Original release R1](R1.md) · [Original draft](draft.md) · [Argument critique](critique-A.md) · [Literature critique](critique-B.md) · [Author response](author-response.md)

## The resulting argument

R1 separates an authentic participant judgment, evidence about wider judgments, and a report's institutional role. Fair sampling alone does not justify transferring that role to the selected final report: the rules for continuing, stopping, and choosing reports also need an account. Repeated consultation can be justified through a separate mandate or argument; the essay identifies what the appeal to fair sampling leaves unanswered.
