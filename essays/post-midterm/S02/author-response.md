# Response to the two independent reviews

## Editorial decision and limits

I retain the limited insufficiency thesis, not a general objection to repeated consultation. Both reviews distinguish that thesis from stronger claims the draft does not establish. Their most important criticism is nevertheless sound: the opening did not specify enough to show that the mayor was making the stronger claim under examination. I will make that specification explicit, distinguish three different kinds of dependence, and compare the argument with closer predecessors.

The result is offered as a bounded clarification about the attribution of citizen warrant. I do not assume that this revision is better than the draft, that its application is historically first, or that the example establishes actual misconduct. The significance objection remains a live limitation.

## Review A

### M1: Closure need not confer comparative authority — accepted

The mere sequence reject–reject–approve, followed by closure, does not establish that earlier reports were overridden. It could implement a legitimate aggregate test; the same sequence could also occur under a fixed-last rule. The draft's move from closure to supersession was insufficiently supported.

R1 §1 therefore separates the bare sequence from the target case. In the target case, the council has commissioned an advisory appraisal to which officials must answer whether favorable or unfavorable, not an existential endorsement search. The mayor controls recommissioning, repeats the unchanged task because of rejection, and treats the approving report as the appraisal that now supplies the consultation's citizen warrant. Fair recruitment is offered as the reason for that privileged attribution. These are additional hypothetical facts, not discoveries about a real mayor or inferences from the endpoint alone.

R1 §§3 and 6 also explain that an approving report can merely witness satisfaction of an independently justified threshold. Its being the stopping event need not give its reasons greater weight. The essay challenges a justification for a claimed role; it does not condemn every completed first-approval process.

### M2: Outcome dependence is not sponsor control — accepted

R1 §3 distinguishes (i) selecting by verdict, (ii) the sponsor's discretionary control of the rule or its application, and (iii) justification of the resulting role. An external gate could remain unchanged if the mayor reversed preferences. Such a gate can be independent of this sponsor while being outcome-dependent. It can also be justified; outcome neutrality is not made a condition of independence or legitimacy.

In the specified target case, sponsor control is established by the mayor's discretion and counterfactual continuation policy, not by the probability formula. The further normative premise concerns fidelity to the review's claimed function. A sponsor's satisfaction with an answer does not by itself explain why that answer discharges an outside-appraisal task. A separately justified mandate or substantive comparative argument can supply the missing reason. This premise is not presented as a general theory of democratic legitimacy.

### m1: A report-specific defect is not always needed for reopening — accepted

The draft's demand for a demonstrated defect was too broad if applied to every rerun. R1 §5 permits bounded replication to test stability, investigate uncertainty, or develop arguments from unchanged materials. It distinguishes those rationales from the particular excuse that rejection itself proves misunderstanding. A defensible reopening need not introduce new information or establish an error, but must explain the new inquiry's purpose and how either result will be used.

### Countermodels and mathematical qualifications — retained

The externally assigned asymmetric gate is a serious countermodel to a prohibition, not a negligible exception. R1 §6 explains its pathwise equivalence to an any-approval-in-three aggregate test and includes the review's loss-based illustration. My separate exact-rational check reproduces expected losses of 47/128, 11/8, and 55/64 for the any-approval, single-panel, and majority-of-three rules under the stated assumptions. These assumptions do not describe actual assemblies, and expected-loss minimization does not exhaust democratic justification.

The full-history likelihood qualification is retained and sharpened. For the specified reject–reject–approve data the likelihood ratio is 1/3 under either compatible protocol. Conditional on the first two rejections, the third approval has likelihood ratio 3. The coarser event “approval by three attempts” has ratio 63/37. No universal evidential penalty is inferred from stopping. The perfectly correlated-verdict exception and the possibility of equal terminal membership chances also remain.

### External importance and the significance objection — unresolved

The example establishes a possibility and a limit on inference, not the frequency or practical impact of report shopping. The remaining insight may be judged an application of familiar institutional-justification principles rather than a substantial separate contribution. R1 §§2 and 8 state that limit rather than treating the reviewers' qualified support as a quality verdict.

## Review B

### Closer selective-uptake literature — accepted after primary-source consultation

I read the relevant original passages rather than relying only on the review's characterization:

- Setälä (2017): publisher HTML lines 548–570 and 596–620, within the more extensive returned windows preserved in `sources/004-faq-and-uptake-passages.json` and `sources/006-uptake-boundaries-and-rules-context.json`. Strategic commissioning, selective uptake, public responses, the limits of publicity, and corrective iteration are substantial prior overlap.
- Fernández-Martínez, Font, and Smith (2023): author-provided chapter text, printed pp. 233–234, 237–239, and 249–250; especially extraction lines 310–328 and 604–667. The chapter makes justification central without requiring obedience to every recommendation. Publisher metadata supports the 2023 date; a later platform upload is not the publication year. Successful text access and inherited failed downloads are distinguished.

R1 §2 does not claim to discover post-deliberation control or the need to justify selective uptake. It asks a narrower follow-up: even when officials explain their policy choices, what warrants presenting one report as the consultation-completing appraisal? The distinction matters because a policy explanation, an accurate evidential account, and a role-conferring justification answer different questions. Whether this extension has enough additional significance remains open.

### Rerandomization and optional stopping predecessors — accepted

I consulted Stern et al. (2020), version 4, §4, especially extraction lines 194–214, and Hendriksen, de Heide, and Grünwald, version 3, §2.1, especially lines 168–281. The new captures are preserved under `sources/005-*`, `008-*`, and `010-*`.

R1 credits the existing repeated-honest-randomization mechanism. Its all-public reports remove the concealed-discarded-draw feature of Stern et al.'s example; they do not refute that predecessor. Likewise, the full-history qualification is an application consistent with existing stopping-rule independence, not a new statistical theorem. I do not extend that qualification to arbitrary priors, coarsened observations, or all goals of statistical analysis.

### Lafont's nearby parity argument — accepted with a limit

I reread printed pp. 91–92, including extraction lines 376–425, and the evidential-use discussion on pp. 96–97. R1 §2 identifies the shared-credentials objection as closely related to Lafont's point that minority participants also received the information and deliberated. The new application concerns priority across completed panels and an institutional designation rule. I do not attribute that application to Lafont or claim it is the first such extension.

### Exact observation in the likelihood table — accepted

R1 §4 explicitly distinguishes approval within the cap with the stopping index unreported from approval at attempt three under the known first-approval rule. The latter already reveals the binary reject–reject–approve history. Reading all report texts is unnecessary for that particular inference. Public texts can of course matter to evaluating arguments beyond the binary model.

### Normative inference and the promised task — accepted, without adopting a universal prohibition

The shared-feature insufficiency argument also applies to fixed-last and report-lottery rules. R1 §3 says so. The extra objection to the specified mayor comes from the mismatch between a sponsor-controlled favorable-answer rule and the role invoked to claim outside appraisal. Even there the conclusion is that the offered recruitment-based justification fails, not that no further defense could possibly succeed. Endorsement search and justified asymmetric gates remain genuine limits.

### Episode boundaries — accepted as a warrant-continuity test, not a complete taxonomy

R1 §5 uses the proposal and question, the outstanding review obligation, the authorizing mandate, and the prior reports' continuing relevance to identify continuity. A new budget line or name cannot alone erase an obligation. Material changes or a genuinely new authorization can warrant a fresh inquiry, but do not automatically make earlier objections irrelevant. Borderline cases remain disputable; the essay does not supply an exhaustive rule for classifying them.

### Recruitment and source precision — accepted

The revised conclusion states that free, informed judgment formation is stipulated in addition to honest recruitment. Recruitment alone does not guarantee it. The Flanigan comparison refers to probabilities for members of the input pool, subject to representation constraints, not equal unconditional chances for every resident. Lafont's authority and evidential-use references have separate page ranges. The temporal-selection model remains a different research object, not a failed attempt to solve report standing. No load-bearing false attribution was established by either review, so I do not invent one or discard supported comparisons.
