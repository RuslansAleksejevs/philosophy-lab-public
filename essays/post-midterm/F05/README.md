Attending to an inner image may sharpen it; trying to hold a passing thought still may destroy its movement. Even an accurate account of the experience after inspection can therefore leave its earlier state uncertain. The essay asks which restrictions on such changes would let a later observation support a claim about the past.

[Original release R1](R1.md) · [Original draft](draft.md) · [Argument critique](critique-A.md) · [Literature critique](critique-B.md) · [Author response](author-response.md)

## What changed

R1 removes an incorrect generalization about fine-grained features, separates occurrence within an interval from presence at the same time, and distinguishes deduction from probabilistic evidence. Its core observation is directional: if inspection can destroy a feature but cannot create it, later presence can establish earlier presence; the reverse restriction supports a different inference.

The contribution organizes and applies known distinctions. Whether real observation methods satisfy the required restrictions, and whether the application adds enough, remain open.
