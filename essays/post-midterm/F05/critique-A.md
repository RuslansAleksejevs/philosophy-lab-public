# F05 — Argument and counterexample review

## Outcome

**Potentially defensible, with two important local corrections and unresolved application warrant.** The central directional deductions are valid. I find no fatal objection to the expressly limited thesis. The general comparison between coarse and fine features is false without further restrictions, and the inventory discussion needs a clearer account of interval quantification and completeness. Neither finding warrants setting aside the core argument. The manuscript does not establish that an actual human inspection routine meets its conditions; it explicitly declines to do so.

Locations below refer to the unchanged, SHA256-verified `author/draft.md`, counting its blank lines. The entire manuscript, including bibliography (lines 119–131), was read. This is the argument review only, not a literature review or a rewrite. No other review was accessed.

## Charitable reconstruction

The thesis is not that destructive inspection reliably discovers earlier presence in humans, or that productive inspection reliably discovers earlier absence. It is that interference does not by itself defeat every retrospective inference. Its evidential consequences depend on the feature, temporal target, direction of inference, and grounds for the transition restriction (lines 7–11, 33–39, 111–117).

The argument has the following structure:

1. Fix one conscious feature and two temporal targets; grant accurate knowledge of its later presence or absence (lines 21–23). This is a concession isolating temporal transmission, not a proof of local introspective reliability.
2. A positive backward deduction excludes absence-to-presence: `F₁ → F₀`. A negative backward deduction excludes presence-to-absence: `F₀ → F₁`, hence `¬F₁ → ¬F₀` (lines 25–31).
3. Neither inference requires preservation in both directions. Loss can coexist with a sound positive deduction; gain can coexist with a sound negative deduction. The restrictions must cover spontaneous changes as well as inspection-caused changes (lines 33–39).
4. Correct later judgments can consequently fail to distinguish earlier persistence from inspection-induced production. Equal likelihoods, if established, leave the odds between those histories unchanged (lines 49–65).
5. Retained evidence or direct awareness of a temporally extended episode can provide other routes. They are not forced into the two-feature-preservation model (lines 67–79).
6. Successive correct judgments do not by themselves establish an earlier simultaneous inventory. Training and repeated observations also do not automatically establish transport to uninspected contexts (lines 81–111).

For a fixed, well-defined proposition pair, the deductions in step 2 are elementary but sound. A substantive epistemic application additionally needs an appropriate, warranted restriction over relevant histories—not just a conditional that happens to be true in the actual history. The essay largely recognizes this distinction.

## Concerns, ranked

### A1. Major local scope error: increasing specificity does not generally strengthen no gain

**Location:** line 41, especially its last sentence; compare lines 21, 27, and 43.

The text says that the relevant prohibition on gain becomes more demanding as the historical claim becomes more specific. That does not generally follow. Let `G` be some imagery and `F` be square imagery, so `F → G` at each time. The two no-gain restrictions prohibit different sets of transitions:

| Possible routine/history | No gain of G | No gain of F |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No imagery becomes circular imagery; squares are never introduced | Violated | Satisfied |
| Circular imagery becomes square imagery; some imagery is always present | Satisfied | Violated |

Thus `G₁ → G₀` and `F₁ → F₀` are generally incomparable restrictions. Even requiring a nonvacuous fine-feature case does not repair the ordering: a routine can preserve existing squares, sometimes create circles from nothing, and never introduce squares. Its square findings permit the fine-grained backward deduction although it fails coarse no gain.

The square conclusion is stronger than the some-imagery conclusion. But the respective transition premises need not be ordered in the same way. Confusing those two comparisons is the invalid step. Line 43's specific case—inspection supplies details to a pre-existing image—is nevertheless sound.

**What would resolve it:** limit the comparison to a specified class of refinement-producing processes, or acknowledge that each feature introduces a different restriction rather than a uniformly stronger one. This is a major correction to a general claim in the framework, not a refutation of its two deductions.

### A2. Major ambiguity: an interval inventory is not automatically a simultaneous inventory

**Locations:** line 21's question about any imagery during an interval; lines 73–75 allowing noninstantaneous targets; lines 81–91, especially 89 and 91; line 113's simultaneous totality.

The manuscript needs to distinguish these propositions:

- each listed feature occurred somewhere in the target interval;
- all listed features occurred together at some point in that interval;
- all listed features were present at one specified target time;
- all listed features persisted throughout the target interval.

If line 21 defines presence existentially, then applying the stated positive bridge separately gives `∀j ∃t∈I F_j(t)`, not `∃t∈I ∀j F_j(t)`. A tone occurring only early in I and a shape occurring only late in I satisfy the first inventory and not the second. Individually valid backward implications to occurrence-in-I do not change that fact. This is a counterexample to treating the interval-level logical bridges as sufficient for simultaneity, not to the bridges themselves.

There is an important charitable qualification. If no gain instead means a pointwise prohibition on every new onset from a common earlier time through each later inspection, later presence can establish presence at that common time. Such a stronger process restriction can secure simultaneity. Line 35 gestures toward this reading by covering onset over the whole transition. But it is stronger than simply asserting the implication between existential interval predicates in lines 21 and 31. The manuscript does not specify which temporal quantification governs its inventory application. If line 89 merely identifies some requirements, not sufficient requirements for a simultaneous inventory, there is no invalid inference there; the ambiguity remains in the explanatory payoff.

There is also a coverage issue on the negative side. Excluding a tone and a shape establishes neither general mental emptiness nor absence of an untested bodily sensation. The two-feature countermodel in lines 85–87 is valid as a stipulated toy model and does not need empirical support. But an actual total negative inventory additionally needs a justified exhaustive domain or a narrower conclusion about the tested features. Directional transport does not supply exhaustiveness.

**What would resolve it:** identify the temporal quantifier and domain for each inventory conclusion, and say whether the conditions are only partial requirements. The simultaneous example itself should be retained: it correctly shows why truthful reports at different inspection times cannot simply be conjoined as one-time reports.

### A3. Uncertain / major for an applied reading: the bridge's warrant remains schematic

**Locations:** lines 33, 37–39, 63–65, and 99–111.

The main nonlogical premise in any use of the framework is that a contextual process has the relevant directional restriction. The manuscript offers no actual routine shown to satisfy it. Its calibration discussion lists possible supports and then correctly explains why each might share the disputed assumption. It therefore does not yet demonstrate a discriminating calibration case for unattended consciousness.

A strong objection is that a thin-view model could predict the same task performance, previous reports, and retained presentations as a rich-view model while generating conscious imagery only during checking. Repeating or triangulating those particular measures would not discriminate the models. This matters to lines 103–109, not because triangulation is inherently circular, but because its success depends on genuinely different predictions or independently justified constraints.

**Author's strongest reply:** the essay is expressly a conditional reconstruction, not a validated human method. Lines 105–109 acknowledge the common-error problem and theory dependence. For that modest aim, lack of a worked calibration case is a limitation, not a failed deduction. It becomes a major unresolved concern only if the manuscript is read as establishing practical first-person authority about unattended states. I do not require an empirical experiment merely to accept its logical result.

**What would move the assessment:** a concrete case in which proposed rival histories disagree about an available measure, with an explanation of why the measure does not presuppose the disputed consciousness claim. In its absence the applied reach remains unresolved.

### A4. Minor clarification: no gain/no loss need a process-level or epistemic scope

**Locations:** lines 25–33, 37, and 63.

As actual-world material implications, the conditions are too cheap to identify a reliable method. If both `F₀` and `F₁` happen to be true, both implications are true even if the procedure is a random imagery generator. Their actual truth does not give an observer grounds for retrospective inference.

The appropriate scope could be a stipulated class of possible trials, relevant epistemic alternatives, or justified contextual counterfactuals. Line 37's specified circumstances and line 63's contextual alternatives strongly suggest this intended reading. The manuscript already excludes a complete knowledge analysis at line 33, so this is not an unaddressed fatal epistemic objection. Stating the scope would nevertheless prevent a true token implication from being mistaken for a tested transition property.

### A5. Minor scope risk: deductive restrictions are not necessary for ordinary evidential support

**Locations:** line 25's must language, line 89's must language, and lines 97 and 111; compare the explicit limits at lines 33 and 63.

An illustrative, not empirical, probabilistic counterexample helps fix the boundary. Suppose earlier presence has prior probability 0.5, later presence has probability 0.9 given earlier presence, and probability 0.1 given earlier absence. A perfectly accurate later positive judgment raises the probability of earlier presence to 0.9, although gain and loss are both possible. This does not establish knowledge or an exceptionless deduction, but it is genuine evidential support without either prohibition.

Accordingly, line 25's necessity is correct for unrestricted deductive validity from the stated premise, not for all retrospective evidence. Line 33 says almost exactly what is needed to avoid the stronger reading. Preserve that qualification when the essay moves to authority, expertise, and successful inventories. A stochastic extension is optional; consistently respecting the deductive boundary is not.

### A6. Minor, conditional on interpretation: matching an observed outcome is weaker than equal likelihood

**Locations:** lines 55–57.

Two histories can both yield the reported clear auditory image while assigning different probabilities to that report. For example, a persistence model could assign it probability 0.9 and a production model probability 0.2. Observing it would then favor the former. Compatibility alone does not establish evidential neutrality.

Line 57's equal-likelihood claim is correct, conditional on the same background evidence and nonzero likelihoods. On the charitable reading, line 55 stipulates two deterministic models that predict the same report with certainty; there is no error. The concern is only the possible slide from agreeing on what actually happened to equality of the evidential likelihoods. The essay's neutrality result should retain the latter premise.

## Strengths and dissent

The best feature is the separation of truthful local description from justified historical transport. The two truth-functional deductions withstand the obvious creation/loss objections because their premises explicitly exclude the relevant transition, not all interference. Line 35 avoids confusing an intervention's causal effect with all intervening change. Lines 41–45 correctly distinguish existence, detail, and attentional status, notwithstanding A1's overgeneralized comparison. The negative-inventory countermodel is particularly clear: every local absence can be true while global emptiness is false.

The essay also takes the strongest escape routes seriously. Reliable memory, retained information, and genuinely temporally extended awareness are not dismissed; expertise is not declared worthless; ordinary knowers are not required to formulate a theory. Demanding intact experience, explicit introspective calibration by each subject, or elimination of every conceivable history would misread the draft.

A stricter dissenting reviewer could reasonably judge the contribution too schematic: familiar logic supplies labels, while the case that would make those labels useful remains conditional. I do not infer philosophical failure from that alone. A sound limiting argument can be valuable without a new empirical method. Conversely, accepting the modest thesis is not accepting an explanation of real disagreement between trained and untrained observers, nor establishing a new practical route to unattended experience.

## What would actually defeat or narrow the thesis?

- With local accuracy and a warranted direction-specific bridge held fixed, a contrary earlier state is logically impossible. Empirical discoveries cannot refute that conditional; they can show that its premises fail in a given application.
- A spontaneous onset defeats a particular positive deduction; spontaneous disappearance defeats a particular negative deduction. Mere vividness changes need not defeat an existence claim. This is the right granularity of challenge.
- If reliable retained evidence or awareness of the earlier phase already supplies warrant, the temporal-feature bridge is not required. The manuscript admits this; such cases narrow its domain rather than refute it.
- If global presence means co-occurrence, interval-level feature implications alone may be insufficient, as A2 explains. If the process restriction instead anchors every feature to one shared target time, that objection is answered.
- The unqualified monotonic specificity claim is defeated by A1's two process classes. Correcting it leaves the core asymmetry intact.

**Final assessment:** retain the central thesis as potentially defensible. Correct the specificity comparison and settle the inventory's time/domain semantics. Keep real-method calibration, probabilistic authority, and novelty explicitly bounded. No fatal concern is established; the two major local concerns and the unresolved applied warrant should remain visible rather than being hidden by the valid elementary deductions.
