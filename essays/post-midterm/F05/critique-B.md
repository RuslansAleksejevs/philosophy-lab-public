# F05 — Role B: literature, source accuracy, contribution boundary

## Bottom line

The two Boolean inferences are valid. The draft also handles its historical sources with unusual care: it distinguishes author manuscripts from published versions, limits Schooler–Schreiber to their abstract, and does not claim that hypothetical inspection routines are established human methods. **I found no established source-attribution or quotation error in the checked claims.**

The contribution is nevertheless smaller than the opening prospectus suggests. The creation-versus-discovery problem and the accurate-local-report/false-global-inventory pattern have actual predecessors, not merely adjacent discussions. What remains is a useful, explicitly conditional organization of these problems by inference direction, especially the negative-inventory countermodel. This is not a novelty certification. Revision should make that boundary sharper, fix the temporal scope of the inventory conditions, and separate deductive guarantees from ordinary evidential support.

## 2. Additional close work and serious alternatives

**Blackmore is a missing comparison, not a decisive refutation.** Her 2002 article discusses probe-created retrospective streams, the refrigerator-light difficulty and the possibility that practice changes experience itself. The author-hosted earlier draft of her 2012 chapter directly contrasts inspection-time findings with uninspected episodes and contrasts present inquiry with immediate retrospection. These are close to F05's history/inspection distinction and training discussion. However, her rejection of a determinate probe-independent stream is much stronger than F05's conditional realism about F₀ and F₁. The draft can reject that stronger conclusion while recognizing the antecedent. Consulted locations: 2002 PDF pp. 6–8, lines 318–386, plus HTML lines 68–80; 2012 chapter draft lines 18–28, 120–161. No claim is made that her text contains the exact paired criterion. B09, B13–B14, B17. [Blackmore 2002](https://www.susanblackmore.uk/articles/there-is-no-stream-of-consciousness/), [2012 chapter draft](https://www.susanblackmore.uk/chapters/turning-on-the-light-to-see-how-the-darkness-looks/).

**An explanatory rather than independently certified calibration is a live alternative.** Block's copyedited prepublication target article explicitly proposes inference to the best explanation across psychological and neural evidence. §8 also considers using fallible measurements to model measurement error. This challenges any strengthened demand that transition principles must first receive completely independent certification. F05 mostly avoids that demand already (lines 103–111), so the alternative narrows its target rather than refutes its logic. A worthwhile revision would explain which *particular* explanatory argument its directional analysis changes. I read the opening and selected §§7–8, not the full empirical overflow argument. B16–B17, preprint pp. 12–13, lines 446–497. [Block, prepublication target article](https://www.mind-and-brain.de/fileadmin/downloads/Lectures/Block-09282006.pdf).

**Empirical creation is disputed, not simply an established premise.** Sergent et al.'s primary abstract reports better detection, orientation discrimination and visibility after postcueing and interprets the result as triggering conscious perception. That supports considering productive interventions seriously; it does not demonstrate an introspection routine satisfying no loss. Nor does the abstract alone defeat the retention alternative. Only the primary abstract was read; the full experiment was not audited. B15. [Sergent et al., primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23246406/).

## 3. The crucial inference: valid conditional logic, unfinished epistemology

At lines 25–33, the result is elementary but sound:

| Granted later knowledge | Transition excluded | Licensed earlier conclusion | Transition still allowed |
|---|---|---|---|
| F₁ | ¬F₀ ∧ F₁ | F₀ | F₀ ∧ ¬F₁ |
| ¬F₁ | F₀ ∧ ¬F₁ | ¬F₀ | ¬F₀ ∧ F₁ |

This resembles a one-sided diagnostic guarantee: grant perfect reading of the later state, then require no false historical positives or no false historical negatives. Calling the conditional no gain or no loss does not establish it. The important philosophical question remains what warrants the conditional for a specified feature, procedure, population and time span.

Two revisions matter:

1. **Keep guarantee and evidence apart.** Lines 25 and 89 use requirement language; line 33 correctly restricts the result to sufficient deductive bridges. A procedure permitting both gains and losses can still provide strong evidence. In a toy model, with prior P(F₀)=0.5, P(F₁|F₀)=0.99 and P(F₁|¬F₀)=0.01, observing F₁ yields P(F₀|F₁)=0.99 although neither absolute transition prohibition holds. This is not a proof of knowledge; it shows that the prohibitions are not necessary for strong evidential support. Specify when the discussion concerns entailment, defeasible knowledge or merely evidence. The existing caveats help but do not consistently discipline the requirement language.

2. **Offer a non-stipulated payoff.** Lines 37–39 establish possible informative interference by constructing routines with the desired transitions. That refutes a universal prohibition on reactive evidence, but no identified opponent is shown to hold that prohibition. The consulted predecessors are more discriminating. A strengthened contribution needs either a concrete disputed inference whose evaluation changes, or a defensible restricted calibration example. It need not claim a universally nonreactive introspection method.

The equal-likelihood point at line 57 is correct under common background evidence and defined posterior odds. It is basic probability, not independent novelty. The retention/record route at lines 71–79 is also a genuine escape from these particular feature-preservation conditions; the draft properly allows it. The proposal consequently applies to a restricted route from a later feature, not to all introspection or retrospective knowledge.

## 4. Inventory scope needs repair

The positive inventory has the established structural antecedent noted above. The two-feature negative countermodel at lines 85–87 is valid: both local absence reports can be true while their joint historical conclusion is false. I did not find that exact countermodel in the passages consulted. That is an unresolved priority question, not a finding of novelty.

The proposed sufficient inventory conditions at line 89 need a sharper time semantics. Line 21 initially asks whether imagery occurred *at any time during an interval*. If Fᵢ(I₀) means existence somewhere in I₀, obtaining every Fᵢ(I₀) does not establish simultaneity:

`for every i, there exists t in I₀ with Fᵢ(t)`

does not entail

`there exists t in I₀ such that every Fᵢ(t)`.

A tone can occupy the first half of I₀ and a shape the second. Even impeccable backward attributions that each occurred somewhere in I₀ do not show co-presence. This diagnoses an ambiguity in the move from binary interval predicates to simultaneous inventories, not a failure of the two Boolean implications. Stronger trajectory-wide prohibitions might fix it, but must be distinguished from the displayed endpoint implications.

For a simultaneous inventory, specify a common target time, or a common appropriately defined interval-wide predicate, and justify each bridge to that same target. For a complete emptiness verdict, also state the coverage assumption: correct exclusion of a finite list is not exclusion of every possible conscious feature. These corrections would make the negative example and inventory extension materially more useful.

## 5. Classification and required changes

- **Established attribution error:** none found in the checked load-bearing claims; no final-edition collation claimed.
- **Established structural antecedent:** successive true local checks yielding a false simultaneous positive inventory; creation/discovery ambiguity and pre/post inspection distinction.
- **Likely substantial overlap:** the general later-accuracy/historical-warrant separation and training-transfer cautions. The cited predecessors already do more than issue a blanket warning.
- **Related but distinct:** extensional temporal experience; temporal aggregation of hedonic judgments; Blackmore's stronger rejection of a determinate stream; Block's explanatory calibration.
- **Inaccessible or limited evidence:** failed cited Phillips PDF, failed Schooler PDF download, blocked PMC opens, unavailable Block Citeseer copy; selected manuscripts/abstracts only. Every failure remains in the source ledger even when another route succeeded.
- **Unresolved novelty:** exact paired feature-relative criterion and exact negative inventory countermodel. This search cannot establish that either is unprecedented.

Required revision: (1) give the positive-inventory precedent direct credit; (2) compare Blackmore without adopting her stronger metaphysics; (3) maintain version-specific locators; (4) fix common-time/interval quantification and inventory coverage; (5) consistently distinguish deductive guarantees from defeasible evidence; (6) identify an actual inferential payoff beyond restating the condition for valid backward inference.

**What survives:** a clear, compact framework showing how current first-person accuracy and historical accuracy can come apart, organized by two one-directional constraints and illustrated by dual inventory failures. That can be a worthwhile philosophical reconstruction. On the consulted evidence it is not a newly discovered general problem of introspection, a demonstrated human calibration technique, or a certified novel theorem.
