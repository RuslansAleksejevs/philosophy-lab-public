# Response to the two assigned reviews

Both reviews concern the same unchanged draft. I read the entire draft and bibliography and both reviews, verified every exact assigned input, and rechecked the official rules before additional research. This response is written before the clean R1 manuscript. The revision is one editorial pass, not a further competition or review cycle. It does not assume that R1 is better overall.

## Response to Review A

### A1: Feature specificity — accepted

The draft incorrectly orders transition restrictions by specificity. Square imagery entails imagery, but no gain of square imagery neither entails nor follows from no gain of imagery. Creating a circle from nothing can violate the coarse restriction without violating the fine one; replacing a circle with a square can do the reverse. R1 §2 removes the general ordering and uses these two process comparisons. It retains the sound special case in which an inspection supplies detail to imagery already present. Stronger conclusions and stronger transition premises are different comparisons.

### A2: Time and inventory coverage — accepted

R1 §1 defines occurrence somewhere in an interval explicitly. Section 5 distinguishes that predicate from presence at a shared target time and from persistence throughout an interval. Its sufficient positive-inventory bridges all terminate at the same target time; their conjunction therefore licenses simultaneity there. A separate pair of displayed quantifiers shows why occurrence of each feature somewhere does not license co-occurrence. Pointwise target attribution does not assume that experience consists of instantaneous atoms: target-time facts may depend on an extended episode.

The negative countermodel is retained, with its transitions made explicit. R1 distinguishes absence of every listed feature from total experiential emptiness. The latter additionally requires a justified coverage premise. Even universal no-loss for the listed features does not supply that premise. Thus temporal transport and completeness remain separate tasks.

### A3: Applied bridge warrant — accepted as an unresolved limitation, not a refutation

The revised essay still does not validate an actual human routine. I reject any implication that the valid conditional needs an empirical discovery in order to be defensible. But the review is right that the conditional alone cannot establish practical authority about unattended experience. R1 §§3 and 6 now anchor the framework to Schwitzgebel's actual coached visual-clarity comparison and Phillips's discussion of cueing. The payoff is an assessment of which calibration claim a particular argument needs, not an empirical demonstration that this claim is true.

For the visual case, evidence that naive scanning introduces detail challenges one historical positive inference. It does not by itself establish the no-loss-of-detail condition needed for a coached negative inference. Nor does a possible loss defeat that inference without contextual grounds. R1 does not decide which observer is right. This is a concrete burden-of-argument diagnosis, and its unsettled outcome is stated in the manuscript and OUTCOME.json.

### A4: Scope of transition conditions — accepted

R1 §1 defines no gain/no loss over a specified class of relevant histories for a feature, procedure, population, and time span, rather than as an accidentally true token material implication. The implications are formal representations of those scoped restrictions. A restriction needs warrant before it serves as an epistemic premise. Relevant histories do not include every imaginable sceptical scenario merely by being imaginable.

### A5: Deduction versus evidence — accepted

R1 uses requirement language only for entailment from the specified experiential premise. A toy Bayesian calculation shows that gains and losses can both occur while a later observation supplies strong historical evidence. It is labeled a stipulation, not human data or a proof of knowledge. Sections 3 and 6 also allow defeasible explanatory grounds, so the deductive framework is not silently promoted to a universal test of justified belief.

### A6: Compatibility versus equal likelihood — accepted as a clarification

The draft's conditional equal-likelihood observation was sound, but the preceding case could suggest that compatibility suffices. R1 §3 explicitly distinguishes deterministic equal predictions, equal positive likelihoods under common background evidence, and mere compatibility. The ratio claim is conditional; no equality is attributed to the real empirical theories without support.

## Response to Review B

### Attribution audit and publication versions — retained with narrower claims

The review found no established attribution error, and my selected primary checks support the limited claims used in R1. James's retention and transitive-thought passages receive separate page ranges. Schooler–Schreiber is cited only for the clean abstract, not for damaged full-text OCR. Schwitzgebel section references remain references to the dated author manuscripts. Phillips 2011 §4.3 is presented as a metaphysical account that allows an escape route, not a proof of retrospective warrant.

The exact Phillips 2018 published-format PDF failed in this session. I consulted its verified assigned final-version passages and independently retrieved the accepted manuscript. R1 cites final §§3–4 with those provenance qualifications in the bibliography; the accepted version numbers the corresponding material §§2–3. I do not merge their pagination or section numbers. The failed retrieval remains archived.

### Direct positive-inventory precedent — accepted

R1 §§3 and 5 explicitly credit Schwitzgebel 2008 §vi for the successive-clarity/simultaneity error. It no longer presents accurate local reports plus a false positive inventory as a new discovery. Schwitzgebel 2007 already separates inspection-time findings from pre-inquiry experience; this, too, is credited as a close antecedent, not merely a generic warning.

### Blackmore — accepted comparison, stronger conclusion not adopted

I consulted the author-hosted 2002 article's discussion of inspection, retrospective streams and changed experience, and the relevant observations in the earlier draft of the 2012 chapter. R1 §4 recognizes their close relation to the historical-projection and training problems. Blackmore's stronger rejection of a determinate probe-independent stream is not a consequence of my framework. Two determinate earlier histories can yield the same inspection result, so non-discrimination alone does not establish indeterminacy. This is a reason to reject that inference, not a comprehensive refutation of Blackmore's other arguments.

### Block and explanatory calibration — accepted alternative

I consulted §8 of the copyedited prepublication target article. R1 §6 allows a jointly supported explanatory theory to warrant a transition model without a wholly independent certificate of consciousness. Its objection is only to measures whose agreement is explained equally well by the same disputed assumption. It does not refute Block's empirical overflow argument, which was not fully audited.

### Postcueing and productive interventions — narrowed, no empirical overclaim

I use Phillips's own competing interpretations of postcueing, rather than claim that creation is experimentally settled. The new direct PubMed open returned metadata only; no fresh primary experimental text was obtained. R1 attributes the cueing comparison to Phillips and does not cite Sergent et al. as independently read evidence. The inherited search abstract remains preserved, but is not promoted to a full experimental check.

### Guarantee versus evidential support; inventory semantics — accepted

These are addressed with A2 and A5 above. I use the 0.9/0.1 toy model rather than import competing numerical examples as empirical estimates. Neither review's suggested probability establishes knowledge; that limitation stays explicit.

### Non-stipulated payoff — partly accepted, empirical demand rejected

The old imagined suppressive/productive routines still illustrate logical possibility, but no longer carry the whole case for usefulness. The existing visual-clarity debate supplies a concrete inferential application. The revised contribution is a feature-specific reconstruction of its two directional burdens and of the negative-inventory failure. I do not claim that the literature's authors uniformly commit a crude anti-reactivity fallacy or overlook these problems. A demand to demonstrate a successful human calibration method would exceed this essay's restricted thesis; the absence of such a method nevertheless limits its applied reach.

### Contribution boundary — accepted

R1 describes an organization and application of familiar distinctions. It makes no priority claim for the paired criteria or negative countermodel. Finite searches cannot establish their novelty. It does not claim general introspective pessimism is refuted, nor that first-person accuracy automatically extends to uninspected experience.

## What remains unresolved

1. No human inspection method has here been shown to satisfy either direction-specific restriction, particularly for wholly unattended experience. The framework therefore does not settle the rich/thin debate or the coached-vision disagreement.
2. Explanatory calibration and transfer between trained, untrained, attended and unattended contexts remain substantive, defeasible tasks; no full empirical audit was performed.
3. Priority of the exact paired framework and negative-inventory construction remains undetermined.
4. Source access remains bounded: no final-edition collation of the Schwitzgebel texts, no complete Schooler–Schreiber reading, and no fresh recovery of the exact Phillips 2018 final PDF. The available passages suffice for the modest attributions, not comprehensive literature conclusions.
