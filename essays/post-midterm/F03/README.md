If a philosopher makes a mistake about what a sentence says, how much should that weaken confidence in their judgment about reality? If a clearer formulation removes the mistake, how much confidence should return? The essay examines these questions in a debate about essence: what belongs to a thing's nature.

## What changed

The critics exposed a problem with the draft's treatment of a seemingly neutral error. Once the original judgment is included in the evidence, the error can weaken its support even if that error alone is equally likely under competing theories. R1 rebuilds the example around the combined evidence from judgment, error, and repair.

The numerical example illustrates this conditional argument; its probabilities are not psychological measurements. A successful repair can favor an account of how judgments arise without settling the metaphysics. The relative strength of the proposed mechanisms and the contribution's novelty remain open.
