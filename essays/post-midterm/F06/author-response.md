# Response to the two reviews

Both assigned reviews and the complete draft, including its bibliography, have been read. This response is written before the revised manuscript. The revision will preserve the conditional thesis, but narrow its trigger and rebuild its main argument. It will not treat either review's recommendation as proof that a change improves the essay.

## Review A: argument and counterexamples

### M1 — A persuasive assurance is not an authorization condition

**Accepted; the scope will be narrowed.** Political influence alone does not establish a division of authority. R1 §2 will distinguish reporting, reconsideration of an already accepted commitment, and a reservation of practical authority to discontinue specified governance. The stronger reservation must be adopted by a competent public body, either explicitly or through a sufficiently clear authoritative account of what its mandate approves. Its holder, object, grounds, transition horizon, and accepted risks must be identifiable. Individual reliance is neither sufficient nor necessary.

The openly authorized office-closure case is therefore not classified as a breach merely because review helped persuade voters. A mandate can validly reserve deliberation while accepting substantial dependence. Conversely, implementers cannot turn an adopted practical reservation into deliberation alone through their own interpretation. Where the public record does not settle which was adopted, the special duty is not established; clarification and ordinary duties of candour remain relevant. This sacrifices some of D0's apparent coverage. I reject an automatic inference from any influential promise of review to the stronger duty.

### M2 — Benefits and dependence can share a cause

**Accepted.** R1 §5 will work through scarce specialists rather than assume all dependence is administrative waste. Pooling staff can improve reliability while making alternatives harder to operate. Its real gains remain reasons for continuation. The comparison will be between feasible packages of trial, preparation, and forgone benefits, not the implemented system and an imaginary costless fallback.

Two variants will matter. If shared clinical staffing can coexist with a funded alternative allocation process, saving that preparation cost needs authorization-sensitive justification. If any adequate exit sacrifices indispensable care, the original practical reservation cannot simply be delivered by better planning: the mandate must be narrowed or the dependency risk separately accepted. This is a burden of justification, not a general causal test separating good consequences from bad ones. The manuscript will not call every implementation-created constraint illegitimate.

### M3 — The adverse-review test mixes risk, responsiveness, and substantive rejection

**Accepted; the test will be replaced with a staged inquiry in R1 §4.** An unfavorable measurement is not already a legitimate verdict to terminate. First identify the authorized failure grounds and consider whether they could warrant discontinuation after counting genuine benefits and ordinary transition costs. Then ask whether a feasible, rights-respecting route can respond to those grounds within the accepted transition constraints. Finally identify additional impairment attributable to the chosen design relative to feasible preparations.

The maintained-fallback/modestly-missed-target case is a functioning review, not a disabled power. A usable route can rationally remain unchosen. Also, one route must be responsive to each covered failure, not merely one failure selected by implementers. A control-restoring fallback that reproduces a serious data-security defect is not responsive to that second ground.

Preparation will be risk-sensitive: likelihood, severity, precaution cost, shared coverage across scenarios, and threats to other essential services matter. Remote foreseeable risks need consideration, not unlimited insurance. A mandate can accept residual risk; an adopted promise cannot be weakened silently. No complete probability threshold or decision algorithm will be claimed.

### m1 — Transition time

**Accepted.** R1 §1 will stipulate that the first county has no funded or staffed transition deliverable within the horizon adopted for the review. The relevant problem is not the impossibility of an overnight stop. R1 §4 will explain that a tolerable horizon must track the severity of continuing exposure and continuity needs, not an arbitrary universal period.

### m2 — Different bodies control implementation and money

**Accepted.** R1 §6 will allocate duties by power. Administrators can preserve records, formulate contingency plans, seek funds, warn the authorizer, and avoid steps outside the mandate. The appropriating body must consider financing, rescoping, or declining the trial. No agency will be assigned an unconditional duty to spend money it cannot lawfully obtain. The institutional constraint does not disappear because powers are divided.

### m3 — Three judgments can use the same evidence and vote

**Accepted as clarification.** R1 §8 will distinguish present protection, substantive policy authorization, and past conduct without isolating their evidence. A transparent decision can both protect users and confer durable authorization. What fails is automatic vindication by renewal, not combined decision-making.

### U1 — Additional normative ground or familiar application?

**Partly addressed, not conclusively resolved.** R1 §3 will compare otherwise identical implementations under different legitimate mandates. In one, practical rejection is reserved; in the other, specified durable exposure is already accepted. The same welfare argument cannot give implementers equal discretion in both. The reservation creates an additional reason to seek permission before sacrificing exit, and a remedial reason to restore a public power even where nobody predicts that it will be exercised. This is an application of limits on delegated authority, not a new foundational moral value. General autonomy or justice may also explain it. The manuscript will not claim irreducibility to every existing theory.

### U2 — Disputed independent duties

**Retained as a substantive limit.** Essential assistance is stipulated as an independent obligation in the example. The exact strength of recipients' control claims is not stipulated as a settled right. Review can legitimately investigate their weight. R1 §§4 and 6 will acknowledge that neither a reservation nor the exit test settles contested rights or the status of newly created benefits. This leaves difficult applications conditional on further moral judgment.

### U3 — How much specification is required?

**Accepted as a scope problem, not a demand for an algorithm.** The revision adds the distinctions that prevent false classification. It does not pretend that proportionality can be settled mechanically. Objectives may change through an authorized revision; keeping an effective decision open does not require freezing everything learned in the experiment. The core example survives, while ordinary ambiguous review clauses remain outside the established result.

## Review B: literature, accuracy, and contribution

### Load-bearing checks and the closest comparisons

**Accepted.** Fresh publisher passages confirm the close comparison with [Bergen](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11948-015-9697-2) and [van de Poel](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11948-015-9724-3). R1 §7 will acknowledge their stopping tests, proactive preparation, and defeasible ethical duties. These are predecessors, not merely strategic advice. [Listokin's sunset discussion](https://yalelawjournal.org/pdf/752_qxhyobos.pdf), pp. 534–536, also prevents presenting the connection between reversibility and permission under uncertainty as new.

The Brown comparison will explicitly include procedural expectations. Selected pages of the assigned [accepted manuscript](https://ueaeprints.uea.ac.uk/id/eprint/65003/1/Accepted_manuscript.pdf), especially pp. 16–18 and 25, support that correction. The revised argument will concern a valid allocation of public authority that remains binding without actual predictive confidence in effective refusal, not simply an absence of confidence in policy permanence. This is a narrower contrast, not a refutation of Brown.

The Green comparison will identify already anticipatory risk-management responsibility and use the direct locator, [§II.B](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/jopp.12231), particularly the passages corresponding to rendered lines 194–217. The county example will not be represented as automatically falling within Green's category of characteristic legislative transitions.

The Levinson–Sachs comparison will emphasize morally acceptable execution of a reserved verdict, rather than just obstruction of majority preferences. Absence of strategic intent is not offered as a new distinction. The Dorf–Sabel comparison will retain its modest scope: selected original-reprint passages already connect successful services to adequate political institutions. No absence claim about either complete theory will be made.

Wallsten's fresh opening failed. His assigned, SHA-verified publisher-text capture was read and will be cited as policy commentary about political constituencies, not a verified general empirical law. Its close framing and transition-planning overlap will be acknowledged. The failed new opening remains saved.

### B1 — Rebuild the literature section

**Accepted.** R1 §7 will start with responsible social experimentation and Listokin, then locate the limited authority-based argument among entrenchment and transition theories. Its candidate contribution is the combination of a specific authorization trigger, service/governance separation, and distinct repair reasons. It is not the invention of exit planning or an exhaustive novelty finding.

### B2 — Brown and Green boundaries

**Accepted**, as described above. The distinction concerns what grounds the public institution's duty, not discovery of anticipation or expectation-independent assistance.

### B3 — Defend the bridge and positive costs

**Accepted.** R1 §§2–3 will explain why implementation that foreseeably converts temporary control into unavoidable extended control changes the scope of the authorized imposition. Positive preparation follows where it is a reasonable necessary condition of carrying out the limited project. If such preparation cannot reasonably be secured, the duty is to seek changed authorization, rescope, or not proceed on that limited mandate. It is not an unlimited demand to purchase an exit.

### B4 — A noncircular risk acceptance

**Accepted.** R1 §5 will identify what changed authorization must address: the disputed means, likely extended duration, who bears continuing burdens, plausible adverse outcomes despite which dependence may force continuation, feasible alternatives and their opportunity costs, and residual uncertainty. A competent process must independently justify those exposures and respect rights. Neither disclosure nor asserting that preservation is expensive constitutes permission.

### B5 — Range and horizon

**Accepted**, through the responsive-route and risk distinctions answering A M3 and m1. Multiple routes may be needed; complete historical reversal is not required.

### B6 — Conduct and outcome

**Retained.** There will be no rule of automatic termination, no equation of compensation with renewed authorization, and no retrospective permission supplied merely by success. Continuation costs can still be relevant evidence in a later valid authorization.

## Remaining boundaries

The revised argument will still depend on defensible public authority, contested rights, and proportionate risk judgments. It will establish no general interpretation of vague review language. It will not settle whether the authority-based reason is independently original relative to every broader account of autonomy or experimental ethics. Kuyper's inaccessible full text and Collingridge's unconsulted book remain unresolved inherited leads, not cited evidence. Independent confirmation of Dorf–Sabel's terminal publisher page remains unavailable; assigned corroboration is preserved without treating this as a proved citation error. All inherited retrieval records and new failures are retained. Hashing, copying, or extracting a source is not represented as full reading.
