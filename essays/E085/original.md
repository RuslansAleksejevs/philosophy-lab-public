# Inquiry Before the Question

**Abstract.** Norms of inquiry are standardly indexed to questions: what an inquirer ought to do is
fixed relative to some Q. I argue that this indexing cannot govern the stage at which formulating
the question is itself the work, and that a question-indexed norm reaches that stage only by
borrowing its content. I propose that inquiries are individuated, and their norms indexed, by
*aporiai*: sets of an agent's own commitments that she cannot, from her present position, jointly
discharge. Because an aporia is fixed by commitments she already has, it can be the parameter of a
norm governing the work that produces the question — which no question can be. The model recovers
question-indexed norms as a special case, separates the apt double-checker from the compulsive one,
makes zetetic negligence intelligible, and answers Meno's second horn. Dewey anticipated much of the
diagnosis; the indexing claim and the machinery are new.

---

## 1. The unit of inquiry

Epistemology has been enlarging its subject matter: instead of asking only what an agent should
believe at a moment, it asks how she should conduct the activity that produces her beliefs. Jane
Friedman calls the norms governing that activity *zetetic*, and her complaint against the older
picture is well put: there is "a kind of temporal parochialism to normative epistemology as it's
currently done that is not obviously well motivated. What's so special about the moment of belief
formation?" (Friedman 2020a: 26). The complaint is right. But the framework built to answer it
inherits a version of the same defect, one stage further back.

That framework treats a *question* as the unit. Friedman's zetetic instrumental principle says: "If
one wants to figure out Q?, then one ought to take the necessary means to figuring out Q?" (2020a:
503). Her ignorance norm says that "subjects shouldn't both know Q? at t and have an interrogative
attitude towards Q? at t" (2020b: §3.1). Her diagram of an inquiry runs from the moment a question
goes onto the agenda, through active investigation, to settlement (2020b: §2). Call the underlying
commitment **Erotetic Primacy**:

> **(EP-i)** *Constitutive.* To inquire at t is to be, at t, in an interrogative attitude towards
> some question Q.
>
> **(EP-ii)** *Indexing.* The norms governing an inquiry take that question as a parameter: what the
> inquirer ought to do, and whether she is doing well, is fixed relative to Q.

Friedman does not assert (EP-i) unrestrictedly. She notes that "[t]here may well be parts of inquiry
during which we don't quite have a clear question before our minds", her focus being on
"determinately question-directed inquiry" (2020a: 9–10), and wonders whether inquiry might begin
"before S has even really formulated a question they want to answer", adding: "I'm not sure there's
a clear answer" (2020b: §3.2). The target is therefore not a straw man; but the hedge protects
(EP-i) and leaves (EP-ii) untouched. Every norm specific to inquiry *as inquiry* — the instrumental
principle, the ignorance norm, the coherence constraints on research agendas — takes a question in
its parameter slot, and so is silent where Friedman's hedge applies.

Not every proposed norm of inquiry is question-indexed. David Thorstad's general account is not: its
parameter is an act and the reasons the agent possesses, so that "S's X-ing at t is rational if and
only if in X-ing at t, S responds correctly to the normative reasons that she possesses at t"
(Thorstad 2023: §2). But that is the absence of an indexing rather than a rival one, and his account
is deliberately not an account of what individuates an inquiry — as he grants in facing the
objection that he "succeeds by changing the subject" (2023: §6). Among accounts treating inquiry as
a distinctive kind, question-indexing is the standard; the alternative is to give up the kind.

I want to re-index it. What individuates an inquiry is an **aporia**: a set of the agent's own
commitments that she cannot, from the position she is in, jointly discharge. Inquiry is the activity
of removing an aporia by improving that position. Questions are instruments for that work, but
products of it at least as often as starting points. The thesis is a generalization, not a
replacement: question-indexed norms are the special case in which the obstruction is a missing item
of information within a space the agent commands.

## 2. The formulation problem

**(1) Ana.** Ana comes away from her weekly meeting with a colleague feeling flattened, and this
happens for months. She believes nothing false about the meetings and has no complaint she could
state. She replays exchanges, tries descriptions that do not fit ("he interrupts me" — he doesn't;
"he disagrees with me" — he does, but so do others, harmlessly), notices a pattern in *when* the
flattening occurs, and eventually arrives at a formulation: *is he treating my objections as
complaints?* Once she has that question, answering it takes one meeting. The months were not a
prelude to the inquiry; they were the inquiry.

**(2) The uneasy reader.** A reader works through an argument that seems valid, whose premises seem
true, and whose conclusion he cannot accept, suspecting no particular step. After a week he locates
an equivocation between the second and fourth premises. Before that there was no question he was
trying to answer; afterwards, none left to answer.

Contrast a boundary case. A service fails once a day with no pattern in the logs; if the failure
lies in a space of components the engineer already commands, she has a good question — *which
component?* — with a surveyable answer-set, and bisection takes the necessary means to it. That case
becomes Ana's only when no partition she commands has a true cell.

Ana and the reader share what I call the **formulation problem**: at the times that matter there is
no question Q such that the agent has an interrogative attitude towards Q and her activity is well
described as taking the means to figuring out Q. This is not idling. Ana could have been lazier or
less honest; the reader could have given up. There is zetetic quality on display, and none of it is
quality *relative to a question*. I do not claim that this is where most inquiry happens; the
thesis needs only that the region is real and that competence in it is assessable.

## 3. Why the question cannot be the parameter

### 3.1 The dilemma of grain

First reply: Ana does have a question, namely *what is going on in these meetings?* But the guidance
a question-indexed norm delivers is a function of the answer-set the question determines. So: either
her question determines an answer-set she grasps, or it does not.

Take the first horn. Ana cannot, at month two, characterise the candidate complete answers to *what
is going on?*; if she could she would be nearly finished, since what she eventually achieves *is*
the specification of one candidate. To say she grasps the answer-set is to say she already has what
the inquiry was for. This horn is the important one, and nothing below touches it.

Take the second horn: she has the question, but its complete answers are unsurveyable. It is
tempting to say the norms then go idle, since almost nothing is a necessary means to figuring such a
question out. That is too quick, in two ways.

First, necessity is modal, and coarse ends do have necessary means: Ana cannot figure out what is
going on without attending to the meetings, retaining what was said, and declining to deceive
herself, and the instrumental principle forbids omitting these. But these are the necessary means to
*any* coarse end whatever. Such a norm is not vacuous; it is non-discriminating, which is the defect
that matters. What separates Ana's good months from her bad ones is not whether she attended but
*what she attended to*.

Second, Friedman flags that the necessary-means formulation is a deliberate weakening: "While on the
question of strength I am partial to a maximizing norm, we only need a weaker principle for the
arguments to come, so I've formulated ZIP as a demand to take the necessary means to our epistemic
ends" (2020a, preprint §3). A maximizing norm — take the *best* available means to figuring out Q? —
does discriminate. Does it rescue the architecture?

No. To rank means as better or worse *for figuring out Q* is to rank them by expected progress
towards settling Q, and where Q's answer-set is unsurveyable the agent has no independent handle on
expected progress. What she has instead is a judgement about where the trouble lies: she tracks
*when* the flattening occurs rather than cataloguing *what was said*, because she suspects the
defect is in uptake rather than content. That judgement does the ranking. So the maximizing norm is
not false in the pre-question region; it is **parasitic** there, converting a judgement about the
defect into a verdict about means while contributing nothing to the judgement. Hence a
generalization rather than a refutation.

### 3.2 The externally individuated question

Second reply: the question individuates the inquiry from outside, whether or not Ana could state it.
But instrumental norms bind through the agent's own end, and one cannot take the necessary means to
an end one cannot identify; identity becomes retrospective, so that if Ana never reaches the
formulation hers was not a failed inquiry but a non-inquiry, though failed gropings are criticisable
in the same terms as successes; and nothing in her pre-formulation state selects one of the several
questions she passes through as *the* question the episode was into.

## 4. The formal rescue

Decision theory can evaluate questions by the expected value of the information they would yield,
and Friedman notes that this machinery may bear on norms for opening questions (2020b: n. 12). But
it takes as given what the inquirer lacks: expected value of information is computed over a fixed
algebra with a prior on it, and a candidate question must already be a partition of that algebra.
What Ana needs is not a ranking of the partitions in her current space but a new distinction — not
an update on an algebra but a change of algebra. Nor can one evade this by putting the finer
partition into the algebra from the start, since to have it there *is* to have the concept: an agent
who can represent *he treats my objections as complaints* well enough to assign it a prior has
already done Ana's months of work.

The gap is recognised in work on awareness growth. Joe Roussos observes that "in Bayesian models all
resolutions of uncertainty take place by updating pre-existing beliefs … and so Bayesianism leaves
no room for agents to learn about genuinely new states of affairs", and distinguishes *awareness
revision*, "the formation of a new subjective awareness state", from *belief extension* on the new
space (Roussos forthcoming: §1). His own "formal model of awareness revision" is offered as "a
partial proposal" (abstract). So the point is not that nobody models the step, but that such a model
is not yet a norm an inquirer can act under: it says how awareness states are related, not which
distinction this agent, stuck in this way, has reason to look for. This is the formal echo of Meno's
second horn — "if you find what you want, how will you ever know that this is the thing which you
did not know?" (*Meno* 80d, trans. Jowett). Where the answer-set is the achievement, a criterion of
success requiring it in advance cannot be applied; §6.3 supplies another.

## 5. The aporetic model

### 5.1 Position, commitment, aporia

By an agent's **commitments** I mean whatever she is answerable for: her beliefs and acceptances,
the presuppositions of her plans and of the questions she does ask, her intentions, her
expectations, and the standards to which she holds herself. Commitments are *discharged* when they
are met — a belief by being true, a plan by being executed, a standard by being satisfied. By her
**position** I mean her information; her conceptual repertoire, the distinctions she can draw and
the propositions she can entertain; and the modes in which she holds what she holds, with what
security, on what basis, and checked to what degree.

> **Aporia.** A set Γ of S's commitments is an aporia for S at t iff there is no exercise of S's
> capacities from her position at t that discharges every member of Γ.

> **Inquiry.** Inquiry is activity aimed at removing an aporia by improving one's position.

Joint dischargeability can also be restored by abandoning a commitment or by changing one's
circumstances; those are repairs but not inquiries. Inquiry is the species of repair that proceeds
by finding out, by distinguishing, or by coming to understand. This is one notion with a structured
parameter, not a disjunction of "the contents cannot all hold" and "she cannot see how to hold them
together": there is a single impossibility of joint discharge from a position, and three ways a
position can be deficient.

**Conflict.** The contents cannot all hold, so no position would do. The uneasy reader accepts each
premise, accepts the validity, and rejects the conclusion.

**Gap.** The contents are consistent, but her position does not determine what discharge requires.
An *informational* gap is a missing value in a space she commands: her plan specifies one action if
p and another if not-p, and she cannot tell which. A *conceptual* gap is a missing space: the
reconciling description is not in her repertoire. Ana's is a conceptual gap. Her commitments to her
colleague's good faith, to her own reliability as a reader of a room, and to the flattening being
real are perfectly consistent, as her resolution shows; what she cannot do is discharge all three at
once, having no description under which they sit together.

**Strain.** The content is fine and the information is there, but the *mode* of holding falls short
of a standard she also holds: she believes the calculation is right, and also holds herself to not
acting on unchecked arithmetic where a mistake would cost someone money.

The taxonomy predicts the reach of the erotetic framework. Informational gaps are exactly the case
in which a question with a surveyable answer-set is already available — which is why its stock
examples (where are my keys, how many windows) are all of that kind. Conflicts and conceptual gaps
are the cases in which the question is the achievement.

### 5.2 Where questions come in

A question is not what an inquiry is *of* but a tool it may acquire. To formulate one is to commit
to a *shape* for the defect: the obstruction will go once we settle which cell of this partition
obtains. A good question is one whose settlement would restore dischargeability and which the
inquirer can settle; a bad one is well formed, answerable, and beside the point. So arriving at a
question can be an inquiry's main achievement, since it encodes a diagnosis; and inquiries can
succeed without answering any question posable at the outset, since an aporia can also go by
rejecting a presupposition, revising a standard, or drawing a distinction that lets the conflicting
commitments live together.

### 5.3 The norms

> **(AX) Indexing.** The parameter of a zetetic norm is an aporia. An agent has reason to inquire at
> t only if she then has an aporia whose removal the inquiry would serve, and it is that aporia, not
> any question, that fixes what counts as proceeding well.

> **(AIP) Aporetic instrumental principle.** If one has an aporia, one ought to take the necessary
> means to removing it.

> **(LOC) Localization.** Where one cannot articulate one's aporia, articulating it is a necessary
> means to removing it. So (AIP) requires localization work, and ranks probes that discriminate
> between candidate localizations above probes that do not.

> **(FEAS) Feasibility.** Inquiry is the apt repair for an aporia only if some attainable
> improvement in one's position would remove it. Where none would, the aporia calls for revision of
> the commitments, not for inquiry.

> **(PR) Priority.** The urgency of an aporia is a function of the range of one's other commitments
> that cannot be discharged while it stands.

### 5.4 Why an aporia can be a parameter where a question cannot

The obvious objection is that the dilemma of grain recurs. Either Ana grasps her aporia finely, in
which case she has most of the diagnosis; or she grasps it coarsely — "something here doesn't fit" —
which is true of every reflective adult at all times and tells her nothing. One tempting reply is
that an agent has standing access to her own commitments in a way she does not have to an answer-set
she has not conceived. That reply is false in the flagship case: the commitment Ana must articulate
— *I am a reliable reader of what is going on in a room* — is one she discovers by finding it under
strain, and stating it is about as hard as stating the question.

The asymmetry is not about access but about what a norm's parameter can be fixed by. A norm can be
indexed to a parameter the agent cannot articulate, provided the parameter is fixed by something she
already has. An aporia is so fixed, being constituted by commitments she has and the position she is
in. So there is a fact about which of her commitments are not jointly dischargeable, independent of
her ability to state it — which is why her candidate articulations are answerable to something and
can be wrong. "He interrupts me" is not an unpromising question; it is a *false localization*,
falsified by the very commitments that constitute the defect: she attends to the next meeting, he
does not interrupt, and the flattening persists. A question of the right grain is not so fixed:
before she makes it, there is no fact about which refinement of *what is going on?* is hers. A norm
indexed to the fine question has no parameter to take; one indexed to the coarse question reaches
the choice of refinement only parasitically.

That asymmetry converts into guidance, via (LOC). Ana at month two might replay exchanges, try
further descriptions, or track when the flattening occurs; the first two generate candidate
localizations, the third discriminates between them, and (LOC) ranks it above them.

Here is a verdict the two models do not share. Suppose Ana at month three adopts the question *does
he interrupt me?* and settles it: he does not. On the erotetic model this is a completed inquiry — Q
was opened, the means taken, a complete answer is known, and the ignorance norm now forbids
inquiring further. Nothing marks the episode as a failure, because by those lights nothing failed.
On the aporetic model the inquiry failed, since the aporia stands; and the model says what to do
with the failure, because the persistence of the flattening after a true negative answer is evidence
about the *location* of the defect and directs the next localization away from conversational
mechanics and towards uptake. The erotetic model can only say "open another question". The guidance
on offer is thus comparative and local: less than an algorithm, more than a redescription.

## 6. Three payoffs

### 6.1 Double-checking, and when to stop

Elise Woodard has argued persuasively that one can know that p and rationally double-check p,
seeking certainty, resilience, or sensitivity to error (Woodard 2024: §§1–3). The model endorses her
data, and should not claim to supply what she omits: her first footnote says that "at a certain
point, checking again is irrational" and refers the reader to "Friedman (2019a) and Woodard
(forthcoming)". One of those works answers the question, and not in an agent-relative way: Friedman
argues that checking cycles through suspension and belief, and that a checker whose position keeps
improving eventually enters the SPECs zone — circumstances "too good or strong for suspension of
judgment" — after which "they can only continue checking by having an unjustified doxastic attitude"
(Friedman 2019: §4, n. 21).

The aporetic model adds not a rival ceiling but the structure below it. Double-checking is apt when
there is a *strain* aporia: a standard the agent holds herself to that her present holding of p does
not meet. That is a claim about *this* check at *this* time, which an appeal to permanently
available epistemic goods cannot supply. It yields a scaling rule — raise the stakes, or introduce a
reason to fear a specific error, and you introduce a standard the current holding fails — and a
stopping point, since when the standard is met the aporia is gone.

The hard case is the compulsive checker. Her standard — certainty that the door is locked — is one
no amount of checking meets, so she has a *standing* strain aporia; and since it blocks her leaving
the house, (PR) ranks it *high*. The model thus seems to license, even to prioritise, the pathology.
(FEAS) is the repair, and it is not ad hoc: it falls out of inquiry's being the species of repair
that proceeds by improving one's position. No attainable improvement removes her aporia, so her
checking is defective precisely *as* inquiry, and what her urgent aporia requires is the feasible
repair, revision of the standard. Friedman's own description points the
same way: pathological checkers are on "wavy trajectories", where "information is gained at the
check but then later lost" (2019: n. 10) — a failure of exactly the condition (FEAS) states. The
model also explains the appeal of the ignorance norm: for informational gaps the aporia just *is*
the ignorance, so the two agree, and that norm is a correct special case mistaken for a general
rule.

### 6.2 Zetetic negligence

We criticise people for not inquiring. A manager whose stated standards and whose actual practice
cannot both be honoured, and who has never once put the matter to himself, is open to a criticism
that is not merely moral. This is awkward for an architecture whose norms are indexed to a question
he does not have. The charge should not be overstated into circularity, since Friedman supplies
coherence constraints that generate requirements to open questions from other questions one has:
"if you're wondering about whether Joe or Ali went to the party, then plausibly you should also be
wondering about whether Joe went to the party or wondering about whether Ali went" (2020b: §3.1).
That is not circular; it is limited, since it cannot reach the manager, who has no relevant question
open.

On the aporetic model the criticism is straightforward and its ground is the agent's own. An aporia
is constituted by commitments he already has and does not wait on his noticing; negligence is the
failure to detect, or the refusal to acknowledge, a defect of one's own, and because aporiai need
not be felt, a person can be criticised precisely for the absence of the feeling. Whether this makes
the norms *epistemic* I leave open. Against Thorstad's "[t]here are no epistemic norms of inquiry"
(Thorstad 2022: §1), the model grounds zetetic criticism in a defect internal to the agent's
commitments rather than in her interests — though since commitments include intentions and plans, it
may equally look like a defect of agency.

### 6.3 A negative criterion of success

On the erotetic model success is believing a complete answer to Q, which requires possessing Q's
answer-set in advance. Where the answer-set is what the inquiry produces, that cannot be applied. On
the aporetic model the criterion is negative and local:

> **(NC)** An inquiry has succeeded at t when (i) the commitments that constituted the aporia are
> jointly dischargeable by S from her position at t, and (ii) that position accounts for the
> obstruction — it explains why they were not jointly dischargeable before.

You need no advance grasp of the answer, only the ability to tell that the obstruction is gone; and
you have defeasible access to that, because the obstruction was constituted by your own commitments.
Ana need not have known what she would find; she needs to be able to hold together, at last, her
reading of the meetings, her colleague's good faith and her own reliability.

Clause (ii) is what blocks abandonment. Without it, Ana could drop her commitment to her own
reliability, decide she is oversensitive, and satisfy the criterion with what remains. Excluding
abandonment from the definition of *inquiry* does not exclude it from the criterion; clause (ii)
does, because mere dropping explains nothing. If "I am oversensitive here" is adopted because it
explains the pattern, it is a diagnosis and not an abandonment, and calling the inquiry successful
is right. The criterion is nonetheless defeasible — the appearance of relief is what dogmatism
manufactures — and what tests a repair is time: commitments include commitments to act, action on a
false repair fails, and the failure generates fresh undischargeability. That is the same criterion
applied later, not a positive one smuggled in.

Aristotle stated the payoff first. Having said that "it is not possible to untie a knot of which one
does not know", he adds: "besides, a man does not otherwise know even whether he has at any given
time found what he is looking for or not; for the end is not clear to such a man, while to him who
has first discussed the difficulties it is clear" (*Metaphysics* 995a24 ff., trans. Ross).

## 7. Predecessors

**Peirce.** "The irritation of doubt causes a struggle to attain a state of belief. I shall term
this struggle inquiry"; and, against the erotetic picture: "the mere putting of a proposition into
the interrogative form does not stimulate the mind to any struggle after belief. There must be a
real and living doubt, and without this all discussion is idle" (Peirce 1877: §IV). Two contrasts
hold: an aporia need not be felt, whereas Peirce's doubt is an irritation that ends when the feeling
does; and joint dischargeability is not settlement of opinion, which is why the tenacious believer
has not removed his aporia though he has quieted his doubt. A third does not: he derives
truth-aptness in the same section, by way of a doubt created in the place of a belief that does not
"truly guide our actions" (§IV), so the residue is only that the model routes the correction through
undischargeability downstream rather than through felt doubt.

**Dewey.** This is the serious anticipation. Dewey defines inquiry as "the controlled or directed
transformation of an indeterminate situation into one that is so determinate in its constituent
distinctions and relations as to convert the elements of the original situation into a unified
whole"; the original situation "is open in the sense that its constituents do not hang together"
(Dewey 1938: 104–5). He de-psychologises the starting point: "We are doubtful because the situation
is inherently doubtful. Personal states of doubt that are not evoked by and are not relative to some
existential situation are pathological" (106). And he states the formulation problem outright: "It
is a familiar and significant saying that a problem well put is half-solved. To find out what the
problem and problems are which a problematic situation presents to be inquired into, is to be well
along in inquiry" (108). He even asks how the formation of a genuine problem is controlled, and
answers with a localization procedure (108–9). Any claim that a structural, unfelt conception of the
starting point of inquiry is new must therefore be given up.

One difference is substantive and I rest on it. Dewey's unit is a *situation*, and situations are
precognitive: "[t]here is nothing intellectual or cognitive in the existence of such situations,
although they are the necessary condition of cognitive operations or inquiry" (107). Mine is a set
of the agent's own commitments together with her position. The model sits between Peirce and Dewey:
unlike Peirce's doubt it need not be felt; unlike Dewey's situation it is constituted by things the
agent is answerable for. That is what lets the defect ground criticism *of the inquirer*, and what
makes it a parameter an agent can be under a norm with respect to.

**Collingwood** belongs on the other side: he substitutes "for a logic of propositions … a logic of
question and answer" (Collingwood 1939: 37), holds that "the primacy belongs to the questioning
activity" (35), and endorses the principle he found in Bacon and Descartes, that "knowledge comes
only by answering questions, and that these questions must be the right questions and asked in the
right order" (25) — registering the demand without an account of how it is met.

What I claim, then, is: the indexing thesis, which concerns an architecture that did not exist when
Dewey wrote; the argument of §5.4; and the machinery — the position-relative definition, the
taxonomy with its informational/conceptual split, and the norms (LOC), (FEAS) and (PR) with the
payoffs in §6. I do not claim the underlying picture, which is Dewey's; and I have not read Laudan
or Hookway, so anticipation from that quarter cannot be ruled out.

## 8. Objections

**Profligacy.** *Every agent's commitments contain countless undischargeable subsets, so the model
licenses inquiry into everything.* The first part is true and the second does not follow: (AX)
states a necessary condition and (PR) shapes what follows from it. An aporia that blocks nothing
else is not worth repairing; one that blocks a plan or a body of further beliefs is. This yields
something like clutter avoidance as a consequence rather than an axiom, and without appeal to the
triviality of subject matter — which matters, since Friedman denies that the information a zetetic
norm makes us ignore need be "clutter for us" (2020a: §4.5).

**Idle curiosity.** *Wondering why boats are called* S.S. *something involves no defect.* It
involves an informational gap: a commitment to completeness on a topic one cares about, unmet. Such
cases are where inquiry most nearly *is* question-shaped, which tells for the model, since they are
the home region of question-indexing and the model says why.

**Agent-relativity.** *The stopping rule stops when the agent's own standard is met, and the model
cannot criticise standards.* Largely conceded: it criticises a standard only through that standard's
collisions with the agent's other commitments, which, with (FEAS), is enough for the compulsive
checker but is not a theory of which standards it is reasonable to hold. Hence the external ceiling
adopted above, since Friedman's SPECs argument does not depend on the agent's standards at all.

**Vagueness of "commitment".** *The notion carries the weight and is undefined.* It is defined by
its role — whatever the agent is answerable for — and that role is doing work rather than standing
in for it: because commitments include standards the model predicts strain aporiai, and because they
do not require notice it predicts the intelligibility of zetetic negligence. A vaguer notion would
predict neither. A full theory owes an account of how aporiai are counted and when two are the same;
I do not have one.

---

## 9. Conclusion

The zetetic turn corrected epistemology's temporal parochialism by extending evaluation backwards
from the moment of belief to the activity that produces it. But it stopped at the question. Where
the question is the achievement, a question-indexed norm either has no parameter to take or takes a
coarse one and borrows its verdicts from a judgement about where the trouble lies. What an inquiry
is answerable to is a defect in the inquirer's own commitments: a parameter that individuates
inquiries including the ones that fail, that can be possessed before it can be stated, and that
recovers question-indexed norms as a special case. The knot comes first.
