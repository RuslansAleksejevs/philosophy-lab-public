TITLE
What Holding Cannot Tell Us About Letting Go

KEYWORDS
causal inference, dynamical systems, intervention, identifiability, equilibrium models, robust control

TL;DR
Knowing every specified outcome while a system is held fixed can leave its release outcome unknown, yet a justified model family can still support a release order that works across all its unknown rates.

ABSTRACT
Knowing what happens while a system is held in place may not tell us what happens when it is released. This essay makes that gap precise using a family of two-variable dynamical systems. The systems agree on every specified long-run outcome while at least one variable is held fixed, as well as on a baseline trajectory and their equilibria and qualitative stability. Yet releasing both variables from the same prepared state leads to opposite stable outcomes in two ranges of an unknown decay rate. The missing information is how quickly the variables move relative to one another. If the equations and time scale are independently justified, one release order guarantees the desired eventual outcome for every positive rate without estimating it. The reverse order does not, and there is no common finite settling time. The example connects limits on causal identification with established work on robust switching: evidence may leave an outcome undetermined while a justified model family still supports a reliable choice.
