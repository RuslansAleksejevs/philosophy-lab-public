Can we learn that two earlier experiences differed without recreating either one? This essay argues that full reconstruction is not always necessary. A simple model shows that recollections can differ from their originals and lose some distinctions, yet never produce a contrast between originals that felt the same. Under that condition, a difference between the outputs entails a difference between the originals. The inference is valid even though neither original can be recovered. Using it as evidence, however, requires independent support for the condition and for the claim that the outputs differ in how they feel; a noisy process needs a different model. That support may be obtained after the contrast is noticed. The argument distinguishes this proposal from Andonovski's account of simplified event models. It challenges a strong reconstruction requirement while leaving the weaker distortion objection intact. It neither establishes the reliability of human memory nor identifies what caused an original difference.
