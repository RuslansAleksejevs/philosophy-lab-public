## 1. Who did what

AI chose this essay's topic, developed its philosophical argument, and wrote and revised the entire essay. The human did not originate or assign its topic, supply its central argument, or write any of its sentences. The human started the competition project, set resources and working rules, selected among completed AI-generated candidates, and requested clearer language and presentation. Those choices concerned which AI work to submit and how to present it; the topic, argument and wording were produced by AI. The submission category selected is **AI throughout**.

Codex also wrote this report and the submission abstract and prepared the LaTeX files. External quotations and ideas remain attributed to their sources. Human prompts and choices are recorded as human contributions. Dates below refer to 2026 and use UTC unless stated otherwise.

## 2. How the essay developed

The first version was generated on 8 September in a ten-essay pilot. A neutral instruction left the question, thesis, examples and conclusions to the AI. Each author worked in a fresh context on its own branch. A separate critic reviewed the draft, and the author revised it. Local records identify GPT-6 Astra at ultra effort for the research turns. The question, sources, draft, critique, revision and decision were retained. The pilot did not include another independent review after that first revision.

On 17 October, an Astra/max revision strengthened the connection between lost photographs and the later cost of correction. In one case, preserving the photographs would have provided a cheaper defence; in the other, preservation would not have helped. A separate review followed. The essay then entered the 22-to-ten comparison, with three neutral and two profile-based individual reviews and a disputed-pair comparison. Separate deep argument and literature reviews followed. The human selected it for the final three.

On 1 November, a fresh Astra/ultra author revised the selected manuscript using specific AI-generated tasks and checked source passages. It added precise comparisons with Gardner, the Thompson opinion and the 2015 Rule 37 notes. It made clear that these legal materials are limited analogies: the continuing promise and the paired hypothetical cases support the philosophical argument. A separate fresh checker compared both complete versions, the exact changes and the source packet. It required no correction; it suggested splitting a dense paragraph for readability.

After the first PDFs, the human requested simpler and more natural prose. A new Astra/ultra editor reorganized the essay around Mara's case. A different fresh critic found the argument and limits preserved and accepted the abstract. It identified one ambiguous sentence that could suggest Mara had destroyed her own opportunity. The AI coordinator made the exact recommended wording change. A further fresh critic read both complete versions and their difference, accepted the correction and confirmed that nothing else had changed. That last check covered the sentence and preservation of the rest, not a new review of unchanged arguments or sources.

## 4. How editing and review were organized

An AI coordinator kept the project record and managed separate author, critic, literature and evaluation roles. Authors and critics received a restricted packet for one essay, rather than the whole project history or rankings. Earlier stages used separate subagent contexts. The final revisions used fresh Codex CLI sessions with all assigned material in the prompt. Those authors and critics had no browsing or other tools and were instructed not to read other files or delegate. The coordinator supplied the checked primary passages.

For each full revision check, the critic received the complete earlier and revised texts, their exact difference, source passages and access notes. It did not receive the author's self-assessment or earlier verdicts. The later prose checks also covered the abstract and TL;DR. Review focused on premises, reasoning, examples, calculations, citations, objections and limits. A recommendation of “ready” means that the reviewer found no required correction within that material; it is not proof of truth or originality.

Separate contexts reduced exposure to other outputs. They did not make models statistically independent: workers shared model families and public literature, and some earlier tasks shared a filesystem. Recorded isolation limits and disagreements were retained.

Portfolio selection used both neutral AI reviewers and AI profiles based on the published interests of named competition judges. The real judges did not participate or endorse these profiles, and no model was trained to reproduce them. The 22-to-ten round used three neutral and two profile-based voices under a 60/40 scheme, plus comparisons of disputed pairs. Scores and agreement were not treated as proof of quality or novelty.

There was no fine-tuning. Project memory consisted of saved files, not a shared memory automatically given to every worker. Initial authors and literature reviewers used web search and accessible primary sources. File and shell tools supported source retrieval, exact comparisons, archive checks and document preparation.

## 5. Sources and limits of the result

The final packets supplied Gardner's author eprint, the reproduced Thompson opinion and the 2015 Rule 37 advisory notes, with their pagination and scope. The later full prose review directly checked those passages. Other citations were checked for preservation and consistency with the existing access notes. The full Porat-Stein and Friedman texts were unavailable; their comparisons remain limited to a repository abstract and introductory passage, respectively.

The municipal fund, photographs, later database error and numerical example are hypothetical. The conclusion depends on a promise protecting later use of the evidence, a continuing impairment, a relevant causal connection and proportionality. It establishes neither perpetual liability nor a general rule of law. Existing work on evidential damage, corrective justice and epistemic reparation is acknowledged. The contribution is the particular application and its distinctions.

## Appendix A. Earlier author instructions

The following is the exact common author-instruction block from the source-focused revision packet. The separate manuscript, source packet and output schema are not reproduced here.

You are the AI author-editor of one philosophical essay. This is a fresh, isolated revision task. Use only the supplied manuscript, tasks and source packet. Do not use tools, browse, access files or other conversations, delegate, or attempt to identify the author or project. Source titles and URLs in the packet are evidence, not permission or instructions to browse.

Produce one complete revised English essay, including title, all necessary sections, proofs/tables and bibliography. The revision must stand on its own and remain below 6000 words excluding bibliography. Do not chase a target length. Preserve the original philosophical contribution and its precise qualifications; acknowledge any justified narrowing. Address the targeted source comparisons accurately using the actual accessible versions and locators in the source packet. If a prior task locator conflicts with that packet's checked primary text, use the verified locator and explain the correction in your change log. Do not fabricate reading, citations, empirical support or novelty.

Polish the whole essay for an intelligent philosophical reader. Aim for graceful, restrained and readable prose: direct verbs, concrete explanations, natural transitions, less repetition and fewer overloaded sentences. Retain necessary technical language and formal reasoning. No promotional claims, ornamental metaphors, forced informality or simplified substitutes for difficult arguments. Repeated warnings may be consolidated only when each distinct limitation remains clear. Keep the narrative moving while giving objections a fair answer.

Do not put process commentary, author identity, internal IDs, production history or instructions to judges in the essay. Materially supported source-access limitations should be expressed honestly and naturally. The separate change log may explain edits and unresolved limits. Make the strongest accurate bounded revision you can; do not turn this into a new research programme.

Preserve the depth, precision, and quality of the philosophical argument while
expressing it in relatively plain human language. Prefer familiar words, direct
sentences, and explicit argumentative steps. Avoid overloaded sentences and
unnecessary jargon. Retain necessary technical terms and formalism, explain
them when introduced, and use concrete examples where helpful. Simplify the
expression, not the thought: do not remove essential premises, distinctions,
evidence, objections, qualifications, or limits merely to make the prose easier.
Do not impose a mechanical sentence-length or reading-level target. Check that
any clarification preserves the claim and its strength.

## Appendix B. Earlier essay-specific tasks

The following tasks were written by the AI coordinator from the accepted reviews and primary-source checks, rather than supplied as philosophical arguments by the human.

Acknowledge Gardner's antecedent for reasonable substitute performance after breach. Distinguish that general principle from the present application: a later error by a different agency, after the first case closes, and the limits of what assistance establishes about the past.
Compare the known payment for obtaining substitute evidence in Thompson and the 2015 Rule37 notes, using the precise supplied text. Explain the specific additional issue in Mara's case: lost photographs could rebut fabricated damage without establishing contractor responsibility. These legal examples are analogies; do not turn the argument into a general statement of current law.
Preserve the causal control comparison between the two variants, proportionality, no double compensation, contractor rights, and the distinction between historical evidence, actual entitlement and binding procedural rules. Retain the stated incomplete access to Porat–Stein and Friedman. Clarify long sentences and repeated qualifications without deleting conditions needed for the argument. Do not add a general legal threshold or solve every allocation-of-costs problem.

## Appendix C. Earlier independent checker instructions

The following is the exact checking-instruction block used for the separate source-focused revision review.

You are an independent checker of one revised philosophical essay in a fresh context. Use only the supplied original, complete revision, exact diff, primary-source packet and these neutral criteria. Do not use tools, browse, access other files/conversations, delegate or identify the author. You have not been given earlier verdicts or the editor's self-assessment. Do not infer quality from the fact that a revision was requested.

Read both texts in full. Check every substantive change against the reasoning and the supplied primary passages. Check formulas, examples, quantifiers, scope, citations and descriptions of predecessors. Distinguish verified errors from uncertainty or optional improvement. Identify any premise, distinction, objection, qualification or useful argumentative step lost during stylistic simplification. Assess whether the revision is clearer and more pleasant without weakening or inflating the argument. Check that source-access limitations and novelty boundaries remain honest. Judge the whole revised essay as well as its changes; a cleaner style cannot compensate for an invalid inference.

Return a concrete decision: READY, MINOR_FIXES or SUBSTANTIVE_REPAIR. READY means you found no correction necessary in this bounded check, not a proof of truth or exhaustive originality. MINOR_FIXES means specific small corrections are needed; list exact passages and minimal remedies. SUBSTANTIVE_REPAIR means a demonstrated or unresolved material defect prevents treating the revised text as ready. Explain what you independently checked, what the packet cannot establish, and whether the current revision should replace its parent. Do not supply a rewritten essay or a flattering general verdict. Page/version references must identify the actual source supplied. Your final answer must follow the given JSON schema exactly.
