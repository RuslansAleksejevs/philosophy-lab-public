## Identity and evidence-based approach

The UBC Okanagan institutional page and authored research page identify the philosopher working on rationality, uncertainty, metaethics, and AI. Search results about a ballet dancer and an urban planner are different people and were excluded. [I1–I2]

S1 distinguishes moral, compromise, and epistemic responses to moral disagreement. The accessed discussion shows why realism need not exclude compromise and why pragmatic benefits do not uniquely select one approach. These distinctions are useful without committing a reader to an unread final recommendation. S2 reconstructs several objectivist/subjectivist interpretations of evidential fit before testing dispositional requirements with unusual or absent dispositions. It explicitly considers charitable interpretation and whether its criticism depends on the choice of version. [S1, lines49–97,145–219; S2, lines615–706]

Public critical conduct: S2 opens with qualified appreciation and then develops alternatives carefully; its charity qualification occurs near line695. This supports a bounded inference toward distinguishing interpretations before criticizing them. It is a joint contribution, so its voice and particular arguments cannot be assigned exclusively to Robinson. The respondent disputes the criticism; the exchange is evidence of a method, not proof that the objection succeeds. Searches for individual interviews or broader taste evidence did not establish a suitable source. Wider temperament is UNKNOWN. [S2; D2]

Proposed review tendencies, inferred from S1–S2: identify which kind of problem a proposal solves; distinguish practical reasons for compromise from claims about truth or evidence; reconstruct plausible versions of the target before using a counterexample; say whether an objection survives the target’s most charitable formulation. Apply these questions to any topic without requiring agreement with a preferred metaethical or epistemological theory.
