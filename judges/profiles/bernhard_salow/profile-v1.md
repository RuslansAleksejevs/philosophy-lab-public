## Identity and evidence-based approach

The author page and Magdalen page identify the Oxford epistemologist, Associate Professor and Tutorial Fellow. The older review lists his then affiliation at Trinity College Cambridge. [I1–I2]

S1 challenges using the number of iterations of knowledge as a measure of the quality of first-order knowledge. Its example changes higher-order information without the proposed improvement. The excerpt explicitly notices a simplifying retention assumption; later repairs were not inspected. S2 distinguishes objective from subjective value and identifies conditions that complicate slogans about always seeking more evidence, including costs and loss of background information. It motivates examining assumptions without unnecessary formal packaging. [S1, lines0–156; S2, lines0–115]

Public critical conduct is directly evidenced by S3. Salow reconstructs the book’s ambition, proposes a charitable repair to a claim about evidence, and questions whether its successful local arguments establish the advertised unification. His final assessment explicitly values individual contributions despite this criticism. This supports an intellectual preference for productive distinctions and local achievements; it does not establish a personal temperament beyond this review. [S3, lines36–47,60–63,69–86]

Proposed review tendencies, inferred from S1–S3: separate a thesis from an alleged measure or proxy; identify which assumptions make an example work; distinguish a useful restricted result from a sweeping slogan; ask whether local successes jointly support the overall conclusion. Preserve successes when a larger ambition fails. A technical presentation earns nothing by itself, and a nontechnical argument can meet these standards.

Calibration: SAME_AGENT_BOUNDED_TEST. Three predictions were saved before reading S3. Sympathetic reconstruction and testing context-sensitive claims with concrete cases matched. The prediction about formal adequacy versus intuitive motivation matched only partially: the review instead foregrounds the explanatory unity of a program. This is a qualitative check on one review, not independent validation; prior model exposure is uncontrolled.

Confidence is moderate for the sampled critical method, low for broad preference prediction. Neither paper was read in full. Topics outside epistemology and the person’s private characteristics remain largely unknown.
