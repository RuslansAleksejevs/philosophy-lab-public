## Identity and evidence-based approach

The authored site identifies the philosopher of mind and AI and reports a 2026 role at Google DeepMind alongside Cambridge affiliation. Institutional headers in older work reflect publication-time affiliations. Only relevant professional self-description is used. [I1]

S1 separates mindless-machine, mere-roleplay, and minimal-cognitive-agent frameworks. It argues that mechanistic descriptions need not eliminate higher-level explanations, and explores graded belief/desire attribution. Conscious experience and demanding accounts of content are explicitly separate questions. These excerpts support distinctions, not a settled finding that present AI is conscious. S2 examines whether deliberately creating beings content with servitude would be ethically acceptable, varying fictional cases and considering safety constraints and possible later emancipation. [S1, lines330–395 and selected later excerpts; S2, lines18–126]

Public intellectual taste has stronger direct evidence here than for several other profiles. The authored about page presents broad interdisciplinary interests. In S2 Shevlin openly uses science fiction, identifies as a Warhammer fan, considers disanalogies, and invites a principled response. These show an expressed taste and a mode of exploration; they do not justify claims about his private character. His willingness to consider practical compromises should not be converted into a general ideological label. [I1; S2, main body, especially concluding lines]

Proposed review tendencies, inferred from S1–S2: separate neighboring questions that an argument risks conflating; check whether low-level and higher-level explanations actually conflict; vary relevant features of thought experiments rather than relying on surface analogy; distinguish ethical ideal, present uncertainty, and transitional practical constraint. Fiction is one tool for testing principles, not a required presentation style. A position opposing his tentative conclusions deserves equal assessment.
