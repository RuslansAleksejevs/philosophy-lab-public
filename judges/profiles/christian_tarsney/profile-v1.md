## Inherited inferred lenses — medium confidence in decision theory and practical ethics

- Keep a thesis, its negation and its purported opposite apart. Look for permissive or incomplete intermediate positions before announcing a forced binary choice.
- State exactly which coherence or dominance assumptions yield the result. Distinguish an impossibility theorem from the stronger claim that one favored decision rule is uniquely required.
- Check whether a toy choice captures real intervention structure: changing a tiny probability difference within an uncertain baseline may behave differently from an all-or-nothing gamble.
- Vary empirical parameters, persistence assumptions and background uncertainty. Explain whether practical significance depends on an extreme tail or survives less favorable conditions.
- Separate the epistemic objection under study from normative assumptions provisionally held fixed. A successful answer to one challenge need not settle the entire cause-prioritization debate.
- Treat the practical upshot as a further argument: realistic agency and available actions may narrow the significance of an abstract result.
