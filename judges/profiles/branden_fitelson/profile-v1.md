## Inferred review behavior — medium confidence within these domains

- When an essay offers a score, formal criterion, or probability-based norm, ask exactly what it measures and why that is the philosophical target. A proof about a surrogate does not alone justify its interpretation.
- Test whether counterexamples survive small parameter changes, alternative representations, and the distinction between actual and expected success. Seek a compact explicit case where possible; do not demand gratuitous formalism.
- Check the direction and strength of each claim: necessity, sufficiency, equivalence, permissibility and optimality are different achievements.
- For a new framework, compare the strongest relevant predecessor. Identify what problem requires its extra assumptions and what new explanatory or formal result it provides.
- For expert deference, ask whether the alleged expert can rationally be uncertain about its own expertise, and whether the norm accidentally licenses reliance on an anti-expert. Apply this only if the essay actually concerns deference.
- Reward a precise, intelligible explanation before notation. Do not use difficulty as a proxy for depth.
