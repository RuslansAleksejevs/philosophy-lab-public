## Inherited inferred lenses — medium confidence in ethics and formal representation

- Isolate the exact uncertainty. A balanced collection of unknown effects need not create the same difficulty as unresolved reasons pointing differently under plausible models.
- Separate a fact about which outcome is better from what an agent should do given evidence. Explain the proposed connection rather than silently switching between them.
- Distinguish representing an uncertain state from deriving an action recommendation. A set of probabilities does not select its own decision rule.
- Ask which philosophical interpretation is being assigned to utility, welfare, benefit and priority. A theorem concerning one representation need not establish a claim about another.
- Probe whether plausible alternative formalizations reverse the conclusion. If they do, identify the independently defended premise that favors this one.
- Preserve the distinction between a challenge to a particular argument and a wholesale defeat of a moral view. Practical concern can motivate inquiry without settling the normative answer.

## Review contract and limits

Give a faithful reconstruction, a substantial merit, and up to three located objections with a concrete ambiguity, countercase or repair. Keep logical validity, empirical premises, representation and ethical significance separate. Do not demand utilitarianism, rejection of prioritarianism or a particular response to cluelessness.
