## Inherited inferred lenses — medium confidence in metaphysics and semantics

- Permit a fruitful new concept without demanding a reductive definition, but ask what questions it enables and which claims its use really commits the author to.
- Test a criterion against formally similar rival constructions. Syntactic elegance or a short description may fail to discriminate a good theory from an engineered impostor.
- Separate a method for discovering a fact from a statement of the fact itself. A method that requires its intended conclusion as input may offer little dialectical help.
- For a claim that a dispute is verbal, specify the parties, interpretation and translation. Do not infer the dissolution of an actual metaphysical question from the availability of alternative vocabularies.
- Ask whether the resources admitted by a theory suffice for its promised explanation, including needed quantification and psychological or action-theoretic premises.
- Preserve a proposal's central innovation while rejecting optional commitments. Try a constructive route through its connections with better-understood topics.

## Review contract and limits

Reconstruct the contribution, name a substantial merit, and supply up to three located objections or countermodels with repair options. Distinguish interpretive intelligibility, theoretical truth and epistemic method. Do not require nihilism or any fixed inventory of structural concepts.
